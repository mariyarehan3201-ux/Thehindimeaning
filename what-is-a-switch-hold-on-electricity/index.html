What Is a Switch Hold on Electricity?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

A switch hold on electricity is an official administrative block placed on a residential or commercial electric meter in deregulated energy markets (most notably in Texas under ERCOT and the Public Utility Commission of Texas). It legally prevents a customer from switching electric utility providers until an unpaid deferred payment balance is satisfied or meter tampering investigations are resolved.

The Regulatory Purpose of an Electricity Switch Hold

In deregulated retail electric markets—most prominently across Texas governed by the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) and regulated by the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT)—consumers enjoy the market freedom to shop among dozens of competing Retail Electric Providers (REPs). If a customer is unhappy with their electric kilowatt-hour rate, they can easily sign up with a competitor online and switch service in a few mouse clicks.

Historically, however, this competitive flexibility created an abusive loophole: customers would accumulate thousands of dollars in unpaid electricity bills with Provider A, and right before their power was disconnected for non-payment, they would jump to Provider B, leaving Provider A with uncollectible bad debt. To balance consumer flexibility with retail provider solvency, the PUCT established the official Switch Hold Rule under Texas Administrative Code (16 TAC § 25.480).

A switch hold is a digital lock placed on your physical Electric Service Identifier (ESI ID)—the unique 17-to-22-digit number identifying your physical electric meter. The Transmission and Distribution Utility (TDU, such as Oncor, CenterPoint Energy, AEP Texas, or TNMP) marks the ESI ID in the central ERCOT database as blocked. Any attempt to switch retail electric providers will be automatically rejected by the automated ERCOT clearinghouse until the hold is legally cleared. The table below delineates the two primary legal reasons a switch hold is placed on an electric meter.

Switch Hold Classification Regulatory Trigger Condition Governing Texas Administrative Rule Requirements to Remove Hold
Payment Arrangement / DPP Hold Customer enrolled in a Deferred Payment Plan for unpaid bills 16 TAC § 25.480(l) Pay off full deferred balance in cash or installments
Meter Tampering / Diversion Hold Physical meter bypass, broken seals, electric theft 16 TAC § 25.126 Pay tampering investigation fees & repair charges
Prior Resident Hold (New Tenant) Previous tenant vacated apartment leaving active hold 16 TAC § 25.480(m) Submit New Occupant Statement (NOS) & signed lease
Erroneous System Hold Administrative data entry error by REP or TDU PUCT Consumer Protection Division File formal expedited complaint with PUCT

How a Switch Hold Impacts New Tenants and Homebuyers

While the switch hold mechanism was designed to prevent dishonest bill skipping, it frequently ensnares completely innocent consumers. The most common scenario occurs when a tenant signs a lease on a new apartment or buys a foreclosure home, calls an electric company to turn on power, and is stunned to hear: 'We cannot turn on your electricity because there is an active switch hold on this meter.'

The previous occupant may have enrolled in a deferred payment plan for an unpaid $800 electric bill and abruptly moved out in the middle of the night without notifying their provider. The retail electric provider leaves the switch hold active on that physical meter address in the ERCOT database. Because the switch hold attaches to the physical ESI ID meter rather than the person's Social Security Number, the new, innocent tenant is locked out of establishing electric service.

Fortunately, the Public Utility Commission of Texas anticipated this problem and established strict consumer protection rules. A retail electric provider is legally forbidden from holding a new, unrelated occupant liable for a prior resident's unpaid utility debt. The law provides an expedited legal mechanism to remove prior-resident switch holds in 24 to 48 hours. The table below compares the process for existing debtors versus new occupants.

Occupant Classification Financial Responsibility for Balance Documentation Required to Lift Hold Time to Resolve Switch Hold
Existing Customer on DPP 100% responsible for paying deferred debt Proof of final payment transaction receipt Within 24 to 48 hours of payment clearing
New Unrelated Tenant $0 (Zero responsibility for prior tenant debt) Signed lease agreement + New Occupant Statement (NOS) Within 24 hours of document verification
New Homebuyer (Purchase) $0 (Zero responsibility for prior owner debt) Closing disclosure, deed, or utility bill Within 24 hours of document submission

Step-by-Step: Removing a Switch Hold as a New Occupant

If you are a new renter or buyer blocked by a prior occupant's switch hold, you can resolve the issue swiftly by following a precise four-step compliance process.

First, download the official Public Utility Commission of Texas New Occupant Statement (NOS) form from your chosen electric provider's website. The NOS is a formal legal affidavit in which you swear under penalty of perjury that you are a new occupant of the premise and have zero financial or familial relationship to the previous occupant who incurred the debt.

Second, gather your primary proof of occupancy. This is typically a complete, signed residential lease agreement showing your move-in date and landlord contact info, or an official HUD-1 closing statement if you purchased the home. Submit the NOS and lease to your new retail electric provider's Switch Hold Department via email or secure portal. By law, the new REP must transmit the removal request to the TDU (Oncor, CenterPoint) within one business day, allowing your power to be turned on immediately.

How to Remove a Switch Hold on Your Electric Meter

Step-by-step procedure to clear a prior resident's switch hold and activate your electricity service.

  1. Obtain Your 17-22 Digit ESI ID Number

    Check your electric meter casing or ask your landlord for your unique Electric Service Identifier (ESI ID) number.

  2. Complete the Texas New Occupant Statement (NOS)

    Download and fill out the official PUCT New Occupant Statement affidavit affirming you are a new tenant with no relation to prior debtors.

  3. Attach Certified Proof of Occupancy Documentation

    Provide a complete copy of your newly signed lease agreement showing lease start dates, or a real estate closing settlement statement.

  4. Submit Documents to Your Chosen Electric Provider

    Email the NOS and lease to your provider's switch hold team; the provider is mandated by law to clear the hold with the TDU within 24 hours.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What causes a switch hold on an electric meter?

A switch hold is triggered when a customer enters a deferred payment plan for an unpaid bill, or when meter tampering/electric diversion is detected.

Q2: Can an electric company make me pay the previous tenant's bill?

No. Under Public Utility Commission of Texas rules, a new unrelated occupant can never be forced to pay a prior tenant's unpaid electricity debt.

Q3: What is a New Occupant Statement (NOS)?

A formal legal affidavit submitted by a new renter or buyer certifying they just moved in and are unrelated to the person who left the unpaid bill.

Q4: How long does it take to remove an electricity switch hold?

Once you submit a completed NOS and lease agreement, the electric provider and TDU typically remove the switch hold within 24 to 48 business hours.

Q5: What states have switch holds on electricity?

Switch holds are most common in Texas's deregulated ERCOT electricity market, though similar utility blocks exist in other deregulated states.

Q6: Can I switch electric companies if I have a switch hold?

No. The ERCOT central database will automatically block any attempt to switch retail electric providers until the switch hold is formally cleared.

Q7: Who do I complain to if an electric company refuses to remove a switch hold?

You can file an expedited consumer complaint directly with the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT) Customer Protection Division.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

A switch hold on electricity is an administrative safeguard that prevents customers from switching electric providers while carrying unpaid deferred balances or unresolved meter tampering charges. If you owe the debt, paying your deferred arrangement in full is the only path to removal. If you are an innocent new tenant or homebuyer, submitting a signed lease and New Occupant Statement legally forces the electric utility to lift the hold within 24 hours, restoring your freedom to choose power.
