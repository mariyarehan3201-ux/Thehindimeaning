The Architectural Advantage: In-Wall Recessing vs. Freestanding Boards

Traditional freestanding ironing boards represent an awkward, inefficient relic of domestic utility design. They require dragging a wobbling metal apparatus out of a closet, untangling electrical cords, and struggling to fold the legs back into place, often leading to closet clutter and accidental drops.

Iron-A-Way revolutionized this workflow by designing a cabinet that recesses flush between standard interior wall studs spaced 16 inches on center. The cabinet protrudes less than two inches into the room, concealed behind unfinished, stained oak, birch, or mirrored shaker cabinet doors. When opened, the solid ventilated steel ironing board glides effortlessly down into position on heavy-duty gas springs or counterbalance hinges, supported by rigid telescoping structural legs that eliminate wobble under heavy commercial pressing pressure.

The Multi-Layered Electrical Safety Architecture

The defining engineering feature that separates Iron-A-Way from generic wall boards is its comprehensive, multi-redundant electrical safety system. Leaving an electric iron plugged in and unattended on a fabric ironing board is one of the leading causes of residential electrical structure fires.

Iron-A-Way models feature an integrated 60-minute mechanical safety shut-off timer, a built-in illuminated power switch, and a heavy-gauge internal insulated heat shield compartment capable of storing an iron immediately after use while still scorching hot (up to 450°F). Crucially, the cabinet incorporates a positive-action mechanical micro-switch: the instant the ironing board is lifted toward its upright closed position, power to the internal electrical outlet and spotlight is automatically severed. Even if an iron is left fully turned on, closing the cabinet door physically shuts off electrical current, preventing accidental fires.

Installation Mechanics: Recessed Framing vs. Surface-Mount Collars

Installing an Iron-A-Way center is typically executed during new home construction or major laundry room remodeling, where the wall cavity is open and accessible. Carpenters frame a standard rough opening (14-3/8 inches wide) by installing horizontal 2x4 headers between two vertical studs. Electricians run a dedicated 120V, 15-amp branch circuit into the top electrical knockout of the cabinet. The cabinet is then leveled, plumbed, and secured directly to the side studs using heavy structural lag screws.

However, if the desired installation location involves a solid masonry wall, an exterior concrete basement wall, or an interior wall packed with plumbing drain pipes that cannot be moved, Iron-A-Way manufactures optional wood surface-mount trim collars. The collar wraps around the exterior of the cabinet, allowing the unit to be mounted directly to the surface of the drywall without opening the wall cavity.

How to Install a Recessed Iron-A-Way Cabinet

Step-by-step roadmap to frame a rough opening between studs, connect 120V electrical wiring, and secure an in-wall ironing center.

  1. Select Wall Location and Locate Wall Studs

    Use a stud finder to identify a 16-inch on-center stud bay free of plumbing pipes, pocket doors, or structural HVAC ducts.

  2. Cut Drywall and Frame 14-3/8 Inch Rough Opening

    Cut drywall cleanly, installing horizontal 2x4 top and bottom headers to establish the exact rough opening height specified by the model.

  3. Rough-In 120V 15-Amp Electrical Supply Cable

    Route a 120V non-metallic Romex electrical cable through the top knockout hole into the cabinet's internal junction box.

  4. Slide Cabinet into Wall and Level Assembly

    Slide the cabinet into the framed opening, inserting shims until the unit is level, plumb, and square with the finished drywall.

  5. Drive Structural Screws and Attach Cabinet Door

    Drive structural cabinet screws through the side mounting holes into the studs, connect electrical pigtails, and hang the door.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does an Iron-A-Way ironing board need to be plugged in?

Electric Iron-A-Way models require hardwiring into a standard 120V, 15-amp electrical circuit to power the internal safety timer, spotlight, and iron outlet. Non-electric models require zero wiring.

Q2: Can an Iron-A-Way be surface mounted without cutting into the wall?

Yes. Iron-A-Way manufactures optional hardwood surface-mount trim collars that wrap around the cabinet, allowing the entire unit to be mounted directly on the drywall surface.

Q3: What is the auto-off safety feature on Iron-A-Way?

Electric models feature a dual safety system: an automatic 60-minute countdown timer, plus a mechanical micro-switch that physically cuts all electrical power to the iron the moment the board is folded up.

Q4: Can you store an iron in the cabinet while it is still hot?

Yes. Iron-A-Way cabinets feature a premium insulated heat barrier storage compartment engineered to safely house an iron immediately after use, even when heated to 450°F.

Q5: Do Iron-A-Way ironing boards swivel?

Yes. Custom and Deluxe models feature a full 90-degree left and right swiveling mechanism, allowing you to angle the board to accommodate tight laundry room corners and left-handed users.

Q6: What are the rough opening dimensions for an Iron-A-Way?

Most standard models require a rough opening width of exactly 14-3/8 inches (to fit between 16-inch on-center studs), with heights ranging from 41-1/4 inches to 51-1/4 inches depending on the model.

Q7: What height should a built-in ironing board be mounted at?

The bottom of the cabinet rough opening is typically positioned between 26 and 30 inches above the finished floor, placing the deployed ironing board at an ergonomic working height of 35 to 36 inches.

Q8: Can an Iron-A-Way be installed in a bathroom?

Yes, provided the electrical circuit is protected by a certified Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter (GFCI) pursuant to National Electrical Code (NEC) wet room standards.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

An Iron-A-Way built-in ironing center is the gold standard for luxury laundry room organization, custom master closet suites, and boutique hospitality design. By replacing cumbersome folding boards with a flush in-wall cabinet, integrating heavy steel swiveling ironing surfaces, and incorporating multi-layered electrical safety switches that cut power when the door closes, Iron-A-Way elevates daily garment care into a seamless, elegant, and fire-safe experience.
