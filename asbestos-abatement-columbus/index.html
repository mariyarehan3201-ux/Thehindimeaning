Asbestos Abatement Columbus

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

Asbestos abatement in Columbus, Ohio, is governed by stringent environmental standards enforced by the Ohio Environmental Protection Agency (Ohio EPA) under Ohio Administrative Code (OAC) Chapter 3745-20. Across Columbus, Bexley, Upper Arlington, and surrounding Franklin County communities, historic and mid-century properties frequently contain hazardous asbestos that requires certified containment, removal, and disposal.

Ohio EPA Regulatory Framework and Notification Rules

In Columbus and across the state of Ohio, environmental compliance for asbestos remediation is overseen by the Ohio EPA Division of Air Pollution Control. Ohio Administrative Code Chapter 3745-20 incorporates federal NESHAP standards, requiring building surveys prior to renovation or demolition in commercial, public, industrial, and institutional facilities. Ohio law mandates that all physical abatement work be performed by contractors licensed through the Ohio Department of Health (ODH).

For projects involving demolition, or renovations that disturb regulated asbestos-containing material (RACM) exceeding 160 square feet, 260 linear feet, or 35 cubic feet, a formal Ohio EPA Project Notification form must be submitted at least ten working days prior to beginning work. Compliance officers from the Ohio EPA Central District Office conduct unannounced site inspections across Columbus to ensure negative-air enclosures, HEPA filtration systems, and decontamination facilities meet state specifications.

Common Building Materials in Columbus and Abatement Costs

Columbus’s housing stock features architectural styles spanning several eras. Historic brick homes in German Village, Victorian Village, and Clintonville often contain gravity furnace duct wrap, boiler insulation, and decorative plaster. Mid-century ranch homes and suburban developments in Worthington and Dublin commonly contain acoustic popcorn ceiling textures, 9x9-inch vinyl asbestos floor tiles, and exterior cement Transite siding shingles.

Abatement pricing across the Columbus metropolitan area reflects project complexity, containment requirements, and local waste disposal tipping fees. Removing fifty linear feet of basement heating pipe wrap generally costs between $1,400 and $2,800. Popcorn ceiling scraping across a 1,200-square-foot home ranges from $4,500 to $9,000, while whole-house remediation projects can exceed $12,000. All hazardous waste must be double-bagged in six-mil poly, labeled with EPA markings, and transported to licensed facilities such as the Solid Waste Authority of Central Ohio (SWACO) landfill.

Hiring an ODH-licensed abatement contractor carrying dedicated asbestos pollution liability insurance protects property owners against unexpected property damage or toxic fiber release.

Securing an independent third-party air clearance certificate upon project completion provides documented proof of clean indoor air quality for future home sales.

How to Execute Asbestos Abatement in Columbus

Steps for Columbus property owners managing asbestos abatement.

  1. Schedule an Ohio Certified Asbestos Inspection

    Retain an ODH-certified building inspector to survey and sample suspect materials prior to remodeling.

  2. Obtain Competitive Bids from ODH-Licensed Contractors

    Solicit itemized proposals from at least three licensed contractors carrying valid pollution liability insurance.

  3. Submit the Ohio EPA Ten-Day Project Notification

    Ensure the contractor submits the notification to Ohio EPA Central District Office at least ten working days prior to work.

  4. Verify Independent Clearance Air Testing

    Retain an independent industrial hygienist to conduct PCM clearance air sampling before containment barriers are removed.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does Columbus require a permit for asbestos removal?

Ohio EPA requires a formal 10-day notification for commercial projects and renovations exceeding regulated material thresholds.

Q2: Can Columbus homeowners remove asbestos themselves?

Ohio law allows owner-occupants of single-family detached homes to perform work on their primary residence, though licensed contractors are recommended.

Q3: How much does asbestos removal cost in Columbus?

Standard residential projects range from $1,400 for basement pipe wrap to $8,000+ for whole-home popcorn ceiling removal.

Q4: Where can asbestos waste be disposed of in Columbus?

Asbestos waste must be manifested and hauled to licensed landfills permitted for asbestos, such as SWACO's designated disposal cell.

Q5: What historic Columbus neighborhoods have the highest asbestos risk?

Older neighborhoods such as German Village, Victorian Village, Clintonville, and Bexley have extensive historic asbestos use.

Q6: What agency licenses asbestos contractors in Ohio?

The Ohio Department of Health (ODH) licenses and regulates all asbestos abatement contractors, supervisors, and workers.

Q7: What is the standard clearance air level in Ohio?

Phase Contrast Microscopy (PCM) clearance requires airborne fiber counts below 0.01 fibers per cubic centimeter (f/cc).

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Asbestos abatement in Columbus requires balancing historic home preservation with strict Ohio EPA regulatory compliance. By retaining ODH-certified contractors, honoring state ten-day notification waiting periods, and confirming air clearance through independent industrial hygienists, Columbus property owners protect family health and preserve real estate value.
