Full Form of CHA: Custom House Agent Roles and Licensing

Full Form
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 18, 2026

The full form of CHA is Custom House Agent. Regulated under Section 146 of the Indian Customs Act, 1962, and governed by the Customs Brokers Licensing Regulations (CBLR), a Custom House Agent—now officially designated as a Customs Broker—is a licensed professional or corporate entity authorized to act as an accredited intermediary between commercial importers/exporters and national customs authorities for cargo clearance.

Understanding CHA: The Vital Conduit in International Trade

International cross-border commerce involves intricate legal documentation, tariff classification systems, foreign exchange declarations, and stringent compliance audits. For commercial importers and exporters, navigating port customs terminals, bonded warehouses, and maritime container freight stations (CFS) presents enormous bureaucratic and regulatory complexity. A Custom House Agent (CHA) operates as a licensed legal expert specializing in customs tariff codes, duty assessment procedures, and statutory clearance formalities.

Under Indian maritime and aviation law, while an importer or exporter possesses the legal right to clear their own cargo, the vast majority engage licensed CHAs. Customs brokers maintain direct digital interfaces with national automated clearance networks (such as ICEGATE and the Indian Customs EDI System), preparing statutory declarations, verifying documentation, and ensuring duty payments conform to the Customs Tariff Act.

Core Responsibilities and Daily Functions of a CHA

The operational duties of a licensed customs broker span pre-shipment documentation, fiscal duty valuation, and cargo physical examination. The table below outlines primary operational domains managed by CHAs.

Operational Domain Statutory Documentation Prepared Primary Regulatory Function
Import Clearance Bill of Entry (Home Consumption or Warehousing) Classifies goods under 8-digit HSN codes, calculates customs duties
Export Formalities Shipping Bill / Export Declaration Form Verifies export incentive eligibility (Drawback, RoDTEP), clears port entry
Regulatory Approvals Allied Agency Clearances (FSSAI, CDSCO, Plant Quarantine) Submits samples for laboratory testing and phytosanitary clearance
Physical Cargo Examination Customs Examination Order / Inspection Endorsement Coordinates container destuffing and verification by customs dock appraisers

Accurate classification under the Harmonized System of Nomenclature (HSN) is critical. Misdeclaring an 8-digit tariff code can cause substantial revenue loss or attract heavy penalties, cargo confiscation, and prosecution under customs anti-smuggling statutes. CHAs conduct exhaustive scrutiny of commercial invoices, packing lists, and test reports to verify precise classification.

Licensing Examination and Statutory Governance (CBLR)

Becoming a licensed Customs Broker requires passing one of the most demanding statutory professional examinations in maritime logistics. The table below details the qualification hierarchy under the Customs Brokers Licensing Regulations.

Licensing Phase / Tier Examination / Statutory Basis Eligibility and Evaluation Criteria
Regulation 6 (Written Exam) Computer-Based Online Examination (CBLR) Graduate degree with 55% marks or MBA/CA/Law qualification
Regulation 6 (Oral Interview) Panel Viva Voce by Customs Commissioners Tests working knowledge of Foreign Trade Policy, Baggage Rules, IP Laws
Regulation 7 (License Issuance) Form B License Issued by Principal Commissioner Requires furnishing statutory execution bonds and financial bank guarantees
Regulation 13 (Authorized Clerks) Form G / Form H Card Examinations Authorizes operational staff to represent broker inside customs docks

Under statutory obligations, a CHA must verify the authenticity of the client's Importer Exporter Code (IEC), Goods and Services Tax Identification Number (GSTIN), and physical operational address using Know Your Customer (KYC) documentation. Failure to exercise due diligence makes the broker legally liable for aiding commercial fraud.

How an Importer Works with a CHA to Clear Container Cargo

Review the procedural stages involved when an importer engages a licensed Custom House Agent to clear sea freight shipments through port customs.

  1. Hand Over Pre-Alert Shipping Documents to CHA

    Provide the CHA with master bill of lading (MBL), commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and purchase order.

  2. Filing Prior or Advance Bill of Entry on ICEGATE

    The CHA files an electronic Bill of Entry before vessel arrival to initiate automated Risk Management System (RMS) processing.

  3. Track Customs Duty Assessment and Verification

    Customs appraising officers verify valuation, exemptions, and anti-dumping duties, issuing an electronic duty payment challan.

  4. Coordinate Port Wharfage and Delivery Orders

    The broker remits ocean freight dues to shipping lines to secure the Delivery Order (DO) and pays port container handling charges.

  5. Complete Dock Inspection and Secure Out-of-Charge

    Customs dock inspectors verify container seal numbers, examine goods if flagged by RMS, and issue the final Out-of-Charge (OOC) order.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the full form of CHA?

CHA stands for Custom House Agent, now officially referred to as Customs Broker.

Q2: Which law governs Custom House Agents in India?

They are governed by Section 146 of the Customs Act, 1962, and the Customs Brokers Licensing Regulations (CBLR).

Q3: What is the difference between a CHA and a Freight Forwarder?

A CHA is a legally licensed specialist clearing goods through customs, while a freight forwarder organizes physical transport logistics.

Q4: What is the qualifying examination to become a CHA?

Candidates must pass the national Regulation 6 written and oral examination conducted by the Directorate General of Performance Management.

Q5: What is a Bill of Entry?

A Bill of Entry is a mandatory legal declaration submitted to customs specifying cargo description, quantity, value, and duty payable.

Q6: Can an individual import goods without a CHA?

Yes, importers can self-clear their own shipments via ICEGATE, but most hire CHAs due to legal complexities and terminal logistics.

Q7: What KYC documents must a CHA collect from clients?

The CHA must collect verified copies of the client's PAN card, GSTIN certificate, Importer Exporter Code (IEC), and bank solvency proofs.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The Custom House Agent (CHA), officially termed Customs Broker, serves as the indispensable logistical engine powering international supply chains. By ensuring statutory customs compliance, optimizing cargo transit times, and protecting national customs revenue, licensed CHAs bridge commercial trade with national economic security.
