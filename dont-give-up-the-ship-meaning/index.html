Don't Give Up the Ship Meaning

Meaning
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

The phrase 'Don't give up the ship' is one of the most celebrated and stirring battle cries in United States naval history. Uttered as the dying dying command of Captain James Lawrence aboard the frigate USS Chesapeake on June 1, 1813, during the War of 1812, the phrase was subsequently immortalized by Master Commandant Oliver Hazard Perry. Perry emblazoned the words in bold white letters across a coarse blue battle ensign, hoisting it aboard his flagship USS Lawrence during the pivotal Battle of Lake Erie on September 10, 1813. Today, the slogan stands as an enduring American military symbol of indomitable courage, perseverance in adversity, and unwavering resolve.

The Tragic Death of Captain James Lawrence

The origin of the phrase lies in a bloody naval duel fought during the height of the War of 1812. On June 1, 1813, the thirty-one-year-old Captain James Lawrence sailed his frigate, USS Chesapeake, out of Boston Harbor to engage the veteran British Royal Navy frigate HMS Shannon, commanded by Captain Philip Broke. Within minutes of opening fire, British cannonades swept the Chesapeake's decks, mortally wounding Lawrence with small-arms fire through the abdomen.

As crew members carried their dying captain below to the surgeon's cockpit, Lawrence looked up at his officers and issued his final, desperate command: 'Tell the men to fire faster and not give up the ship; fight her till she sinks!' Although the battered Chesapeake was ultimately boarded and captured by British marines, Lawrence's heroic dying words were immediately published in newspapers across the young republic, transforming him into a national martyr.

Oliver Hazard Perry and the Triumph at Lake Erie

Lawrence's close friend and fellow naval officer, Oliver Hazard Perry, was deeply shaken by Lawrence's death. Tasked with building an American fleet from raw forest timber on the shores of Lake Erie, Perry named his 20-gun brig flagship the USS Lawrence in tribute. In secret, Perry commissioned Margaret Forster Stearns and women in Erie, Pennsylvania, to sew a massive eight-by-nine-foot dark blue silk-and-wool battle flag featuring Lawrence's dying words: 'DONT GIVE UP THE SHIP'.

On September 10, 1813, as the American squadron engaged the British fleet at Put-in-Bay, Perry hoisted the flag to the main royal masthead. When the USS Lawrence was shot to pieces with four out of five crewmen killed or wounded, Perry performed an act of daring courage: he lowered a rowboat, hauled down his famous blue flag, and rowed through heavy cannon fire to transfer command to the fresh brig USS Niagara. He re-hoisted the flag, broke through the British battle line, and forced the surrender of the entire Royal Navy squadron—the first time in history an entire British naval fleet was captured intact.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Who first said 'Don't give up the ship'?

Captain James Lawrence of the US Navy said it on June 1, 1813, as his dying command during the War of 1812 aboard the USS Chesapeake.

Q2: Who put 'Don't give up the ship' on a flag?

Oliver Hazard Perry had the words sewn onto his famous blue battle flag to honor his friend James Lawrence during the Battle of Lake Erie.

Q3: What war was 'Don't give up the ship' from?

It originated during the War of 1812, fought between the United States and the United Kingdom.

Q4: Did James Lawrence's crew give up the ship?

Despite Lawrence's command, the battered USS Chesapeake was boarded and captured by British sailors of HMS Shannon.

Q5: What was Oliver Hazard Perry's famous victory message?

After winning the Battle of Lake Erie under the flag, Perry wrote: 'We have met the enemy and they are ours: two ships, two brigs, one schooner and one sloop.'

Q6: Where is the original 'Don't give up the ship' flag today?

The original preserved battle flag hangs in Memorial Hall at the United States Naval Academy in Annapolis, Maryland.

Q7: What does the phrase mean in modern everyday language?

It means to persevere with courage, refuse to surrender, and keep fighting through extreme difficulty without abandoning hope.
