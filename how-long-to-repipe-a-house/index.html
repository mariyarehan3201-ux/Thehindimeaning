How Long to Repipe a House?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Repiping a house typically takes between 2 and 5 business days from start to finish. A standard single-family home (1,800 to 2,500 sq ft with two to three bathrooms) requires 2 to 3 days for active plumbing installation and municipal pressure testing, followed by 1 to 2 days for drywall patching, texturing, and painting.

The Full Project Lifecycle: Plumbing Installation to Drywall Finish

When an older home's galvanized steel, defective polybutylene, or aging copper plumbing begins developing recurring pinhole leaks, dropping water pressure, or producing discolored rusty water, a whole-house repipe becomes unavoidable. However, the prospect of having plumbers cutting into walls and replacing hundreds of feet of water lines naturally leads homeowners to ask: how long does it actually take to repipe a house?

The total project duration is divided into two distinct operational phases: the Mechanical Plumbing Phase executed by licensed plumbers, and the Restoration Phase executed by drywall and finish specialists. For an average residential home, the entire project from initial drop-cloth layout to final textured drywall paint takes between two and five days.

Crucially, repiping a house does not require you to vacate your home or stay in an expensive hotel. Professional repiping crews shut off the main water supply strictly during daytime working hours (typically 8:30 AM to 4:30 PM). Before technicians leave your home each evening, they connect temporary bypass lines and restore active, pressurized water service to your bathrooms and kitchen, ensuring your family has functional toilets, sinks, and showers overnight. Key Factors Influencing the Project Duration

While standard homes require two to three days of plumbing work, the exact timeline for your specific property depends on structural architectural variables and material choices. Understanding these factors prevents unrealistic expectations.

The first major factor is Home Size and Bathroom Count. A modest 1,200 square-foot ranch home with one or two bathrooms can frequently be repiped by a skilled four-man plumbing crew in a single intensive working day. Conversely, a 4,500 square-foot multi-story estate featuring four or five bathrooms, an outdoor kitchen, multiple wet bars, and hose bibs across a wide perimeter will require four to six days of active pipe installation.

The second critical variable is Foundation and Accessibility Architecture. Homes built over accessible crawlspaces or spacious attics allow plumbers to route flexible PEX piping quickly beneath floorboards or overhead through rafters with minimal drywall cutting. However, homes built on solid concrete slab foundations require overhead rerouting through ceiling joists or cutting into interior walls to bypass corroded underground copper lines, adding a full day of labor. PEX vs. Copper: Installation Speed Differences

The choice between flexible Cross-Linked Polyethylene (PEX) tubing and traditional rigid copper pipes is the single greatest determinant of mechanical installation speed.

PEX tubing is exceptionally flexible and can be pulled continuously through pre-drilled holes in wooden wall studs and attic joists just like electrical wiring. A single 100-foot run of PEX can travel from the central distribution manifold all the way to a master bathroom shower without a single intermediate joint or soldered fitting. Furthermore, modern PEX-A expansion fittings take barely 15 seconds to connect using an automated power expansion tool. Consequently, repiping a home with PEX is 40 to 60 percent faster than installing copper.

Rigid copper pipe, by comparison, requires manual measuring, cutting with rotary pipe cutters, deburring, applying flux paste, and soldering (sweating) every individual 90-degree elbow and T-junction using an open propane torch inside tight, flammable wooden wall cavities. Soldering hundreds of copper joints is intensely labor-intensive, adds significant fire watch safety requirements, and extends the plumbing phase by an additional two to three business days.

How to Plan and Prepare for a Whole-House Repipe

Step-by-step homeowner guide to streamline the repiping timeline and minimize household disruption.

  1. Clear All Under-Sink Cabinets and Work Zones

    Remove all cleaning supplies and personal items from beneath every bathroom and kitchen sink, and clear a 3-foot perimeter around your water heater.

  2. Coordinate Daytime Water Outage Schedules

    Plan laundry, dishwashing, and morning showers before 8:30 AM, and store several gallons of tap water for drinking and manual toilet flushing.

  3. Schedule City Plumbing Inspector in Advance

    Ensure your plumbing contractor schedules the municipal building inspector for Day 3 so pressure testing sign-off is not delayed.

  4. Conduct Final Plumbing and Drywall Inspection

    Walk through every room with the contractor on Day 5, testing water pressure on every fixture and verifying seamless drywall texture matching.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: How long does a whole house repipe take?

A complete whole-house repipe typically takes 2 to 3 days for plumbing work, followed by 1 to 2 days for drywall patching, totaling 3 to 5 days.

Q2: Will I have water during a house repipe?

Yes. Water is shut off only during daytime working hours (approx. 8:30 AM to 4:30 PM) and restored every evening for normal nighttime use.

Q3: Do I have to move out of my house while it is being repiped?

No. You can comfortably remain living in your home throughout the entire project since water service is restored every evening.

Q4: Is PEX faster to install than copper?

Yes. Flexible PEX is 40% to 60% faster to install than rigid copper because it requires no torch soldering and can be snaked through walls.

Q5: Why does repiping take longer on a concrete slab foundation?

Because pipes buried in concrete cannot be easily accessed; plumbers must execute a complete overhead reroute through the attic and drop lines into walls.

Q6: How long does the city plumbing inspection take?

The city inspector typically spends 15 to 30 minutes on-site verifying the pressure gauge holds at 100+ PSI without leaks before signing the permit.

Q7: How long does drywall repair take after repiping?

Drywall patching, taping, mudding, texture matching, and priming typically takes 1 to 2 days after plumbing sign-off.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Repiping a house takes between two and five days from initial setup to final textured drywall restoration. Utilizing modern flexible PEX-A piping enables experienced crews to complete the mechanical plumbing phase in just two to three days while ensuring water is restored every evening for your family. By understanding the daily project milestones and planning for municipal inspections and drywall patching, you can navigate a whole-house repipe smoothly and protect your home's water integrity for decades.
