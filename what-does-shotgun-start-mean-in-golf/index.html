What Does Shotgun Start Mean in Golf?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

In tournament golf, charity scrambles, and corporate outings, a "Shotgun Start" is an event organization format where all competing groups of golfers tee off simultaneously on different holes across the entire golf course at the exact same scheduled minute, rather than teeing off consecutively from the first hole.

The Historical Origin of the Shotgun Start

Standard recreational golf relies on consecutive tee times?"where a group of four golfers tees off from Hole 1, followed eight to ten minutes later by the next group. For a major tournament with 144 players (36 foursomes), consecutive tee times require six continuous hours just to send every golfer onto the course, meaning the first group finishes at 1:00 PM while the final group finishes after sunset at 7:00 PM.

The ingenious solution known as the Shotgun Start was invented in May 1956 by head golf professional Jim Russell at the Walla Walla Country Club in Washington state. Seeking a way to get large fields of golfers onto the course together so everyone could enjoy post-round dinner banquets and award ceremonies simultaneously, Russell devised a plan to assign foursomes to different holes across the property.

To signal golfers spread across 150 acres of rolling fairways that it was time to hit their opening tee shots, Russell stepped out onto the clubhouse porch and fired an actual 12-gauge shotgun into the air. The booming blast echoed through the valley, prompting all golfers to swing at the same instant. Today, digital sirens, air horns, or synchronized GPS golf cart displays have replaced firearms, but the iconic name remains.

How Hole Assignments Work: The 'A' and 'B' System

A championship golf course features 18 holes. If each hole hosts one foursome, a shotgun tournament accommodates exactly 72 golfers. However, most popular charity events feature full fields of 100 to 144 players. To accommodate these larger fields, tournament directors assign two groups to specific holes, utilizing an "A" and "B" designation.

For example, if your team is assigned to "Hole 4A," your group has primary honors to tee off on Hole 4 the moment the starting horn sounds. The group assigned to "Hole 4B" waits patiently near the cart path. Once Group 4A strikes their tee shots and drives up the fairway toward their second shots, Group 4B tees off.

From that moment on, play progresses in natural numerical sequence: after completing Hole 4, your group moves to Hole 5, then Hole 6, eventually wrapping around from Hole 18 to Hole 1, and finishing your round on Hole 3. Why Shotgun Starts Dominate Charity and Corporate Events

The primary advantage of a shotgun start is community cohesion and commercial efficiency. Because all thirty-six groups complete their rounds within fifteen minutes of each other, everyone returns to the clubhouse clubhouse patio at the exact same time.

This allows event organizers to host immediate seated charity banquets, conduct live auctions, award raffle prizes, and announce winning teams without making morning players wait five hours for late afternoon finishers to tally scorecards. Furthermore, golf courses can rent out the entire facility for private corporate buyouts during shotgun outings.

How to Prepare For and Play in a Shotgun Start Golf Tournament

Follow these step-by-step procedures to locate your cart, find your starting hole, and navigate a shotgun start smoothly.

  1. Arrive at Least One Hour Before Start Time

    Check in at the registration desk, collect your tournament gift bag, confirm your team's assigned starting hole, and warm up on the range.

  2. Locate Your Assigned Staged Golf Cart

    Find your cart in the staging area by looking for your name or assigned hole number (e.g., Hole 7A) printed on the front windshield tag.

  3. Attend the Mandatory Rules Meeting

    Gather near the putting green fifteen minutes before start time to listen to the head pro's announcements regarding scramble rules and contests.

  4. Drive in the Cart Convoy to Your Assigned Hole

    Follow the marshal or cart caravan out to your starting hole and park safely on the cart path without driving across the tee box.

  5. Wait for the Horn and Maintain Pace of Play

    Do not hit a ball until the official air horn sounds; play promptly, keep up with the group ahead, and return your scorecard to the pro shop.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does shotgun start mean in golf?

A tournament format where groups tee off simultaneously on all 18 holes across the golf course at the exact same scheduled time.

Q2: Why is it called a shotgun start?

In 1956, golf pro Jim Russell fired an actual 12-gauge shotgun from the clubhouse porch to signal players to tee off on different holes.

Q3: What is the difference between Hole 5A and 5B?

Group 5A tees off first when the horn sounds; Group 5B waits and tees off as soon as Group 5A clears the fairway.

Q4: Do you finish on the hole you started on?

No, you finish on the hole immediately preceding your starting hole (for example, if you start on Hole 7, your final hole is Hole 6).

Q5: How long does an 18-hole shotgun start tournament take?

A shotgun tournament typically takes between 4.5 and 5 hours, depending on field size and whether it is a casual scramble format.

Q6: Can anyone play on the course during a shotgun start?

Generally no; the entire 18-hole golf course is closed to public play because every hole is occupied by tournament competitors.

Q7: Why do charity golf tournaments use shotgun starts?

Shotgun starts ensure all 144 players finish together, allowing organizers to hold awards banquets and charity auctions immediately.

Q8: What signal is used today instead of a shotgun?

Modern golf courses use loud air horns, weather sirens, or synchronized electronic messages displayed on GPS golf cart screens.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The shotgun start is one of the most brilliant operational innovations in golf history. By transforming an otherwise fragmented, all-day sporting event into a synchronized, energetic community experience, the shotgun format maximizes camaraderie, speeds up tournament play, and makes charity scrambles enjoyable for golfers of all skill levels. Understanding hole staging and pacing ensures your team tees off with confidence and enjoys a fantastic day on the links.
