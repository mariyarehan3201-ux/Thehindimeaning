How to Diagnose and Reset the Service Tire Monitor System

Standard procedure for clearing TPMS monitor system warnings.

  1. Observe If the Tire Icon Flashes Upon Starting the Engine

    Confirm whether the horseshoe light flashes for 60 to 90 seconds on startup; a flashing light confirms a dead sensor or communication fault, not low air.

  2. Check Which Wheel Displays Dashes ('--') on the Dash

    Scroll your digital cluster to the Tire Pressure screen; the wheel position displaying blank dashes or '0 PSI' indicates the exact failing sensor.

  3. Perform a TPMS Relearn Procedure After Tire Rotations

    If you recently rotated tires, enter TPMS Relearn mode in vehicle settings and trigger each wheel in order (LF, RF, RR, LR) with a TPMS relearn tool.

  4. Scan Wheels with a Handheld TPMS Diagnostic Tool

    Visit a tire shop where technicians use a handheld radio frequency tool to scan each sensor through the tire sidewall to check battery life and signal strength.

  5. Replace Aging Sensors with OEM-Frequency Replacements

    If your vehicle is over 7 years old and one sensor battery dies, replace all four sensors simultaneously to avoid multiple dismounting fees as other batteries fade.

Q1: What is the primary significance of service tire monitor system chevy meaning?

It means the computer cannot communicate with one or more tire pressure sensors, usually caused by a dead sensor battery, broken sensor, or uncalibrated tire rotation.

Q2: How is 'Service Tire Monitor' different from 'Low Tire Pressure'?

'Low Tire Pressure' means tires need air (light is steady). 'Service Tire Monitor' means the sensor itself is broken or dead (light flashes for 60-90 seconds on startup).

Q3: Can I drive my car with the Service Tire Monitor warning on?

Yes, you can drive safely, but the system cannot warn you if you get a flat tire; you should manually check tire pressures with a hand gauge until repaired.

Q4: How long do TPMS sensor batteries last?

Direct TPMS sensor batteries last between 7 and 10 years (or 100,000 miles); once the sealed internal battery dies, the entire sensor must be replaced.

Q5: Why did my tire light come on after a tire rotation?

The car's computer still thinks the sensors are in their old locations; you must perform an electronic TPMS Relearn procedure so the car maps the new tire positions.

Q6: Can Fix-a-Flat ruin a TPMS sensor?

Yes. Liquid aerosol tire sealants coat the sensor's pressure port with sticky chemical gunk, frequently destroying the sensor and requiring replacement.

Q7: How much does it cost to replace a TPMS sensor?

Replacement sensors typically cost between $45 and $90 per wheel, plus nominal labor to dismount and rebalance the tire at a professional tire shop.
