How to Measure for a Window Well Cover?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Installing a heavy-duty basement window well cover is an essential home improvement project that prevents accidental falls, blocks torrential rainwater and melting snow, stops decomposing leaves from rotting in your basement light wells, and keeps pests from nesting against your foundation. However, because steel, corrugated metal, and plastic window wells deform easily under heavy backfilled soil pressure, ordering an off-the-shelf or custom polycarbonate cover requires capturing precise width, outward projection, and foundation wall dimensions.

The Anatomy of Basement Window Wells and Cover Types

Basement window wells are manufactured in three common geometric shapes: semi-circular (round), rectangular, and elongated u-shaped profiles. The well structure itself typically consists of galvanized corrugated steel bolted directly into the concrete foundation, prefabricated composite plastic, or stacked masonry pavers. Over years of seasonal freezing and thawing, backfilled dirt exerts massive lateral pressure on the exterior curve of the well, frequently pushing the center inward or flaring the outer corners outward.

When selecting a cover, homeowners must choose between standard flat covers, sloped low-profile covers, and bubble dome covers designed to accommodate basement windows that extend above the rim of the well. If your sliding or hopper basement window pane protrudes higher than the top flange of the steel well, a flat cover will obstruct window operation or fail to sit flush. Measuring requires identifying both the well's physical dimensions and any structural obstructions like siding overhangs, electrical conduits, or gas meters.

The Three Vital Dimensions: Width, Projection, and Foundation Rise

Measuring for a window well cover requires recording three fundamental dimensions: the Width along the foundation, the Outward Projection away from the house, and the Maximum Center Projection. To measure the Width, place your tape measure against the exterior foundation wall and measure the straight-line distance from the inside left edge of the well flange to the inside right edge. If you are ordering a cover that caps over the outer metal rim, also record the outside-to-outside width.

The Outward Projection represents the distance the well extends perpendicular from the house siding out to the farthest exterior point of the curved front rim. Measure this projection at three distinct points: near the left corner, at the absolute center peak, and near the right corner. Due to soil pressure, the center measurement is almost always different from the side projections. Finally, measure the Foundation Rise: if your basement window frame projects above the top rim of the well, measure vertically from the top edge of the well lip up to the highest point of the window frame or siding trim.

Egress Safety Codes and Emergency Escape Compliance

If your window well serves a finished basement bedroom or living space, it is legally classified as an emergency escape and rescue opening under the International Residential Code (IRC Section R310). Egress building codes mandate that any window well cover installed over an escape window must be operable from the inside without the use of keys, tools, or special knowledge. The cover must unlatch effortlessly under light pressure and must maintain an unobstructed clear opening of at least 5.7 square feet.

When measuring an egress well cover, verify that internal ladder steps or escape rungs do not extend above the rim where they could jam a flat cover. For egress installations, select lightweight polycarbonate covers equipped with quick-release interior spring pins or gas-assist lift struts. Never install padlocks, heavy exterior pavers, or screw-down fasteners that would trap basement occupants during a house fire.

How to Measure a Window Well for a Custom Cover

A step-by-step homeowner's guide to recording width, projection, and clearance measurements for a basement window well cover.

  1. Measure the Width Along the Foundation Wall

    Extend your tape measure along the foundation from the far-left interior edge of the well rim to the far-right interior edge.

  2. Measure the Maximum Outward Projection

    Measure perpendicularly from the exterior foundation wall straight out to the farthest exterior point of the well's front curve.

  3. Measure Side Projections Near Corners

    Measure projection 6 inches in from both the left and right corners to determine if soil pressure has bowed the center of the well inward.

  4. Check for Vertical Window Protrusions

    Measure vertically from the top rim of the well up to the highest point of the window frame to see if a sloped or bubble cover is required.

  5. Inspect Egress Ladders and Siding Overhangs

    Confirm that egress ladders do not protrude above the well lip and verify that house siding allows a minimum 1/2-inch clearance for cover flashing.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Do you measure a window well from the inside or outside?

Always measure both inside-to-inside (to ensure the cover clears the opening) and outside-to-outside (so the cover lip caps over the exterior flange).

Q2: What is the standard size of a basement window well?

Common standard sizes are 37 to 42 inches in width with a 12-to-18-inch projection for utility wells, and 48 to 60 inches wide by 36 inches deep for egress wells.

Q3: Can a window well cover hold the weight of an adult?

Heavy-duty polycarbonate and steel mesh safety covers are rated to support 250 to 500 pounds, easily supporting an adult or child who steps onto them.

Q4: What type of cover do I need if my window sticks up above the well?

You will need a sloped low-profile cover (for 1 to 3 inches of protrusion) or a high-profile bubble dome cover (for 4 to 12 inches of protrusion).

Q5: Are window well covers required to open from the inside?

Yes, under IRC building codes, any cover over a basement bedroom egress well must open easily from the inside without keys or tools in an emergency.

Q6: How do you keep a window well cover from blowing away in high winds?

Use quick-release interior security clips or flexible stainless steel attachment springs that secure the cover to the metal well rim from underneath.

Q7: Do plastic window well covers turn yellow over time?

Cheap polystyrene covers yellow quickly in sunlight. Always choose UV-treated polycarbonate, which resists yellowing and impact damage for over 10 to 15 years.

Q8: Can I cut a polycarbonate window well cover to size myself?

Yes, polycarbonate sheets can be cut to fit foundation irregularities using a standard jigsaw equipped with a fine-toothed metal cutting blade.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Measuring for a window well cover is a simple ten-minute home safety task that yields significant long-term protection for your basement and foundation. By capturing accurate inside and outside widths, measuring center and side projections, checking for vertical window protrusions, and honoring IRC egress building codes, you ensure your new cover fits like a glove, shields your home from rain and snow, and keeps your family safe.
