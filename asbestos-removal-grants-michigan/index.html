Asbestos Removal Grants Michigan

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Asbestos removal grants in Michigan provide vital public financial assistance to homeowners, municipalities, land banks, and commercial redevelopers tasked with remediating legacy environmental hazards across the Great Lakes State. Due to Michigan's rich industrial heritage and extensive stock of pre-1980 residential housing and historic manufacturing facilities in cities like Detroit, Flint, Grand Rapids, and Saginaw, asbestos is prevalent in heating insulation, plaster, and flooring. Accessing state and federal grant funding through agencies like EGLE and MSHDA helps property owners eliminate toxic materials without bearing exorbitant out-of-pocket costs.

State Programs: EGLE and MSHDA Housing Initiatives

The State of Michigan administers primary environmental funding through the Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy (EGLE) and the Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA). EGLE's Brownfield Redevelopment Grants and Loans provide millions of dollars annually to local units of government, brownfield authorities, and economic development corporations to fund environmental assessments, asbestos surveys, and pre-demolition abatement in contaminated commercial properties.

For residential property owners, MSHDA administers the Housing Initiatives Division grants and the Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) housing rehabilitation program. These programs provide low- and moderate-income homeowners with deferred forgivable loans and direct grants to remediate severe health and safety hazards, including crumbling basement steam pipe insulation, deteriorating vermiculite, and hazardous asbestos siding.

Municipal and County Land Bank Programs: Detroit, Flint, and Beyond

At the county and municipal levels, Michigan's robust land bank network plays a prominent role in channeling asbestos remediation funding. The Detroit Land Bank Authority (DLBA) and Genesee County Land Bank Authority in Flint manage aggressive blight elimination initiatives funded through state appropriations and federal American Rescue Plan allocations. These programs mandate certified asbestos inspections and full professional abatement before blighted homes are renovated or demolished.

Additionally, the Michigan Department of Health and Human Services (MDHHS) administers the Weatherization Assistance Program (WAP). When low-income households apply for energy-efficiency furnace upgrades or insulation improvements, technicians frequently discover deteriorating asbestos pipe wrap that halts work. WAP health-and-safety deferral funds can be tapped to abate the asbestos, allowing essential heating modernizations to proceed safely.

Application Procedures and Compliance Guidelines

Securing asbestos removal grant funding in Michigan requires careful documentation and strict procedural compliance. Property owners must first establish property ownership, verify income eligibility through tax returns and pay stubs, and document that the home was constructed prior to 1980. Municipal program officers will dispatch a state-certified asbestos inspector to perform baseline bulk sampling and draft an official scope of work.

All remediation work funded through Michigan state or federal grant programs must be performed exclusively by environmental remediation contractors licensed by the State of Michigan Asbestos Program under the Department of Labor and Economic Opportunity (LEO). Contractors must submit required 10-day state notices, maintain certified negative air containment, and obtain independent clearance air testing before grant funds are disbursed.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Are there grants to remove asbestos from homes in Michigan?

Yes, Michigan homeowners can access grants through MSHDA housing rehabilitation programs, local CDBG grants, and the MDHHS Weatherization Assistance Program.

Q2: Who qualifies for MSHDA asbestos removal grant assistance?

Low-to-moderate income single-family homeowners earning at or below 80 percent of the Area Median Income (AMI) who live in pre-1980 homes typically qualify.

Q3: What is the role of EGLE in Michigan asbestos grants?

EGLE administers Brownfield Redevelopment Grants that fund environmental assessments, asbestos surveys, and pre-demolition abatement for commercial and municipal sites.

Q4: Can Detroit homeowners get grant money to remove asbestos?

Yes, the City of Detroit Housing and Revitalization Department and the Detroit Land Bank Authority offer home repair grants that cover hazardous asbestos abatement.

Q5: Does the Michigan Weatherization program pay for asbestos removal?

Yes, if deteriorating asbestos pipe insulation prevents the installation of a new energy-efficient furnace, WAP health-and-safety funds can pay to abate it.

Q6: Do Michigan asbestos grants have to be paid back?

Most homeowner grants are structured as forgivable deferred loans that are completely forgiven if the owner continues living in the home for 5 to 10 years.

Q7: Can I choose my own contractor under Michigan grant programs?

Homeowners must select from a pre-approved list of contractors licensed by the Michigan LEO Asbestos Program who comply with competitive bidding rules.

Q8: Where can I apply for asbestos removal grants in Michigan?

Apply through your local municipal community development department, county housing commission, regional Community Action Agency, or MSHDA portal.
