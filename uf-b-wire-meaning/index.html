UF-B Wire Meaning

Full Form
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

UF-B stands for Underground Feeder and Branch-Circuit Cable. In electrical engineering, residential construction, and National Electrical Code standards, UF-B designates a specialized non-metallic sheathed cable specifically manufactured and tested for direct burial in outdoor trenches without requiring rigid metal conduit. Unlike standard indoor Romex cable, UF-B features conductors individually embedded in solid, corrosion-resistant PVC insulation that shields the copper cores from prolonged exposure to wet soil, underground moisture, microbial decomposition, and subterranean acidic elements.

The Technical Anatomy and Material Composition of UF-B Cable

To withstand subterranean environments, UF-B cable utilizes a distinct manufacturing architecture that sets it apart from all standard interior wiring materials. Standard indoor electrical cable, known as NM-B, bundles insulated conductors inside a loose paper wrapping and an outer thermoplastic sleeve that can easily accumulate moisture if exposed to damp earth. In contrast, UF-B cable does not feature loose air voids or paper liners. Every insulated copper conductor and the bare copper grounding wire are encapsulated within a monolithic, solid vinyl jacket that cures as a solid mass around the wiring.

Because the outer gray jacket adheres directly to each individual wire insulation, stripping UF-B cable requires patience, specialized wire stripping tools, or careful longitudinal razor scoring to avoid nicking the underlying copper. The outer vinyl jacket is compounded with specialized ultraviolet stabilizers, rendering it resistant to solar degradation when transitioning from underground trenches to exterior walls, outdoor subpanels, garden lampposts, detached garages, and swimming pool pump circuits. Furthermore, the cable carries an official rating for sunlight resistance and wet location service under Article 340 of the National Electrical Code.

National Electrical Code Trench Depths and Installation Rules

Installing UF-B cable requires adherence to strict trench burial depths specified under NEC Table 300.5 to prevent accidental severance by shovels, garden tillers, and landscaping equipment. For standard residential branch circuits protected by a conventional 15-amp or 20-amp circuit breaker, direct burial UF-B cable must be buried at a minimum depth of 24 inches from the top of the cable to the final finished ground grade. However, if the circuit is protected at its origin by an approved Class A Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter and operates at 120 volts or less with a maximum rating of 20 amps, the mandated burial depth is reduced to 12 inches.

Where UF-B cable emerges from the subterranean trench to connect to outdoor receptacles, junction boxes, or main building penetrations, it must be protected against mechanical damage. Installers must sleeve the cable in Schedule 80 rigid PVC conduit or rigid metal conduit from the required trench depth up to at least 8 feet above grade. Running bare, unprotected UF-B cable along the ground surface or leaving it exposed along low foundation walls where lawn mowers or string trimmers operate is a hazardous code violation.

Sizing, Ampacity Calculations, and Stripping Best Practices

An essential technical consideration when working with UF-B cable is ampacity derating. Although the individual copper conductors within modern UF-B cable are manufactured with 90-degree Celsius insulation, the NEC stipulates that the allowable ampacity of Type UF cable must be calculated using the 60-degree Celsius temperature rating column found in NEC Table 310.16. Consequently, 14 AWG copper UF-B cable is limited to a maximum continuous overcurrent protection of 15 amperes, 12 AWG is rated for 20 amperes, and 10 AWG is rated for 30 amperes. Ignoring this 60-degree rule can cause overheating inside insulated wall penetrations.

Stripping UF-B cable presents a notable challenge for DIY homeowners and apprentice electricians because the solid vinyl outer jacket fills every crevice between conductors. The safest technique involves using a dedicated utility knife to slice the outer jacket along the flat grounding conductor, which carries no delicate insulation. Once peeled back, the outer jacket can be trimmed with side cutters, leaving the individual hot and neutral conductors fully intact without slicing into their protective inner insulation.

How to Safely Install Direct Burial UF-B Wire

Follow these step-by-step instructions to trench, lay, protect, and terminate outdoor UF-B direct burial electrical cable according to electrical code standards.

  1. Call 811 Before Beginning Any Excavation

    Contact your local underground utility notification center at least several days before digging to identify buried gas pipes, water mains, and telecommunication lines.

  2. Excavate Trench to Legal Code Depth

    Dig a clean trench at least 24 inches deep for standard circuits, or 12 inches deep if the circuit is 120V and protected by a GFCI breaker at its source.

  3. Prepare Smooth Bedding and Lay UF-B Cable

    Remove sharp stones from the trench bottom, lay down a 2-inch bed of sand if the ground is rocky, and unroll the UF-B cable flat without kinks or tension.

  4. Install Schedule 80 PVC Conduit at Transitions

    Sleeve the cable inside Schedule 80 PVC conduit where it exits the trench and rises vertically up the building foundation to prevent physical damage.

  5. Backfill with Sand and Install Warning Tape

    Cover the cable with 4 to 6 inches of clean soil or sand, lay bright electrical warning tape 12 inches above the cable, and backfill the remainder of the trench.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does the B stand for in UF-B cable?

The B signifies that the individual conductor insulation is rated for 90 degrees Celsius, representing an improvement over older 60-degree rated Type UF cables.

Q2: Can UF-B cable be installed indoors inside walls?

Yes. UF-B may be used for interior wiring following the same installation rules as NM-B cable, although its stiffer jacket makes it more cumbersome to pull and strip.

Q3: Does UF-B wire need to be inside conduit underground?

No. UF-B is specifically certified for direct burial in the earth without conduit, provided it is buried at the correct legal depth specified by the National Electrical Code.

Q4: What is the minimum burial depth for UF-B wire?

The standard depth is 24 inches for general residential branch circuits, but it can be reduced to 12 inches if protected by a GFCI breaker on a 120V 20A circuit.

Q5: Can I run UF-B wire inside a concrete slab?

UF-B can be buried under a 4-inch thick concrete slab, but it should not be cast directly into poured concrete where curing chemicals and settling can stress the jacket.

Q6: Why is UF-B cable so difficult to strip?

Unlike indoor Romex, which has loose space inside the jacket, UF-B has solid vinyl injected around every conductor, creating a solid mass that resists peeling.

Q7: Can UF-B wire be exposed to direct sunlight?

Yes. UF-B cable jackets are treated with ultraviolet inhibitors and carry a sunlight-resistant rating for outdoor surface runs into junction boxes.

Q8: What size breaker should be used with 12 AWG UF-B cable?

Under NEC rules, 12 AWG copper UF-B must be sized using the 60-degree Celsius column, which limits overcurrent protection to a maximum 20-amp circuit breaker.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

UF-B cable is an indispensable wiring solution for supplying dependable electrical power to outdoor landscapes, detached workshops, patio lighting, and yard accessories. Its solid PVC construction ensures resilience against soil moisture and decay. By respecting mandatory trench burial depths, providing physical conduit protection where the wire surfaces, and calculating breaker sizes from the 60-degree Celsius ampacity column, you guarantee a safe, fully code-compliant electrical installation that endures for decades.
