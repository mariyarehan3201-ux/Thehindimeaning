How Do I Turn Myself in for a Warrant?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how do i turn myself in for a warrant is a critical, life-altering procedure for individuals who discover they have an outstanding arrest or bench warrant. Living with an active warrant subjects you to the constant threat of public arrest during routine traffic stops, workplace embarrassing arrests, or unexpected home raids by law enforcement. Choosing to voluntarily surrender demonstrates respect for the court, significantly improves your chances of obtaining affordable bail or release on your own recognizance (ROR), and allows you to control the timing and conditions of your surrender.

Understanding Warrant Classifications: Arrest vs. Bench Warrants

To surrender strategically, you must first determine the specific legal nature of the active warrant. An Arrest Warrant is issued by a judge following a criminal complaint or grand jury indictment where prosecutors establish probable cause that you committed an underlying criminal offense. Conversely, a Bench Warrant is issued directly from the judicial bench when a defendant fails to comply with court rules, such as failing to appear (FTA) for a scheduled court date, missing mandatory probation appointments, or failing to pay court-ordered child support or criminal fines.

The underlying classification dramatically affects how the surrender is handled. In many jurisdictions, non-violent misdemeanor bench warrants can be resolved through a 'Walk-In Court' or 'Motion to Quash Warrant' scheduled by a criminal defense attorney, allowing you to recall the warrant without spending a single minute inside a correctional holding cell. In contrast, felony arrest warrants require formal booking and processing through county jail detention.

Surrender Preparation: Retaining Counsel and Pre-Arranging Bail

The single most impactful action you can take prior to surrendering is retaining a licensed criminal defense attorney. An experienced attorney can contact the prosecutor or court clerk, verify the exact charges and bond amount, and in some cases, negotiate a voluntary surrender hearing where the prosecutor agrees not to oppose your immediate release. If you cannot afford private counsel, you can apply for the Public Defender at your initial appearance.

Furthermore, you should pre-arrange a surety bond with a licensed commercial bail bond agent before walking into the police station or county jail. A bail bondsman can verify your bail amount, collect paperwork from your family cosigners, and stand ready to post bond the moment your booking and fingerprinting process is complete. This eliminates agonizing delays and prevents you from spending days sitting in holding cells waiting for friends to organize funds.

Timing Your Surrender and Exercising Fifth Amendment Rights

The day of the week and time of day you choose to surrender significantly impacts your detention experience. The optimal time to turn yourself in is early Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday morning (between 6:00 AM and 8:00 AM). Surrendering mid-week ensures that court clerks, prosecutors, judges, and bail bondsmen are working normal business hours, allowing your booking and arraignment to occur on the same day. Never turn yourself in on a Friday afternoon or before a holiday weekend, as you risk remaining trapped in jail until the courts reopen on Monday.

Throughout the booking and intake process, you must strictly exercise your constitutional rights under the Fifth and Sixth Amendments. While you must answer basic administrative booking questions (name, date of birth, home address), you should never discuss the facts of your case with arresting officers, detectives, or fellow inmates. Politely and unequivocally state: 'I am exercising my right to remain silent and I request to speak with my attorney.'

How to Safely Turn Yourself in on an Outstanding Warrant

Follow these five tactical steps to coordinate your surrender, minimize jail detention, and resolve a warrant.

  1. Confirm the Exact Warrant Details

    Have a criminal defense attorney or bail bond agent verify the warrant number, charge, and pre-set bail amount.

  2. Pre-Arrange Bail Bond Paperwork

    Set up a surety bond with a bail agent so they can post your release immediately after booking is finalized.

  3. Choose a Tuesday or Wednesday Morning

    Surrender between 6:00 AM and 8:00 AM on a weekday morning to ensure prompt court processing before evening.

  4. Bring Only Identification and Essential Meds

    Take your driver's license, prescription medication in original bottles, and leave all personal jewelry and phones at home.

  5. Invoke Your Right to Remain Silent

    State clearly to law enforcement that you choose to remain silent and request an attorney without answering interrogation questions.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is it better to turn yourself in than get caught?

Yes, voluntary surrender demonstrates accountability, significantly increasing your chances of lower bail or release on your own recognizance (ROR).

Q2: Can an attorney quash a warrant without me going to jail?

Yes, for non-violent misdemeanor bench warrants, defense lawyers can often file a motion to quash and accompany you to court without jail booking.

Q3: What is the best day of the week to turn yourself in?

Tuesday or Wednesday morning (between 6 AM and 8 AM) is ideal because judges and court staff are on duty to handle quick arraignments.

Q4: How long will I be in jail after turning myself in?

If bail is pre-arranged or set, booking typically takes 4 to 12 hours; without bail, you remain detained until your arraignment (within 24 to 48 hours).

Q5: Can I bring my cell phone into jail when turning myself in?

You can bring it, but it will be confiscated, cataloged into inmate property, and held until your release; memorizing key phone numbers is crucial.

Q6: What happens if I turn myself in on a Friday night?

You will likely sit in county jail all weekend until regular court calendars resume on Monday morning.

Q7: Can police question me when I surrender?

They may attempt to question you, but you have the constitutional right under the 5th Amendment to remain silent and demand an attorney.

Q8: Does a warrant ever expire if you ignore it?

No, criminal arrest and bench warrants remain permanently active in law enforcement computer registries until recalled or served.
