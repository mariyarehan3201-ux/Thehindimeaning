Asbestos Siding Removal Atlanta

Informational

Asbestos siding removal in Atlanta, Georgia, is a common architectural remediation challenge across the metropolitan area's historic neighborhoods and mid-century suburbs. Historic bungalows, Victorians, and post-war ranches throughout Fulton, DeKalb, Cobb, and Gwinnett counties frequently feature Transite cement-asbestos shingles. Architectural Context in Atlanta and Identification of Transite Shingles

The widespread presence of asbestos siding across metropolitan Atlanta is a direct reflection of the city's twentieth-century residential building expansion. During the post-World War II housing boom, builders cladded thousands of modest wood-frame cottages and suburban ranch homes with Transite shingles—rigid siding tiles manufactured from Portland cement reinforced with fifteen to thirty percent chrysotile mineral fibers. Furthermore, in historic neighborhoods like Inman Park, Grant Park, West End, Kirkwood, and Cabbagetown, property owners in the 1950s and 1960s frequently retrofitted aging Victorian timber clapboard with fireproof asbestos shingles to eliminate recurring exterior painting costs.

Identifying asbestos siding in Atlanta requires visual and tactile examination. Typical shingles measure twelve by twenty-four inches and exhibit distinctive bottom-edge contours, such as wavy, scalloped, straight, or thatched patterns. Surfaces often feature embossed faux cedar wood-grain textures, and the shingles are secured by small exposed drive-nails along the bottom overlapping lip. Under federal EPA and Georgia Environmental Protection Division classifications, intact asbestos-cement siding is categorized as Category II non-friable asbestos. In its undisturbed state, the fibers remain tightly bound, but sawing, pressure washing, or breaking shingles releases hazardous respirable dust.

Georgia EPD Regulations and City of Atlanta Permitting

The regulatory framework governing asbestos removal across Atlanta is administered by the Georgia Department of Natural Resources Environmental Protection Division under the Georgia Rules for Air Quality Control, Chapter 391-3-1-.02(9)(b)7. In Georgia, professional asbestos abatement contractors must be licensed by the EPD. For commercial properties, institutional buildings, and multi-family residential structures containing more than four dwelling units, contractors must file an official project notification with the Georgia EPD at least ten working days prior to initiating siding disturbance.

Within the City of Atlanta, the Office of Buildings within the Department of City Planning oversees structural building and alteration permits. When homeowners or general contractors submit plans involving exterior wall modifications, building officials require confirmation of whether suspect exterior materials have been tested. While owner-occupants of detached single-family residential properties are legally exempt from commercial contractor licensing rules, local municipal codes strictly prohibit uncontained dust generation and mandate compliant disposal at state-permitted landfills.

Disposal Facilities and Fulton/DeKalb Landfill Compliance

Proper disposal of asbestos siding in the Atlanta metropolitan area is subject to stringent environmental oversight. Under Georgia EPD regulations, asbestos waste cannot be placed in curbside residential bins or dropped off at standard municipal construction recycling centers. Siding tiles must be kept continuously wetted during extraction, stacked carefully, and wrapped in two layers of six-mil polyethylene sheeting, securely sealed with duct tape and labeled with OSHA hazardous material warning statements.

The waste must be transported by a permitted carrier accompanied by an official Georgia Waste Shipment Record to a landfill authorized to accept non-friable asbestos. Within the greater Atlanta region, permitted facilities include the Seminole Road Landfill in DeKalb County, the Richland Creek Landfill in Gwinnett County, and designated municipal solid waste disposal facilities in surrounding counties. Landfill operators document the exact burial coordinates within the active working face to ensure permanent isolation from groundwater and future excavation.

How to Plan Asbestos Siding Removal in Atlanta

Step-by-step roadmap for homeowners executing compliant siding abatement in metro Atlanta.

  1. Confirm Asbestos Content Through Laboratory Testing

    Collect a small sample from an existing broken shingle and submit it to an accredited local environmental laboratory for PLM confirmation.

  2. Obtain Competitive Bids from Georgia EPD-Licensed Contractors

    Solicit detailed removal proposals from at least three licensed Georgia abatement companies experienced with residential exterior siding.

  3. Secure Necessary Building Permits from City of Atlanta

    Submit your exterior alteration permit application to the City of Atlanta Office of Buildings, detailing your approved abatement contractor.

  4. Implement Thorough Ground Containment and Wet Removal Protocols

    Ensure the crew lays heavy polyethylene sheeting ten feet outward from the foundation and continuously mists shingles during manual unfastening.

  5. Retain Signed Georgia Waste Shipment Manifests

    Secure signed copies of the official waste manifest confirming legal disposal at a permitted municipal solid waste landfill in the Atlanta region.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does asbestos siding removal cost in Atlanta?

Complete professional removal in metro Atlanta typically costs between $8 and $16 per square foot, averaging $8,000 to $16,000 for a typical single-family home.

Q2: Can I install new siding over asbestos siding in Georgia?

Yes, installing rigid foam insulation and new vinyl or fiber-cement siding directly over intact asbestos siding is legally permitted and cost-effective.

Q3: Can you pressure wash asbestos siding in Atlanta?

No, power washing asbestos siding is strictly prohibited by federal and state regulations because high water pressure pulverizes the matrix and spreads fibers.

Q4: Does Georgia EPD require a permit for residential siding removal?

Single-family detached homeowner projects are generally exempt from state 10-day notices, but municipal City of Atlanta building permits are required.

Q5: Where can you dispose of asbestos siding in Atlanta?

Asbestos siding must be taken to permitted municipal solid waste landfills, such as Seminole Road in DeKalb County, accompanied by a waste manifest.

Q6: How long does it take to remove asbestos siding from an Atlanta house?

A certified abatement crew typically completes full residential siding removal and site cleanup in two to four working days.

Q7: Is exterior asbestos siding dangerous to live with in Atlanta?

Intact, undisturbed siding poses zero health risk because chrysotile fibers are encapsulated within Portland cement; hazards only arise if cut, broken, or sanded.

Q8: Does having asbestos siding hurt home resale value in Atlanta?

Well-maintained siding rarely impacts appraised value, but cracked shingles or unpermitted amateur removals can lead to buyer repair credits or delays.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Asbestos siding removal in Atlanta requires balancing architectural preservation, public health safety, and strict Georgia EPD compliance. Whether property owners choose professional abatement, protective vinyl overlays, or elastomeric paint encapsulation, proactive management prevents dangerous fiber release. Engaging licensed local environmental professionals guarantees that historic Atlanta homes are updated safely, protecting household health and preserving neighborhood property value.
