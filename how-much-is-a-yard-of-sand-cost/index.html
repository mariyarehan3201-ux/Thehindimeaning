How Much Is a Yard of Sand Cost?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Whether you are installing a paver patio, leveling an above-ground pool, mixing concrete, or replenishing a children play sandbox, calculating aggregate expenses is crucial for project planning. A cubic yard of bulk sand typically costs between $20 and $60 per yard picked up at a quarry or landscape yard, or roughly $40 to $90 per yard delivered directly to your job site. Understanding sand granulometry, weight conversions, and regional delivery logistics ensures you order the exact material grade without unexpected cost overruns.

Sand Classifications, Grain Texture, and Material Costs

Sand is categorized by particle diameter, purity washing procedures, and angularity, all of which directly influence its price and engineering suitability. Masonry sand, also referred to as fine washed sand or bricklayer mortar sand, is finely screened and clean, averaging $35 to $55 per cubic yard. It provides an ultra-smooth bedding surface ideal for under-pool liners, mortared brick joints, and paver finishing. Concrete sand, often designated as coarse ASTM C33 sand, contains jagged angular granules that interlock under compaction, making it the premier choice for foundational sub-bases and structural concrete blending at $25 to $45 per yard.

Specialty sand varieties command premium pricing due to specialized processing and strict safety testing. Playset sand undergoes extensive washing, vacuum sorting, and tumble drying to eliminate toxic crystalline silica dust, sharp mineral shards, and staining iron oxides, retailing between $50 and $80 per cubic yard bulk or $4 to $7 per 50-pound bag. Meanwhile, polymeric sand, which incorporates synthetic binding additives activated by water to lock paver joints against weed growth and ant infestations, is packaged in bags and costs significantly more, reaching $25 to $40 per 50-pound sack.

Weight Calculations, Coverage Mathematics, and Delivery Fees

Ordering bulk aggregates requires converting volumetric cubic yards into payload weight to avoid exceeding trailer or truck axle capacities. One cubic yard of dry sand weighs approximately 2,600 to 2,700 pounds, or roughly 1.3 to 1.35 tons. When sand is damp or saturated with rainwater, its weight can surge to 3,000 to 3,300 pounds per yard, representing a critical safety consideration if you plan to haul material in a half-ton pickup truck bed whose payload rating rarely exceeds 1,500 to 2,000 pounds.

Bulk material delivery is generally the most practical solution for orders exceeding two cubic yards. Landscape suppliers and aggregate haulers typically impose a flat delivery drop fee ranging from $50 to $120 for trips within a 10 to 15-mile radius, plus an additional $3 to $6 per loaded mile for remote locations. Many suppliers establish a minimum load threshold of three to five yards or apply a partial-load surcharge of $30 to $50 if ordering smaller volumes on a commercial tri-axle dump truck.

Purchasing Bulk Yardage vs. Bagged Sand at Home Centers

Determining whether to buy bagged sand from big-box home improvement retailers or bulk delivery from a commercial quarry comes down to total volume. A standard commercial bag of sand contains 50 pounds, which equals roughly 0.45 cubic feet. Because one cubic yard contains 27 cubic feet, it takes roughly 54 to 60 fifty-pound bags to equal a single cubic yard of loose sand. At $4.50 to $6.00 per 50-pound bag, a cubic yard purchased in retail bags costs between $240 and $360, compared to $40 to $75 for bulk delivered material.

However, bagged sand offers distinct advantages for minor patch repairs, localized mortar tuckpointing, or tight urban spaces lacking dump truck access. Bags can be carried through backyard gates, stored indoors out of the rain indefinitely, and utilized incrementally. For any project demanding more than one cubic yard, ordering bulk dump delivery saves hundreds of dollars and spares countless hours of manual bag lifting and plastic disposal.

How to Measure and Order a Yard of Sand in 5 Steps

Follow these five straightforward steps to calculate exact volume, choose aggregate grades, and arrange seamless bulk sand delivery.

  1. Calculate Project Cubic Feet

    Measure your project length and width in feet, multiply them to find square footage, and multiply by your planned depth in feet.

  2. Convert Measurements into Cubic Yards

    Divide your total cubic feet by 27 to determine the required cubic yards, and add 10 to 15 percent for compaction and settling.

  3. Select the Correct Granular Grade

    Choose coarse concrete sand for paver installations, fine mason sand for pool liners, or certified play sand for play areas.

  4. Contact Local Aggregate Yards for Delivered Quotes

    Inquire with regional quarries or bulk landscaping yards, confirming material cost per yard and flat-rate delivery charges.

  5. Prepare the Dump Site and Delivery Access

    Mark a clear, overhead-wire-free drop zone on a sturdy driveway or tarped surface with room for a commercial dump truck to maneuver.

Frequently Asked Questions (10 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does one cubic yard of sand weigh?

One cubic yard of dry sand weighs approximately 2,600 to 2,700 pounds (about 1.35 tons), and wet sand can exceed 3,000 pounds.

Q2: How many bags of sand make a cubic yard?

It takes approximately 54 to 60 fifty-pound bags of sand to equal one cubic yard of bulk volume.

Q3: What is the cheapest type of sand you can buy?

Unscreened utility or fill sand is the most economical, usually priced between $15 and $30 per cubic yard before delivery.

Q4: How deep should sand be under an above-ground pool?

A uniform layer of two to three inches of clean, masonry sand provides a cushioned, level base that protects pool vinyl liners from puncturing.

Q5: Can a half-ton pickup truck haul a full yard of sand?

Generally no, because a yard of sand weighs 2,600 pounds or more, which exceeds the 1,500 to 2,000-pound payload capacity of most half-ton trucks.

Q6: What is the difference between concrete sand and mason sand?

Concrete sand has coarse, angular particles for structural strength and drainage, while mason sand is finely screened with smaller grains for mortar and smooth bedding.

Q7: How much does sand delivery cost?

Delivery drop charges typically range from $50 to $120 for local delivery within 10 to 15 miles, regardless of load size.

Q8: Does polymeric sand come in bulk cubic yards?

No, polymeric sand contains specialized chemical polymer binders and is sold exclusively in sealed 50-pound bags to prevent moisture activation.

Q9: How much sand is needed for a 100 square foot patio base?

At a standard one-inch screed depth, you will need approximately 0.31 cubic yards (roughly 850 pounds) of coarse concrete sand.

Q10: Does sand wash away if left uncovered?

Yes, unprotected sand piles will erode rapidly during heavy rainstorms, so cover outdoor bulk deliveries with a weighted waterproof tarp.
