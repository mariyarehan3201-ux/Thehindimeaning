If a Tow Truck Damages Your Car Are They Liable?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Discovering that your vehicle has suffered mechanical or cosmetic damage while being towed—whether from a highway breakdown, an accidental private property impound, or municipal parking enforcement—triggers an immediate and contentious legal battle over financial responsibility. Many motorists assume they are powerless because towing companies print aggressive disclaimers on receipts claiming they are 'not responsible for damage.' Under the established common law doctrine of bailment and commercial transportation torts, towing operators owe a strict legal duty of reasonable care and are legally liable for damages caused by improper rigging, negligence, or mechanical incompetence.

The Legal Doctrine of Bailment for Mutual Benefit

In property tort law, the relationship between a vehicle owner and a commercial towing company is legally classified as a 'Bailment for Mutual Benefit.' A bailment is created the moment the tow truck operator hooks onto your vehicle and assumes physical custody and control of your personal property.

Under this legal relationship, the bailee (the towing company) is held to a standard of 'ordinary reasonable care.' The operator is legally obligated to transport the vehicle safely using industry-standard equipment without causing damage. While towing companies routinely attempt to evade liability by printing sweeping exculpatory clauses on their paperwork—such as 'Not Responsible for Drivetrain or Body Damage'—contract law firmly establishes that a commercial enterprise cannot contract away its liability for its own gross negligence or statutory failure of care.

The Critical Threat: Flat-Towing All-Wheel-Drive (AWD) Vehicles

By far the most catastrophic and expensive mistake tow truck drivers make is improperly towing All-Wheel-Drive (AWD) or 4WD vehicles. In an AWD vehicle (such as a Subaru, Audi Quattro, or modern crossover SUV), the front and rear axles are mechanically or hydraulically linked through a central differential, transfer case, or viscous coupling.

Automobile manufacturer owner's manuals explicitly state that AWD vehicles must NEVER be towed with two wheels resting on the ground. When a negligent driver uses a standard wheel-lift to hoist the front two wheels off the pavement while leaving the rear wheels rolling at highway speeds, the center differential spins violently without lubrication, generating extreme friction heat that welds differential gears together and destroys the transmission in less than five miles. AWD vehicles must ALWAYS be transported flat on a flatbed rollback trailer, or have auxiliary free-spinning towing dollies placed under the rolling wheels.

The Evidentiary Blueprint: Proving Causation and Overcoming Denials

When you file a damage claim, the towing company's standard defense is claiming that the damage was 'pre-existing.' Overcoming this denial requires establishing an airtight evidentiary paper trail.

Whenever your car is being towed, take detailed, high-resolution photographs and walk-around video of your vehicle's exterior, bumpers, tires, and dashboard instrument cluster before the driver hooks up the truck. If your car is towed involuntarily from private property, immediately photograph the vehicle upon retrieving it at the impound yard before driving away. Demand a certified mechanical inspection from an independent dealership or ASE-certified technician detailing that the damage (such as sheared suspension control arms or differential metal shavings) is consistent with recent towing trauma.

How to File a Damage Claim Against a Towing Company

Follow this step-by-step roadmap to document towing damage, obtain certified repair estimates, and secure reimbursement from insurance.

  1. Photograph Damage Immediately at Tow Lot

    Inspect your vehicle thoroughly at the impound or drop-off lot, taking clear timestamped photos of scraped bumpers, tires, or fluid leaks.

  2. Obtain an Itemized Mechanical Diagnostic Report

    Have a certified dealership or mechanic inspect the car, writing a formal diagnostic note attributing damage directly to improper towing.

  3. Demand the Tow Company's On-Hook Insurance Info

    Request the name, policy number, and claims phone number for the towing operator's commercial On-Hook Cargo and Garagekeepers insurance.

  4. Send a Formal Written Demand Letter via Certified Mail

    Draft a formal legal demand letter detailing the incident, repair estimates, and photo proof, giving them 14 days to compensate you.

  5. File a Claim in Municipal Small Claims Court

    If the towing company denies liability or ignores your demand, file a small claims action under bailment tort law to recover full damages.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can a tow company legally avoid liability with a disclaimer on their receipt?

No. Towing companies cannot contract away their legal liability for negligence. Common law bailment requires reasonable care, and boilerplate disclaimer clauses on receipts are routinely voided by judges in court.

Q2: What happens if a tow truck driver tows an AWD car on two wheels?

Towing an All-Wheel-Drive vehicle with two wheels on the ground destroys the center differential and transmission within miles. The towing company is 100% legally liable for the complete cost of a new transmission.

Q3: What is On-Hook Cargo Insurance for tow trucks?

On-Hook Cargo insurance is a specialized commercial policy that covers damage caused to a customer's vehicle while it is hooked up, lifted, or transported by the tow truck.

Q4: How do I prove the tow truck damaged my car?

Proof requires before-and-after photos, timestamped impound lot photos, the towing receipt showing vehicle make/model, and a certified diagnostic report from an independent mechanic attributing damage to towing.

Q5: Can I refuse to pay the impound fee if my car was damaged?

It is best to pay the impound fee under protest and retrieve your vehicle immediately to prevent compounding daily storage fees, and then sue to recover both the repair costs and the towing fee in court.

Q6: Will my personal auto insurance cover damage caused by a tow truck?

Yes, if you carry Comprehensive or Collision coverage, your insurer will pay for repairs (minus your deductible) and will pursue subrogation against the towing company's insurance to recover your deductible.

Q7: Why do tow truck drivers hook onto suspension control arms?

Lazy or untrained operators use heavy J-hooks on delicate aluminum suspension lower control arms rather than proper frame tie-down slots, bending the control arms and ruining the car's alignment.

Q8: What is Garagekeepers insurance?

Garagekeepers Legal Liability insurance covers customer vehicles against physical damage, vandalism, theft, or fire while parked inside the towing company's commercial storage or impound yard.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

A towing company is legally liable for any damage they inflict upon your vehicle through negligence or improper towing practices, regardless of what fine print appears on their receipts. Document the condition of your vehicle with photos before and after towing, verify whether your car requires flatbed transport, file an immediate written claim with the towing company's On-Hook Cargo insurance carrier, and do not hesitate to pursue a claim in small claims court if they refuse to pay for repairs.
