Can You Get a CDL with 2 DWI in Texas?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

No, under standard federal and Texas state statutes, you cannot obtain or hold a Commercial Driver's License (CDL) if you have been convicted of two separate Driving While Intoxicated (DWI) offenses. Under Title 49 of the Code of Federal Regulations and Texas Transportation Code Chapter 522, a second DWI conviction results in a mandatory lifetime commercial driving disqualification, with narrow administrative reinstatement eligibility only possible after ten full years under strict rehabilitation conditions.

Federal Mandates and Texas Transportation Code Chapter 522

Commercial motor vehicles?"including 80,000-pound tractor-trailers, hazardous material (HazMat) tankers, and passenger transit buses?"possess immense kinetic energy, making safe vehicle operation a vital public safety concern. Consequently, commercial driving is not governed solely by individual state laws, but by stringent national standards established by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA). Under 49 CFR Section 383.51, federal law dictates mandatory disqualification periods that all fifty states must enforce without exception to remain eligible for federal highway funding.

In the State of Texas, these federal safety mandates are codified directly into state law under Texas Transportation Code Chapter 522 (the Texas Commercial Driver's License Act). Under Section 522.081, the Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS) is legally required to disqualify an individual from operating a commercial motor vehicle for life if the person is convicted of two or more major disqualifying offenses. Driving While Intoxicated (Texas Penal Code 49.04), driving with a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.04 or higher in a commercial vehicle, or refusing to submit to chemical breath or blood testing under implied consent laws are classified as major disqualifying offenses.

A critical nuance that many drivers fail to realize is that the lifetime commercial ban applies even if both DWI offenses occurred decades ago, and even if both offenses occurred while operating a private personal passenger vehicle rather than a commercial truck. The federal regulatory framework does not distinguish between personal and commercial vehicle operating records when evaluating lifetime disqualifying violations. The Narrow 10-Year Reinstatement Pathway Under Texas Law

While Texas Transportation Code Section 522.081(b) mandates lifetime disqualification for a second DWI, Texas law?"incorporating federal exceptions under 49 CFR Section 383.51(a)(5)"?"provides a single, highly regulated pathway for conditional reinstatement after a mandatory waiting period. Under Texas Administrative Code Section 16.94, an individual who has been disqualified for life due to two alcohol-related driving offenses may petition the Texas Department of Public Safety for a one-time restoration of commercial driving privileges, but only after serving a minimum of ten consecutive years of total commercial disqualification.

Meeting the ten-year threshold is merely the preliminary administrative hurdle. To obtain reinstatement, the applicant must file a formal petition with the Director of the Texas DPS and submit unassailable documentary proof of complete personal rehabilitation. The driver must demonstrate that they have maintained a completely clean driving record during the entire ten-year period, with zero moving violations, zero administrative license revocations (ALR), and zero arrests for alcohol or drug-related offenses.

Furthermore, the petitioner must complete an accredited, state-certified voluntary chemical dependency rehabilitation program, pass comprehensive psychological and substance abuse evaluations conducted by certified addiction professionals, and submit to random Department of Transportation (DOT) drug and alcohol screenings. If the Texas DPS grants reinstatement, it is strictly conditional: if the driver commits even a minor moving traffic infraction or subsequent alcohol offense, their CDL is revoked permanently with zero future right of appeal. Commercial Fleet Hiring Realities and Insurance Barriers

Even if an individual successfully navigates the complex administrative legal process and obtains a restored Commercial Driver's License from the Texas DPS after ten years, they confront a second, often insurmountable commercial barrier: commercial fleet insurance underwriting. Commercial trucking carriers are heavily regulated corporate entities whose operating margins depend on maintaining affordable commercial fleet liability insurance.

Commercial auto insurance underwriters routinely pull ten-year Motor Vehicle Records (MVR) and Comprehensive Automated Liability Tracking reports on all commercial drivers. Insurance underwriters generally apply strict underwriting guidelines that classify any driver with two lifetime DWIs as an uninsurable catastrophic risk. Major national freight carriers (such as Schneider, Swift, and Werner) enforce strict zero-tolerance hiring policies that permanently reject applicants with multiple lifetime alcohol-related driving offenses. The few employment options that remain for reinstated drivers are typically limited to small, regional independent companies or operating as an owner-operator paying exorbitant, high-risk commercial insurance premiums.

How to Petition for CDL Reinstatement After a 10-Year Disqualification in Texas

A step-by-step administrative guide for disqualified drivers to petition the Texas DPS for commercial license restoration.

  1. Verify the Full 10-Year Disqualification Period Has Elapsed

    Order an official certified 10-year Texas DPS driving record (Type AR). Verify that exactly ten consecutive years (120 full months) have passed since the effective date of your second DWI commercial disqualification.

  2. Maintain an Impeccable Non-Commercial Driving Record

    Ensure your standard Class C personal driver license has remained in full compliance throughout the ten-year period, with zero moving violations, zero suspensions, and zero criminal arrests.

  3. Complete a State-Certified Alcohol Rehabilitation Program

    Enroll in and graduate from a state-licensed intensive chemical dependency rehabilitation program. Obtain certified completion certificates and letters from licensed substance abuse counselors.

  4. Undergo a Comprehensive Substance Abuse Professional (SAP) Evaluation

    Schedule an evaluation with a certified DOT Substance Abuse Professional (SAP). Complete all clinical recommendations and pass a observed 10-panel DOT drug and alcohol screening.

  5. File a Formal Petition for Reinstatement with the Texas DPS Director

    Retain an administrative license defense attorney to draft and file a formal legal petition for CDL reinstatement with the Texas DPS Driver License Division, submitting all rehabilitation exhibits.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can you get a CDL with 2 DWIs in Texas?

Under standard law, no. A second DWI conviction triggers a mandatory lifetime CDL disqualification under federal FMCSA rules and Texas Transportation Code 522. You can only petition for restoration after 10 years.

Q2: Does a DWI in a personal car affect your Texas CDL?

Yes. Federal and Texas laws apply equally to personal vehicles. A DWI committed while driving your personal car or pickup truck counts as a major disqualifying offense against your CDL.

Q3: How long does a second DWI disqualify a CDL in Texas?

A second DWI conviction results in a lifetime disqualification. However, Texas allows drivers to petition the Department of Public Safety for conditional reinstatement after 10 consecutive years.

Q4: Can you get an occupational hardship CDL in Texas?

No. Federal regulations strictly prohibit states from issuing occupational, hardship, or conditional commercial driver licenses for any disqualified CDL driver.

Q5: Will trucking companies hire a driver with 2 past DWIs?

It is exceptionally difficult. Major motor carriers and commercial auto insurance underwriters enforce strict underwriting guidelines that classify drivers with multiple lifetime DWIs as uninsurable.

Q6: What is the legal BAC limit for CDL drivers in Texas?

While the legal blood alcohol concentration (BAC) limit is 0.08 for personal drivers, it is 0.04 when operating a commercial motor vehicle, and zero tolerance for commercial HazMat drivers.

Q7: Can an expunged DWI still disqualify a CDL in Texas?

If a DWI charge was dismissed and formally expunged from your record, it does not count as a conviction. However, administrative license suspensions (ALR) on your driving record may still be reviewed by the DPS.

Q8: What happens if a reinstated CDL driver gets a traffic ticket?

If a driver granted conditional reinstatement after 10 years commits any subsequent moving violation or alcohol offense, their CDL is permanently revoked with zero future right of reinstatement.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

While Texas law provides a narrow administrative pathway to petition for CDL reinstatement after ten full years, obtaining a commercial license with two DWIs is an extraordinary uphill battle. The combined weight of federal FMCSA lifetime bans, Texas DPS administrative hearings, and commercial insurance underwriter exclusions makes commercial driving careers virtually inaccessible for individuals with multiple drunk driving convictions. If you currently face a pending second DWI charge in Texas, aggressively fighting to avoid conviction is the only reliable way to protect your commercial driving career.
