How to Install a RV Air Conditioner?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Upgrading or replacing a rooftop air conditioner on a recreational vehicle is one of the most rewarding DIY mechanical improvements an RV owner can accomplish. Modern RV air conditioners deliver superior energy efficiency, quieter compressor performance, and improved cooling capacity compared to aging factory equipment. Nearly all RV manufacturers standardize rooftop openings at fourteen by fourteen inches, allowing for direct drop-in replacement across major brands like Dometic, Coleman-Mach, and Furrion. Installing a rooftop unit requires proper physical handling, precise gasket positioning, watertight sealing, correct 120-volt wiring, and uniform bolt torquing.

Evaluating Rooftop RV Air Conditioners and Power Requirements

Before purchasing a replacement air conditioner, you must verify your RV electrical infrastructure and cooling demands. Rooftop RV air conditioners are typically rated at either 13,500 British Thermal Units (BTU) or 15,000 BTU. A 13,500 BTU unit draws approximately 12 to 14 running amps on standard 120-volt alternating current, while a 15,000 BTU unit requires 14 to 16 amps. Because RV air conditioning compressors experience high inductive inrush current upon startup (often exceeding 45 to 55 amps for a fraction of a second), installing an aftermarket soft-start kit is highly recommended if you plan to power the unit using a portable generator or a lithium inverter system.

You must also determine whether your RV utilizes a ducted or non-ducted distribution configuration. Non-ducted installations feature a ceiling assembly (air distribution box / ADB) mounted directly beneath the roof opening, discharging chilled air straight into the living space through adjustable directional louvers. Ducted systems connect the rooftop discharge plenum into insulated air distribution ducts running horizontally through the RV ceiling cavity. Selecting an AC model compatible with your existing ceiling plenum and wall thermostat prevents complex wiring modifications.

Safety Preparation and Removing the Old Rooftop Unit

Safety is the primary consideration during RV air conditioner installation. Disconnect the RV from 30-amp or 50-amp shore power pedestals, shut down onboard generators, and switch off the main inverter. Trip the dedicated air conditioner circuit breaker inside the power distribution panel and verify zero voltage at the unit using a non-contact electrical tester. Inside the RV, remove the plastic ceiling filter grille and unscrew the four long steel mounting bolts securing the internal metal clamping frame to the rooftop unit.

Carefully disconnect the 120-volt Romex wiring (black hot, white neutral, and bare copper ground) and any low-voltage 12-volt thermostat communication cables. On the RV roof, use a plastic scraper to slice through old silicone or lap sealant anchoring the old unit base pan. Lift the old AC unit straight up to break the bond of the compressed foam gasket; never pry violently against the rubber roof membrane, as tearing EPDM or TPO roofing material will create extensive water leaks. Use two capable adults and an extension ladder or a rented mechanical lift to hoist the heavy old unit down safely.

Positioning the New Unit, Wiring, and Proper Gasket Compression

Clean the perimeter of the fourteen-inch roof opening thoroughly using mineral spirits followed by isopropyl alcohol to remove all dirt and old sealant residue. Inspect the structural wooden framing inside the roof opening; if rot or water damage is present, replace the wooden blocking before proceeding. Verify that your new air conditioner possesses a fresh, uncompressed closed-cell foam gasket adhered to the underside of its base pan. Never apply silicone, caulk, or roof lap sealant to the foam gasket; the gasket is engineered to create a watertight compression seal entirely on its own.

Carefully lower the new air conditioner into place, centering it precisely over the 14x14-inch opening. From inside the cabin, position the metal ceiling clamping frame and thread the four long mounting bolts upward into the threaded receptacles of the rooftop unit. Tighten the bolts gradually in a crisscross pattern using a hand ratchet. Tighten each bolt until the thick foam gasket is compressed by exactly forty to fifty percent of its original height (most modern gaskets feature yellow indicator tabs that line up when proper compression is achieved, roughly 40 to 50 inch-pounds of torque). Wire the 120V junction box, connect the thermostat harness, install the decorative ceiling shroud, and restore power for testing.

How to Mount and Wire a Replacement RV Rooftop Air Conditioner

A comprehensive guide to safely removing an old camper AC, positioning a new unit, and securing the mounting hardware.

  1. De-Energize Shore Power and Verify Zero Electrical Current

    Unplug the RV from shore power, shut off the generator and inverter, switch off the AC circuit breaker, and test wiring with a multimeter.

  2. Remove Interior Ceiling Assembly and Disconnect Wiring

    Unscrew the lower air distribution cover, uncouple the 120V Romex leads and 12V thermostat wires, and remove the four mounting bolts.

  3. Hoist Down Old Unit and Prep the 14x14 Roof Curb

    Lift the old air conditioner off the roof, scrape away old debris without tearing the membrane, and clean the perimeter with alcohol.

  4. Position the New Unit Over the Opening Centered

    Carefully place the new air conditioner onto the roof and align its foam gasket squarely over the clean fourteen-inch ceiling hole.

  5. Torque Mounting Bolts to Fifty Percent Gasket Compression

    Install the interior ceiling clamping frame, tighten the four corner bolts evenly until the foam gasket compresses by fifty percent, and reconnect wiring.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Do you need to put sealant or silicone on the RV AC foam gasket?

No, never put silicone, caulk, or lap sealant on the foam gasket; the closed-cell foam is engineered to seal under mechanical bolt compression alone.

Q2: Can I run a 15,000 BTU RV air conditioner on a 30-amp electrical service?

Yes, a 30-amp RV service provides 3,600 total watts, which easily powers a single 15,000 BTU air conditioner drawing around 1,800 running watts.

Q3: What is an RV AC soft starter?

A soft starter is an electronic module that reduces compressor startup inrush current by up to 70 percent, allowing the AC to start on small portable generators.

Q4: How tight should the RV air conditioner mounting bolts be?

Mounting bolts should be tightened evenly to approximately 40 to 50 inch-pounds of torque, compressing the one-inch foam gasket down to roughly half an inch.

Q5: What size is the standard RV roof opening for air conditioners?

Virtually all modern travel trailers, motorhomes, and fifth wheels use a standardized 14-inch by 14-inch roof opening.

Q6: Can one person install a rooftop RV air conditioner alone?

While one person can complete the interior wiring, lifting the 80 to 100-pound unit onto the roof safely requires at least two capable adults or a mechanical lift.

Q7: How long does it take to replace an RV air conditioner?

A standard replacement on an existing 14x14 opening typically takes between two and three hours from teardown to final testing.

Q8: Why is water dripping from my RV air conditioner into the living room?

Water dripping inside usually indicates loose mounting bolts failing to compress the gasket, a torn gasket, or blocked exterior condensate drain slots.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Installing an RV rooftop air conditioner is an accessible project that delivers substantial comfort during hot summer road trips. The keys to a successful installation are ensuring that all electrical power is completely de-energized, lifting the unit onto the roof safely, and centering the base pan over the standard 14x14 opening. Avoiding the urge to apply liquid caulk to the neoprene gasket and torquing the interior mounting bolts evenly to fifty percent compression guarantees a leak-free, whisper-quiet cooling system for thousands of miles.
