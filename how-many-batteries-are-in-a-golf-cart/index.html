How Many Batteries Are in a Golf Cart?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how many batteries are in a golf cart depends on the electrical voltage of the vehicle's powertrain (typically 36 volts or 48 volts) and the battery chemistry installed. In traditional lead-acid electric golf carts, the battery compartment houses either 4, 6, or 8 deep-cycle batteries wired together in a series circuit to achieve the target operating voltage. However, in modern golf carts upgraded with lithium-ion (LiFePO4) technology, the entire battery pack is frequently consolidated into a single lightweight 36V or 48V drop-in battery.

Determining Voltage: 36-Volt vs. 48-Volt Golf Cart Systems

Electric golf carts manufactured by industry leaders like Club Car, EZ-GO, and Yamaha operate on either 36-volt or 48-volt direct current (DC) electrical systems. Because individual deep-cycle flooded lead-acid batteries are manufactured in standard units of 6 volts, 8 volts, or 12 volts, engineers configure groups of batteries wired in series (positive terminal connected to negative terminal) so their individual voltages add up to the vehicle's total system requirement.

In a 36-volt golf cart (common in vintage models and entry-level fleet carts), the pack consists exclusively of six 6-volt batteries (6 x 6V = 36V). In higher-performance 48-volt carts (which offer superior hill-climbing torque and top speed), manufacturers use one of two configurations: six 8-volt batteries (6 x 8V = 48V) or four 12-volt batteries (4 x 12V = 48V). Certain heavy-duty utility and commercial carts even use eight 6-volt batteries (8 x 6V = 48V) to maximize amp-hour reserve capacity.

How to Identify the Voltage of Your Golf Cart Batteries

If you recently purchased a used golf cart and are unsure how many volts each battery possesses, you can easily determine battery voltage without even using a digital voltmeter. Every flooded lead-acid battery features removable plastic fill caps on top where distilled water is added. Inside each battery, every physical cell produces approximately 2.1 volts of electricity.

To calculate individual battery voltage, simply count the number of water fill holes (cells) on top of one battery and multiply by 2: a 6-volt battery has 3 fill holes (3 x 2V = 6V); an 8-volt battery has 4 fill holes (4 x 2V = 8V); and a 12-volt battery has 6 fill holes (6 x 2V = 12V). Count the total number of batteries under the seat, multiply by that individual voltage, and you will know the exact voltage of your cart.

4 Fill Holes 8 Volts 4 Cells x 2 Volts = 8V 6 batteries = 48V system (Most common modern setup)

The Modern Shift: 1 Single Lithium-Ion Battery vs. 6 Lead-Acid Batteries

In recent years, the golf cart industry has undergone a massive technological shift toward Lithium Iron Phosphate (LiFePO4) battery systems. A traditional pack of six 8-volt lead-acid batteries weighs over 400 pounds, requires constant weekly distilled water top-offs, leaks corrosive sulfuric acid that rots the aluminum or steel battery tray, and degrades within 3 to 5 years.

Modern lithium conversions replace all 4, 6, or 8 lead-acid batteries with a single 48-volt lithium battery pack weighing only 80 to 100 pounds. This removes over 300 pounds of dead weight from the cart, dramatically improving acceleration, braking distance, and hill climbing. Furthermore, a single lithium battery lasts 10 to 12 years (3,000+ cycles), charges in 2 to 3 hours instead of 8 to 10 hours, and requires zero maintenance.

How to Inspect and Count Golf Cart Batteries

Follow these five straightforward steps to identify your golf cart's battery setup and calculate system voltage.

  1. Lift the Main Bench Seat to Access the Compartment

    Tilt the seat forward to expose the battery tray, motor cables, and solenoid switch.

  2. Count the Total Number of Physical Batteries

    Note whether there are 4, 6, or 8 individual rectangular battery cases secured in the hold-down brackets.

  3. Count the Cell Caps on Top of a Single Battery

    Count the circular acid fill caps on one battery: 3 caps = 6V, 4 caps = 8V, 6 caps = 12V.

  4. Multiply the Battery Count by the Individual Voltage

    Calculate total voltage (e.g., 6 batteries x 8 volts = 48 volts) to determine system rating.

  5. Inspect Wiring Cables for Series Connection

    Verify that heavy-gauge battery cables alternate from positive (+) to negative (-) terminal across all batteries.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How many batteries does a 48V golf cart have?

A 48V golf cart typically has either six 8-volt batteries or four 12-volt batteries (or a single 48V lithium battery).

Q2: How many batteries does a 36V golf cart have?

A 36V golf cart almost always uses six 6-volt deep-cycle batteries wired together in series.

Q3: Is it better to have 6 8-volt batteries or 4 12-volt batteries?

Six 8-volt batteries provide greater amp-hour capacity, longer driving range, and longer overall lifespan than four 12-volt batteries.

Q4: Can you put just one battery in a golf cart?

Yes, you can install a single 36V or 48V lithium-ion (LiFePO4) drop-in battery to replace all multi-battery lead-acid packs.

Q5: How long do golf cart batteries last?

Flooded lead-acid batteries last 4 to 6 years with proper maintenance, while lithium batteries last 10 to 12 years.

Q6: Can you mix old and new golf cart batteries?

No, never mix old and new batteries; old batteries have higher internal resistance that degrades the new batteries prematurely.

Q7: How much does a full set of golf cart batteries cost?

A full set of 6 lead-acid batteries costs $900 to $1,500, while a complete lithium conversion kit costs $1,800 to $2,800.

Q8: Do gas golf carts have batteries?

Yes, gas golf carts have a single standard 12-volt automotive starter battery to start the engine and power lights.
