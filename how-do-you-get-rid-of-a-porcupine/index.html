How Do You Get Rid of a Porcupine?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how do you get rid of a porcupine is a critical wildlife management challenge for rural homeowners, forest property owners, and pet owners across North America. North American porcupines (Erethizon dorsatum) are large nocturnal rodents possessing approximately 30,000 sharp, barbed quills. While docile and non-aggressive by nature, their intense cravings for sodium and wood nutrients cause extensive structural damage to homes, vehicle wiring, and timber, while posing severe impalement hazards to inquisitive dogs.

Porcupine Biology: Why They Invade Yards and Chew Everything

To effectively deter porcupines, one must understand their dietary physiology. Porcupines are strict herbivores that feed primarily on tree bark, inner phloem, twigs, evergreen needles, and buds. In autumn and winter, they routinely girdle prized ornamental and timber trees (including pines, hemlocks, and maples), stripping the bark in a complete ring around the trunk, which effectively strangles and kills the tree.

Furthermore, porcupines experience a chronic dietary sodium deficiency due to their potassium-rich vegetarian diet. This physiological craving drives them to gnaw relentlessly on anything containing mineral salts or human perspiration. They chew wooden tool handles, canoe paddles, plywood adhesives, composite deck boards, synthetic automobile brake lines, and rubber radiator hoses, causing thousands of dollars in property damage.

Tree Banding, Physical Exclusion, and Removing Salt Attractants

The most dependable non-lethal method for protecting trees against porcupine girdling is metal tree banding. Wrap a 24-to-30-inch-wide band of smooth aluminum flashing around the tree trunk at least three feet above the ground. Because porcupines are clumsy climbers that rely on sinking their sharp curved claws into rough bark, they cannot gain traction on smooth aluminum, completely preventing access to upper branches.

Property owners must also practice strict salt hygiene. Store winter road de-icing salt in airtight, heavy-duty plastic bins inside sealed sheds. Never leave salt-stained muddy work boots, gardening gloves, leather saddles, or wooden shovels on porches or in open carports. Wash vehicle undercarriages frequently during winter months to remove road brine that attracts porcupines to chew rubber wiring and brake hoses.

Live Trapping, Safe Transport, and Quill First Aid for Pets

When a porcupine refuses to leave, live trapping using a large wire cage trap (32x12x15 inches) is the most humane solution. Place the trap near the base of damaged trees or active den entrances (such as rock crevices, hollow logs, or beneath outbuildings). Bait the trap with fresh apple slices smeared with salty brine. Once captured, drape a thick canvas tarp or blanket over the trap to calm the animal. Check local wildlife regulations regarding permitted relocation distances (typically 5 to 10 miles into remote state forestland).

If a curious dog encounters a porcupine, immediate veterinary care is critical. Never attempt the old myth of cutting quill ends with scissors; this does not deflate quills and makes them splinter. Porcupine quills are barbed with thousands of microscopic backward-facing scales that pull the quill deeper into muscle tissue with every muscle twitch. Restrain your dog immediately and transport them to an emergency veterinarian for sedation and complete surgical quill extraction.

How to Safely Trap and Remove a Porcupine

Follow these five humane steps to trap, transport, and deter porcupines from your property.

  1. Secure and Store All Sodium Attractants

    Lock away road de-icing salt, salty vehicle parts, and wooden tools inside sealed outbuildings.

  2. Install Aluminum Flashing on Prized Trees

    Wrap 30 inches of smooth aluminum flashing around trunks at least 3 feet high to prevent climbing.

  3. Set a Large Wire Live Trap Along Den Paths

    Position a 32-inch steel live cage trap along fence lines or active trails leading to the porcupine's den.

  4. Bait the Trap with Salt Brine and Apples

    Place sliced apples and a salt-soaked cloth inside the trap behind the trip plate.

  5. Cover the Trap and Relocate According to Law

    Drape a heavy tarp over the cage to calm the animal, then transport it to approved remote wildlife habitat.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can a porcupine shoot its quills?

No, porcupines cannot throw or shoot quills; quills detach easily upon physical contact when the porcupine thrashes its tail.

Q2: Do porcupines come out during the day?

Porcupines are predominantly nocturnal, though they occasionally feed in high tree canopies on quiet, sunny winter days.

Q3: What smell repels porcupines?

Strong capsaicin (hot pepper) sprays, predator urine (cougar/coyote), and ammonia rags can temporarily deter porcupines from chewing wood.

Q4: Can porcupines kill trees?

Yes, porcupines kill trees by girdling them—chewing a complete 360-degree ring of inner bark around the trunk, halting nutrient flow.

Q5: What is the best bait to catch a porcupine?

Apples soaked in salt brine, carrots, or a cloth saturated with salty water are the most effective baits due to their intense salt cravings.

Q6: Is it legal to trap and relocate a porcupine?

Wildlife regulations vary by state; many jurisdictions permit live trapping and relocation, while others require licensed wildlife control operators.

Q7: What should I do if my dog gets quilled by a porcupine?

Do not cut the quills; keep your dog calm to prevent them from pawing them deeper, and take them immediately to a vet for sedated extraction.

Q8: Why do porcupines chew on cars?

Winter road salt accumulates on vehicle tires, brake lines, and undercarriages, which porcupines chew to satisfy sodium deficiencies.
