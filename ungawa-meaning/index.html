Hollywood Origins: Johnny Weissmuller and the Tarzan Lexicon

In pop culture history, film studies, and linguistic folklore, the rhythmic exclamation Ungawa (frequently spelled Oongawa, Ungawa, or Ugh-Gawa) is most famous as a fictionalized cinematic command popularized in mid-twentieth-century Hollywood films. Most notably, in the classic 'Tarzan the Ape Man' films of the 1930s and 1940s starring Olympic swimming champion Johnny Weissmuller, Tarzan shouted 'Ungawa!' as an all-purpose jungle command to command respect and communicate with wild beasts.

In the cinematic scriptwriting of the era, the word was treated as an all-purpose imperative meaning 'stop,' 'go,' 'look,' 'get down,' or 'surrender.' When Tarzan faced a charging bull elephant, roaring lion, or hostile river crocodile, a booming 'Ungawa!' would cause the animal to freeze in obedience. While entirely fabricated as a simplified cinematic dialect by Hollywood writers, the catchy phonetic rhythm permanently imprinted the word onto global pop culture consciousness.

Linguistic Roots and 1960s Black Power Political Reinterpretation

Despite Hollywood fictionalized deployment, linguistic historians trace possible authentic roots to the East African Bantu language family, specifically Kiswahili. In Swahili, the verb root 'unga' means to unite, connect, or join together (with 'ungana' meaning to unite with one another). In various coastal dialects, related phonetic expressions conveyed collective assembly or shared strength.

In a remarkable cultural inversion during the 1960s American Civil Rights Movement and Black Power era, African American activists reclaimed and repurposed the word. Recognizing its rhythmic resonance, student organizers and community marchers transformed it into a powerful, defiant political cadence: 'Ungawa, Black Power!' By adopting the word, activists stripped away paternalistic Hollywood jungle caricatures, converting the sound into an empowering celebration of African unity, self-determination, and pride.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does ungawa mean in Tarzan?

In Tarzan movies, ungawa was a fictional, all-purpose command meaning 'stop,' 'look,' 'go,' or 'down,' used by Tarzan to talk to animals.

Q2: Is ungawa a real African word?

In Swahili, 'unga' or 'ungana' means to unite, connect, or join together; Hollywood likely adapted this root into its fictional cinema catchphrase.

Q3: What did ungawa mean in the Black Power movement?

In the 1960s, civil rights activists reclaimed the phrase as a powerful rhythmic chant: 'Ungawa, Black Power!' meaning strength and unity.

Q4: Who created the word ungawa?

Hollywood scriptwriters for the 1930s Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer Tarzan films starring Johnny Weissmuller popularized the word.

Q5: Why is ungawa used in cheerleading chants?

Because of its catchy percussive rhythm, 1970s and 80s marching bands and cheerleaders adopted 'Ungawa, we got the power!' as a stadium rally cheer.

Q6: How do you pronounce ungawa?

It is pronounced phonetically as 'oon-GAH-wah' (or 'uhn-GAH-wuh') with heavy rhythmic emphasis on the middle syllable.

Q7: Did the original Tarzan book by Edgar Rice Burroughs use ungawa?

In the original novels, Burroughs created an elaborate ape language called 'Mangani,' but the specific word 'ungawa' was largely popularized by Hollywood films.
