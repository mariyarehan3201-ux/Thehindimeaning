How to Sell a Car Privately in Iowa?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Selling a used motor vehicle privately in Iowa is an outstanding way to maximize your financial return, often yielding fifteen to twenty-five percent more cash than a trade-in offer from an automotive dealership. However, navigating a private vehicle transaction in the Hawkeye State demands strict compliance with the Iowa Department of Transportation (Iowa DOT) Motor Vehicle Division regulations. Failing to properly execute title assignments, release official liability notices, or remove your license plates can leave you legally liable for parking tickets, towing fees, or vehicular accidents caused by the new buyer.

The Iowa Vehicle Title: Anatomy of a Lawful Assignment

The vehicle title (Certificate of Title) is the supreme legal document certifying vehicle ownership in Iowa. To transfer ownership lawfully, you must hold a clean, physical Iowa paper title free of active bank liens. If your title lists a financial institution as a secured lender, that lien must be formally released by the county treasurer's office with an official stamped cancellation or a separate notarized Form 411029 (Cancellation of Security Interest) before you can convey ownership to a private buyer.

When completing the transfer on the back of the title, proceed with extreme caution: even a minor mistake, strike-through, crossed-out word, or stray pen mark will invalidate the title document, forcing you to pay fees and wait weeks for a replacement duplicate title. The seller must sign and print their legal name exactly as it appears on the front face of the title. If the title lists two owners joined by the conjunction 'AND,' both owners must sign; if joined by 'OR,' either individual owner can legally sign over the title.

The Iowa Bill of Sale Law and the 5% One-Time Registration Fee

Effective January 1, 2020, Iowa state law enacted a mandatory Bill of Sale requirement across all ninety-nine counties. Previously, buyers could self-report purchase prices, leading to widespread under-reporting to dodge state taxes. Today, the county treasurer will flatly refuse to transfer a title or issue license plates unless accompanied by a formal, signed Bill of Sale. The bill of sale must include the full legal names of both buyer and seller, physical addresses, the full 17-digit Vehicle Identification Number (VIN), year, make, model, exact sale date, and the final agreed purchase price.

This document is critical because Iowa assesses a 5% One-Time Registration Fee (the state's equivalent of auto sales tax) based directly on the purchase price stated on the bill of sale. If the reported purchase price is significantly below fair market value (such as selling a vehicle to a family member for one dollar), the county treasurer may require additional gift affidavits or assess taxes based on official National Automobile Dealers Association (NADA) book value guides.

The Golden Rule in Iowa: Keep Your Plates and File Form 411107

In Iowa, license plates belong to the person, not the vehicle. The single most dangerous mistake an Iowa vehicle seller can make is letting the buyer drive away with the seller's license plates attached to the bumper. If that buyer runs through automated toll booths, commits hit-and-run accidents, or abandons the vehicle on the highway shoulder, law enforcement and municipal impound lots will trace the registration directly to you, issuing citations and thousands of dollars in impound fees.

Always bring a screwdriver to the sale closing and physically remove both the front and rear Iowa license plates before handing over the keys. The buyer can legally drive the newly purchased vehicle directly to their home or local county treasurer's office without plates for up to thirty days, provided they carry their signed bill of sale and title in the glove box. Finally, within thirty days of the sale, submit Iowa DOT Form 411107 (Notice of Sale of Vehicle) to your local county treasurer. Filing this form officially updates state police databases that you no longer own the vehicle, shielding you from all future civil liabilities.

How to Execute a Private Vehicle Sale in the State of Iowa

A complete step-by-step guide to signing over an Iowa vehicle title, drafting a bill of sale, and releasing state liability.

  1. Ensure Clear Title and Release Existing Liens

    Confirm you have your physical Iowa paper title; if a bank was listed as a lienholder, verify that the lien has been formally released.

  2. Meet the Buyer in a Safe Public Location

    Conduct daytime test drives at a well-lit public venue (such as a local police station parking lot), checking the buyer's driver's license.

  3. Secure Guaranteed Payment Inside a Bank

    Execute the financial closing inside the buyer's bank, having the bank teller verify and issue a certified cashier's check or cash payment.

  4. Complete the Title Assignment and Bill of Sale

    Carefully sign the back of the title, record odometer mileage, and have both parties sign the mandatory Iowa official Bill of Sale.

  5. Remove License Plates and File Notice of Sale

    Unscrew your front and rear license plates to keep, and immediately submit Iowa DOT Form 411107 (Notice of Sale) to your county treasurer.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Do license plates stay with the car when sold in Iowa?

No. In Iowa, license plates belong to the seller. You must remove your plates before handing over the vehicle; the buyer must obtain their own plates.

Q2: Can an Iowa buyer drive a car home without license plates?

Yes. In Iowa, a private buyer can drive the vehicle for up to 30 days without plates, provided they carry the signed title and bill of sale in the car.

Q3: Is a bill of sale mandatory to sell a car in Iowa?

Yes. As of January 1, 2020, Iowa state law mandates a formal signed Bill of Sale for all vehicle title transfers in every Iowa county.

Q4: Does an Iowa car title transfer need to be notarized?

Standard Iowa title assignments between private individuals do not require a notary, but signatures must be written cleanly without errors.

Q5: What is Iowa Form 411107 and why should sellers file it?

Form 411107 is the Notice of Sale of Vehicle. Filing it with your county treasurer legally notifies the state that you sold the car, releasing all liability.

Q6: How much tax does a private buyer pay on a car in Iowa?

The buyer pays a 5% One-Time Registration Fee (auto sales tax) to the county treasurer based directly on the purchase price on the bill of sale.

Q7: What if there is a mistake written on the Iowa title?

Do not cross it out or use white-out. Any alterations void the title, requiring you to apply for an official duplicate replacement title (Form 411033).

Q8: Can I get a refund on my remaining Iowa license plate registration?

Yes. When you surrender your plates to the county treasurer with an unexpired registration exceeding $10, you can apply for a credit or partial refund.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Selling a car privately in Iowa is a highly rewarding financial transaction that puts thousands of extra dollars directly into your pocket when executed with procedural discipline. By preparing a spotless title, executing a formal Bill of Sale, gathering secure payment inside a banking branch, removing your license plates, and filing your Notice of Sale with the county treasurer, you guarantee a seamless, profitable, and completely protected vehicle sale.
