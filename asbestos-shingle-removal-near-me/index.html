Evaluating Siding Removal vs. Encapsulation Alternatives

Exterior asbestos cement siding shingles (frequently referred to by historical trade names such as Transite) were installed on millions of twentieth-century homes across North America. Composed of Portland cement reinforced with fifteen to thirty percent chrysotile asbestos fibers, these rigid shingles provided exceptional fireproofing, rot resistance, and durability. However, homeowners frequently face removal considerations when updating home aesthetics or replacing damaged exterior facades.

Under EPA regulations, exterior cement shingles are classified as Category II non-friable materials. As long as shingles remain intact and un-sanded, mineral fibers remain locked inside the cement matrix, presenting negligible risk to residents. Therefore, before undertaking full removal, homeowners should consider whether encapsulating the siding with quality paint or installing modern vinyl siding over the intact shingles is a safer, less costly alternative.

What to Look for in a Local Abatement Contractor

If full shingle removal is necessary for your home, hiring a qualified local remediation contractor is the single most critical decision. General roofing or siding contractors who lack hazardous materials licensing frequently shatter shingles with crowbars or throw debris into open dumpsters, severely contaminating the property and violating federal Clean Air Act rules.

Legitimate local abatement services employ certified technicians holding state asbestos credentials. Technicians mist shingles with amended water, gently pull fasteners with specialized shingle nail pullers to lower panels intact, and wrap pallets in six-mil polyethylene sheeting. Contractors must carry dedicated pollution liability insurance specifically covering asbestos contamination.

Waste Disposal and Ground Containment Practices

During exterior shingle removal, certified crews lay continuous six-mil polyethylene drop cloths extending at least ten feet out from foundation walls to catch any falling chips or dust. Technicians work from top to bottom, removing shingles intact without snapping or breaking them.

All collected shingles must be stacked on sturdy wooden pallets, wrapped in heavy-duty six-mil plastic, and bound with industrial banding tape. The waste must travel under an official Waste Shipment Record manifest to a state-permitted municipal solid waste landfill equipped with a dedicated asbestos cell, where materials are buried under six inches of clean earth daily.

How to Hire Local Asbestos Shingle Removal

  1. Measure Exterior Siding Square Footage

    Calculate the total wall square footage of your home to estimate project scope and material quantities.

  2. Solicit Itemized Bids from Licensed Firms

    Contact at least three state-licensed hazardous abatement contractors for written line-item proposals.

  3. Verify Contractor Pollution Insurance

    Request a certificate of insurance directly from the underwriter confirming specific asbestos pollution coverage.

  4. Require Waste Manifest Sign-Off

    Ensure the contractor provides a countersigned Waste Shipment Record from a permitted landfill before final payment.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to remove asbestos siding per square foot?

Professional removal typically costs between $7 and $15 per square foot, including labor, scaffolding, and disposal fees.

Q2: Can I install vinyl siding directly over asbestos shingles?

Yes. Siding over intact shingles is an EPA-approved encapsulation method that adds insulation without hazardous disturbance.

Q3: Is it illegal to remove asbestos siding yourself?

Single-family homeowners can legally remove siding in most states, but dropping, breaking, or power-washing shingles is illegal.

Q4: How do professionals remove asbestos shingles safely?

Technicians lay plastic ground sheets, mist shingles with water, pull nails with specialty tools, and lower panels intact.

Q5: Can asbestos shingles be discarded in a regular dumpster?

No. Placing asbestos siding in regular trash is illegal; it must travel under manifest to a permitted hazardous landfill.

Q6: Does homeowners insurance pay for asbestos shingle removal?

Standard policies exclude voluntary removal unless the siding is damaged by a covered sudden peril like storm or fire.

Q7: How long does whole-house asbestos siding removal take?

A standard 1,500 to 2,500 square foot residential home typically takes two to four days to complete safely.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Searching for asbestos shingle removal near me requires vetting licensed contractors who follow wet extraction and ground containment practices. While full removal costs seven to fifteen dollars per square foot, certified execution permanently eliminates hazardous materials and prepares your home for modern siding.
