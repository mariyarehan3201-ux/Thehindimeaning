How Much Is a DNA Test in NC?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Knowing how much is a DNA test in NC is a crucial consideration when resolving paternity questions, establishing child support, or handling child custody disputes in North Carolina District Courts. Standard at-home paternity kits used for informal curiosity cost between $80 and $190, but they lack legal validity in court. For legal matters, court-admissible paternity tests with strict chain-of-custody protocols cost between $250 and $475 across North Carolina clinics. Expecting parents can also access non-invasive prenatal paternity tests (NIPP) for $1,200 to $1,800 as early as seven weeks into pregnancy.

North Carolina District Court Standards and Legal Testing

In North Carolina, paternity determinations are governed under North Carolina General Statutes (N.C.G.S.) Chapter 49. When parentage is contested in family court, judges will only admit genetic test results performed by laboratories accredited by the American Association of Blood Banks (AABB). These legal tests, priced between $250 and $475, require positive identification of all participants through government IDs, biometric photography, and witnessed specimen collection using sterile buccal cheek swabs sealed in tamper-evident containers.

Over-the-counter home collection kits sold online or at pharmacies cannot be presented as evidence in North Carolina child support or custody hearings. Because anyone could theoretically submit another person cheek swab or cotton swab specimen, judges treat non-chain-of-custody tests as hearsay. Investing in an accredited legal test from the outset eliminates the need to pay twice when informal test results are rejected by county clerks or family court judges.

North Carolina Child Support Enforcement (IV-D) Procedures

Under North Carolina Title IV-D child support programs administered through county Departments of Social Services (DSS), genetic testing is routinely coordinated when an unmarried mother applies for financial support. County child support enforcement caseworkers arrange testing appointments at local DSS offices or designated partner medical clinics across Charlotte, Raleigh, Greensboro, Winston-Salem, and Durham. The state government covers the upfront laboratory contract costs.

If the genetic evaluation confirms that the alleged father has at least a 97 percent probability of paternity (the statutory benchmark under N.C.G.S. Section 8-50.1), the court establishes legal paternity and issues a child support order. The judge typically orders the adjudicated father to reimburse the state testing fee, which ranges from $25 to $60 depending on county contract rates. If the test excludes the alleged father, the state absorbs the testing cost entirely.

Prenatal Options and Amending NC Vital Records

Expectant mothers in North Carolina increasingly utilize non-invasive prenatal paternity testing (NIPP), which costs between $1,200 and $1,800. This procedure extracts circulating cell-free fetal DNA from the pregnant woman blood draw and profiles it against buccal swab DNA from the alleged father. Available as early as the seventh week of gestation with zero risk of miscarriage, NIPP offers conclusive genetic confirmation long before delivery, allowing parents to prepare parenting agreements and child support expectations prior to hospital admission.

Following the issuance of a certified DNA test result and accompanying court judgment, the father name can be formally added to the child North Carolina birth certificate. The North Carolina Office of Vital Records in Raleigh processes Affidavit of Parentage and court-ordered amendments for a state processing fee of $15, with certified replacement certificates costing $24 each. This administrative step ensures the child enjoys rights to inheritance, health insurance coverage, and social security survivor benefits.

How to Arrange a Certified Legal DNA Test in North Carolina

Follow these five straightforward steps to obtain a legally binding DNA paternity test in North Carolina.

  1. Select an AABB-Certified Testing Laboratory

    Ensure the diagnostic laboratory holds active AABB accreditation to comply with North Carolina General Statutes.

  2. Coordinate Professional Sample Collection

    Schedule testing at an approved North Carolina clinical site where a neutral third-party collector oversees procedures.

  3. Provide Government Identification and Photos

    Bring government-issued photo IDs for adults and hospital records or birth certificates for children to verify identity.

  4. Execute Witnessed Buccal Swab Collection

    Complete painless cheek swab swabbing witnessed by the collector, who seals samples into tamper-resistant security envelopes.

  5. File Certified Notarized Results with Court

    Obtain the official notarized parentage report to present to your North Carolina District Court judge or county DSS caseworker.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much is a court-approved DNA test in North Carolina?

A legal, court-admissible paternity test in North Carolina typically costs between $250 and $475 at accredited clinics.

Q2: Does North Carolina DSS pay for paternity testing?

County DSS offices pay upfront for child support cases, but if paternity is confirmed, the father is ordered to reimburse $25 to $60.

Q3: What legal standard proves paternity in North Carolina?

Under N.C.G.S. 8-50.1, genetic test results showing a probability of 97% or greater create a rebuttable presumption of paternity.

Q4: Can I use a Walgreens or CVS DNA test in North Carolina court?

No, drugstore home test kits cannot be used in North Carolina courts because they lack witnessed chain of custody.

Q5: How much does a prenatal DNA test cost in North Carolina?

Non-invasive prenatal paternity (NIPP) tests cost between $1,200 and $1,800 across North Carolina testing providers.

Q6: How long do DNA results take in North Carolina?

Accredited legal paternity tests take between two and five business days after all biological samples arrive at the laboratory.

Q7: Can a father be forced to take a DNA test in North Carolina?

Yes, a North Carolina District Court judge can issue an enforceable order compelling an alleged father to submit to genetic testing.

Q8: How do I add a father to a North Carolina birth certificate?

Submit a court order or voluntary Affidavit of Parentage with certified DNA results to the NC Office of Vital Records with required state fees.
