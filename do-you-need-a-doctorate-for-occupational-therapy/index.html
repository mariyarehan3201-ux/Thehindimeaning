Do You Need a Doctorate for Occupational Therapy?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 12, 2026

Students pursuing a career in occupational therapy frequently encounter conflicting guidance regarding educational requirements. While physical therapy shifted to a mandatory clinical doctorate over a decade ago, the occupational therapy profession maintains a dual-entry educational model. To become a licensed occupational therapist in the United States, you do not need a doctorate; you can enter clinical practice by earning either an accredited Master's Degree (MSOT) or an Occupational Therapy Doctorate (OTD).

The Dual-Degree Entry Model: Master's Versus Clinical Doctorate

The governing accrediting body for occupational therapy education is the Accreditation Council for Occupational Therapy Education (ACOTE). In 2014, ACOTE proposed a mandate that would have required all entry-level occupational therapy programs to transition exclusively to the doctoral level by 2027. However, after extensive advocacy and feedback from clinicians, universities, and the American Occupational Therapy Association (AOTA), the mandate was officially rescinded in 2019.

As a result, ACOTE reaffirmed the dual-entry degree standard, ensuring that both the Master of Science in Occupational Therapy (MSOT) and the Occupational Therapy Doctorate (OTD) remain fully accredited pathways to clinical practice. Regardless of which degree a graduate completes, both MSOT and OTD holders sit for the exact same National Board for Certification in Occupational Therapy (NBCOT) examination and receive identical state clinical licenses.

Curriculum Differences: What the OTD Adds Beyond Clinical Skills

Both MSOT and OTD programs cover the foundational clinical science necessary for direct patient care, including functional neuroanatomy, pediatrics, assistive technology, psychosocial rehabilitation, and 24 weeks of mandatory full-time Level II fieldwork. The primary difference lies in the additional year of doctoral coursework. An OTD curriculum emphasizes advanced clinical research, healthcare administration, policy advocacy, program development, and higher education teaching methodologies.

The hallmark of an OTD program is the mandatory 14-week Doctoral Capstone experience and project. During the capstone, doctoral students work in specialized clinical settings, research labs, community wellness programs, or policy think tanks, producing an in-depth scholarly project. If your career ambitions include clinical leadership, university teaching, or pioneering community-based programs, the OTD offers distinct strategic advantages.

Salary Reality and Deciding Which Degree Is Right for You

A common misconception among prospective students is that earning a doctorate automatically guarantees a substantially higher clinical salary in hospital or clinic settings. In practical clinical healthcare, insurance reimbursement rates from Medicare, Medicaid, and private insurers are identical whether an occupational therapy session is billed by an MSOT or an OTD clinician. Consequently, entry-level clinical salaries in outpatient, school, and skilled nursing environments are virtually indistinguishable between the two degrees.

Deciding between an MSOT and an OTD comes down to your personal financial situation, career trajectory, and professional aspirations. If your primary goal is to treat patients as an exceptional clinician while minimizing graduate student debt and entering the workforce one year earlier, the MSOT is an outstanding choice. If you aim to become a university professor, conduct published research, or direct healthcare organizations, investing in an OTD provides the requisite credentials.

How to Choose and Apply to an Occupational Therapy Program in 5 Steps

Follow these five strategic steps to determine whether an MSOT or OTD program best matches your career goals and complete your applications.

  1. Clarify Your Long-Term Career Vision

    Determine whether your primary objective is direct clinical patient care (MSOT favored) or research, academia, and healthcare leadership (OTD favored).

  2. Fulfill Standard Undergraduate Prerequisites

    Complete required undergraduate science courses in anatomy, physiology, developmental psychology, and statistics with a strong GPA.

  3. Accumulate Occupational Therapy Shadowing Hours

    Shadow licensed occupational therapists across multiple settings, such as pediatric sensory clinics, hospitals, and nursing homes.

  4. Apply Through the OTCAS Portal

    Prepare your personal statement, letters of recommendation, and transcripts through the Occupational Therapist Centralized Application Service.

  5. Evaluate Program Costs and Financial Aid Packages

    Compare total in-state tuition, living costs, and capstone opportunities before accepting an admission offer to minimize debt.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is a doctorate required to practice occupational therapy?

No, a doctorate is not required. You can practice as a fully licensed occupational therapist with either an accredited Master's (MSOT) or Doctorate (OTD).

Q2: Did ACOTE mandate a doctorate for occupational therapists?

ACOTE initially proposed a single-accreditation doctoral mandate, but that proposal was officially rescinded in 2019, keeping the dual-entry system.

Q3: Do OTD therapists get paid more than MSOT therapists?

In direct patient care, insurance reimbursement is identical, so entry-level salaries for MSOT and OTD clinicians are generally comparable.

Q4: How long does it take to earn an MSOT versus an OTD?

An MSOT typically takes 2 to 2.5 years of graduate study, while an entry-level OTD requires 3 years, including a 14-week doctoral capstone.

Q5: Can an MSOT therapist later earn a doctorate?

Yes, practicing clinicians with a master's degree can enroll in flexible, online Post-Professional OTD (PP-OTD) programs while continuing to work.

Q6: Do MSOT and OTD graduates take different licensing exams?

No, all graduates take the exact same National Board for Certification in Occupational Therapy (NBCOT) OTR examination to earn licensure.

Q7: What is an Occupational Therapy Assistant (OTA)?

An OTA completes a two-year associate degree and provides therapeutic interventions under the supervision of a licensed occupational therapist (OTR).

Q8: Why would someone choose an OTD over an MSOT?

Students choose an OTD if they desire to teach at the university level, conduct research, develop community programs, or pursue clinical administration.
