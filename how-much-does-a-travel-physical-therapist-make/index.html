How Much Does a Travel Physical Therapist Make?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a travel physical therapist make requires analyzing weekly blended pay packages, tax-free GSA housing stipends, hourly taxable wages, and contract locations. On average, a travel physical therapist (PT) makes between $1,800 and $2,800 in gross weekly pay, translating to an annual income of $90,000 to $135,000+ for working 46 to 48 weeks per year. In high-demand rural or crisis staffing markets, specialized travel PT contracts can reach $3,000 to $3,400 per week.

Weekly Blended Pay Packages: Taxable Wages vs. Tax-Free Stipends

Travel healthcare compensation differs fundamentally from traditional permanent staff W-2 salaries. Staff physical therapists earn an average annual salary of $85,000 to $98,000 ($40 to $48 per hour fully taxable). In contrast, staffing agencies structure travel PT compensation around a 'weekly blended rate' consisting of a modest taxable hourly base wage combined with substantial non-taxable government per diem stipends for housing, meals, and incidentals.

Under IRS guidelines, healthcare travelers maintaining a qualifying permanent primary residence (tax home) receive tax-free stipends benchmarked against federal General Services Administration (GSA) per diem rates. A typical $2,200 weekly travel PT package might comprise $800 in taxable hourly pay ($20/hr for 40 hours) and $1,400 in non-taxable housing and meals stipends. Because nearly two-thirds of earnings are exempt from federal and state income taxes, net take-home pay is dramatically higher.

Clinical Practice Settings and Geographic Pay Multipliers

Compensation for travel physical therapists varies significantly across clinical environments. Skilled Nursing Facilities (SNFs) and Home Health agencies consistently offer the highest weekly pay packages ($2,300 to $3,000 weekly) due to strict Medicare documentation requirements, driving distance demands, and chronic therapist shortages. Hospital-based outpatient orthopedic clinics generally sit at the lower end of the travel pay spectrum ($1,800 to $2,200 weekly).

Geographic location also dramatically impacts earnings. States with high rural demand or strict state licensing backlogs—such as Alaska, California, Texas, Washington, and North Dakota—offer top-tier weekly compensation ($2,400 to $3,200+). Highly desirable vacation hubs in Florida, the Carolinas, and coastal Southern California typically offer lower pay packages ($1,750 to $2,100 weekly) because traveler competition for those assignments is intense.

IRS Tax Home Rules, Benefits, and Travel Physical Therapy Compact

To legally accept tax-free lodging and meal per diems, a travel physical therapist must maintain an authentic 'tax home' under IRS Publication 463. This requires paying fair market rent or maintaining recurring living expenses in your permanent home community while duplicating living expenses at your travel assignment location. Failing to maintain a legitimate tax home reclassifies all stipends as taxable income, triggering severe back-tax penalties.

Staffing agencies also provide health insurance, 401(k) matching, and travel reimbursements ($250 to $500 per assignment). Furthermore, licensing mobility has been dramatically streamlined by the Physical Therapy Compact (PT Compact). Licensed physical therapists residing in compact member states can purchase compact privileges online in minutes for $50 to $150 per state, eliminating months of state licensing paperwork.

How to Maximize Your Earnings as a Travel Physical Therapist

Follow these five career steps to secure high-paying travel physical therapy contracts.

  1. Establish and Document a Legitimate IRS Tax Home

    Maintain detailed financial records showing fair market rent or mortgage payments at your permanent tax home to protect tax-free stipends.

  2. Obtain Multi-State Licensure via the PT Compact

    Acquire compact privilege approvals across high-paying member states to jump on fast-booking emergency contracts.

  3. Work with 2 to 3 Competing Staffing Recruiters

    Partner with multiple agencies (Aya, Med Travelers, AMN) to compare weekly pay packages for identical facility openings.

  4. Target High-Paying Settings: Home Health and SNFs

    Accept home health or skilled nursing contracts, which pay 15% to 30% more than outpatient orthopedic clinics.

  5. Secure Cost-Effective Temporary Lodging

    Utilize platforms like Furnished Finder or medium-term rentals to keep housing expenses below your GSA tax-free stipend allowance.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a travel physical therapist make a year?

A travel physical therapist typically makes between $90,000 and $135,000+ gross annually, working 46 to 48 weeks per year.

Q2: What is the average weekly pay for a travel PT?

The average weekly pay ranges between $1,800 and $2,800 gross, consisting of taxable base wages and tax-free living stipends.

Q3: Do travel physical therapists make more than permanent staff?

Yes, due to tax-free stipends and urgent staffing premiums, travel PTs typically earn 25% to 50% more net take-home pay than permanent staff.

Q4: Can a new grad physical therapist do travel therapy?

Yes, many agencies hire new Doctor of Physical Therapy (DPT) graduates, though completing clinical rotations in SNF or acute settings is helpful.

Q5: How long are travel physical therapy contracts?

Standard travel contracts are 13 weeks in duration, though assignments can frequently be extended for up to 26 or 39 weeks.

Q6: What is a tax home in travel therapy?

A primary residence where you incur ongoing living expenses and maintain community/work ties, required to receive tax-free per diem stipends.

Q7: What is the PT Compact?

An agreement between participating states that allows physical therapists to purchase instant practice privileges in other member states.

Q8: Do travel PT agencies pay for housing?

Agencies provide either agency-placed housing or a tax-free housing stipend, with 95% of therapists taking the cash stipend to maximize savings.
