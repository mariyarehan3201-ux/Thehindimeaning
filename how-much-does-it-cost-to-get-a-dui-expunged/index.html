How Much Does It Cost to Get a DUI Expunged?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

A driving under the influence (DUI) conviction carries lingering social and professional repercussions, casting a shadow over employment background checks, apartment leasing applications, and professional licensing boards. If you are seeking to clear your record, the total cost to get a DUI expunged or sealed generally spans from $800 to $3,500 when hiring a private criminal defense attorney. Choosing to petition pro se (filing independently without legal representation) requires only $150 to $600 in mandatory court filing fees, fingerprint processing, and criminal record abstract surcharges.

Criminal Defense Legal Retainers and Representation Tiers

Retaining an experienced criminal defense lawyer represents the largest financial component of clearing a DUI conviction. For standard first-time misdemeanor DUI convictions where all probationary terms, court fines, and alcohol education classes were successfully completed, attorneys generally quote flat fees between $800 and $2,000. Under this flat-rate retainer, the attorney obtains certified case records, drafts formal statutory motions, serves notice upon the prosecuting district attorney, and appears at the formal expungement court hearing on your behalf.

The cost of legal representation increases when cases involve procedural complications or enhanced criminal penalties. If your underlying case involved aggravated circumstances, probation revocations, unpaid restitution balances, or felony DUI charges, legal fees often climb to between $2,200 and $4,500. Complex petitions require extensive evidentiary briefing, character witness affidavits, and contested evidentiary court hearings to persuade a presiding judge that granting record relief serves the interests of justice.

Mandatory Court Filing Fees, Background Reports, and Filing Costs

Even when you choose to handle your expungement petition independently, municipal and state judicial systems levy mandatory administrative fees to process record-clearing paperwork. Superior and district courts assess civil filing fees that typically range from $100 to $350 per petition. In several jurisdictions (such as California under Penal Code 1203.4), statutory filing fees range from $60 to $150, though indigent petitioners who demonstrate financial hardship can petition for a complete fee waiver.

Before the court docket will accept an expungement petition, applicants must submit certified criminal history documentation to verify that no pending criminal charges or active arrest warrants exist. Obtaining an official state Department of Justice (DOJ) Live Scan fingerprint background check costs between $25 and $75, while ordering certified court dockets and police arrest incident reports generally adds $15 to $50.

State Statutory Variations, Waiting Periods, and Career Dividends

The legal feasibility and total expense of expunging a DUI vary dramatically based on the state in which the conviction occurred. States like California, Illinois, and Ohio permit the dismissal or sealing of misdemeanor DUIs upon successful completion of probation, whereas states like Texas, New York, and Florida strictly prohibit the expungement or sealing of driving under the influence convictions under standard circumstances. Understanding local state statutory mandates prevents wasting legal retainer funds on ineligible filings.

From an economic and career standpoint, successfully expunging a DUI yields an immense return on investment. With an expunged record, you can legally answer that you have never been convicted of a crime on standard private employer job applications. Clearing a DUI background check removes artificial earning ceilings, safeguards commercial driver and professional healthcare licenses, and unlocks lucrative career advancements that dwarf the initial legal expense.

How to Petition for a DUI Expungement in Five Steps

Follow these five legal steps to prepare, file, and finalize a formal petition to expunge or seal your DUI conviction.

  1. Verify Statutory Eligibility and Waiting Periods

    Confirm that your probation period has fully expired, all court fines and restitution are paid, and no new criminal charges are pending.

  2. Order Certified Court Dockets and Criminal Record

    Obtain certified copies of your final court disposition from the clerk of court and complete a state Live Scan fingerprint background check.

  3. Draft Formal Petition for Record Dismissal or Sealing

    Complete state-approved expungement forms, detailing your case number, date of conviction, code violation, and grounds for rehabilitation relief.

  4. File Documents and Formally Serve Prosecuting Agencies

    File your petition with the court clerk, pay the filing fee, and formally serve copies on the District Attorney and arresting police agency.

  5. Attend Judicial Hearing and Distribute Expungement Order

    Appear at the court hearing with counsel, obtain the signed dismissal order from the judge, and verify that state DOJ databases are updated.

Frequently Asked Questions (10 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can a DUI really be expunged from your record?

In many states like California, Ohio, and Washington, a DUI conviction can be expunged or dismissed under penal codes, though certain states like Florida and Texas prohibit DUI expungements.

Q2: How long after a DUI can you get it expunged?

You must typically wait until your probation has officially ended (usually three to five years), though select states allow early termination of probation for good cause.

Q3: Will an expunged DUI show up on an employment background check?

For private corporate employers, an expunged DUI will no longer appear on background checks; however, law enforcement agencies and state licensing boards can still view sealed records.

Q4: Does expunging a DUI clear your DMV driving record?

No, expungement clears your criminal court record, but your administrative DMV driving record typically retains DUI points or suspensions for five to ten years.

Q5: Can I expunge a DUI without hiring a lawyer?

Yes, you can file a pro se petition directly with the court clerk using state-approved forms, which costs only $150 to $600 in filing and fingerprint fees.

Q6: What happens if the district attorney objects to my DUI expungement?

If the prosecutor objects, the judge will schedule a contested evidentiary hearing where your lawyer must present evidence demonstrating rehabilitation and community service.

Q7: Can a second or third DUI be expunged?

Multiple DUIs are significantly harder to expunge and face intense prosecutorial opposition, but they are technically eligible in select states if all statutory waiting periods are satisfied.

Q8: How long does the entire expungement process take?

From filing the initial petition to receiving the signed judge order, the expungement process typically takes between two and six months depending on court caseloads.

Q9: Can an expunged DUI still be used as a prior offense?

Yes, if you are arrested for a subsequent DUI within your state lookback period (typically 7 to 10 years), the expunged conviction can still be used to enhance new criminal penalties.

Q10: Does a DUI expungement restore firearm rights?

Misdemeanor DUIs generally do not impact firearm rights, but if a felony DUI is reduced to a misdemeanor and expunged, firearm rights can be restored in select jurisdictions.
