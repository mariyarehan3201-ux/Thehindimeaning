Fort Ord Military Base, Cannery Row, and Maritime Hazards

The primary historical source of asbestos exposure in Monterey County was the United States Army's Fort Ord, which spanned over 28,000 acres and housed tens of thousands of troops from World War I until its closure in 1994. The rapid mid-century construction of thousands of military barracks, administrative headquarters, motor vehicle maintenance shops, and central heating boiler facilities utilized immense volumes of asbestos pipe lagging, transite siding, acoustic ceiling tiles, and floor mastics. Military personnel and civilian maintenance workers who repaired base infrastructure inhaled substantial amounts of toxic dust.

Furthermore, Monterey's famous Cannery Row housed dozens of sardine canning factories and fish reduction plants. In these industrial plants, high-pressure steam boilers, fish reduction cooking retorts, and steam pipelines were lagged with friable asbestos insulation and high-temperature gaskets. Cannery mechanics, boilermakers, and commercial boatyard shipwrights who serviced marine engines in the harbor worked in unventilated compartments where asbestos fibers accumulated heavily.

California Asbestos Law and Monterey County Court Procedures

Asbestos personal injury and wrongful death lawsuits in Monterey are governed by California tort law and filed in the Monterey County Superior Court or coordinated dockets in the San Francisco Bay Area. Under California Code of Civil Procedure (CCP) Section 340.2, the statute of limitations for personal injury asbestos claims is one year from the date the claimant first suffered disability and either knew, or through reasonable diligence should have known, that the disability was caused by asbestos exposure.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is California's statute of limitations for an asbestos lawsuit in Monterey?

Under California CCP § 340.2, plaintiffs have one year from the date of disability and diagnosis, or one year from death for wrongful death claims.

Q2: What is trial preference under California CCP Section 36?

Trial preference allows terminally ill or elderly asbestos plaintiffs to have their cases brought to trial within 120 days of petition approval.

Q3: Can military veterans and civilian workers at Fort Ord file claims?

Yes, veterans and civilian contractors exposed to asbestos building materials and motor pool parts at Fort Ord can recover damages.

Q4: Where do Monterey residents go for specialized mesothelioma treatment?

Patients frequently receive care at Stanford Cancer Institute in Palo Alto, UCSF in San Francisco, or CHOMP in Monterey.

Q5: Can former Cannery Row workers file asbestos claims?

Yes, cannery mechanics, boiler tenders, and maintenance workers exposed to steam piping and boilers have viable legal claims.

Q6: Can family members in Monterey sue for take-home asbestos exposure?

Yes, California courts permit family members who inhaled fibers from laundering a worker's contaminated clothing to file claims.

Q7: Do I have to appear in a courtroom if I file an asbestos lawsuit?

Rarely; most cases settle out of court, and video depositions are routinely conducted in the comfort of your Monterey home.

Q8: How much does it cost to hire an asbestos attorney in Monterey?

Attorneys work on contingency, meaning clients pay no upfront fees and legal costs are deducted only from successful recoveries.
