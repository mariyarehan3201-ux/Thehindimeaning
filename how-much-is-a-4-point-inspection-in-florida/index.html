How Much Is a 4 Point Inspection in Florida?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Understanding how much a 4 point inspection in Florida costs is essential for homeowners, real estate buyers, and landlords navigating the state rigorous property insurance marketplace. Typically mandated by insurance underwriters for Florida homes exceeding twenty to thirty years of age, a standalone 4 point inspection costs between $100 and $200. Because homeowners frequently pair this review with a wind mitigation inspection to unlock valuable premium discounts, bundled inspection packages commonly run between $175 and $300.

The Four Critical Systems Evaluated During the Inspection

Insurance carriers in Florida require a 4 point inspection specifically to assess high-risk components prone to catastrophic claims. The four vital areas evaluated include the roof covering, the electrical panel and wiring, the plumbing distribution system and water heater, and the central heating and air conditioning (HVAC) infrastructure. Unlike an exhaustive buyer real estate inspection that spans cosmetics and minor appliances, the 4 point inspection focuses strictly on safety hazards, material age, and loss-prevention liabilities.

The inspection results determine whether an underwriter like Citizens Property Insurance Corporation or a private admitted insurer will issue or renew a homeowners policy. The licensed inspector documents each system, records manufacturer serial numbers, notes expected remaining useful lifespan, and photographs key components. If any of the four systems fail to meet carrier minimum standards, insurance coverage will be denied until corrective repairs are certified.

Pricing Variables and Bundling with Wind Mitigation

The cost of a 4 point inspection varies based on geographic county, property square footage, and whether you bundle services. In high-density South Florida markets such as Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach counties, standalone prices skew slightly higher ($150 to $225) due to stricter building enforcement and higher business overhead. In Central and North Florida, standalone inspections are frequently available for $100 to $150.

Savvy Florida homeowners almost always bundle a 4 point inspection with a wind mitigation inspection. A wind mitigation inspection examines roof geometry, hurricane clips, decking nail patterns, and storm shutter protections. Under Florida law (FS 627.711), insurance companies are required to grant substantial annual premium credits for wind-resistant features. While each inspection costs $125 to $150 separately, inspectors bundle both for $200 to $275, yielding insurance savings that often exceed $1,000 annually.

Navigating Inspection Deficiencies and Re-Inspections

Receiving an unsatisfactory mark on a Florida 4 point inspection report is common in homes built before 1990. Underwriters will flag outdated Zinsco or Federal Pacific Electric Stab-Lok breaker panels due to known fire hazards, ungrounded two-prong outlets, cloth-sheathed wiring, or grey polybutylene plumbing lines prone to sudden joint burst failures. Furthermore, water heaters older than ten to twelve years are frequently flagged for mandatory replacement.

When deficiencies are cited, the inspector delivers an official report highlighting required corrections. Homeowners must hire licensed trade contractors—such as an electrician or master plumber—to perform remediations. Once work is completed and permits are finalized, the inspector performs a quick re-inspection (often for a nominal $50 to $75 fee) to issue a clean report that allows your insurance policy to bind without delay.

Frequently Asked Questions (9 Questions Answered)

Q1: Why is a 4 point inspection required in Florida?

Florida insurance companies require it on older homes (usually 20 to 30 years or older) to verify that roof, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC systems do not pose high claim risks.

Q2: Can a home fail a 4 point inspection?

The inspection does not formally pass or fail; it documents conditions, but insurance carriers will refuse coverage if hazardous issues like bad panels or old roofs are noted.

Q3: How long is a Florida 4 point inspection valid?

Most insurance underwriters consider a 4 point inspection report valid for one year from the date of the inspection.

Q4: What electrical panels fail a 4 point inspection?

Federal Pacific Electric (FPE) Stab-Lok, Zinsco, Challenger, and fuse boxes are routinely rejected by Florida insurance underwriters.

Q5: Does polybutylene pipe fail a 4 point inspection?

Yes, virtually all Florida insurance carriers will reject a home with interior polybutylene supply pipes until a whole-house replumb is completed.

Q6: How much does a 4 point and wind mitigation combo cost?

Bundling both inspections typically costs between $175 and $300, saving $50 to $100 compared to booking them separately.

Q7: Who pays for the 4 point inspection during a home sale?

In most real estate transactions, the buyer pays for the inspection as part of securing homeowner insurance financing, though terms can be negotiated.

Q8: Can a homeowner do their own 4 point inspection?

No, Florida insurance carriers only accept reports signed by licensed home inspectors, building contractors, architects, or professional engineers.

Q9: What happens if my water heater is over 15 years old?

Most Florida insurers will require you to replace an operating water heater over 12 to 15 years old before issuing or renewing your policy.
