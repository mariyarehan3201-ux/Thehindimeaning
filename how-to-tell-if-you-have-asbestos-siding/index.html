How to Tell if You Have Asbestos Siding?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

How to tell if you have asbestos siding is a common question among owners of vintage homes seeking to remodel, repair, or purchase older residential properties. Manufactured primarily from the 1920s through the late 1970s, asbestos-cement siding shingles—commonly referred to in construction trades as transite—possess distinct physical textures, dimensions, and structural characteristics that distinguish them from modern vinyl, wood, or fiber cement cladding.

Primary Architectural and Physical Identification Markers

Asbestos-cement siding shingles were one of the most popular exterior cladding choices of the twentieth century, celebrated for their exceptional fireproofing qualities, immunity to insect damage, and resistance to weather rot. If your home was constructed or renovated between 1920 and 1978, there is a strong probability that original shingle cladding contains commercial chrysotile or amosite asbestos fibers blended with Portland cement. Recognizing key physical indicators provides a strong basis for preliminary identification.

One of the clearest visual indicators of asbestos siding is the dimensional shape and profile of the tiles. Asbestos shingles were typically manufactured in standard rectangular dimensions of twelve inches in height by twenty-four inches in width. The bottom edges of these shingles frequently feature distinctive decorative cuts, including wavy scalloped profiles, angled hexagonal cuts, or clean straight lines. Furthermore, because these tiles were made of dense pressed cement, they feel extremely rigid, hard, and stony to the touch, entirely unlike flexible vinyl or softer wood shake siding.

Fastener Patterns, Corner Assemblies, and Surface Textures

Another definitive physical marker of asbestos siding is the method used to fasten the tiles to the house framing. Because asbestos-cement tiles are heavy and brittle, manufacturers pre-drilled small holes near the bottom corners of each tile during factory fabrication. Installers secured these tiles using exposed exterior nails driven through the face of the shingle near the bottom edge. In contrast, modern lap siding and vinyl profiles utilize concealed nailing hems positioned at the top of each course, completely hiding fasteners from view.

The corner details of the exterior wall assembly also provide critical diagnostic clues. When installing asbestos shingles, builders rarely mitered the fragile cement corners. Instead, they capped exterior corners with specialized pre-formed metal corner clips, typically fabricated from aluminum, zinc, or copper, to conceal the butt joints between intersecting walls. If your building exterior features twelve-by-twenty-four-inch rigid tiles secured with exposed face nails and accented by stamped metal corner caps, the siding is almost certainly asbestos-cement transite.

Surface texture is equally telling. Most asbestos shingles feature heavily textured, embossed surfaces designed to simulate coarse wood grain, heavy tree bark, or vertical striations. These stamped textures were applied under hydraulic pressure during manufacturing. Despite decades of exposure to rain, snow, and sunlight, the underlying cement matrix does not warp, curl, rot, or decay. However, unpainted tiles often exhibit a chalky white or grayish cementitious patina on unweathered backside surfaces.

While visual markers provide strong circumstantial evidence, definitive confirmation requires laboratory testing. Never attempt to break, saw, drill, or sand a suspected shingle to inspect its core, as this fractures the matrix and releases respirable fibers. To confirm asbestos content with certainty, property owners collect a small broken piece or scrape a tiny fragment from an already damaged edge, place it in a sealed zip-top bag, and submit it to a laboratory accredited under NVLAP for Polarized Light Microscopy (PLM) analysis.

How to Inspect and Confirm Suspected Asbestos Siding

Non-destructive evaluation protocol for determining whether exterior shingles contain asbestos.

  1. Verify the Age of the Structure

    Check municipal property tax assessments or deed records to determine if the home was constructed or extensively renovated between 1920 and 1978.

  2. Conduct a Visual Inspection of Dimensions and Fasteners

    Check if the shingles measure roughly twelve by twenty-four inches, feature visible exposed nail heads near bottom edges, and utilize metal corner caps.

  3. Examine Surface Hardness and Weathering

    Gently touch the material to verify a rigid, dense, stone-like texture that shows zero rotting, wood warping, or insect damage despite decades of weather exposure.

  4. Submit a Fragment for Laboratory PLM Testing

    Collect a small existing broken piece or have a certified inspector take a wet sample for Polarized Light Microscopy analysis at an NVLAP-accredited lab.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What year was asbestos siding discontinued in homes?

Manufacturing of asbestos-cement siding phased out in the United States in the late 1970s following EPA Clean Air Act regulations, ending by the early 1980s.

Q2: Is asbestos siding dangerous if left untouched?

No, intact asbestos siding is non-friable, meaning fibers are locked inside the cement matrix and cannot become airborne unless cut, sanded, or broken.

Q3: Can you power wash asbestos siding?

No, power washing asbestos siding is strongly discouraged because high-pressure water streams can erode the surface and release asbestos fibers into the air and soil.

Q4: What are the common dimensions of asbestos shingles?

The vast majority of vintage asbestos-cement shingles were manufactured in panels measuring twelve inches high by twenty-four inches wide.

Q5: How do you tell the difference between asbestos siding and modern fiber cement?

Modern fiber cement siding was introduced in the late 1980s and is blind-nailed with smooth joints, whereas asbestos siding uses exposed face nails and metal corner caps.

Q6: Can asbestos siding be painted?

Yes, painting asbestos siding with high-grade 100% acrylic latex exterior paint is an excellent, approved encapsulation method that seals the surface.

Q7: Do home inspectors check for asbestos siding?

General home inspectors frequently identify suspect asbestos siding based on vintage and appearance, but standard inspections exclude definitive laboratory testing.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Determining whether your property has asbestos siding is an essential first step before undertaking exterior painting, window replacements, or siding renovations. Intact asbestos siding that is in sound condition poses virtually zero risk to household health as long as it remains undisturbed. By identifying physical markers and confirming suspicions through accredited laboratory testing, homeowners can make informed decisions regarding encapsulation, repainting, or certified removal.
