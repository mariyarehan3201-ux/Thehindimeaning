Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does a la carte background check mean for employers?

An a la carte background check allows employers to order individual screening components—such as a single county criminal search, motor vehicle report, or education verification—without paying for bundled packages.

Q2: Can an employer run a background check without candidate consent?

No, under the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), employers must provide a standalone written disclosure and obtain explicit written authorization before conducting an employment background check.

Q3: What POS system does Chick-fil-A use in its restaurants?

Chick-fil-A utilizes a proprietary, custom-built enterprise point-of-sale platform running on specialized enterprise touchscreen hardware, tightly integrated with mobile ordering apps and kitchen display systems.

Q4: How do you become a Voice over IP (VoIP) reseller or provider?

Aspiring VoIP providers partner with established wholesale telecommunications carriers (via white-label reseller programs), configure cloud PBX software, secure FCC regulatory registrations, and market to local businesses.

Q5: What is a chart of accounts for a construction company?

A construction chart of accounts is a specialized financial ledger organized to track job-costing categories, separating direct job materials, subcontractor labor, equipment rental, and work-in-progress assets.

Q6: How often should utility patents be renewed with the USPTO?

In the United States, utility patents mandate maintenance fee payments at three and a half, seven and a half, and eleven and a half years after the original grant date to prevent patent expiration.

Q7: What is the advantage of cloud-hosted VoIP over legacy landlines?

Cloud VoIP eliminates expensive on-premise PBX hardware, reduces international and long-distance costs, supports remote mobile workers, and provides seamless CRM and voicemail-to-email integrations.

Q8: Why is SOC 2 compliance important when selecting commercial software?

SOC 2 Type II certification independently verifies that a software vendor maintains rigorous operational controls protecting customer data security, availability, processing integrity, and confidentiality.
