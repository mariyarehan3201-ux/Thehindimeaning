CHA Full Form in Shipping: Custom House Agent Guide

Full Form
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 17, 2026

In international trade, maritime logistics, freight forwarding, and port operations, the full form of CHA is Custom House Agent (formally designated as a Customs Broker under modernized customs regulations). Operating under license granted by the Principal Commissioner or Commissioner of Customs under the statutory provisions of the Customs Act of 1962 and the Customs Brokers Licensing Regulations (CBLR), a CHA acts as the legally authorized intermediary between commercial importers/exporters and Indian Customs authorities. A CHA prepares statutory documentation, classifies goods under the Harmonized System (HS) code, calculates duties, files electronic Bills of Entry and Shipping Bills, coordinates port inspections, and expedites the legal clearance of ocean and air cargo.

International supply chains represent a complex intersection of international transport, national regulatory oversight, and statutory tax collection. When ocean freighters dock at major ports such as Nhava Sheva (JNPT) or Chennai Port, cargo cannot simply be unloaded and driven away; every container must undergo statutory clearance under the Customs Act of 1962. The licensed professional responsible for navigating this high-stakes regulatory interface is the Custom House Agent (CHA), officially redesignated as a Customs Broker under the Customs Brokers Licensing Regulations (CBLR). Without the expertise of a CHA, international commerce would grind to a bureaucratic halt.

The fundamental responsibility of a Custom House Agent is to ensure full legal compliance while minimizing cargo dwell times at sea ports, air cargo complexes, and Inland Container Depots (ICDs). Importers and exporters rely on CHAs to accurately classify commodities under the eight-digit Harmonized System of Nomenclature (HSN) codes, calculate applicable customs tariffs (including basic customs duty, integrated GST, and anti-dumping duties), and determine eligibility for government free trade agreements (FTAs) or export incentive schemes like RoDTEP.

Beyond digital document filing, Custom House Agents serve as physical representatives on the ground. When customs authorities flag a container for first-check or second-check examination, the CHA's licensed staff (holding Regulation G or Regulation H photo identity cards) attend the container freight station (CFS). They coordinate container de-stuffing, assist customs examination officers in inspecting sample goods, facilitate testing from allied agencies (such as FSSAI, Plant Quarantine, or Drug Controller), and obtain the crucial 'Out of Charge' (OoC) order permitting cargo dispatch into the domestic economy.

With India actively modernizing its logistics infrastructure through initiatives like 'Turant Customs', Faceless Assessment, and electronic cargo tracking, the role of the Custom House Agent has transformed from physical paperwork processing to high-tech regulatory consulting. Modern CHAs provide data analytics, supply chain risk mitigation, and advisory services, serving as essential navigators of international trade.

How to Obtain a Custom House Agent (Customs Broker) License in India

  1. Meet Educational and Professional Eligibility Criteria

    Hold a graduate degree from a recognized university along with a master's or professional degree (such as CA, CS, MBA, LL.B., or specialized customs certification).

  2. Apply for the CBLR Regulation 6 Written Examination

    Submit your online application through the National Academy of Customs, Indirect Taxes and Narcotics (NACIN) during the annual national licensing notification.

  3. Clear the Computer-Based Written and Oral Exams

    Pass the rigorous computer-based objective examination covering customs tariffs, valuation, trade agreements, and port procedures, followed by a viva-voce oral interview.

  4. Furnish Financial Security and Receive License

    Provide statutory financial surety bonds and bank guarantees to the Commissioner of Customs to obtain your formal Customs Broker License (Form D) for nationwide operations.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the full form of CHA in shipping?

CHA stands for Custom House Agent, an authorized professional responsible for clearing import and export shipments through customs borders.

Q2: Is CHA now called a Customs Broker in India?

Yes, under the Customs Brokers Licensing Regulations (CBLR 2013 and CBLR 2018), the term Custom House Agent was officially redesignated as Customs Broker to align with global trade terminology.

Q3: What law governs the licensing and conduct of CHAs in India?

The Customs Act of 1962 (Section 146) alongside the Customs Brokers Licensing Regulations (CBLR) 2018 governs CHA licensing, duties, and professional conduct.

Q4: What is a Bill of Entry filed by a CHA?

A Bill of Entry is a legal declaration submitted electronically on the ICEGATE portal by the CHA outlining the nature, quantity, HS code, and value of imported goods for duty assessment.

Q5: What is a Shipping Bill filed for export cargo?

A Shipping Bill is the statutory document filed by a CHA on ICEGATE to obtain customs clearance and 'Let Export Order' (LEO) for goods shipped abroad.

Q6: Can an individual or company operate as a CHA without a license?

No, Section 146 of the Customs Act strictly prohibits any person or business from acting as a customs broker without a valid license issued by the Commissioner of Customs.

Q7: What is the ICEGATE portal used by CHAs?

ICEGATE (Indian Customs Electronic Gateway) is the national digital portal through which CHAs file customs documentation, pay customs duties, and track container clearances.

Q8: What is the difference between a Freight Forwarder and a CHA?

A freight forwarder arranges physical transportation and cargo space on ships or aircraft, whereas a CHA specifically handles regulatory customs documentation and border tax clearance.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Custom House Agents (Customs Brokers) are indispensable gatekeepers and enablers of international shipping and logistics. Armed with comprehensive knowledge of customs law, tariff classification, and port operations, CHAs ensure that commercial shipments clear regulatory borders smoothly, cost-effectively, and in full compliance with the law.
