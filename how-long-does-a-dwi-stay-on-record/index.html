How Long Does a DWI Stay on Record?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how long does a dwi stay on record is a vital question for drivers convicted of driving while intoxicated. In states that use the acronym DWI rather than DUI (such as Texas, New York, New Jersey, Missouri, and North Carolina), the long-term impact of a conviction is governed by state-specific statutory frameworks. A DWI conviction impacts your life across multiple parallel dimensions: administrative driving records, auto insurance underwriting, criminal history records, and statutory lookback periods for repeat offenses.

Driving Records vs. Criminal History Repositories

To understand how long a DWI stays on your record, you must distinguish between your Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV/DPS) driving record and your state Criminal Justice Information repository. Your driving record reflects your license eligibility, administrative suspensions, and points. In states like Texas and New York, a DWI conviction remains on your certified driving record for a minimum of 10 years, and in many jurisdictions, permanently.

On your official criminal history record (maintained by state police repositories and the FBI), a DWI conviction is permanent for life. Unless your state offers specific statutory relief—such as an Order of Nondisclosure in Texas or conditional sealing in New York—a criminal DWI never expires on its own. Private commercial background screening firms generally report convictions for 7 years under state consumer reporting rules, but criminal courts and law enforcement maintain permanent access.

The Statutory Lookback Period for Repeat Offenses

The most dangerous legal consequence of a DWI on your record is the 'Statutory Lookback Period.' A lookback period is the timeframe during which a prior DWI can be used by prosecutors to elevate a new arrest into a higher-tier crime with mandatory jail sentences. Lookback periods vary dramatically by state.

For example, in New York and North Carolina, the statutory lookback period is 10 years and 7 years respectively; a second offense within that window triggers mandatory ignition interlock devices and substantial license revocations. In stark contrast, Texas enforces a Lifetime Lookback Rule. In Texas, a DWI conviction from thirty years ago never expires for enhancement purposes; any subsequent DWI arrest automatically constitutes a second or third offense (a third offense being a Third-Degree Felony carrying 2 to 10 years in Texas state prison).

Record Sealing Mechanisms: Texas Orders of Nondisclosure and Missouri Expungements

While a DWI cannot be completely deleted in most jurisdictions, certain states have introduced modern relief mechanisms. In Texas, the passage of House Bill 3016 created an 'Order of Nondisclosure' for eligible first-time DWI offenders who had an alcohol concentration below 0.15% BAC and caused zero accident damage. After completing probation and a 2-to-5-year waiting period, a judge can seal the DWI, legally hiding it from private employers and the public.

Similarly, in Missouri, state statutes permit first-time misdemeanor DWI offenders to petition for formal expungement after maintaining a clean, crime-free record for five years post-sentence. Once expunged in Missouri, the record is legally closed, restoring the individual's civil status. However, in states like New Jersey and North Carolina, DWI convictions remain permanently ineligible for expungement.

How to Mitigate the Long-Term Impact of a DWI on Your Record

Follow these five strategic steps to check your record and pursue available sealing remedies.

  1. Pull Your Certified State Driving Record

    Order an official certified driving transcript from your state DMV/DPS to verify points and suspension dates.

  2. Request Your State Police Background Check

    Obtain your official state criminal history record to confirm how the court disposition was entered.

  3. Determine State Statutory Sealing Eligibility

    Research whether your state (like Texas or Missouri) permits Orders of Nondisclosure or expungements for DWIs.

  4. Maintain Clean Driving and Insurance for 5 Years

    Avoid moving violations to ensure auto insurance rates reset to standard tiers after 3 to 5 years.

  5. File a Formal Petition for Record Sealing

    Retain a criminal defense attorney to file for statutory nondisclosure once your state waiting window elapses.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How long does a DWI stay on your driving record?

In most states, a DWI remains on your official DMV driving record for 10 years, and permanently in states like Texas and North Carolina.

Q2: Does a DWI ever drop off your criminal record?

No, criminal records are permanent for life unless your state has enacted specific expungement or nondisclosure laws.

Q3: What is the lookback period for a second DWI in Texas?

Texas has a lifetime lookback period; a prior DWI conviction never expires and will always enhance a future arrest.

Q4: Can a first-time DWI be expunged in Texas?

Standard expungement is unavailable for convictions, but eligible first-time offenders can obtain an Order of Nondisclosure under HB 3016.

Q5: How long does a DWI affect car insurance rates?

Auto insurance companies standardly check 3 to 5 years of driving history; insurance surcharges typically phase out after 3 to 5 years.

Q6: Can you go to Canada with a DWI on your record?

No, Canada classifies foreign DWIs as serious criminality, rendering you inadmissible at border crossings without a special waiver (TRP).

Q7: What is the difference between a DUI and a DWI?

Terminology varies by state; in some states DUI is for minors or lower BAC, while in states like Texas and New York, DWI is the standard adult charge.

Q8: Can a DWI stop you from getting a commercial driver's license (CDL)?

Yes, a first DWI suspends a CDL for 1 year (3 years if hauling hazmat), and a second conviction results in a lifetime commercial driving ban.
