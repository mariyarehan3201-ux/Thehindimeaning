Asbestos Walls Removal: Containment & Steps

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 23, 2026

Asbestos walls removal encompasses the systematic demolition and environmental extraction of interior plaster, drywall joint compound, and rigid exterior cement boards containing hazardous mineral fibers. Widely installed throughout residential, commercial, and institutional structures prior to 1980, asbestos wall assemblies release hazardous airborne dust if cut, drilled, or demolished without negative pressure containment.

The Composition of Asbestos Wall Assemblies

Asbestos was integrated into interior and exterior wall assemblies in multiple distinct forms throughout the twentieth century. In residential homes, the most common hazard is not typically the gypsum plasterboard sheet itself, but rather the multi-layered drywall joint compound (mud) and textured decorative finishes applied over wall seams and screw indentations. Joint compounds formulated between 1945 and 1978 regularly contained 5% to 15% chrysotile asbestos fibers to prevent shrinkage cracking.

In historic homes built prior to 1950, traditional multi-coat lath and plaster walls frequently incorporated asbestos fibers into the brown base coat and finish coat for fireproofing and acoustic dampening. Additionally, commercial, industrial, and agricultural buildings often featured rigid asbestos-cement wallboards (Transite), which contained 15% to 30% chrysotile or amosite asbestos compressed with Portland cement.

Drywall Joint Compound (Mud) Gypsum, limestone, chrysotile binder High when sanded or cracked 3% to 15% Chrysotile

Containment Engineering and Negative Air Pressure

Because demolishing interior wall structures inevitably creates substantial particulate dust, professional abatement contractors must establish a complete negative pressure containment enclosure before breaching the wall substrate. The work area is hermetically sealed using two layers of flame-retardant 6-mil polyethylene sheeting taped over all windows, doorways, light fixtures, electrical outlets, and HVAC registers.

A heavy-duty HEPA-filtered air filtration device (negative air machine) runs continuously, exhausting filtered air to the outside of the building envelope. This engineering control maintains a minimum negative pressure differential of -0.02 inches of water column relative to adjacent clean rooms, ensuring that any airborne fibers generated during wall removal cannot escape through minor microscopic gaps in the plastic containment barrier.

Controlled Wet Demolition and Waste Packaging Protocols

Wall removal proceeds systematically using controlled wet demolition techniques. Abatement technicians spray wall surfaces continuously with amended water—a solution of water mixed with liquid surfactant—which penetrates deep into the porous plaster and joint compound, saturating the mineral fibers. Workers score drywall into manageable panels rather than aggressively smashing walls with sledgehammers, minimizing fiber aerosolization.

Demolished wall sections and plaster rubble are loaded immediately into heavy-duty 6-mil polyethylene bags or lined fiberboard drums. Waste bags are sealed using the airtight gooseneck taping technique, washed clean in the decontamination wash room, and loaded into locked, placarded transport containers for delivery to an authorized hazardous waste landfill. Following removal, bare wall studs are HEPA vacuumed and sealed with an approved lockdown encapsulant.

How Professional Contractors Execute Asbestos Wall Abatement

  1. Perform Bulk Sampling Survey

    Collect core samples of drywall, joint compound, and plaster to verify the presence, mineral type, and concentration of asbestos.

  2. Erect Poly Containment and Air Scrubbers

    Seal the room with double 6-mil poly plastic, set up a three-stage decontamination unit, and start HEPA negative air filtration.

  3. Saturate Wall Panels with Amended Water

    Spray the wall surface thoroughly with surfactant solution to keep all plaster and joint compound moist throughout dismantling.

  4. Dismantle Wall Panels Systematically

    Carefully unscrew or pry full wall panels loose using hand tools, avoiding shattering or crushing materials into small pieces.

  5. Apply Lockdown Encapsulant and Test Air

    Spray timber or metal wall framing with polymer encapsulant, HEPA vacuum all surfaces, and pass an independent clearance air test.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can drywall itself contain asbestos?

The interior gypsum core rarely contains asbestos, but the joint compound (mud) and texture applied to walls frequently contains 3% to 15%.

Q2: Is it safe to drill into an asbestos wall?

Drilling releases concentrated plumes of dry dust; if drilling is necessary, technicians use HEPA-shrouded drills with wet shaving cream.

Q3: What is Transite wallboard?

Transite is a brand name for rigid cement-asbestos wallboard widely used for firewalls, exterior cladding, and industrial partitions.

Q4: Can asbestos walls be covered instead of removed?

Yes, installing a new layer of half-inch drywall directly over existing walls (encapsulation) is a legally approved, cost-effective alternative.

Q5: What is a lockdown encapsulant?

A lockdown encapsulant is a specialized liquid polymer sprayed onto exposed wall studs after removal to trap any lingering microscopic fibers.

Q6: How much does it cost to remove asbestos walls?

Removal typically costs between $8 and $20 per square foot, depending on whether walls are single-layer drywall or thick heavy plaster.

Q7: How long does wall abatement take?

A standard residential room typically takes 2 to 3 days, including containment setup, wet removal, drying, and third-party air clearance.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Asbestos walls removal requires rigorous environmental containment, continuous negative pressure, and diligent wet suppression. Homeowners should never attempt to knock down, saw, or dry-sand older plaster or textured drywall without certified testing. Engaging licensed environmental contractors ensures safe, lawful abatement, accompanied by independent air clearance testing to certify the home clean for reconstruction.
