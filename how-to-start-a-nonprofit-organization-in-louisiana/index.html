How to Start a Nonprofit Organization in Louisiana?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Starting a nonprofit organization in Louisiana empowers passionate community organizers, educational innovators, and charitable leaders to build lasting philanthropic institutions across the Bayou State. Louisiana's unique legal heritage—rooted in civil code traditions—establishes distinct procedural steps for incorporating nonprofit corporations, filing initial officer reports, and securing state and federal tax exemptions. By mastering filings through the state's geauxBIZ portal, assembling an independent board of directors, and obtaining federal 501(c)(3) recognition, founders can launch a mission-driven organization capable of receiving deductible gifts and grants.

Incorporating Under Louisiana Non-Profit Corporation Law via geauxBIZ

Forming a nonprofit entity in Louisiana is governed by the Louisiana Non-Profit Corporation Law (Louisiana Revised Statutes Title 12, Chapter 2). The legal process begins by registering an entity account through the Louisiana Secretary of State's geauxBIZ electronic filing platform. Founders must draft Articles of Incorporation that designate a distinguishably unique corporate name containing an appropriate corporate suffix such as 'Corporation,' 'Incorporated,' or 'Limited.'

Crucially, Louisiana requires the simultaneous filing of an Initial Report (Form 399) alongside the Articles of Incorporation. This document lists the names and municipal physical addresses of the organization's initial directors and registered agent. The registered agent must also sign a formal affidavit of acceptance. To ensure compliance with IRS Section 501(c)(3) requirements, the Articles must include explicit language restricting the entity to tax-exempt purposes and dedicating remaining assets upon dissolution exclusively to other exempt charities or governmental bodies.

Board Governance, Bylaws, and Louisiana Director Requirements

Under Louisiana Revised Statutes (La. R.S. 12:224), a nonprofit corporation must have at least three directors, unless the articles or bylaws specify a different number. To satisfy IRS public charity criteria, appointing a board of at least three unrelated individuals with diverse skills in financial oversight, legal compliance, and community outreach is highly recommended.

The board must hold an initial organizational meeting to adopt corporate bylaws, elect executive officers (such as President, Secretary, and Treasurer), and approve a comprehensive Conflict of Interest policy. The bylaws govern internal operational mechanics, including notice periods for annual director meetings, voting quorum standards, term limits, and procedures for amending corporate policies.

Securing Louisiana Department of Revenue Tax Exemptions

Achieving federal 501(c)(3) tax-exempt status from the IRS does not automatically confer state-level tax exemptions in Louisiana. To secure exemption from Louisiana state corporate income tax and franchise tax, the organization must submit an Application for Exemption (Form R-1028) to the Louisiana Department of Revenue (LDR), accompanied by a copy of the Articles of Incorporation, bylaws, and the official IRS determination letter.

For sales and use tax exemptions, Louisiana law is unusually strict. Unlike many states where 501(c)(3) charities enjoy blanket sales tax immunity, Louisiana grants sales tax exemptions only to specific statutory categories of charitable organizations (such as churches, orphanages, and certain free medical clinics). Nonprofits should consult the Department of Revenue to determine whether their specific programmatic activities qualify for state and local sales tax exemption certificates.

How to Form a Louisiana Nonprofit Corporation

Step-by-step roadmap to draft articles of incorporation, appoint initial directors, secure 501(c)(3) recognition, and file for Louisiana state tax exemptions.

  1. Verify Organization Name Availability on geauxBIZ

    Search the Louisiana Secretary of State commercial database to ensure your chosen nonprofit name is available and includes an accepted corporate designator.

  2. Draft and File Articles of Incorporation and Initial Report

    File Form 399 via geauxBIZ including required 501(c)(3) purpose and dissolution clauses, along with the signed registered agent acceptance.

  3. Convene Organizational Meeting to Adopt Bylaws

    Assemble your three-member board of directors to adopt operational bylaws, approve a conflict of interest policy, and elect corporate officers.

  4. Obtain Federal Employer Identification Number (EIN)

    Apply online at the IRS website for an immediate EIN, which is required to open organizational banking accounts and submit tax filings.

  5. Apply for 501(c)(3) Recognition and State Exemption

    Submit IRS Form 1023 or 1023-EZ, and upon receiving determination, submit Form R-1028 to the Louisiana Department of Revenue for state corporate tax relief.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to incorporate a nonprofit in Louisiana?

The Louisiana Secretary of State charges a $75 fee to file nonprofit Articles of Incorporation and the Initial Report through the geauxBIZ portal. Federal 501(c)(3) fees are either $275 (Form 1023-EZ) or $600 (Form 1023).

Q2: How many directors are required for a Louisiana nonprofit?

Louisiana law requires at least three initial directors to form a nonprofit corporation. For IRS 501(c)(3) compliance, appointing at least three unrelated board members ensures proper governance oversight.

Q3: What is an Initial Report (Form 399) in Louisiana?

The Initial Report is a mandatory state document filed simultaneously with the Articles of Incorporation. It records the organization's initial municipal address, director roster, and signed registered agent acceptance.

Q4: Are all 501(c)(3) nonprofits exempt from Louisiana sales tax?

No. Louisiana does not provide a universal state sales tax exemption to all 501(c)(3) charities. Only specific statutory classifications, such as educational entities and certain health facilities, qualify under Louisiana law.

Q5: Does Louisiana require charitable registration before fundraising?

Yes, organizations that solicit funds from the general public in Louisiana must register with the Louisiana Department of Justice / Attorney General's Public Protection Division, unless specifically exempt.

Q6: How often must Louisiana nonprofits file annual reports?

Louisiana nonprofits must file an Annual Report with the Secretary of State each year on or before the anniversary date of their original corporate incorporation.

Q7: What is geauxBIZ?

geauxBIZ is the official multi-agency web portal operated by the Louisiana Secretary of State used to incorporate businesses, file annual reports, and manage corporate registrations in Louisiana.

Q8: Can an executive director serve on the board of a Louisiana nonprofit?

Yes, an executive director can serve on the board, but to avoid conflicts of interest, they should serve in a non-voting capacity or recuse themselves from all discussions regarding their own compensation.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Establishing a successful nonprofit organization in Louisiana requires meticulous navigation of state civil law procedures, transparent corporate governance, and coordinated state and federal tax filings. By utilizing the geauxBIZ portal, enacting comprehensive bylaws, assembling an active independent governing board, and maintaining timely annual reports with both the Secretary of State and the IRS, your nonprofit will build an enduring platform to serve communities across Louisiana.
