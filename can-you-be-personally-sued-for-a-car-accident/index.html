Can You Be Personally Sued for a Car Accident?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 30, 2026

Being involved in a serious motor vehicle collision is a traumatic experience that frequently escalates into acute financial distress when injured claimants threaten litigation against the at-fault motorist. While most vehicular collisions are resolved within the monetary boundaries of standard auto insurance policies, drivers can indeed be sued personally if the resulting bodily injury damages, medical bills, and lost earnings exceed their policy liability limits. When insurance policy maximums are exhausted or if an insurer denies coverage due to policy exclusions, an injured plaintiff can petition civil courts to enter personal judgments against the negligent driver. This legal vulnerability exposes private bank accounts, real estate holdings, investment portfolios, and future employment wages to civil asset seizure, garnishment, and long-term financial insolvency unless protected by robust liability coverage, umbrella insurance, or statutory exemption laws.

When Car Accident Claims Exceed Auto Insurance Policy Limits

The vast majority of vehicular accident claims conclude through private negotiations between insurance adjusters and personal injury attorneys without proceeding to a full civil courtroom trial. Under standard personal auto policies, the insurance carrier maintains a contractual duty to defend the policyholder against third-party liability claims and pay settlements up to the purchased liability limits. However, every auto insurance policy establishes strict monetary caps on bodily injury liability per person, total bodily injury liability per accident, and property damage liability.

In dozens of jurisdictions across the United States, statutory minimum liability coverage requirements remain dangerously low, frequently mandating as little as twenty-five thousand dollars for bodily injury per person and fifty thousand dollars per collision. In a catastrophic traffic crash involving emergency air transport, intensive care hospitalization, spinal trauma, or orthopedic surgery, medical expenses alone can surpass one hundred thousand dollars within seventy-two hours. Understanding what can you sue for in a car accident helps drivers realize that claims encompass not just vehicle repairs, but lifelong medical care, disability modifications, and severe non-economic damages.

When an injured claimant sustains severe damages far exceeding the at-fault driver's policy limits, the plaintiff attorney will typically issue a formal policy limits demand letter. If the policyholder possesses substantial personal net worth, the injured party may refuse a policy limits settlement or demand personal contributions from the at-fault motorist. If negotiations fail, the plaintiff files a personal injury lawsuit naming the driver individually as the defendant. Personal Asset Exposure, Wage Garnishment, and Judgment Enforcement

If a civil court jury enters a verdict that exceeds the defendant's auto insurance coverage, the court records an excess judgment against the individual defendant. At this junction, the plaintiff becomes a judgment creditor possessing powerful statutory tools to enforce collection. The judgment creditor can petition the court for writs of execution, allowing county sheriffs or court marshals to levy liquid bank accounts, seize non-exempt investment brokerage assets, and attach judgment liens to titled real estate properties.

Wage garnishment represents another primary enforcement mechanism utilized by judgment creditors. Under the federal Consumer Credit Protection Act, civil judgment creditors can garnish up to twenty-five percent of an employee's disposable weekly earnings or the amount by which weekly disposable earnings exceed thirty times the federal minimum wage, whichever is less. Several states enforce even stricter garnishment protections, while others permit full statutory wage withholding until the judgment balance, along with post-judgment statutory interest, is satisfied in full. Litigation timelines are critical, as plaintiffs must comply with statutory deadlines; knowing how long can you sue someone after a car accident dictates the timeframe during which a driver remains vulnerable to legal service.

The degree of asset exposure depends significantly on state statutory exemption laws. Certain states provide comprehensive homestead exemptions that protect unlimited equity in a primary residence, while other jurisdictions shield only a modest dollar amount. Similarly, retirement funds held in qualifying ERISA-governed 401(k) plans and Individual Retirement Accounts enjoy strong federal and state statutory protections from ordinary civil judgment creditors. Umbrella Insurance Protection and the Insurer Duty to Settle

The single most effective and cost-efficient defense against catastrophic personal asset exposure is carrying high underlying auto liability limits combined with a personal umbrella liability policy. While standard auto policies often top out at two hundred and fifty thousand or five hundred thousand dollars in bodily injury coverage, a personal umbrella policy provides an additional layer of one million to five million dollars in liability protection for an annual premium that typically costs only a few hundred dollars. Umbrella coverage activates seamlessly once underlying auto policy limits are fully exhausted, providing both excess settlement funds and independent legal defense representation.

In addition to insurance planning, insurance law imposes a strict duty of good faith on auto insurance carriers. Under the doctrine of bad faith, if an injured plaintiff offers to settle all claims within the policyholder's coverage limits, the insurer has a legal obligation to accept that offer if a reasonable insurer would do so given the evidence. If the insurer unreasonably gambles by rejecting a policy-limits demand and the case results in an excess verdict at trial, the policyholder may hold a bad faith claim against their own insurance company to pay the excess amount. Plaintiffs often allege intense psychological trauma, meaning claims for ptsd after a car accident can dramatically escalate non-economic damages beyond standard policy caps.

When an accident involves high exposure, policyholders must remain actively involved in their defense. In formal litigation, depositions represent a critical juncture where liability and credibility are established under oath; preparing for cross-examination and knowing what happens after a deposition in a car accident case is essential when defending high-exposure personal injury litigation. Policyholders facing personal asset exposure should consider hiring independent personal counsel to monitor the insurance-provided defense team and insist on settlement within policy limits.

How to Protect Your Personal Assets After a Serious Car Crash

A strategic legal and financial roadmap for drivers to safeguard personal assets and respond effectively when facing a personal injury lawsuit.

  1. Immediately Notify Your Auto Insurance Provider

    Report the collision immediately to your insurer, provide factual documentation, and request immediate assignment of an insurance defense attorney.

  2. Demand a Good-Faith Settlement Within Policy Limits

    Send a written letter instructing your insurance carrier to settle all claims within your liability coverage limits to shield you from personal financial exposure.

  3. Retain Independent Personal Defense Legal Counsel

    If claim damages clearly exceed policy limits, hire an independent attorney to protect your personal interests and ensure the insurer acts in good faith.

  4. Audit Personal Assets and State Exemption Protections

    Work with an attorney to review your state homestead exemptions, retirement account protections, and joint asset holdings to understand potential vulnerability.

  5. Procure Umbrella Liability Coverage for Future Protection

    Upgrade your auto liability limits to at least $250,000/$500,000 and add a $1,000,000 or greater personal umbrella liability policy to insulate future earnings.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can someone sue me personally if I have auto insurance?

Yes, an injured claimant can sue you personally if their medical bills, lost wages, and pain and suffering damages exceed the liability limits of your auto insurance policy, or if your insurer denies coverage.

Q2: What happens if a court judgment is higher than my auto insurance limits?

Your insurance company pays up to your policy limit, and you become personally responsible for paying the remaining excess judgment through your personal savings, investments, property, or garnished wages.

Q3: Can a car accident judgment creditor take my house?

In states with strong homestead exemptions like Florida or Texas, primary residences are heavily protected. In states with low homestead exemptions, a creditor can place a judgment lien on your home, preventing a sale or refinancing until paid.

Q4: How much of my paycheck can be garnished after a car accident lawsuit?

Under federal law, wage garnishment is limited to twenty-five percent of disposable earnings or the amount exceeding thirty times federal minimum wage per week, though some states prohibit or further restrict wage garnishment.

Q5: Can filing for bankruptcy eliminate a car accident lawsuit judgment?

Most personal injury judgments resulting from ordinary negligence can be discharged through Chapter 7 or Chapter 13 bankruptcy. However, judgments stemming from driving under the influence or intentional malicious acts are non-dischargeable.

Q6: How does a personal umbrella insurance policy protect me?

A personal umbrella policy provides secondary liability coverage, typically ranging from one million to five million dollars, that activates once your auto policy liability limits are exhausted, paying both excess settlements and legal defense costs.

Q7: Does my insurance company provide a lawyer if I am sued?

Yes, auto insurance policies include a duty to defend, meaning the insurer must hire and pay for an attorney to represent you in court against claims covered under the policy.

Q8: What should I do if I am served with an accident summons and complaint?

Immediately forward the lawsuit papers to your insurance claims adjuster. Failing to respond within the statutory deadline (usually 20 to 30 days) can result in an automatic default judgment against you personally.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

While standard automotive insurance handles the financial resolution of ordinary fender benders, the legal reality is that any negligent motorist can be personally sued when a crash inflicts catastrophic bodily injuries or substantial property destruction exceeding policy caps. Relying on state minimum liability limits leaves your home equity, bank deposits, and future wages dangerously exposed to civil judgment enforcement. Protecting your family requires proactively purchasing robust liability coverage of at least two hundred and fifty thousand to five hundred thousand dollars, backed by an umbrella liability policy. Should you ever receive notice of an excess lawsuit, immediately notify your insurer, demand a good-faith settlement within your policy boundaries, and consult qualified personal legal counsel to protect your financial future.
