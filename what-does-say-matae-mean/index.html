What Does Say Matae Mean?

The Linguistic and Historical Roots of the Command Matte

The word commonly pronounced by English speakers as "matae" or "mat-tay" is the te-form conjugation of the Japanese verb matsu (待つ), which means "to wait." In Japanese martial arts culture, particularly modern combat arts (Gendai Budo) developed in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, standardized Japanese terminology was codified to ensure international uniformity across global competitions.

When Judo founder Jigoro Kano established the Kodokan in Tokyo in 1882, he instituted universal Japanese referee terminology so competitors from any nation could compete together safely without language barriers. Whether competing in Paris, Tokyo, Rio de Janeiro, or New York, every judoka and grappler understands that when the referee shouts "Matte!", all combat must cease instantaneously.

When English-speaking students first join a martial arts dojo, phonetic transcription often leads them to spell the phrase phonetically as "say matae" or "matte." Regardless of spelling, its authority on the competition mat is absolute and immediate.

Common Scenarios That Trigger a Matte Stoppage

A referee does not call Matte arbitrarily; the command is invoked to safeguard athlete health, enforce competition boundaries, and maintain fair play. Understanding the specific situations that trigger a stoppage prevents competitors from being caught off guard.

The most common reason for calling Matte is moving out of bounds. In competitive Judo and sports grappling, combat must take place within the designated contest area (tatami). If both competitors step completely into the safety perimeter zone without initiating an active throwing technique, the referee calls Matte to reset both fighters in the center of the mat.

Other mandatory stoppages occur when a competitor's uniform (judogi or kimono) becomes excessively unraveled, when fingers become dangerously tangled inside an opponent's sleeve or pants cuff, when illegal submission locks are applied, or during ground fighting (ne-waza) when no progressive movement or submission threat is occurring.

Mat Etiquette: What to Do When You Hear Matte

Strict etiquette governs a fighter's behavior the moment Matte is announced. Continuing an attack, executing a throw, or applying a chokehold after the referee has called Matte is considered a severe ethical violation and unsportsmanlike conduct, frequently resulting in immediate disqualification (Hansoku-make).

When you hear Matte, release all grips immediately, step back calmly from your opponent, and return to your assigned starting tape line in the center of the mat. If your belt or uniform jacket has loosened, turn your back toward your opponent and the referee to respectfully adjust your gear before turning around to resume combat.

How to React Safely and Respectfully When a Referee Calls Matte

Follow these step-by-step competitive protocols when Matte is announced during a martial arts match.

  1. Stop All Physical Offense Immediately

    Freeze your motion the millisecond you hear the referee vocalize 'Matte', releasing all collar, sleeve, and joint grips.

  2. Disengage Without Shoving Your Opponent

    Step away smoothly and safely; never push, kick, or taunt an opponent as you break apart from a clinch or ground battle.

  3. Return to Your Assigned Center Position

    Walk directly back to your designated blue or white starting line in the center of the tatami mat and face the referee.

  4. Adjust Your Uniform Respectfully

    If your belt has unfastened, turn 180 degrees away from your opponent and referee to tie your belt and straighten your jacket.

  5. Focus and Await the Command Hajime

    Turn back toward your opponent, assume a balanced ready stance, and wait for the referee to signal 'Hajime' to restart action.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does say matae mean in martial arts?

It refers to the Japanese referee command 'Matte' (待って), meaning 'Wait' or 'Pause action immediately'.

Q2: How is Matte pronounced in Japanese?

It is pronounced 'mah-teh' with a short crisp double-T pause, though often phonetically heard as 'matae' by beginners.

Q3: What happens if you hit an opponent after Matte?

Striking, throwing, or submitting an opponent after Matte is called results in severe penalties or immediate disqualification.

Q4: What martial arts use the command Matte?

It is the official international referee command in Judo, traditional Japanese Jujutsu, and traditional Shotokan Karate.

Q5: What is the command to restart after Matte?

The referee restarts action by announcing 'Hajime' (pronounced ha-jee-may), which translates directly to 'Begin'.

Q6: Why do martial artists turn around to fix their uniform?

Turning away from your opponent and the referee while adjusting your gi or belt is a traditional Japanese sign of modesty and respect.

Q7: What does Sono-mama mean compared to Matte?

Matte breaks fighters apart and resets them standing, while Sono-mama freezes fighters in their exact ground positions.

Q8: Who standardized Japanese commands for international sports?

Kodokan Judo founder Jigoro Kano standardized Japanese referee commands to allow international athletes to compete safely.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The command "Matte" (phonetically heard as "say matae") represents the core discipline, safety, and mutual respect of Japanese martial arts. As a universal international command, it ensures that combatants can test their physical prowess to the absolute limit while trusting that an objective referee will halt action the second safety or rules require it. Honoring Matte instantly on the mats protects both your training partners and yourself from unnecessary injury.
