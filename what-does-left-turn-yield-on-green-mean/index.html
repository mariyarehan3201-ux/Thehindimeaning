What Does Left Turn Yield on Green Mean?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

When approaching a signalized roadway intersection, drivers frequently encounter a regulatory rectangular traffic sign reading "Left Turn Yield on Green" positioned adjacent to a standard circular green light. This sign establishes that while you are legally permitted to make a left turn on a solid green ball, your turn is "permissive" rather than protected?"meaning oncoming vehicles and crossing pedestrians hold absolute legal right-of-way.

The Legal Distinction Between Protected and Permissive Left Turns

Traffic engineers design intersection signal timing to balance vehicle throughput against collision risk. In modern traffic control jurisprudence, left turns across oncoming traffic lanes represent one of the most hazardous maneuvers a motorist can execute. To regulate these movements, intersections employ two foundational signal phases: protected left turns and permissive left turns.

A "Protected Turn" is signaled by a solid green left arrow. During a protected phase, all oncoming vehicular traffic and opposing pedestrian crosswalk signals face solid red stop indications. When the green arrow illuminates, turning motorists have exclusive, uninterrupted right-of-way to complete their turn without interference.

Conversely, a "Permissive Turn" occurs when the green arrow extinguishes and is replaced by a solid circular green ball (or a flashing yellow arrow). At this juncture, the "Left Turn Yield on Green" sign governs your behavior. Oncoming traffic traveling straight through the intersection also has a solid green light and has no obligation to stop or slow down for you. You must remain stationary until a safe, adequate gap in oncoming traffic opens up.

Common Driver Misconceptions and Collision Liabilities

One of the most dangerous driving misconceptions is assuming that a solid green ball grants universal right-of-way. Inexperienced drivers often see the color green and reflexively turn across oncoming lanes without realizing that opposing motorists also have a green light and are proceeding at highway speeds.

In legal tort law and automobile insurance claims, an accident occurring during a permissive left turn almost universally assigns one hundred percent liability to the turning driver. Unless the oncoming vehicle was demonstrably running a red light or traveling at reckless criminal speeds, the left-turning vehicle is legally mandated to yield until the intersection is entirely clear.

The Transition to Flashing Yellow Arrows

In recent years, transportation departments worldwide have begun replacing the traditional circular green ball with a flashing yellow arrow for permissive turns. Decades of Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) studies revealed that drivers understand the flashing yellow arrow far more intuitively than a solid green ball.

A green circle psychologically suggests "go" to the human brain, which can lead to hazardous lapses in judgment. In contrast, a flashing yellow arrow universally conveys caution and yielding, drastically reducing left-turn intersection collisions nationwide.

How to Safely Make a Permissive Left Turn on a Green Light

Step-by-step driving protocol to navigate a permissive left turn safely and without risk of collisions.

  1. Signal Your Intent Well in Advance

    Activate your left turn signal at least 100 feet before reaching the intersection to alert motorists and pedestrians behind you.

  2. Approach and Assess the Signal Display

    Check whether you have a protected green arrow or a solid green circle accompanied by a 'Left Turn Yield on Green' sign.

  3. Advance Straight into the Intersection Slightly

    Creep straight forward into the intersection with your steering wheels kept completely straight?"never turned early.

  4. Scan Oncoming Lanes and Crosswalks

    Look for high-speed oncoming vehicles, motorcycles that may be obscured, and pedestrians stepping off the far corner curb.

  5. Complete the Turn When Clear

    Once a generous gap appears or oncoming traffic stops on yellow, smoothly accelerate through the turn into the closest legal lane.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does left turn yield on green mean I can turn immediately?

No, it means you may turn only after yielding to all oncoming vehicles traveling straight and any pedestrians in the crosswalk.

Q2: What is the difference between a green arrow and a green ball?

A green arrow provides protected right-of-way with oncoming traffic stopped; a green ball requires you to yield to oncoming traffic.

Q3: Should your wheels be turned while waiting to turn left?

No, keep wheels pointed straight; if rear-ended while waiting, turned wheels will push you directly into oncoming head-on traffic.

Q4: Can I enter the intersection while waiting to turn left on green?

In most jurisdictions, you may enter the intersection cautiously, wait for a safe gap, and complete the turn when traffic clears.

Q5: What does a flashing yellow arrow mean?

A flashing yellow arrow has the exact same legal meaning as 'Left Turn Yield on Green'—you may turn after yielding to oncoming traffic.

Q6: Who is at fault if a left-turning car is hit by oncoming traffic?

The left-turning driver is almost always held legally at fault for failing to yield right-of-way on a permissive green signal.

Q7: Do pedestrians have right of way over left-turning vehicles?

Yes, pedestrians crossing with an illuminated 'Walk' signal hold absolute legal right-of-way over vehicles turning left on green.

Q8: What happens if the light turns yellow while I am waiting in the intersection?

Once oncoming traffic slows or stops for the yellow light, you are legally permitted to clear the intersection and complete your turn.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The "Left Turn Yield on Green" rule is a critical pillar of intersection safety. Recognizing the crucial difference between protected arrow turns and permissive circular green signals protects you and other road users from dangerous collisions. When facing a solid green ball or flashing yellow arrow, patience is essential: yield unconditionally to oncoming traffic and pedestrians, and proceed only when your path is completely clear.
