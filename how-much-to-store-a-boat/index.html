How Much to Store a Boat?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Storing a boat typically costs between $50 and $500 per month, with annual expenses ranging from $600 for basic outdoor trailer parking to upwards of $6,000 for climate-controlled indoor dry storage or premium marina wet slips. Your overall boat storage expenditure is determined by the length overall LOA of your vessel, the geographic popularity of local waterways, the storage methodology selected, and seasonal winterization requirements. While parking a trailerable runabout on personal property is free, commercial boat storage facilities charge either flat monthly rates or linear-foot pricing models.

Storage Types: Outdoor Lots, Indoor Facilities, and Wet Slips

Commercial boat storage facilities generally classify storage solutions into four main categories: outdoor uncovered parking, covered canopy storage, fully enclosed indoor storage, and wet slip berthing at marinas. Uncovered outdoor storage is the most economical commercial alternative, charging between $4 and $12 per linear foot monthly, or roughly $50 to $150 per month for a standard 20-foot bowrider. However, outdoor storage leaves your gelcoat, marine upholstery, and electronics vulnerable to damaging ultraviolet radiation, hail, and moisture intrusion, requiring durable shrink wrapping or heavy-duty canvas covers.

Enclosed indoor storage facilities provide maximum defense against environmental deterioration, shielding vessels from freezing temperatures, rodent infestation, and wind damage. Basic unheated indoor warehouse units run $10 to $25 per linear foot monthly ($200 to $500 per month for a 22-foot craft), while specialized climate-controlled boat vaults can exceed $30 to $50 per foot. Meanwhile, marina wet slips allow owners to keep their vessels floating directly in the water for instant cruising access. Wet slips range from $12 to $35 per foot per month in lake regions to over $50 per foot in high-demand coastal ocean ports.

Linear Foot Pricing Formulas and Dimensional Measurements

When contacting storage facilities for pricing quotes, many first-time boat buyers assume that a 21-foot boat only requires 21 feet of storage space. In reality, commercial facilities calculate billing based on total package length or length overall LOA. A 21-foot fiberglass runabout sitting on a single-axle or tandem-axle trailer with an outboard motor tilted in the trailered position and a trailer tongue extended frequently measures 26 to 28 feet in overall storage footprint. If the facility charges $15 per linear foot, your monthly invoice will be calculated on 28 feet ($420 per month) rather than the hull manufacturer 21-foot designation.

Furthermore, vertical clearance and beam width play critical roles in indoor storage availability. Standard self-storage roll-up doors measure 8 to 10 feet in height, whereas wakeboard boats equipped with fixed wake towers, radar arches, or center consoles with T-tops regularly require 11 to 14 feet of door clearance. Storing oversized vessels with beams wider than 8.5 feet may incur wide-load surcharges or necessitate dedicated commercial warehouse bays, significantly bumping up monthly holding costs.

Ancillary Maintenance: Winterization, Shrink Wrap, and Haul-Outs

Calculating the real financial obligation of boat storage requires budgeting for mandatory seasonal transition services. In northern and temperate climates subject to sub-freezing winter weather, failing to winterize an inboard, sterndrive, or outboard cooling system can cause trapped water to freeze, crack the engine block, and inflict $8,000 to $15,000 in catastrophic mechanical damage. Professional marine winterization packages cost between $250 and $700, covering fuel stabilization, fogging cylinders, draining raw water blocks, and pumping non-toxic propylene glycol antifreeze through manifolds.

Additionally, boaters storing their crafts outdoors typically invest in seasonal shrink wrapping. Marine shrink wrap utilizes thick 7-mil to 9-mil polyethylene plastic heat-shrunk over a wooden support framework, forming a water-shedding barrier that prevents snow accumulation and ice damming. Professional shrink wrapping costs between $15 and $28 per linear foot ($300 to $650 per season). In wet marinas, boaters must also budget for annual haul-out fees ($10 to $20 per foot), bottom pressure washing ($5 to $10 per foot), and periodic anti-fouling bottom paint reapplication ($1,000 to $2,500 every two years).

How to Prepare and Secure a Boat for Long-Term Storage

Follow these five critical preparation steps to prevent mold, mechanical freeze damage, and physical degradation during prolonged storage.

  1. Thoroughly Decontaminate Hull and Interior Upholstery

    Wash the fiberglass hull, clean scum lines, scrub marine vinyl seats with mildew inhibitor, and vacuum all storage compartments completely.

  2. Perform Complete Engine and Water System Winterization

    Drain engine cooling passages, pump non-toxic marine antifreeze through freshwater lines, fog carburetors or intake valves, and stabilize fuel tanks.

  3. Disconnect Marine Batteries and Store on Trickle Chargers

    Disconnect the negative terminals from dual-battery switches, remove marine deep-cycle batteries, and store them on smart trickle tenders indoors.

  4. Install Moisture Absorbers and Proper Ventilation Vents

    Place hanging desiccant moisture bags or chemical damp-check buckets inside cabins and ensure shrink wrap or canvas covers have baffled air vents.

  5. Verify Trailer Mechanics and Engage Physical Security Locks

    Inflate trailer tires to rated PSI, lube wheel bearings, place jack stands under axles to prevent flat spots, and secure a hardened coupler lock.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is it cheaper to store a boat on a trailer or at a marina?

Storing a boat on a trailer in an outdoor storage yard is substantially cheaper ($60 to $200 per month) than renting a marina wet slip ($300 to $1,000+ per month).

Q2: What is dry stack boat storage and how does it work?

Dry stack storage keeps your boat inside an indoor multi-tiered rack warehouse; when you want to use it, marina operators use a giant forklift to launch it into the water.

Q3: Can I store a boat in a standard self-storage unit?

Yes, but you must ensure the unit length accommodates the trailer tongue and tilted outboard motor, and that the roll-up door height clears your windshield or wake tower.

Q4: How much does it cost to shrink wrap a boat for winter?

Professional marine shrink wrapping typically costs between $15 and $28 per foot of boat length, averaging $300 to $600 for a standard 20 to 24-foot vessel.

Q5: Does homeowners insurance cover boat storage on my driveway?

Homeowners policies offer minimal coverage (often capped at $1,000 to $1,500) for small watercraft; dedicated marine hull and liability insurance is necessary for real protection.

Q6: What are the risks of storing a boat in the water year-round?

Year-round wet slip storage exposes the hull to marine growth algae, barnacles, osmosis hull blistering, zinc anode corrosion, and catastrophic sinking from failed bilge pumps.

Q7: Do boat storage facilities provide electrical hookups?

Climate-controlled units and premium wet slips usually provide shore power or 110V outlets for battery tenders, while standard outdoor storage yards rarely offer power.

Q8: How often should you check on a stored boat during the off-season?

Inspect your stored boat at least once every 3 to 4 weeks to check battery charge levels, inspect tie-downs, verify cover tension, and ensure no rodents have nested inside.
