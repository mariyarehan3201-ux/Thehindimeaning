How Do You Get a Bonded Title in Georgia?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how do you get a bonded title in georgia is the established legal process for motorists and vehicle purchasers who acquire an automobile, truck, motorcycle, or travel trailer without obtaining a legitimate Certificate of Title. Administered by the Georgia Department of Revenue (DOR) Motor Vehicle Division under Georgia Code Title 40, a bonded title utilizes an official surety bond (Form MV-46) to protect previous owners, lenders, and the state against competing ownership claims while granting you full legal title, license plates, and highway registration.

Statutory Eligibility and Prerequisites Under Georgia Law

To obtain a bonded title in the State of Georgia, applicants must satisfy strict statutory eligibility criteria. First, the applicant must be a legal resident of Georgia or active-duty military personnel stationed at a military installation within the state. Non-residents cannot obtain a Georgia bonded title. Second, the vehicle must be a model year that legally requires a certificate of title in Georgia (model year 1986 or newer; vehicles manufactured in 1985 or older are title-exempt and transfer via bill of sale).

Furthermore, the vehicle must be completely unencumbered by outstanding liens or salvage brands. If an unsatisfied security interest exists from a previous bank or title lender, you cannot obtain a bonded title until a formal Form T-4 Lien Release is secured. Additionally, vehicles branded as junk, parts-only, or scrap are legally ineligible for bonded titles under Georgia administrative regulations.

Form T-22B Inspection and Form MV-46 Surety Bond Acquisition

The practical bonded title workflow begins by having the vehicle physically inspected by a certified Georgia law enforcement officer (such as a county sheriff's deputy, city police officer, or Georgia State Trooper). The officer inspects the public VIN plate, door jamb federal safety decals, and checks the National Crime Information Center (NCIC) database, executing Georgia Form T-22B (Certification of Inspection).

Next, you must purchase a Georgia Motor Vehicle Title Bond (Form MV-46). The bond amount is set by law at exactly twice (200%) the average retail value of the vehicle as determined by the Georgia Department of Revenue Motor Vehicle Assessment Manual, with a mandatory statutory minimum of $5,000. You obtain this bond through a commercial surety agency licensed in Georgia, typically paying a one-time premium between $100 and $250 for standard vehicles.

Filing at the County Tag Office and 4-Year Bond Expiration

Once your documentation package—including Form MV-1, Form MV-46, Form MV-46A, Form T-22B, and bills of sale—is assembled, you submit the dossier in person to your local County Tax Commissioner Tag Office. You must present your valid Georgia driver's license, proof of active Georgia auto insurance, pay the $18 state title application fee, and pay applicable Title Ad Valorem Tax (TAVT) based on the vehicle's fair market value.

The Georgia Department of Revenue in Atlanta reviews the dossier and issues a Certificate of Title carrying a 'Bonded' brand. This surety bond remains legally active for exactly four years from the issuance date. If no prior titled owner or undisclosed lienholder files a valid claim against the bond during this four-year statutory period, the bond automatically expires, and you can apply to have the bonded brand removed, receiving a clean, standard Georgia title.

How to Apply for a Bonded Title in Georgia

Follow these five clear steps to successfully obtain a bonded title through your Georgia county tag office.

  1. Complete Form T-22B Law Enforcement Inspection

    Have a Georgia police officer or sheriff inspect the VIN and certify the vehicle is clear of theft records.

  2. Determine Vehicle Value with County Tax Office

    Obtain the official state retail vehicle valuation from your local county tag commissioner office.

  3. Purchase a 4-Year Form MV-46 Surety Bond

    Buy a title bond for double the state-assessed value (minimum $5,000) from an authorized Georgia surety agency.

  4. Notarize Form MV-46A Ownership Affidavit

    Draft a sworn affidavit explaining under penalty of perjury how you acquired the vehicle and why the title is missing.

  5. Submit Dossier to Local County Tag Office

    Present all forms, proof of insurance, Georgia ID, and pay the $18 title fee and applicable TAVT.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How long does a Georgia bonded title stay bonded?

The title remains bonded for 4 years; after 4 years without any claims, you can request a standard clean Georgia title.

Q2: How much does a bonded title cost in Georgia?

The surety bond costs between $100 and $200 for standard vehicles, plus an $18 title fee and state Title Ad Valorem Tax (TAVT).

Q3: Can you sell a car with a Georgia bonded title?

Yes, you can legally sell, trade, or transfer a vehicle with a bonded title; the bonded status transfers to the new owner.

Q4: Do cars older than 1986 need a bonded title in Georgia?

No, vehicles manufactured in 1985 or earlier are exempt from title requirements in Georgia and can be registered on a bill of sale.

Q5: Who can inspect my vehicle for Form T-22B?

Any certified Georgia law enforcement officer (city police, county sheriff, or Georgia State Patrol) can complete Form T-22B.

Q6: Can out-of-state residents get a Georgia bonded title?

No, bonded titles in Georgia are strictly available only to legal Georgia residents and active-duty military stationed in the state.

Q7: What is Georgia Form MV-46A?

Form MV-46A is the mandatory notarized affidavit where the applicant explains under oath the history of how the vehicle was obtained.

Q8: Can I get a bonded title if the car has an active bank lien?

No, any existing lien must be formally released using Georgia Form T-4 before a bonded title application will be processed.
