How to Start Up a Screen Printing Business?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Starting an independent screen printing business offers creative entrepreneurs and trade craftspeople an exceptionally high-margin manufacturing model in the custom apparel and merchandise industry. Despite the rise of digital direct-to-garment (DTG) and direct-to-film (DTF) technologies, traditional screen printing remains the gold standard for bulk orders, providing unmatched vibrant color opacity, durability, and unbeatable per-unit cost efficiency on orders of 24 units or more. Successfully establishing a commercial print shop requires mastering screen darkroom chemistry, choosing the right carousel press, and securing reliable wholesale garment distributor accounts.

The Screen Printing Process: Mesh Counts, Emulsions, and Darkrooms

At the center of screen printing is the technical art of screen preparation. The process begins by degreasing a monofilament polyester mesh screen stretched tightly onto an aluminum frame. The mesh count chosen dictates the level of graphic detail and ink deposit: lower mesh counts (110 to 160) are ideal for heavy ink deposits, opaque white underbases, and athletic prints, while higher mesh counts (200 to 305) capture fine halftones, photorealistic graphics, and delicate water-based ink lines.

The screen is coated with a light-sensitive liquid photopolymer or diazo emulsion in a light-safe darkroom. After drying in horizontal screen racks, film positives containing opaque black vector artwork are placed onto the screen and exposed to ultraviolet (UV) light using an exposure unit with a vacuum hold-down blanket. The unexposed emulsion behind the black artwork remains water-soluble and is washed out with a pressure washer, creating a crisp, open stencil through which ink is pressed during printing.

Selecting Production Equipment: Manual vs. Automatic Presses

Launching a screen printing business begins with choosing between a manual rotary carousel press and an automatic pneumatic/electric press. For startup shops operating out of a garage, basement, or light industrial warehouse, a high-quality 4-color / 4-station or 6-color / 4-station manual press (such as a Riley Hopkins, Vastex, or Anatol) represents the ideal balance of capital investment and capability.

In addition to the press, curing equipment is critical. While hobbyists rely on flash dryers or heat presses to cure ink, commercial shops must utilize a continuous infrared or forced-air conveyor dryer. Plastisol inks require reaching a complete core cross-linking cure temperature of 320°F (160°C) throughout the entire ink film; under-cured ink will wash out after the customer's first laundry cycle, destroying brand credibility. Adding an automatic wash-out booth with an integrated filtration system prevents solid inks from contaminating municipal sewers.

Garment Sourcing and Bulk Tier Pricing Economics

A print shop's profitability depends heavily on wholesale blank garment procurement. By registering an official business entity and sales tax exemption certificate, print shop owners gain direct access to premier wholesale distributors—including SanMar, S&S Activewear, and AlphaBroder. These distributors supply blank t-shirts from brands like Bella+Canvas, Next Level, Gildan, and Comfort Colors at true wholesale prices (often $1.50 to $5.00 per shirt).

Shops structure customer quotes using tiered pricing matrices based on garment volume, color count, and print locations. A 100-piece order of single-color t-shirts costing $2.50 wholesale and billed at $8.50 each yields an immediate $600 gross profit for less than two hours of printing work, providing outstanding returns on shop labor and consumables.

How to Build a Screen Printing Business

Follow this step-by-step roadmap to establish your darkroom, procure press equipment, establish wholesale apparel accounts, and print your first commercial order.

  1. Form Business Entity and Register Wholesale Accounts

    Register an LLC, obtain an EIN and sales tax resale license, and open wholesale accounts with distributors like SanMar and S&S Activewear.

  2. Outfit Darkroom and Screen Preparation Area

    Set up a light-safe darkroom with horizontal drying racks, a vacuum UV exposure unit, emulsion scoop coaters, and a washout booth with a pressure washer.

  3. Acquire Carousel Screen Press and Conveyor Dryer

    Purchase a 4-color or 6-color rotary manual printing press, an infrared conveyor curing dryer, flash curing units, and squeegees.

  4. Calibrate Curing Temperatures and Emulsion Exposure

    Conduct step-wedge exposure tests to determine optimal UV burn times, and use a digital laser thermometer to verify 320°F core ink cure in the dryer.

  5. Market to Local Schools, Gyms, and Contractors

    Produce high-quality branded sample garments, reach out to local crossfit gyms, construction contractors, and high school athletic boosters, and close orders.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to start a screen printing business?

A professional manual starter shop can be launched for between $8,000 and $15,000, covering a 4-station manual press, flash unit, conveyor dryer, darkroom exposure unit, screens, inks, and initial apparel inventory.

Q2: What is the difference between plastisol and water-based ink?

Plastisol ink sits on top of fabric fibers, requires 320°F heat to cure, does not dry out in the screen, and provides high opacity. Water-based ink absorbs into the fabric fibers, yielding an ultra-soft, breathable feel but drying faster in screens.

Q3: How many shirts can a manual screen printer print per hour?

A skilled solo printer operating a 4-station manual carousel press can comfortably print 60 to 100 single-color shirts per hour, or 40 to 60 multi-color registered garments per hour.

Q4: Why does screen printed ink wash out after the first wash?

Ink washes out due to under-curing. If plastisol ink fails to reach its full cross-linking temperature of 320°F (160°C) through the entire thickness of the ink deposit, the chemical bonds will not set and the ink will wash away.

Q5: Where do screen printers buy blank t-shirts in bulk?

Commercial printers purchase blank garments directly from major wholesale apparel distributors such as SanMar, S&S Activewear, and AlphaBroder using a state sales tax resale exemption certificate.

Q6: Is screen printing better than DTG (Direct-to-Garment)?

Screen printing is vastly superior for bulk orders (over 24 pieces) due to much faster production speeds, significantly lower per-unit consumable costs, and more vibrant, durable color saturation.

Q7: What equipment is needed to create screen stencils?

Creating stencils requires a film positive printer (inkjet with all-black ink systems), aluminum mesh screens, emulsion scoop coater, photopolymer emulsion, a UV exposure unit, and a high-pressure water washout booth.

Q8: Can I run a screen printing business in a residential garage?

Yes, many successful shops begin in residential two-car garages. However, you must verify 220V electrical availability for the conveyor dryer, establish adequate wastewater disposal, and check local zoning bylaws.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Starting up a screen printing business is a reliable, hands-on manufacturing venture that combines artistic design with tangible physical production. By mastering darkroom screen chemistry, investing in durable manual or automatic rotary presses with reliable conveyor dryers, sourcing blank garments directly through wholesale distributors, and delivering exceptional print quality to local businesses, sports teams, and schools, you can build an enduring, highly profitable printing company.
