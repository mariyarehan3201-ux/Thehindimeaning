How Do I Get a Title for a Camper?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Learning how do i get a title for a camper is essential for outdoor enthusiasts, vintage trailer restorers, and buyers who acquire a travel trailer, fifth wheel, or pop-up camper without an accompanying certificate of title. Because campers are towed recreational vehicles that travel across state lines on public interstates, state motor vehicle agencies strictly regulate their ownership and serial numbering. Securing a lawful camper title protects your investment, permits highway registration and licensing, and enables legal resale.

Camper Classification: Travel Trailers, Pop-Ups, and Weight Exemptions

The legal procedure for titling a camper depends heavily on how your state's Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) classifies recreational vehicles. Motorized RVs (Class A, B, and C motorhomes) have vehicle drivetrains and are invariably treated as titled motor vehicles. However, non-motorized campers—including bumper-pull travel trailers, fifth wheels, teardrops, and pop-up tent trailers—are categorized either as trailers or dedicated recreational house trailers.

Crucially, many states determine camper title mandates based on Gross Vehicle Weight Rating (GVWR) or unladen dry weight. In states like New York, Oklahoma, or Maine, lightweight utility trailers and small pop-up campers weighing under 1,000 to 3,000 pounds may only require an annual registration card and bill of sale rather than a formal title. Conversely, larger dual-axle travel trailers almost always mandate a formal Certificate of Title.

Locating Trailer VINs and Recovering Prior Owner Paperwork

The first step in securing a camper title is locating the Vehicle Identification Number (VIN). Unlike passenger cars where the VIN is visible through the windshield glass, camper VINs are located in exterior structural positions. On bumper-pull travel trailers, the VIN is commonly stamped directly into the metal A-frame tongue near the hitch coupler, etched onto an aluminum DOT data plate on the forward driver-side exterior wall, or listed on a federal safety certification decal inside the main entrance screen door.

If you bought the camper from a private individual who simply misplaced their original title, the fastest and least expensive remedy is having the previous legal owner apply for a Duplicate Certificate of Title. In almost every state, the titled owner can submit a simple duplicate title form online or at a local DMV branch for a modest fee ($15 to $50). Once the replacement title arrives, the seller signs the transfer section over to you alongside a dated bill of sale.

The Bonded Title Route and Homemade Trailer Registration

If the previous owner cannot be reached or the camper has exchanged hands through multiple private sales without paperwork, a Bonded Title is usually the most effective administrative remedy. In this process, you arrange for a physical VIN verification conducted by a state highway patrol officer or certified DMV inspector. Once cleared of theft records, you obtain a surety bond calculated at 1.5 to 2 times the camper's fair market value, allowing the DMV to issue a bonded title.

Alternatively, for heavily restored, gutted, or custom-built campers, titling the unit under a 'Homemade / Specially Constructed' trailer designation may be appropriate. This pathway requires submitting an itemized log of parts, purchase receipts for materials (axles, frame, framing lumber, aluminum siding), an official certified public scale weight slip, and passing a state roadworthiness safety inspection evaluating trailer lights, safety chains, and electric breakaway brakes.

How to Get a Title for a Camper Without Paperwork

Follow these five tactical steps to establish legal ownership and obtain a title for your travel trailer.

  1. Locate and Verify the Chassis VIN

    Find the 17-digit VIN stamped on the trailer tongue, A-frame coupler, or driver-side exterior data plate.

  2. Attempt to Contact the Registered Owner

    Ask the seller to order a duplicate title through their state DMV and sign the ownership assignment.

  3. Schedule a Law Enforcement VIN Inspection

    Have a highway patrol officer or DMV inspector verify the chassis serial number against national theft registries.

  4. Acquire a Trailer Surety Bond

    If the owner cannot be located, buy a bonded title surety bond equal to 1.5x to 2x the camper's appraised value.

  5. Submit Application and Pay Title Fees

    Present inspection forms, bill of sale, bond paperwork, and scale weight slip to your local DMV office.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can you register a camper without a title?

In some states (such as New York or Maine for light trailers), campers under specific weight thresholds can be registered on a transferable registration and bill of sale.

Q2: Where is the VIN located on a travel trailer camper?

The VIN is usually stamped into the metal tongue/frame near the hitch coupler, or on a federal safety certification sticker on the front exterior left side.

Q3: How much does it cost to get a bonded title for a camper?

Surety bonds for campers typically cost $100 to $150 for trailers valued under $6,000, plus local DMV title application and plate fees.

Q4: Can I title a camper as homemade?

Yes, if you built or extensively rebuilt the trailer from bare frame components, you can apply for a homemade trailer title with weight slips and receipts.

Q5: What happens if a camper VIN has been painted over or ground off?

You must schedule an inspection with state highway patrol auto theft investigators, who can use chemical etching or assign a new state-issued VIN plate.

Q6: Can I insure a camper without a title?

Insurance carriers generally require proof of legal ownership (a title, transferable registration, or active bonded application) to issue comprehensive and collision policies.

Q7: How long does it take to get a replacement camper title?

A duplicate title ordered by the previous owner takes 1 to 3 weeks; a bonded title process typically takes 3 to 6 weeks.

Q8: Do pop-up campers have titles?

In many states yes, but in states with lightweight trailer exemptions (under 1,500 to 3,000 lbs), pop-ups may be issued a registration card only.
