Asbestos Siding Removal Maryland

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

Asbestos siding removal in Maryland is strictly regulated by the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) under Code of Maryland Regulations (COMAR) 26.11.21. With thousands of historic row houses in Baltimore, suburban homes in Montgomery and Prince George’s counties, and rural properties across the Eastern Shore clad in mid-century Transite shingles, homeowners must navigate state licensing, safe removal practices, and certified disposal rules.

Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) Regulatory Rules

Under COMAR 26.11.21, the Maryland Department of the Environment establishes stringent operational standards for managing asbestos-containing materials. Any project disturbing regulated asbestos in commercial buildings, public schools, rental housing, or major residential redevelopment must be executed by an MDE-licensed asbestos abatement contractor utilizing certified supervisors and workers. Maryland requires contractors to notify MDE at least ten working days prior to initiating any abatement project involving regulated quantities.

While Maryland regulations allow owner-occupants of single-family detached homes to perform non-commercial work on their primary personal dwelling, state environmental authorities strongly urge homeowners to retain licensed professionals. Asbestos cement siding shingles—commonly known as Transite—are rigid and brittle. Attempting to pry, hammer, or drop shingles causes shattering, immediately transforming non-friable exterior tiles into hazardous airborne respirable dust that can contaminate adjacent properties.

Costs, Techniques, and Disposal Across Maryland

Professional exterior asbestos siding removal across Maryland follows precise containment protocols. Technicians construct ground drop perimeters using heavy six-mil polyethylene sheeting extending ten feet outwards from the foundation walls to catch any stray chips. Each shingle is soaked with surfactant-amended water and individual galvanized siding nails are carefully unseated or cut with specialized hand pliers, allowing shingles to be lowered whole without breaking.

Pricing for professional siding removal in Maryland typically ranges from $7 to $13 per square foot of exterior wall surface. For an average 1,500-square-foot Cape Cod or split-level home requiring complete siding strip-down, total abatement costs generally range between $6,000 and $12,500. This cost covers labor, ground containment, HEPA air filtration, EPA waste labeling, and transit to authorized regional disposal facilities such as the Baltimore County Western Acceptance Facility or Eastern Landfill.

Many Maryland homeowners evaluate siding encapsulation as a cost-effective alternative. Applying rigid foam insulation directly over sound, unbroken asbestos shingles and covering it with modern vinyl or fiber-cement siding avoids disturbance costs entirely.

However, if shingles are cracked, broken, or missing, complete professional abatement provides the cleanest solution, permanently removing toxic minerals from the property deed.

How to Manage Asbestos Siding Removal in Maryland

Steps for Maryland homeowners planning exterior siding remediation.

  1. Conduct Laboratory Bulk Sample Testing

    Send a small, wet corner fragment of siding to an accredited lab to confirm chrysotile content via PLM analysis.

  2. Solicit Estimates from MDE-Licensed Contractors

    Obtain itemized bids from at least three contractors carrying active Maryland Department of the Environment licenses.

  3. Confirm MDE Ten-Day Project Notification

    Verify that the contractor files the mandatory ten-day advance notification with MDE before starting commercial or rental work.

  4. Obtain Hazardous Waste Manifest Documentation

    Secure signed copies of the official hazardous waste manifest showing legal delivery to a permitted Maryland disposal facility.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does Maryland allow homeowners to remove asbestos siding themselves?

Maryland allows owner-occupants of detached single-family homes to perform work, though licensed contractors are strongly advised.

Q2: What agency regulates asbestos abatement in Maryland?

The Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) oversees all asbestos regulations under COMAR 26.11.21.

Q3: How much does asbestos siding removal cost in Maryland?

Costs typically range between $7 and $13 per square foot, averaging $7,000 to $13,000 for a standard residential home.

Q4: Can vinyl siding be installed directly over asbestos shingles in Maryland?

Yes, encapsulating sound asbestos shingles with rigid foam board and new vinyl siding is legal and widely practiced.

Q5: Where is asbestos siding disposed of in Maryland?

Waste must be double-bagged in 6-mil poly, labeled with EPA warnings, and hauled to permitted facilities like Baltimore County Western Landfill.

Q6: What is the penalty for illegal asbestos removal in Maryland?

MDE can issue administrative civil penalties up to $25,000 per day for unpermitted asbestos disturbance or improper disposal.

Q7: How can I tell if my Maryland siding contains asbestos?

Rigid cement shingles with vertical striations installed between 1930 and 1980 almost always contain 12% to 20% chrysotile asbestos.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Asbestos siding removal in Maryland requires balancing state environmental regulations with practical exterior modernization. By partnering with MDE-licensed abatement contractors, enforcing wet dismantling techniques, and ensuring legal disposal at authorized landfills, Maryland property owners protect community air quality and increase property value.
