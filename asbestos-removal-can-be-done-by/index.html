Asbestos Removal Can Be Done By

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Asbestos removal can be done by licensed abatement contractors, certified environmental technicians, and, under strictly limited legal circumstances, individual property owners. Because the inhalation of microscopic mineral fibers leads to incurable lung scarring and fatal malignancies, federal, state, and international regulatory agencies enforce stringent guidelines dictating who is legally authorized and technically qualified to perform abatement work. Understanding who can safely and legally remove asbestos is essential for maintaining workplace compliance and safeguarding residential health.

Licensed and Certified Asbestos Abatement Contractors

In the overwhelming majority of commercial, industrial, institutional, and residential scenarios, asbestos removal can be done by state-licensed asbestos abatement contractors. These specialist firms employ supervisors and workers who have completed extensive classroom curriculum and hands-on practical training accredited under the EPA Model Accreditation Plan (MAP) and OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1101. Certifications must be renewed annually through refresher courses, written examinations, and comprehensive occupational pulmonary health exams.

Licensed contractors are legally authorized to handle both friable and non-friable asbestos-containing materials (ACM). They possess the specialized engineering equipment necessary to establish airtight negative-pressure containment enclosures, three-stage decontamination airlocks, and True HEPA-filtered air filtration systems. Furthermore, licensed abatement companies carry dedicated commercial pollution liability insurance, shielding property owners from catastrophic environmental and toxic tort legal liabilities.

Homeowner Single-Family Residential Exemptions and Limitations

In certain jurisdictions across North America, statutory environmental rules grant a narrow exemption: asbestos removal can be done by homeowners on their own private, owner-occupied single-family detached homes. Under federal EPA NESHAP rules, private single-family homes with four or fewer dwelling units are frequently exempt from federal commercial contractor notification mandates, allowing owners to legally perform DIY tear-outs of vinyl tiles, siding, or textured drywall.

However, this legal exemption does not apply universally. Numerous states and municipal air quality management districts—such as those in California, Washington, New York, and Massachusetts—have enacted local regulations completely overriding federal exemptions, making uncertified DIY asbestos removal illegal even in private homes. Furthermore, even where legally tolerated, health agencies like the EPA, CDC, and OSHA strongly caution homeowners against DIY removal, as amateur abatement almost inevitably results in catastrophic, whole-home fiber contamination.

Who Should Never Attempt Asbestos Removal

While licensed contractors and certain exempt homeowners are authorized under law, there are broad categories of tradespeople by whom asbestos removal should never be done. General building contractors, handyman services, plumbing technicians, HVAC installers, and demolition laborers must never remove asbestos without holding dedicated state asbestos abatement licenses. Untrained tradespeople using standard shop vacuums, reciprocating saws, or dry scraping tools generate immense toxic plumes, endangering themselves, building occupants, and neighboring properties.

Furthermore, building maintenance and custodial personnel in schools, hospitals, and commercial office towers are strictly restricted under OSHA regulations from performing major abatement work. Custodians are only permitted to perform minor, non-friable Class III maintenance operations (such as replacing a single broken vinyl floor tile) after completing an approved 16-hour OSHA Operations and Maintenance (O&M) training course, and only when using specialized mini-enclosures or glove-bags.

How to Determine Who Can Remove Asbestos in 5 Steps

Follow these five procedural steps to identify authorized, legally compliant professionals for your asbestos project.

  1. Determine Building Classification and Property Type

    Identify whether the property is a private single-family residence, multi-family apartment, or commercial facility.

  2. Check Regional and State Environmental Laws

    Consult your state department of environmental protection or local air quality board regarding DIY abatement legality.

  3. Identify the Friability and Quantity of the Material

    Determine if the material is friable (like pipe wrap) or non-friable (like siding) to establish the necessary license class.

  4. Verify Contractor Licensing on State Portals

    Look up prospective abatement firms on your state official regulatory registry to confirm active licensing and insurance.

  5. Hire an Independent Hygienist for Project Clearance

    Contract an independent third-party environmental consultant to supervise containment and conduct final air clearance.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can asbestos removal be done by a regular contractor?

No, general contractors cannot legally remove regulated asbestos unless they hold specialized state asbestos abatement licenses.

Q2: Can a homeowner remove asbestos themselves?

In some states, homeowners can legally remove asbestos from their own single-family homes, though safety agencies strongly discourage it.

Q3: Can plumbers remove asbestos pipe wrap?

No, plumbers cannot strip or remove asbestos pipe insulation; they must hire a licensed abatement contractor to glove-bag the pipes first.

Q4: Who certifies asbestos removal workers?

Workers are certified through EPA-accredited training providers and state environmental health licensing departments.

Q5: What is an independent asbestos hygienist?

An independent hygienist is a certified professional who performs air testing and inspections to certify that removal was done safely.

Q6: Can landlords remove asbestos from their rental properties?

No, landlords cannot perform DIY asbestos removal on rental properties; federal and state laws mandate licensed contractors.

Q7: What happens if an unlicensed person removes asbestos?

Unlicensed removal exposes workers to toxic dust, contaminates buildings, and triggers severe municipal and EPA civil fines.

Q8: How can I verify if an asbestos company is really licensed?

Visit your state Department of Environmental Protection or Department of Labor website to search their active contractor database.
