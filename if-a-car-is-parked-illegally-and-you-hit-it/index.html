If a Car is Parked Illegally and You Hit It

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

If you hit an illegally parked car, you are almost always held predominantly at fault for the collision under standard traffic laws. Under civil tort law, a moving driver owes an overarching legal duty to maintain a vigilant lookout and avoid striking stationary objects, though the illegally parked owner may share partial comparative fault if the vehicle created an unforeseeable road hazard.

The Legal Presumption: Moving Vehicles vs. Stationary Objects

A widespread misconception among motorists is that if a vehicle is parked illegally?"such as parked in a designated red fire lane, blocking a private driveway, double-parked on a city street, or parked too close to an intersection?"the owner of that parked car is automatically 100 percent liable for any collision that occurs. Motorists frequently believe that an illegal parking violation strips the parked vehicle owner of all legal protections.

Under traffic safety codes and civil tort jurisprudence across all fifty states, this legal assumption is completely false. The fundamental legal doctrine governing collisions with stationary objects establishes a powerful legal presumption of negligence against the moving driver. Under statutory rules of the road, every licensed driver has an affirmative, continuous legal duty to maintain a proper lookout, travel at a safe speed appropriate for conditions, and maintain control of their vehicle to avoid colliding with stationary objects on or near the roadway.

The law reasons that regardless of whether a vehicle is legally parked, illegally parked, or disabled with hazard lights on, a parked vehicle is stationary. A stationary object cannot steer into your path, accelerate, or execute sudden evasive maneuvers. Therefore, in the vast majority of insurance claim adjudications, the moving driver is assigned the primary share of liability (typically 70% to 100%) for failing to observe what was plainly visible. When Does an Illegally Parked Car Share Comparative Fault?

While the moving driver bears the primary presumption of negligence, the owner of an illegally parked car is not completely immune from liability. Under modern Comparative Negligence laws, an illegally parked vehicle owner can be assigned a significant percentage of legal fault if the illegal parking created an extraordinary, unforeseeable hazard that significantly contributed to the crash.

To successfully assign comparative fault to an illegally parked vehicle owner, the moving driver must prove that the vehicle placement constituted more than a passive civil parking violation; it must have created a dynamic hazard that a reasonably prudent driver could not foresee or avoid. Classic examples include parking an unlit, dark-colored vehicle at night around a sharp blind curve where sightlines are physically obstructed, leaving a disabled vehicle partially jutting into an unlit 65-MPH highway travel lane without hazard lights or reflective triangles, or parking where official municipal signs forbid parking specifically because turning vehicles lack sufficient physical clearance.

In states operating under Modified Comparative Fault (such as Texas, Illinois, and Ohio), if the evidence demonstrates that the illegally parked vehicle owner was 50 percent or more at fault for creating a lethal, unseeable hazard, the moving driver can recover vehicle repair costs from the parked car owner's insurance policy. In Pure Comparative Fault states (like California and Florida), even if the moving driver was 80 percent at fault, they can still recover 20 percent of their vehicle damages. The Criminal Trap: Hitting an Illegally Parked Car and Leaving the Scene

The most catastrophic mistake a motorist can make after striking an illegally parked car is driving away. Many drivers justify leaving the scene by reasoning: "The other car was parked illegally in a fire lane anyway, so it is their own fault!" In the eyes of criminal law, this reasoning is completely invalid.

Under motor vehicle codes across all fifty states, hitting any unattended parked vehicle?"regardless of whether it is legally parked, illegally parked, or abandoned?"imposes a mandatory legal duty on the striking driver. You must immediately stop your vehicle at the scene, locate the owner, or leave a conspicuous written note securely attached to the damaged vehicle containing your full name, address, telephone number, vehicle registration number, and automobile insurance carrier. Fleeing the scene transforms a simple property damage insurance claim into a criminal charge of Leaving the Scene of an Accident (Hit-and-Run), resulting in criminal misdemeanor convictions, mandatory driver license suspensions, and high fines.

How to Handle Hitting an Illegally Parked Vehicle Properly

A step-by-step practical guide for motorists to comply with hit-and-run laws, document evidence, and manage insurance claims after hitting a parked car.

  1. Stop Immediately and Remain at the Collision Scene

    Never drive away. Stop your vehicle in a safe location immediately adjacent to the struck vehicle to avoid criminal hit-and-run misdemeanor charges.

  2. Photograph the Illegally Parked Vehicle and Street Markings

    Take extensive photos showing the illegal parking violation: painted red curbs, 'No Parking' signs, fire hydrants, blocked sightlines, and point-of-impact damage.

  3. Attempt to Locate the Owner or Contact Local Police

    If in a commercial lot, notify store customer service to page the vehicle owner. If on a public street, call the local non-emergency police line to report the collision.

  4. Leave a Secure Written Contact Note if Owner Is Unavailable

    If the owner cannot be found, write a clear note with your full name, phone number, address, insurance policy number, and place it securely under the windshield wiper.

  5. Report the Claim to Your Insurance Carrier with Photos

    Notify your auto insurance company promptly. Provide your photos demonstrating the illegal parking violation so adjusters can argue comparative negligence.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Who is at fault if you hit an illegally parked car?

The moving driver is almost always held primarily at fault (70% to 100%) for failing to maintain a proper lookout. However, the parked owner may share partial comparative fault if they created an unavoidable hazard.

Q2: Is it a hit and run if you hit an illegally parked car and leave?

Yes. Fleeing the scene after hitting any parked vehicle?"regardless of whether it was parked illegally?"is a criminal hit-and-run misdemeanor that carries license suspensions and criminal fines.

Q3: Can an illegally parked car owner be held 100% at fault?

Rarely. An illegally parked car owner is only held predominantly at fault in extreme situations, such as parking an unlit dark vehicle around a sharp blind curve at night without emergency flashers.

Q4: What should I write on a note if I hit a parked car?

State laws require you to leave your full legal name, current address, phone number, vehicle registration number, and automobile insurance company name and policy number.

Q5: Does collision insurance cover hitting an illegally parked car?

Yes. Your personal automobile collision coverage pays to repair your vehicle (minus your deductible), while your property damage liability coverage pays to repair the parked vehicle.

Q6: Will my insurance rates increase if I hit an illegally parked car?

Yes, in most cases. Because hitting a stationary object is classified as an at-fault collision, your insurance company will likely apply an insurance surcharge upon policy renewal.

Q7: What if the car was parked illegally in a marked fire lane?

Parking in a fire lane is a parking infraction, but it does not excuse a moving driver from seeing and avoiding the car. The moving driver remains primarily at fault for failing to maintain a proper lookout.

Q8: Can police issue a parking ticket to the illegally parked car after a crash?

Yes. Responding police officers frequently issue a moving violation citation to the striking driver and a separate parking citation to the illegally parked vehicle owner.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

If you hit an illegally parked car, recognize that insurance adjusters will presumptively hold you primarily at fault for failing to maintain a proper lookout. However, if the vehicle placement created an unforeseeable, unlit, or blind hazard, you can establish comparative negligence to offset your liability. Never leave the scene without leaving your contact information, document the illegal parking placement with extensive photographs, and consult an attorney if significant injuries or property damages are at stake.
