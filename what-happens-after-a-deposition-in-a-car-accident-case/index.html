What Happens After a Deposition in a Car Case?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 30, 2026

After a deposition in a car accident case concludes, the litigation moves into a pivotal post-discovery phase where sworn testimony is transcribed, analyzed, and leveraged to determine whether the claim will settle through mediation or proceed to a jury trial. Rather than signifying the immediate end of the lawsuit, the deposition serves as a critical factual benchmark. In the weeks following the examination under oath, court reporters produce the certified transcript, attorneys evaluate witness credibility and liability admissions, defense insurers frequently order an Independent Medical Examination, and both legal teams engage in intense settlement negotiations or formal court-ordered mediation.

Court Reporter Transcription, Transcript Review, and the Errata Sheet

Once the formal questioning under oath ends and the court reporter turns off the stenographic recording equipment, the lawsuit enters a critical post-deposition evaluation window. Depositions are stenographically recorded verbatim by a licensed court reporter. Over the subsequent two to four weeks, the reporter transcribes their shorthand notes and audio backups into an official, certified deposition transcript. This bound or electronic transcript documents every question, response, attorney objection, and marked exhibit introduced during the deposition.

Under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 30(e) and corresponding state rules of civil procedure, the deponent has the legal right to read and sign the deposition transcript. Once the certified transcript is prepared, the plaintiff or defendant witness is granted a statutory thirty-day review window. During this review, you meet with your attorney to examine the transcript line by line for transcription inaccuracies, typographical blunders, or phonetic misunderstandings committed by the court reporter.

If errors are discovered, your attorney prepares an official legal document known as an Errata Sheet. The errata sheet lists the specific page and line numbers of the error, the revised wording, and the specific reason for the correction. While correcting obvious transcription errors is standard practice, attempting to make substantive changes to factual answers—such as changing an admission of driving speed from forty miles per hour to twenty-five—is legally hazardous. Opposing defense counsel can highlight these alterations before a jury to attack your credibility and impeach your character during trial.

Depositions frequently uncover severe liability and policy limit exposures. For instance, when analyzing whether can you be personally sued for a car accident, testimony regarding gross recklessness or catastrophic medical bills often prompts defense carriers to negotiate within policy limits to shield the insured from personal liability.

Similarly, in multi-vehicle collisions where passengers are injured, deposition testimony often determines whether can a passenger sue the driver in an accident by clarifying driver distraction or shared comparative negligence.

Defense Independent Medical Examinations (IMEs) and Expert Discovery

Following a plaintiff deposition in a personal injury action, the defense insurance carrier almost invariably exercises its statutory right to request an Independent Medical Examination (IME), frequently referred to in legal practice as a Defense Medical Examination. Armed with your sworn deposition testimony regarding your physical pain, range-of-motion limitations, prior accidents, and daily life restrictions, the insurance defense counsel hires a specialized orthopedic surgeon or neurologist to conduct a comprehensive physical evaluation.

It is crucial to recognize that an IME doctor is not your treating physician and maintains zero doctor-patient confidentiality. The defense medical examiner is paid hundreds or thousands of dollars by the insurance company to scrutinize your medical records, perform orthopedic tests, and compile a written report. Their primary objective is frequently to conclude that your soft tissue tears, herniated discs, or joint pain stem from pre-existing age-related degenerative disc disease, prior athletic injuries, or subjective exaggeration, rather than the force of the vehicle crash.

Simultaneously, both legal teams engage in expert witness discovery. Accident reconstruction engineers analyze vehicle crush damage, download Event Data Recorder (EDR) black box data, and examine skid marks to calculate vehicle speeds and angles of impact. Vocational rehabilitation specialists and forensic economists calculate future loss of earning capacity and lifetime medical care plans based on testimony elicited during depositions.

Litigants often worry about worst-case trial outcomes when reviewing defense expert reports. Evaluating legal realities like what happens if you lose a car accident lawsuit underscores why the vast majority of insurance companies and injured plaintiffs prefer negotiating structured compromises at mediation rather than risking catastrophic trial verdicts.

Settlement Negotiations, Mandatory Mediation, and Final Trial Preparation

The weeks following deposition completion represent the most active settlement window in automobile accident litigation. Before depositions take place, insurance adjusters evaluate claims based primarily on dry medical bills and cold police reports. However, once depositions conclude, defense attorneys send detailed evaluation letters to insurance claims adjusters. These reports grade the plaintiff credibility, honesty, likability, and jury appeal, assessing whether a jury would find the plaintiff sympathetic and deserving of substantial non-economic compensation for pain and suffering.

If the plaintiff performed exceptionally well on the witness stand—remaining calm, consistent, polite, and articulate—the insurance defense carrier frequently raises its settlement reserve valuation. Your personal injury attorney will draft an updated settlement demand package reflecting deposition admissions, updated medical liens, and expert economic projections. If direct negotiations between attorneys stall, courts routinely order the parties to attend mandatory alternative dispute resolution through formal mediation.

During mediation, a neutral mediator—usually a retired state judge or seasoned trial lawyer—shuttles between separate conference rooms containing the plaintiff team and the insurance defense team. Over several hours of intense negotiations, the mediator highlights evidentiary weaknesses in both sides, facilitating compromise. If a settlement agreement is executed, the insurer typically disburses settlement funds within thirty to sixty days. If mediation fails, both legal teams immediately file pretrial motions in limine, finalize exhibit lists, and prepare for jury selection (voir dire) at trial.

How to Navigate the Post-Deposition Phase in a Car Accident Lawsuit

Follow these essential procedural steps after giving your sworn deposition to protect your legal claims, correct transcript errors, and support settlement negotiations.

  1. Debrief Immediately With Your Attorney

    Spend fifteen to thirty minutes with your personal injury attorney right after the deposition to review key questions asked, identify any misstatements, and discuss the defense counsel strategy.

  2. Review the Certified Transcript and Errata Sheet Within 30 Days

    When the court reporter delivers the transcript, read every page carefully. Note any typographical, phonetic, or transcription errors on an official errata sheet with your attorney.

  3. Prepare for the Defense Independent Medical Examination (IME)

    If scheduled for an IME, review your accident medical history. Be honest, polite, and cooperative during physical tests, but refrain from discussing legal fault or settlement figures with the defense doctor.

  4. Continue Medical Treatment and Preserve All Expense Records

    Continue attending physical therapy, chiropractic appointments, or specialist visits. Keep detailed records of co-pays, mileage, and out-of-pocket medical expenses to keep damages updated.

  5. Participate Actively in Settlement Mediation Conferences

    Attend your scheduled mediation session with an open mind. Work collaboratively with your attorney to weigh insurance settlement offers against the risks, expenses, and stress of a jury trial.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How long after a deposition does a car accident case settle?

Most car accident cases settle within thirty days to four months following a deposition, often during informal attorney negotiations or court-ordered formal mediation sessions.

Q2: What is an errata sheet after a deposition?

An errata sheet is an official legal form where a deponent lists typographical or transcription errors found in the court reporter certified transcript, providing corrections within a 30-day window.

Q3: Can you change your answers on an errata sheet?

While witnesses can technically correct transcription errors, making substantive factual changes to sworn testimony can be used by opposing counsel to attack your credibility and impeach you at trial.

Q4: Why does the insurance company request an IME after a deposition?

The defense schedules an Independent Medical Examination to have their hired medical expert evaluate your physical injuries, look for pre-existing conditions, and challenge the severity of your damages.

Q5: Does a deposition mean your car accident case is going to trial?

No, taking depositions is a standard discovery procedure. In fact, over 95 percent of personal injury lawsuits settle before reaching a jury trial, often immediately after depositions expose case strengths.

Q6: What happens if you gave bad testimony during your deposition?

If you made inconsistent or damaging statements, your attorney will work to mitigate the impact during transcript review, gather supporting medical or witness evidence, or adjust settlement expectations.

Q7: Who attends the post-deposition mediation conference?

Mediation is attended by the plaintiff, plaintiff attorney, defense attorney, insurance claims adjuster, and a neutral mediator who shuttles between rooms to help negotiate a settlement.

Q8: How long does it take to receive settlement money after an agreement?

Once a settlement release is signed and returned, insurance companies typically issue settlement checks within thirty to sixty days, after which medical liens and legal fees are satisfied before funds are disbursed.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

A deposition is one of the most critical turning points in a car accident lawsuit, bridging the initial fact-finding phase with final settlement negotiations or trial preparation. While it is natural to hope for an instantaneous resolution once questioning ends, the post-deposition phase requires patience as transcripts are certified, medical evaluations are finalized, and settlement offers are negotiated. By reviewing transcripts carefully, attending required defense medical exams, and staying in close communication with your attorney, you position your personal injury claim for maximum compensation.
