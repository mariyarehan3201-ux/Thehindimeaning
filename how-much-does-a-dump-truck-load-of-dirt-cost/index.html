How Much Does a Dump Truck Load of Dirt Cost?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a dump truck load of dirt cost requires analyzing dirt grade, truck capacity, and travel distance from the quarry or supplier. On average, a standard tandem-axle dump truck load delivering 10 to 14 cubic yards of dirt costs between $200 and $600 for standard fill dirt, and $350 to $800 for screened, nutrient-rich topsoil. Large tri-axle or semi-end dump trucks carrying 16 to 22 cubic yards range from $400 to $1,200 fully delivered.

Cost Breakdown: Fill Dirt vs. Screened Topsoil vs. Engineered Fill

The classification and quality of soil dramatically influence pricing per truckload. Common unprocessed fill dirt—used primarily for grading low spots, backfilling retaining walls, and raising building pads—is the least expensive option, priced at $8 to $18 per cubic yard before delivery. A full tandem dump truck load of fill dirt generally costs $180 to $400 delivered within a 15-mile delivery radius.

In contrast, screened topsoil that has been mechanically filtered through rotating trommel screens to remove rocks, root matter, and heavy clay clumps costs $25 to $45 per cubic yard ($350 to $700 per dump truck load). Premium garden-blend soils enriched with organic compost, mushroom manure, and peat moss command $35 to $60 per cubic yard, pushing a 12-yard tandem dump truck load to $500 to $850 delivered.

Dump Truck Sizes: Single, Tandem, Tri-Axle, and Semi-End Dumps

Dump trucks vary significantly in volumetric capacity and legal highway weight limits. A standard single-axle dump truck carries 5 to 7 cubic yards (6 to 8 tons) and is ideal for residential driveways with tight turns and low overhead tree branches. A tandem-axle dump truck carries 10 to 14 cubic yards (12 to 16 tons) and represents the industry standard for residential landscaping and pool excavation projects.

For large commercial grading, commercial foundation backfilling, or multi-acre acreage development, contractors utilize heavy tri-axle dump trucks carrying 14 to 18 cubic yards, or semi-end dump tractor-trailers hauling 20 to 25 cubic yards. While semi-trailers offer the lowest per-yard delivery rate, their massive weight can easily crack residential asphalt driveways and damage underground sprinkler systems.

Delivery Logistics, Access Obstacles, and Spreading Labor

Quarries and aggregate supply yards typically include a base delivery charge for deliveries within a 10 to 15-mile radius, typically billing $75 to $150 per truckload. For locations outside their primary distribution zone, haulers bill an additional $3 to $7 per loaded mile, or charge an hourly truck rate of $95 to $160 per hour. If a job site requires split deliveries or tailgating (slowly dumping dirt in a spread row), extra fees may apply.

Homeowners must also budget for site preparation and spreading labor. A dump truck driver simply deposits the dirt in a single large pile. Moving 12 cubic yards (roughly 30,000 pounds) of soil by hand with wheelbarrows requires 20 to 30 hours of exhausting labor. Hiring a skid steer (Bobcat) or compact track loader with an operator costs $90 to $150 per hour ($350 to $600 for half-day grading), ensuring proper slope and compaction.

How to Order a Dump Truck Load of Dirt

Follow these five steps to calculate volume, select the right soil grade, and receive bulk dirt efficiently.

  1. Calculate Cubic Yardage with Length, Width, and Depth

    Multiply length by width in feet, multiply by depth in feet, and divide by 27 to calculate the exact cubic yards needed.

  2. Select the Correct Dirt Grade for Your Project

    Choose unscreened fill dirt for raising subgrades, clean certified fill for foundation pads, and screened topsoil for sod and gardens.

  3. Inspect Delivery Access and Overhead Hazards

    Ensure the delivery path has a 10-foot wide clearance and 18 feet of vertical clearance free of power lines and tree branches.

  4. Mark the Designated Dump Spot Clearly with Tarps or Cones

    Place safety cones, spray chalk, or heavy tarps on the driveway to give the truck driver an unmistakable target.

  5. Arrange Spreading Equipment or Compact Machinery

    Reserve a skid steer rental or hire a grading contractor to spread and compact the soil before heavy rains create muddy ruts.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a dump truck load of fill dirt cost?

A tandem dump truck carrying 10 to 14 cubic yards of fill dirt typically costs between $200 and $450 fully delivered.

Q2: How much does a dump truck load of topsoil cost?

A tandem dump truck carrying 10 to 14 cubic yards of screened topsoil costs between $350 and $700 delivered.

Q3: How many cubic yards are in a dump truck of dirt?

A standard tandem dump truck holds 10 to 14 cubic yards; single-axle trucks hold 5 to 7 yards; tri-axle trucks hold 14 to 18 yards.

Q4: How many tons are in a dump truck load of dirt?

A tandem dump truck load of dry dirt weighs approximately 12 to 16 tons; wet clay or compact topsoil can exceed 18 tons.

Q5: Can a dump truck crack my concrete driveway?

Yes, a loaded tandem or tri-axle dump truck weighs 45,000 to 65,000 lbs and can easily crack standard 4-inch residential concrete.

Q6: What is clean fill dirt?

Clean fill is natural soil free of trash, hazardous chemicals, asphalt, large concrete chunks, and organic plant debris.

Q7: How much does a cubic yard of dirt cover?

One cubic yard of dirt covers 324 square feet at 1 inch deep, 162 square feet at 2 inches deep, or 81 square feet at 4 inches deep.

Q8: Do dump truck drivers spread the dirt?

Generally no; drivers drop the load in a single pile, though some experienced drivers may perform a tailgate spread along a wide driveway.
