How Much Is It to Side a House?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Replacing the exterior siding on your home is one of the most transformative home improvement investments you can make, instantly rejuvenating curb appeal, elevating energy efficiency, and protecting structural framing from weather damage. On average, the cost to side a house ranges between $6,000 and $22,000 for a typical 1,500 to 2,500 square-foot single-family home, with overall costs averaging $5 to $14 per square foot of exterior wall surface installed. Material selection—spanning economical vinyl, durable fiber cement, classic cedar wood, or modern metal—is the single largest factor dictating your final invoice.

Siding Material Classes, Durability, and Cost Per Square Foot

In the exterior cladding industry, pricing is universally calculated by the siding square, where one square equals exactly 100 square feet of finished wall surface area. Vinyl siding remains the most popular and budget-friendly option across North America, costing $400 to $800 per square installed ($4 to $8 per square foot). Vinyl is lightweight, immune to insect rot, never requires painting, and is available in standard hollow-back profiles or insulated foam-backed panels that boost thermal R-value performance.

For homeowners seeking increased durability, architectural depth, and fire resistance, fiber cement siding (dominated by James Hardie HardiePlank) has become the gold standard, costing $800 to $1,500 per square installed ($8 to $15 per square foot). Composed of Portland cement, sand, and cellulose wood fibers, fiber cement is non-combustible, impervious to woodpeckers and termites, and carries a 30 to 50-year lifespan. Natural wood siding (such as Western red cedar lap or shingles) costs $900 to $1,800 per square, requiring regular staining, while engineered wood (LP SmartSide) delivers wood warmth with synthetic resins for $600 to $1,100 per square.

Tear-Off Labor, Weather Barriers, and Sheathing Repairs

A significant portion of your siding contractor estimate encompasses demolition labor, waste disposal, and wall preparation beneath the visible panels. Removing and disposing of old existing siding typically costs $1,000 to $3,500 ($1.00 to $2.50 per square foot), depending on whether your home has a single layer of old vinyl or brittle, heavy asbestos-cement or lead-painted wood shingles requiring hazardous disposal containment.

Once old siding is stripped, contractors must inspect the underlying oriented strand board (OSB) or plywood wall sheathing. Replacing rot-damaged sheathing sheets adds $75 to $120 per 4x8-foot sheet. Installing a modern weather-resistive vapor barrier (such as DuPont Tyvek home wrap) along with flashing tapes around window frames ($0.50 to $1.20 per square foot) is vital to stop wind-driven rain infiltration while allowing interior wall moisture vapor to escape harmlessly.

Energy Efficiency Improvements, ROI, and Contractor Selection

Re-siding your house offers an ideal opportunity to upgrade exterior thermal envelope performance. Contractors can install a continuous layer of rigid extruded polystyrene (XPS) or polyisocyanurate foam insulation board beneath your new siding ($0.60 to $1.50 per square foot). This continuous thermal barrier breaks thermal bridging across wood wall studs, reducing heating and cooling utility costs by 8 to 15 percent and keeping rooms noticeably quieter and more comfortable year-round.

From a financial return standpoint, siding replacement consistently ranks among the highest return-on-investment exterior remodeling projects in the annual Remodeling Cost vs. Value Report. Vinyl siding replacements routinely recoup 65 to 75 percent of their cost at home resale, while fiber cement siding recoups 70 to 82 percent. Always collect three detailed bids from factory-certified siding installers (such as James Hardie Elite Preferred contractors) carrying active licensing and comprehensive workers compensation insurance.

How to Plan and Execute a House Siding Project in 5 Steps

Follow these five strategic milestones to calculate wall area, select cladding materials, and manage your siding installation.

  1. Calculate Exterior Wall Square Footage

    Measure perimeter wall lengths and heights, subtract large window and door openings, and divide by 100 to estimate siding squares needed.

  2. Select Siding Material and Architectural Profile

    Choose between affordable vinyl, resilient fiber cement, or modern steel based on your neighborhood home values and maintenance goals.

  3. Collect Multiple Itemized Contractor Quotes

    Solicit bids from at least three licensed siding specialists, verifying that estimates itemize tear-off, sheathing repair, house wrap, and soffits.

  4. Inspect Underlying Sheathing and Weather Barrier

    Conduct a visual walkthrough with the project manager after old siding is removed to identify any hidden dry rot or termite damage.

  5. Perform Final Punch List and Magnet Sweep

    Examine caulking around windows and corner seams, test exterior lights, and ensure the crew runs a rolling magnetic sweep for stray nails.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to side a 2,000 square foot house?

Siding a 2,000 square-foot home typically costs between $9,000 and $18,000 for vinyl, or $16,000 to $28,000 for fiber cement.

Q2: What is the cheapest siding option for a house?

Standard hollow-back vinyl siding is the most affordable cladding material, costing between $4.00 and $7.50 per square foot installed.

Q3: How long does new siding last?

Vinyl siding lasts 25 to 30 years, engineered wood lasts 30 to 40 years, and fiber cement or steel siding can easily last 50 years or longer.

Q4: Does new siding increase home value?

Yes, siding replacement consistently recoups 65 to 80 percent of its project cost at home resale while dramatically enhancing curb appeal.

Q5: Can you put new siding over old siding?

While technically possible with vinyl over flat wood, it is not recommended because it traps moisture, hides underlying rot, and creates alignment issues.

Q6: How long does it take to side a whole house?

A professional crew of four to five installers typically completes a full residential siding replacement in 5 to 10 business days.

Q7: What is a square of siding?

In the exterior construction trade, a square is a standardized unit of measurement representing exactly 100 square feet of surface wall area.

Q8: Why is James Hardie fiber cement more expensive than vinyl?

Fiber cement requires specialized diamond-blade cutting tools, heavier multi-person labor to lift panels, silica-dust safety gear, and premium paint finishing.
