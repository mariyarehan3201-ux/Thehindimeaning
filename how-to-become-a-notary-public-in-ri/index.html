How to Become a Notary Public in RI?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Becoming a commissioned Notary Public in Rhode Island allows you to serve as an official public witness to important legal, real estate, and financial transactions across the Ocean State. Overseen by the Rhode Island Department of State Notary Division under the Uniform Law on Notarial Acts, securing your commission involves meeting statutory eligibility requirements, mastering notarial guidelines, submitting an official application, and executing an oath of office. This guide outlines the complete credentialing path, state seal standards, and notary best practices.

Rhode Island Notary Qualifications and Eligibility Framework

In Rhode Island, notaries public are public officials appointed under Title 42, Chapter 30.1 of the Rhode Island General Laws (RIGL), known as the Revised Uniform Law on Notarial Acts (RULONA). Administered through the Office of the Rhode Island Secretary of State, notaries serve as impartial witnesses tasked with verifying signer identities, confirming voluntary willingness to execute documents, and administering legally binding oaths. Operating with integrity prevents fraudulent conveyances, identity theft, and disputed commercial contracts.

To qualify for a Rhode Island notary commission, an individual must be at least 18 years of age and either reside in Rhode Island or maintain an active place of employment or business practice within the state. Applicants must be able to read, speak, and write the English language fluently and demonstrate clean moral character without disqualifying felony convictions. Attorneys admitted to the Rhode Island Bar and certified Rhode Island certified public accountants are also eligible and benefit from streamlined application filing protocols.

Application Process, Knowledge Review, and Swearing of Oaths

Prospective notaries begin by reviewing the official Rhode Island Notary Public Manual published by the Department of State. While Rhode Island does not currently administer a proctored standardized written examination, applicants must thoroughly review statutory notarial rules, acknowledgments, jurats, and verification on oath or affirmation. The application form requires candidates to attest under penalty of perjury that they comprehend notarial responsibilities and agree to uphold all state statutes.

Applicants complete the official Notary Public Application online or via paper submission, pay the statutory $80 non-refundable application fee, and provide required employment verification if applying as a non-resident commuter from Connecticut or Massachusetts. Once the application is approved by the Department of State, the applicant receives an appointment notice. The applicant must appear in person before an authorized official, such as a commissioned notary public, to take their constitutional oath of office before their official commission certificate is formally issued.

Official Seal Regulations, Journal Practices, and Modern Notary Services

Every commissioned Rhode Island notary must procure an official notarial seal before performing any official acts. Under RULONA, the seal may be either an inked rubber stamp or an embossing seal, although an inked stamp is universally favored for legible photocopying and digital archiving. The seal must clearly display the notary's legal name exactly as commissioned, the words "Notary Public," the jurisdiction "State of Rhode Island," and the notary's commission identification number and expiration date.

Rhode Island state law requires all commissioned notaries to maintain an official notarial journal. The journal can be a permanent, bound paper book with sequentially numbered pages or a tamper-evident electronic journal for remote online notarizations. Each journal entry must document the date and time of the notarial act, document description, signer's printed name and physical address, verified method of identification, and fees collected. Maintaining comprehensive journal records provides undeniable legal protection if a signed deed or power of attorney is contested in probate or civil court.

How to Apply for a Rhode Island Notary Public Commission

Step-by-step procedure to complete your application, pay state fees, take your oath, and start notarizing in Rhode Island.

  1. Verify Statutory Qualifications and Eligibility

    Ensure you are at least 18 years old, a Rhode Island resident or business commuter, and possess a clean criminal background record.

  2. Study the Official Rhode Island Notary Manual

    Review the comprehensive manual provided by the Secretary of State covering acknowledgments, jurats, oath administration, and identity vetting.

  3. Submit the Official Application and State Fee

    Complete the Notary Public Application online or by mail, including employer verification if applicable, and pay the eighty-dollar fee.

  4. Take the Official Constitutional Oath of Office

    Receive your appointment notification and take your oath before an authorized official or commissioned notary, returning the executed document.

  5. Purchase an Approved Seal and Sequential Notary Journal

    Order an official Rhode Island rubber stamp conforming to state design laws and a durable, sequentially numbered notarial record journal.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to become a notary in Rhode Island?

The state application fee is eighty dollars for a four-year commission, plus approximately thirty to sixty dollars for an official stamp and record journal.

Q2: How long is a Rhode Island notary commission good for?

A Rhode Island notary commission remains valid for four years from the date of official commission issuance.

Q3: Can out-of-state residents become Rhode Island notaries?

Yes, individuals residing in bordering states like Massachusetts or Connecticut can qualify if they maintain an active workplace or business in Rhode Island.

Q4: Does Rhode Island require a surety bond for notaries?

No, Rhode Island statutory law does not require notaries public to purchase or post an individual commercial surety bond.

Q5: Is keeping a notary journal mandatory in Rhode Island?

Yes, under Rhode Island RULONA statutes, notaries public are legally mandated to maintain an official chronological journal of all notarial acts.

Q6: Does Rhode Island allow Remote Online Notarization (RON)?

Yes, commissioned Rhode Island notaries can register to perform remote online notarizations using state-approved, tamper-evident technology platforms.

Q7: Can a Rhode Island notary solemnize marriage ceremonies?

No, a Rhode Island notary public does not hold statutory legal authority to solemnize marriages unless they hold a distinct recognized religious or judicial title.

Q8: What fee can a Rhode Island notary charge per notarial act?

Rhode Island law allows notaries to charge up to twenty-five dollars per notarial act, with clear advance fee disclosure provided to the customer.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Becoming a notary public in Rhode Island is a distinguished public service that demands ethical diligence, statutory compliance, and rigorous attention to detail. By following the application protocols established by the Rhode Island Department of State, procuring proper stamps and journals, and performing impartial identity verifications, you establish yourself as a dependable pillar of integrity in the Ocean State's legal and business community.
