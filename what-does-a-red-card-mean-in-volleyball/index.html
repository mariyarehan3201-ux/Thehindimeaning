What Does a Red Card Mean in Volleyball?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

In competitive volleyball governed by the International Volleyball Federation (FIVB), USA Volleyball, and the NCAA, a red card carries a fundamentally different meaning than it does in soccer. In volleyball, receiving a standalone red card does not eject or remove the player or coach from the match. Instead, a red card represents a formal "Penalty" issued for rude conduct or repeated minor infractions, resulting in the immediate loss of rally—meaning the opposing team is awarded one point and the right to serve.

The FIVB Sanction Scale and Misconduct Hierarchy

To preserve sportsmanship and game flow, international volleyball employs a graduated misconduct sanction scale administered by the first referee perched on the officiating stand. Referees categorize behavioral violations into three distinct categories: minor misconduct, rude conduct, and offensive or aggressive conduct. While soccer uses a simple yellow-for-warning and red-for-ejection system, volleyball utilizes cards individually and in combination to signal distinct levels of administrative punishment.

Minor misconduct is initially handled verbally or through a yellow card, which acts strictly as a formal team warning and carries zero point deductions. If a player, substitute, or coach continues to argue with line judges, displays contemptuous gestures toward opponents, or kicks equipment, the first referee displays a standalone Red Card. This formal penalty immediately transfers one point and the subsequent service rotation to the opposing squad. The penalized individual remains in the match and can continue playing immediately.

Decoding Expulsion vs. Disqualification Combinations

The most frequent source of confusion among casual volleyball spectators is witnessing the referee display both red and yellow cards simultaneously. The exact physical manner in which the referee holds these cards communicates whether a participant is being temporarily benched or permanently ejected from the facility.

When the first referee holds both the red card and yellow card together in a single hand, it signals "Expulsion." An expelled player or coach must immediately vacate the court and bench area, spending the remainder of the current set in the team's locker room or designated penalty area. Once the set concludes and teams switch sides, the expelled individual is legally permitted to return to the match. In contrast, when the referee holds the red card in one hand and the yellow card in the other hand held aloft, it signals "Disqualification." Disqualification is an absolute match ejection requiring the offender to leave the entire sports facility for the rest of the day.

Impact on Team Strategy, Rotation, and Scorekeeping

Receiving a red card penalty impacts tactical momentum profoundly. Because volleyball is a game of rapid side-outs and psychological momentum, conceding an unearned point and relinquishing service can turn a tightly contested 23-23 set into set point for the opponent. Furthermore, if a player is expelled (Red and Yellow together), the team must execute an immediate legal substitution to replace the expelled athlete; if no legal substitutes remain on the bench, the team is declared incomplete and forfeits the set.

The official scorekeeper plays a critical role in logging every sanction on the official FIVB scoresheet. The scorekeeper records the exact set number, the offending player or coach jersey number, the team, the current score, and the precise sanction code. Sanctions carry progressive consequences: a player who has already received a red card penalty cannot receive another red card in the same match; their next rude conduct infraction automatically triggers an expulsion or disqualification.

How to Manage Court Conduct and Avoid Volleyball Sanctions

Follow these sportsmanship protocols for volleyball players and coaches to avoid costly penalty cards during competitive matches.

  1. Designate the Team Floor Captain for Official Inquiries

    Ensure only the designated floor captain approaches the first referee to respectfully ask for rule clarifications during match stoppages.

  2. Accept Referee Judgment Calls Without Dramatic Gestures

    Never kick the ball, throw water bottles, or yell across the net at line judges after close ball-handling or touch decisions.

  3. Heed Preliminary Verbal and Yellow Card Warnings

    Recognize that a yellow card is a formal team-wide warning; adjust emotional intensity immediately to avoid escalating to a red card penalty.

  4. Maintain Professional Conduct Across the Bench Area

    Instruct substitute players and assistant coaches to remain seated or within the warm-up zone without shouting insults at opposing players.

  5. Request Formal Match Protests Through Proper Channels

    If a certified rule misapplication occurs, have your floor captain register a formal protest protocol with the down referee before play resumes.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does a red card mean you are ejected in volleyball?

No. A single red card alone is a penalty that gives the opposing team one point and the serve; the player remains in the game.

Q2: What card ejects a player in volleyball?

A player is ejected when the referee holds up both the red card and yellow card simultaneously (together in one hand or in separate hands).

Q3: What is the difference between a yellow card and a red card in volleyball?

A yellow card is a warning that costs zero points; a red card is a penalty that immediately awards a point and serve to the opposing team.

Q4: What does red and yellow card in one hand mean?

It means 'Expulsion.' The player or coach must leave the court and remain in the locker room for the rest of the current set only.

Q5: What does red and yellow card in separate hands mean?

It means 'Disqualification.' The player or coach is completely ejected for the remainder of the entire match and must leave the building.

Q6: Can a coach get a red card in volleyball?

Yes, coaches, assistants, and bench personnel can receive yellow, red, expulsion, and disqualification cards for unsportsmanlike behavior.

Q7: What triggers a red card in volleyball?

Rude conduct, profanity toward officials, aggressive taunting across the net, or repeated minor misconduct after a prior yellow card warning.

Q8: Does a red card penalty give the other team a point?

Yes, the opposing team is immediately awarded one point and the right to serve the ball on the next rally.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

In volleyball, a standalone red card represents a penalty that awards one point and the serve to the opposing team, rather than an ejection from the match. Real ejections occur only when red and yellow cards are displayed simultaneously—either together for a single-set expulsion or in separate hands for full-match disqualification. Understanding these nuanced rules allows players, coaches, and fans to interpret officiating signals with precision and maintain supreme respect for court discipline.
