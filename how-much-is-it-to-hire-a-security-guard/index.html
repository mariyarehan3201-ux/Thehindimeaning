How Much Is It to Hire a Security Guard?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Whether securing a corporate construction jobsite, organizing a high-profile VIP wedding, deterring retail loss, or protecting executive personnel, hiring dedicated private security guards provides vital peace of mind. Nationally, hiring an unarmed security guard costs between $20 and $45 per hour, while an armed security guard ranges from $35 to $75 per hour, and off-duty police officers or executive protection agents command $60 to $120 per hour. Understanding guard licensing tiers, insurance liabilities, and contract minimums ensures you deploy optimal physical protection tailored to your risk environment.

Guard Licensing Classifications: Unarmed, Armed, and Off-Duty Police

The primary factor determining security guard billing rates is the legal authority, lethal armament, and specialized training level of the security officer. Unarmed security guards represent the most common commercial tier, billed at $20 to $40 per hour. Unarmed guards undergo state-mandated security officer licensing (often called a guard card), background checks, and basic de-escalation training. They serve primarily as visible physical deterrents, observing and reporting suspicious activities, managing visitor access logs, and monitoring CCTV security consoles.

Armed security guards command higher rates of $35 to $70 per hour. Armed officers hold advanced state firearms certifications, undergo recurring weapons qualifications, and complete tactical conflict resolution training. They are typically stationed in high-cash environments, financial institutions, cannabis dispensaries, luxury jewelry boutiques, or volatile commercial perimeters. At the highest protection tier, hiring active or retired off-duty police officers or military veteran executive protection specialists costs $65 to $130 per hour, providing full law-enforcement arrest powers and tactical bodyguard experience.

Contract Terms, Shift Lengths, and Hidden Agency Surcharges

When partnering with a commercial private security agency, you will rarely pay purely the bare guard wage. Security agency billing invoices cover statutory guard wages plus payroll taxes, state workers compensation insurance (which carries high risk rates for security personnel), comprehensive general liability insurance policies ($1M to $5M coverage limits), uniform provisions, and 24/7 central dispatch overhead. Consequently, a security company billing $30 per hour may pay the individual guard roughly $16 to $19 per hour.

Agencies universally enforce minimum shift duration requirements, typically mandating four to six continuous hours per guard per deployment. Booking temporary guards for one-off weekend events, holiday parties, or last-minute emergency situations often incurs holiday premiums (time-and-a-half or double time) or rapid-deployment surcharges of 20 to 35 percent. Conversely, negotiating long-term recurring contracts (such as 24/7 year-round coverage for a residential condominium or warehouse facility) allows you to secure discounted volume rates.

Hiring Independent Freelance Guards vs. Licensed Security Agencies

Property owners occasionally attempt to lower costs by hiring independent freelance security guards directly from online classifieds, hoping to pay $18 to $25 per hour cash without agency markup. While this seems economical upfront, it exposes the hiring party to catastrophic financial and legal liability. If an independently hired guard is injured during an altercation on your premises, you can be held directly liable for medical bills and lost wages under employer liability laws.

Furthermore, if an armed independent guard unlawfully discharges a firearm, uses excessive physical force, or commits false imprisonment, you lack the protection of a multi-million-dollar corporate liability policy. Licensed security agencies carry comprehensive insurance umbrellas, conduct rigorous multi-state criminal background screenings and drug tests, and immediately dispatch replacement officers if an assigned guard calls in sick, guaranteeing uninterrupted post coverage.

How to Hire a Professional Security Guard in 5 Steps

Follow these five vital steps to assess threats, vet security firms, and establish an effective guard post order.

  1. Perform a Property Security Risk Assessment

    Identify specific vulnerabilities: cash handling, unruly crowds, dark perimeter fence lines, or late-night inventory deliveries.

  2. Decide Between Unarmed and Armed Personnel

    Choose unarmed guards for customer service and access control, or armed guards for high-risk assets and volatile environments.

  3. Request and Verify Licensing and Insurance

    Demand proof of current state security business licensing and an official Certificate of Insurance listing you as an additional insured.

  4. Establish Clear Written Post Orders

    Draft detailed instructions defining guard patrol routes, uniform attire, incident reporting procedures, and emergency contact chains.

  5. Conduct an On-Site Briefing on Day One

    Walk the premises with the security supervisor and officers to inspect blind spots, alarm panels, and emergency exit routes.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does an unarmed security guard cost per hour?

An unarmed security guard typically costs between $20 and $40 per hour through a licensed commercial security agency.

Q2: How much does an armed security guard cost per hour?

An armed security guard generally costs between $35 and $70 per hour, reflecting higher firearms licensing, training, and liability insurance.

Q3: Can a security guard make an arrest?

Private security guards generally possess citizen arrest powers, allowing them to detain individuals committing a felony or misdemeanor breach of peace until police arrive.

Q4: What is the minimum number of hours to hire a security guard?

Most professional security agencies enforce a minimum shift requirement of four to six consecutive hours per guard.

Q5: How much does it cost to hire an off-duty police officer?

Hiring an off-duty police officer typically costs between $65 and $120 per hour, often arranged through local police department secondary employment offices.

Q6: Do security guard rates increase on holidays?

Yes, hiring guards on recognized holidays such as New Year Eve or Thanksgiving usually incurs overtime rates of 1.5 to 2 times the standard rate.

Q7: What is a mobile patrol and how much does it cost?

A mobile patrol involves a security officer in a marked vehicle conducting periodic random drive-through inspections, costing $30 to $60 per visit or $400 to $1,200 monthly.

Q8: Who is liable if a security guard injures someone on my property?

If you hire a licensed, insured security agency, their commercial general liability insurance absorbs the primary liability, shielding your business.
