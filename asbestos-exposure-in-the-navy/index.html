Asbestos Exposure in the Navy

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

Asbestos exposure in the Navy represents one of the most significant occupational health crises in military history. Because every U.S. Navy surface ship and submarine constructed between the 1930s and the late 1970s utilized thousands of tons of asbestos insulation for fireproofing and thermal protection, Navy veterans represent approximately thirty percent of all malignant mesothelioma diagnoses in the United States.

The Pervasive Use of Asbestos on Naval Vessels

Following catastrophic shipboard fires during World War I and early naval engagements, the Bureau of Ships (BuShips) and military planners mandated the extensive incorporation of asbestos across all naval vessels. Combat ships operated in hostile environments with extreme fire hazards, high-pressure steam boilers reaching 850 degrees Fahrenheit, and massive propulsion turbines. To protect naval personnel and prevent catastrophic hull burns, Navy specifications required asbestos in virtually every compartment from stem to stern.

Naval architects specified chrysotile, amosite, and crocidolite minerals in boiler refractory linings, high-temperature steam pipe lagging, steam turbine blankets, pump packing glands, electrical switchboard panels, and acoustic soundproofing in mess halls and berthing quarters. In the cramped, poorly ventilated confines of below-deck engine rooms and boiler rooms (firerooms), sailors and shipyard machinists worked in confined spaces with minimal ventilation, breathing invisible clouds of pulverized fiber dust generated by engine vibration and artillery shockwaves.

Boiler Rooms / Firerooms High-pressure marine steam boilers Refractory brick, block lagging, boiler mud paste Severe (Continuous airborne fiber dust)

Berthing & Mess Decks Living quarters, bulkheads, decks Acoustic plaster, vinyl deck tiles, fire doors Moderate (Continuous ambient exposure)
Damage Control / Repair Shipboard maintenance shops Welding blankets, asbestos fire suits, packing rope High (Direct cutting and handling)

High-Risk Naval Ratings and Shipyard Overhaul Operations

While all sailors aboard older vessels experienced ambient fiber exposure, specific military occupational specialties (ratings) endured extreme daily contact. Boiler Technicians (BT) and Machinist's Mates (MM) experienced the highest documented exposures. During routine maintenance or catastrophic steam line repairs, these personnel ripped out dry, calcified insulation with bare hands and chisels, hand-mixed powdered asbestos cement in open buckets, and fabricated custom gaskets from compressed sheet stock in unventilated workshops.

Other heavily impacted naval rates included Pipefitters (FP), Hull Technicians (HT), Electrician’s Mates (EM), Damage Controlmen (DC), and Enginemen (EN). Exposure risks peaked during scheduled ship overhauls at naval shipyards like Brooklyn, Norfolk, Puget Sound, Boston, and Long Beach. During overhauls, hundreds of civilian yard workers and ship crew members tore out aging thermal systems simultaneously, creating a haze of toxic dust that engulfed entire dry docks and sleeping quarters.

Boiler Technician (BT) Operation & maintenance of marine boilers Scraping refractory brick & mixing boiler cement Highest lifetime mesothelioma rate
Machinist's Mate (MM) Turbine, pump, & valve maintenance Replacing steam flange gaskets & pump packings Severe incidence of asbestosis & mesothelioma
Pipefitter (FP) / Hull Tech (HT) Plumbing, pipe repairs, & hull welding Cutting thermal pipe lagging & welding blankets High incidence of lung cancer & pleural plaques
Electrician's Mate (EM) Shipboard wiring & electrical switchboards Handling arc-chute panels & cable insulation wrap Moderate to high respiratory disease rate

Veterans diagnosed with mesothelioma, asbestosis, or asbestos lung cancer qualify for 100% disability compensation from the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA). The VA recognizes that service aboard pre-1980 vessels or at naval shipyards constitutes presumptive proof of military toxic exposure when supported by service records.

In addition to VA disability benefits, veterans can file private civil claims and trust fund applications against the private manufacturers who supplied defective asbestos products to the Navy, securing millions in compensation without suing the military or government.

How Navy Veterans Can Access Asbestos Compensation

Step-by-step roadmap for filing VA disability claims and legal compensation.

  1. Gather Military Service Records (DD-214)

    Obtain your official service record, military discharge papers (DD-214), and ship assignment history detailing sea duty dates.

  2. Secure Certified Pathology Diagnosis

    Obtain official pathology biopsy reports and oncologist staging records confirming malignant mesothelioma or lung cancer.

  3. File a Fully Developed VA Disability Claim

    Submit a VA claim with an independent medical nexus letter linking your naval rating (such as BT or MM) to shipboard asbestos.

  4. Pursue Private Asbestos Trust Fund Claims

    Retain a specialized asbestos attorney to file claims against private equipment manufacturers who supplied the Navy with toxic products.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Why was asbestos used so heavily on Navy ships?

The Navy mandated asbestos on all ships for fireproofing, thermal insulation, and heat resistance on high-pressure boilers and steam engines.

Q2: What naval ratings had the highest asbestos exposure?

Boiler Technicians (BT), Machinist's Mates (MM), Pipefitters (FP), Hull Technicians (HT), and Electrician's Mates (EM) had the highest exposure.

Q3: Can Navy veterans get 100% VA disability for mesothelioma?

Yes, the VA rates malignant mesothelioma as a 100% service-connected disability, providing over $3,800 in monthly tax-free disability compensation.

Q4: Can veterans sue the Navy for asbestos exposure?

No, under the Feres Doctrine, veterans cannot sue the military. However, veterans can sue the private corporate manufacturers who sold the products.

Q5: What ships had the worst asbestos exposure?

Aircraft carriers, destroyers, cruisers, battleships, and submarines built prior to 1980 contained massive quantities of asbestos insulation.

Q6: How long after Navy service does mesothelioma appear?

Mesothelioma has an extended latency period, typically developing thirty to fifty years after initial military service exposure.

Q7: Does filing a legal claim affect VA disability benefits?

No, pursuing compensation from private manufacturers or bankruptcy trusts does not reduce or affect your VA monthly disability pension.

Q8: Can surviving spouses of Navy veterans receive benefits?

Yes, surviving spouses can receive VA Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC) and file wrongful death claims against product manufacturers.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Asbestos exposure in the Navy has left a devastating legacy among generations of brave sailors who served their country. By documenting service records, applying for expedited 100% VA disability pensions, and partnering with specialized toxic tort attorneys, Navy veterans and their surviving families secure the justice and financial care they rightfully deserve.
