Rhode Island Regulatory Oversight: The RIDOH Mandates

In the State of Rhode Island, all asbestos abatement activities operate under the jurisdiction of the Rhode Island Department of Health (RIDOH) pursuant to Title 216 of the Rhode Island Code of Regulations, Chapter 50, Subchapter 15, Part 1 (Asbestos Control). The regulation mandates that any project disturbing more than minor quantities of asbestos-containing material must be performed by a state-licensed asbestos abatement contractor utilizing certified supervisors and licensed abatement workers.

Before physical containment construction begins, contractors must submit an official Asbestos Project Notification (APN) to RIDOH accompanied by the statutory state filing fee. Unless an emergency hazard waiver is granted due to catastrophic structural collapse or burst piping, contractors must observe a mandatory ten-day administrative notification period before mobilizing equipment. Execution Inside the Containment Enclosure

During active abatement, licensed technicians erect multi-stage decontamination airlocks consisting of clean, shower, and equipment wash rooms. Continuous negative pressure is maintained using HEPA-equipped air filtration units calibrated to achieve a minimum of four air changes per hour while exhausting clean air to the outside atmosphere. Technicians mist all materials with surfactant-treated water to suppress airborne dust during manual extraction.

Following debris removal, technicians thoroughly clean all surfaces using wet wiping methods and HEPA vacuums, applying an elastomeric lock-down encapsulant to seal the structural substrate. Only after an independent RIDOH-certified air sampling technician collects aggressive clearance samples demonstrating airborne concentrations below 0.01 fibers per cubic centimeter can the plastic containment barriers be removed.

How to Coordinate and Execute Certified Asbestos Removal in Rhode Island

Follow these five professional steps to navigate RIDOH regulatory filings, contractor selection, and clearance certification in Rhode Island.

  1. Commission an Inspection by a RIDOH Certified Inspector

    Retain a licensed Rhode Island asbestos building inspector to collect bulk material samples and determine exact hazard locations.

  2. Select a Licensed RIDOH Abatement Contractor

    Verify that candidate remediation companies hold current contractor licenses issued by the Rhode Island Department of Health.

  3. File the Asbestos Project Notification with RIDOH

    Ensure the contractor submits the formal APN form and appropriate fee to the state ten business days prior to commencing physical work.

  4. Engage an Independent Air Clearance Consultant

    Hire an independent certified air monitoring firm directly to oversee negative pressure logs and execute final aggressive clearance sampling.

  5. Archive Clearance Certificates and Waste Manifests

    Obtain formal laboratory clearance reports and Johnston Central Landfill waste manifests for permanent property real estate records.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Which agency regulates asbestos removal across Rhode Island?

The Rhode Island Department of Health (RIDOH) oversees licensing, project notifications, inspection protocols, and clearance regulations statewide.

Q2: How far in advance must an asbestos project be filed with RIDOH?

Contractors must file an Asbestos Project Notification (APN) at least ten business days prior to beginning physical abatement work.

Q3: Can a homeowner in Rhode Island remove asbestos themselves?

RIDOH regulations strictly regulate friable material removal, and while limited owner exemptions exist, hiring licensed contractors is strongly urged.

Q4: Where is asbestos waste disposed of in Rhode Island?

Regulated asbestos waste must be hauled to the Rhode Island Resource Recovery Corporation (RIRRC) Central Landfill located in Johnston, RI.

Q5: What is an independent air clearance test under Rhode Island rules?

It is an aggressive air sampling procedure conducted by a separate licensed consultant not affiliated with the abatement contractor.

Q6: What is the legal clearance limit for asbestos air testing in RI?

Airborne fiber concentrations inside the containment must be verified below 0.01 fibers per cubic centimeter before barriers can be taken down.

Q7: Are historic Providence triple-deckers prone to asbestos?

Yes, historic multi-family triple-deckers frequently contain asbestos in basement gravity furnace pipe wrap, floor tiles, and siding.

Q8: How can I verify if a Rhode Island contractor is licensed?

You can verify contractor licenses directly through the Rhode Island Department of Health online licensing verification database.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Executing asbestos removal in Rhode Island demands a thorough understanding of state health regulations, meticulous contractor verification, and strict engineering controls.
