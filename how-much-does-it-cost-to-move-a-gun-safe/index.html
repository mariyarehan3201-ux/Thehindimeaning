How Much Does It Cost to Move a Gun Safe?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Gun safes are specifically engineered to resist forced entry, fire damage, and physical removal, making them among the heaviest and most cumbersome household items to relocate. If you need to move a gun safe across your house or to a new residence, professional safe moving services typically cost between $200 and $600 for straightforward ground-floor moves. When moves involve ascending or descending staircases, navigating tight spiral landings, or handling massive vaults weighing over 800 pounds, total moving fees range from $500 to $1,500 or more.

Safe Weight Classifications and Flat-Ground Moving Rates

The physical weight and cubic exterior dimensions of a gun safe represent the baseline pricing criteria used by certified safe riggers and specialty movers. Light to medium residential gun safes, typically housing 12 to 24 long guns and weighing between 250 and 500 pounds, can be moved across ground levels for $200 to $350 using heavy-duty four-wheel dollies, aluminum ramps, and protective neoprene floor runners.

As gun safe capacity increases to 36 to 64 guns, reinforced steel plate thickness and composite fireboard insulation push weights between 600 and 1,200 pounds. Moving safes in this heavy category on flat ground requires three to four trained technicians, steel rigging rollers, and heavy hydraulic toe jacks, commanding flat-ground rates between $350 and $700. Commercial vaults weighing over 1,500 pounds require dedicated commercial machinery moving equipment.

Staircase Surcharges, Tight Landings, and Motorized Equipment

Navigating staircases introduces severe physical complexity and danger, representing the single largest price escalator in gun safe moving quotes. Moving companies levy stair fees ranging from $75 to $200 per flight of stairs, or $10 to $25 per individual step. Safes weighing over 600 pounds cannot be safely maneuvered on stairs by manual manpower alone; companies deploy motorized electric stair-climbing hand trucks (such as Ultra Lift or Escalera machines) that mechanically hoist the safe one step at a time.

Physical architectural constraints also generate supplementary surcharges. If the safe must navigate 90-degree or 180-degree staircase landings, movers may need to hoist the safe vertically or temporarily remove banisters, door frames, or exterior railings. Furthermore, movers lay down interlocking composite Masonite boards or heavy rubber matting across hardwood floors and delicate tile thresholds to distribute concentrated point loads and prevent costly structural flooring fractures.

The Dangers of DIY Moving vs Bonded Specialty Movers

Attempting to move a heavy gun safe yourself with friends and a rental utility dolly is an extraordinarily hazardous undertaking. An 800-pound safe on a staircase carries tremendous kinetic energy; if momentum slips, the safe can crush limbs, fracture vertebrae, break through stair stringers, or crash through exterior walls. Professional safe moving companies carry commercial cargo insurance, general liability policies, and specialized worker compensation coverage that absorb all financial liabilities.

Beyond physical safety, professional riggers understand architectural load-bearing structural limitations. A half-ton safe exerts intense concentrated loads on residential floor joists. Professional movers can evaluate whether your destination floor requires supplemental structural post jacking in the basement or recommend placing the safe against a primary load-bearing foundation wall to eliminate long-term floor sag.

How to Prepare for Professional Gun Safe Moving in Five Steps

Follow these preparation steps to facilitate a smooth, safe, and efficient safe relocation process.

  1. Completely Empty All Firearms and Ammunition

    Remove all firearms, ammunition boxes, bullion, and documents; movers are prohibited by federal regulations from moving loaded safes.

  2. Measure All Doorways, Hallways, and Stairwells

    Measure width and height clearances along the entire moving route from the safe location to the delivery truck, including threshold heights.

  3. Identify Floor Types and Structural Subflooring

    Notify the moving company whether the safe will travel over hardwood, tile, carpet, or concrete to allow them to bring appropriate floor shields.

  4. Take Photographs of Staircases and Turning Landings

    Send high-resolution photos of stairs, landings, and tight doorway angles to the moving estimator to verify equipment clearances.

  5. Designate Final Anchoring Location at Destination

    Identify the exact corner and foundation placement where the safe will be leveled and bolted down to concrete footings.

Frequently Asked Questions (10 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can regular household movers move a gun safe?

Many standard residential moving companies refuse to move safes exceeding 400 pounds; hiring specialized safe movers or industrial machinery riggers is strongly recommended.

Q2: Do I have to empty my gun safe before movers arrive?

Yes, you must remove all firearms, ammunition, and valuables; moving an empty safe protects personal privacy, reduces weight, and complies with mover insurance policies.

Q3: Can a second-floor floor hold the weight of a heavy gun safe?

Safes weighing over 500 to 700 pounds should be placed near load-bearing exterior walls or reinforced with subfloor joist blocking to prevent structural sagging.

Q4: How much does it cost to move a gun safe across the country?

Long-distance safe transport typically costs between $800 and $2,500, billed by weight and mileage through freight carriers or specialized long-haul movers.

Q5: Can removing the safe door make it easier to move?

Yes, the door accounts for roughly 30% to 40% of the total safe weight; lifting the door off its exterior hinges significantly reduces moving weight and center of gravity.

Q6: How much does it cost to bolt down a gun safe?

Professional safe movers typically charge $75 to $150 to drill pilot holes into concrete slabs and secure the safe with heavy-duty steel wedge anchors.

Q7: Will moving a gun safe damage my hardwood floors?

Unprotected moving can crush wood fibers; professional movers lay down rigid 1/4-inch Masonite boards to distribute point loads across the flooring.

Q8: Can a gun safe be transported laying on its side or back?

Yes, safes can be transported on their side or back, provided the internal fireboard insulation and mechanical locking linkage are secure, though upright is preferred.

Q9: What is an electric stair-climbing dolly?

An electric stair-climbing dolly is a battery-powered motorized hand truck equipped with rotating rubber cams or tracks that mechanically climbs stairs while balancing heavy loads.

Q10: How much does it cost to dispose of an old gun safe?

Hauling away and disposing of an unwanted damaged safe typically costs between $200 and $450 depending on scrap metal weight and extraction difficulty.
