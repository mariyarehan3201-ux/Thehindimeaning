23154 a VC

Full Form
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

23154 a VC stands for California Vehicle Code Section 23154(a) VC. This statute establishes California's strict Zero Tolerance Law for motorists on active DUI probation, making it unlawful for anyone on probation for a prior driving under the influence conviction to operate a motor vehicle with a Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) of 0.01 percent or greater.

The Statutory Legal Definition of California Vehicle Code 23154(a) VC

When an individual is convicted of driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs in the State of California under California Vehicle Code Section 23152, the criminal court places the offender on formal or informal summary probation, typically lasting between three and five years. As a mandatory standard condition of DUI probation codified under state law, the probationer agrees to strict statutory restrictions. Foremost among these is California Vehicle Code Section 23154(a) VC.

Under California Vehicle Code § 23154(a) VC, it is unlawful for a person who is on probation for a violation of Section 23152 or Section 23153 to operate a motor vehicle at any time with a Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) of 0.01 percent or greater, as measured by a preliminary alcohol screening (PAS) test or other chemical test. This statute represents California's strict Zero Tolerance Law for motorists serving DUI probation sentences.

While the standard legal limit for adult non-commercial drivers in California is 0.08 percent BAC, a driver on DUI probation is held to an unforgiving 0.01 percent standard. A BAC of 0.01 percent is a microscopic threshold—consuming a single sip of beer, taking over-the-counter cough syrup containing alcohol, or using alcohol-based mouthwash minutes before driving can easily trigger a 0.01 percent reading. Mandatory PAS Chemical Testing and Refusal Penalties

One of the most vital procedural distinctions governing California Vehicle Code Section 23154(a) VC involves roadside Preliminary Alcohol Screening (PAS) breath testing. For standard, non-probationary drivers pulled over for suspected DUI, taking a roadside handheld PAS breathalyzer test prior to an arrest is completely voluntary. Drivers have a statutory right to politely decline roadside PAS testing without penalty (though evidentiary tests become mandatory after arrest).

For a driver on active DUI probation, however, this right does not exist. Under VC § 23154(b) and (c), an individual on DUI probation who is lawfully stopped by law enforcement is legally mandated to submit to a Preliminary Alcohol Screening (PAS) test or other chemical test upon the request of a peace officer. You cannot refuse.

If a probationer refuses to blow into the roadside PAS device, the refusal triggers severe administrative penalties administered by the California Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV). Under California's Implied Consent laws, refusing the PAS test results in an automatic, mandatory 1-year to 3-year driver's license suspension with zero eligibility for a restricted work license. The Dual Repercussions: Traffic Infraction vs. Probation Violation

Understanding the legal structure of a 23154(a) VC citation requires recognizing its dual legal nature. By statutory definition, a violation of Section 23154(a) VC is legally classified as a non-criminal traffic civil infraction, not a misdemeanor crime. On its face, the traffic ticket carries only a financial fine of approximately $250 plus penalty assessments.

However, the true legal catastrophe lies in the collateral consequences. Because you are on active DUI probation, an infraction citation under 23154(a) VC serves as definitive proof that you violated the express terms of your court-ordered probation (which mandated zero driving with any measurable alcohol). The arresting officer forwards the citation directly to the criminal court and county probation department.

The criminal court judge will issue a bench warrant or notice of a formal Motion to Revoke Probation. At a probation violation hearing, the standard of proof is not reasonable doubt—the prosecutor needs only a preponderance of the evidence. If the judge finds you violated probation, the court can revoke probation, impose jail time that was previously suspended from your original DUI conviction (typically 30 to 180 days in county jail), extend your probation for an additional two years, and order you to re-enroll in costly multi-month alcohol education programs.

How to Defend Against a 23154(a) VC Citation

Step-by-step legal defense protocol to challenge a California 0.01% BAC probation citation.

  1. Request an In-Person DMV Hearing Within 10 Days

    Contact the California DMV Driver Safety Branch within exactly 10 calendar days of your citation to demand an administrative hearing and freeze license suspension.

  2. Subpoena Handheld PAS Device Maintenance and Calibration Logs

    Have your defense lawyer subpoena Title 17 calibration logs for the roadside PAS device to prove inaccurate or erratic sensor readings.

  3. Investigate Mouth Alcohol and Non-Beverage Ingestion

    Document if mouthwash, asthma inhalers, acid reflux (GERD), or cold medication created residual oral alcohol mimicking a 0.01% BAC.

  4. Present Proof of Compliance at Criminal Probation Hearing

    Demonstrate ongoing compliance with all other probation terms (AA meetings, community service) to persuade the judge not to impose jail time.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does 23154 a VC mean in California?

It refers to California Vehicle Code Section 23154(a), which makes it illegal for anyone on DUI probation to drive with a BAC of 0.01% or greater.

Q2: Is 23154 a VC a misdemeanor or an infraction?

Section 23154(a) VC is classified as a traffic civil infraction, but it constitutes a direct violation of your underlying criminal DUI probation.

Q3: Can I refuse a breathalyzer if I am on DUI probation in California?

No. Under VC 23154, probationers must submit to an on-scene PAS test upon officer request; refusing triggers a mandatory 1-to-3-year license suspension.

Q4: Can mouthwash cause a 23154(a) VC violation?

Yes. Because 0.01% BAC is a microscopic threshold, alcohol-based mouthwashes can leave residual mouth alcohol that triggers a false reading on a PAS device.

Q5: Will I go to jail for violating 23154 a VC?

The infraction itself carries no jail, but the judge at your probation violation hearing can revoke probation and sentence you to up to 180 days in county jail.

Q6: How long is my license suspended for a 23154(a) VC violation?

The California DMV will administratively suspend your driver's license for a minimum of one full year with zero restricted driving privileges.

Q7: How long do I have to request a DMV hearing after a 23154 VC citation?

You have strictly 10 calendar days from the date of the traffic stop to contact the California DMV Driver Safety Office and schedule an administrative hearing.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

California Vehicle Code Section 23154(a) VC enforces zero tolerance for motorists serving DUI probation, making any measurable alcohol reading of 0.01 percent or greater a devastating violation. While classified technically as a traffic infraction, it triggers mandatory one-year DMV license suspensions and severe criminal probation revocation hearings carrying real jail time. If cited under 23154(a) VC, contact an experienced California DUI defense attorney immediately to contest the PAS calibration and defend your freedom.
