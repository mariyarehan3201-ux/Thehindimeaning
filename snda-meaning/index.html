Defining the SNDA: The Three-Party Commercial Real Estate Agreement

In commercial real estate law, mortgage banking, and commercial leasing, SNDA is an essential acronym that stands for Subordination, Non-Disturbance, and Attornment Agreement. It is a legally binding three-party contract executed between a commercial tenant, a commercial property landlord, and the landlord mortgage lender.

An SNDA governs the complex legal relationship between a commercial tenant and a mortgage lender who holds a security deed of trust against the leased commercial building. Without an SNDA, the legal doctrine of 'first in time, first in right' applies. If a landlord defaults on their commercial mortgage and the bank forecloses, state real estate law grants the lender the automatic legal right to terminate all commercial leases created after the mortgage was recorded, wiping out tenant retail leases and multi-million-dollar restaurant build-outs overnight.

Review the three distinct contractual pillars of an SNDA agreement:

SNDA Contractual Pillar Party Providing the Covenant Primary Legal Mechanism Commercial Protection Conferred
Subordination The Commercial Tenant Tenant agrees their leasehold interest ranks junior (subordinate) to the mortgage lien Protects the lender mortgage priority over pre-existing tenant leases
Non-Disturbance The Mortgage Lender Lender covenants never to evict tenant or disturb lease in event of landlord foreclosure Protects the tenant business operations, leasehold term, and capital investments
Attornment The Commercial Tenant Tenant agrees to legally recognize (attorn to) the lender as their new landlord after foreclosure Protects the lender rental income stream and preserves building occupancy

Why Commercial Tenants Must Demand the Non-Disturbance Clause

To understand why an SNDA is vital, consider a high-end restaurant or medical clinic investing $2 million into specialized interior build-outs inside a commercial shopping plaza. If the landlord business fails and the bank forecloses on the shopping center, the bank could wipe out the restaurant 10-year lease, kicking them out to sell the vacant building to a redevelopment developer.

The Non-Disturbance clause is the tenant ultimate shield. Under this covenant, the mortgage lender legally promises: as long as the tenant continues paying monthly rent and is not in material default under the lease, the lender will never disturb the tenant possession or terminate the lease, even if the bank completely forecloses on the property and evicts the original landlord. In exchange, the lender receives the Subordination and Attornment covenants, ensuring uninterrupted cash flow.

Compare the legal realities of commercial leasing with vs without an SNDA agreement:

Foreclosure Scenario Commercial Lease WITHOUT an SNDA Commercial Lease WITH an SNDA Agreement
Landlord Mortgage Foreclosure Lender can legally extinguish and terminate junior lease immediately Lender bound by Non-Disturbance; lease remains 100% active
Tenant Multi-Million Buildout Tenant evicted; loses all unamortized interior improvements Tenant remains in possession; protects interior capital investment
Rent Payment to New Owner Tenant can walk away from lease without penalty if wiped out Tenant legally 'attorns' to lender; continues paying monthly rent
Lender Foreclosure Auction Vacant building may command lower auction value Occupied building preserves cash flow and commercial capitalization rate

Executing a comprehensive SNDA ensures that commercial tenants and lenders co-exist in harmonious financial security throughout economic downturns.

How Commercial Tenants Negotiate and Execute an SNDA

Follow this legal roadmap when leasing commercial retail, office, or industrial space.

  1. Make SNDA Execution a Mandatory Lease Condition

    During initial letter of intent (LOI) negotiations, require a clause stating the lease is contingent upon delivering a signed SNDA from the landlord lender.

  2. Review the Lender Standard SNDA Form

    Obtain the lender standard agreement; have a commercial real estate attorney inspect carve-outs and liability caps.

  3. Ensure Tenant Offset and Abatement Rights Survive

    Negotiate language ensuring that rent abatement rights (such as free rent periods or casualty repair offsets) remain binding on the lender.

  4. Verify Security Deposit and Tenant Improvement Obligations

    Clarify whether the foreclosing lender will assume responsibility for returning the tenant security deposit and funding tenant improvement allowances.

  5. Record the SNDA in County Public Land Records

    Sign the SNDA before a notary public and have the title company record it in the county land records to provide constructive legal notice to all future buyers.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does SNDA stand for in real estate?

SNDA stands for Subordination, Non-Disturbance, and Attornment Agreement, a three-party contract between a commercial tenant, landlord, and lender.

Q2: Why is an SNDA important for a tenant?

The Non-Disturbance clause guarantees that if the landlord goes bankrupt or faces foreclosure, the bank cannot evict the tenant or terminate their lease.

Q3: What does subordination mean in an SNDA?

Subordination means the tenant agrees their lease rights are junior to the lender mortgage lien on the commercial property.

Q4: What does attornment mean in an SNDA?

Attornment means the tenant agrees to automatically recognize the lender (or foreclosure buyer) as their new landlord without interrupting rent payments.

Q5: Who benefits the most from an SNDA?

Both tenant and lender benefit: the tenant secures uninterrupted business occupancy, while the lender secures ongoing rental cash flow upon foreclosure.

Q6: What happens if a commercial lease has no SNDA?

If the landlord is foreclosed on, the mortgage lender has the legal right to extinguish the lease and evict the tenant under state real estate laws.

Q7: Is an SNDA used in residential leases?

No. SNDAs are used almost exclusively in commercial real estate; residential tenants are protected by federal and state tenant foreclosure protection laws.

Q8: Does an SNDA need to be notarized and recorded?

Yes. SNDAs are formal real property contracts that are typically notarized and recorded in county land deed records.
