What Does Tendered to Delivery Service Provider Mean?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

In multi-tier logistics, domestic parcel forwarding, and e-commerce supply chains, the tracking status 'Tendered to Delivery Service Provider' (or 'Tendered to Partner Carrier') indicates that your primary long-haul shipping consolidator has physically transferred custody of your package to a designated regional last-mile delivery partner—most commonly the United States Postal Service (USPS) or a local contract courier service—who will execute the final residential delivery to your mailbox or doorstep. Understanding how partner handoffs work helps online shoppers avoid tracking confusion, track down delayed mail, and communicate effectively with postal station customer service teams when packages appear stalled between transit carriers.

The Hybrid Shipping Business Model Explained

To provide low-cost shipping options for high-volume retailers, global parcel carriers utilize hybrid logistics networks. Under services like UPS SurePost, FedEx Ground Economy (formerly SmartPost), and DHL eCommerce, the private commercial carrier manages the initial pickup, cross-country freight transportation, and regional airport sortation across intermediate sorting hubs.

However, instead of deploying their own private fleet drivers to visit every rural or residential address, the consolidator tenders bulk containerized parcels to the local USPS destination postal sort facility (such as a Sectional Center Facility or Destination Delivery Unit). Once transferred, the parcel receives a tender scan, signifying that legal and operational custody has passed to the partner carrier. Additionally, destination delivery units (DDUs) receive palletized shipments during early pre-dawn sort shifts, staging packages for route carriers who walk neighborhood beats or drive rural delivery routes six days a week.

Tracking Transitions and Dual Barcode Systems

A frequent source of customer confusion during a tender handoff is tracking visibility. Hybrid packages are labeled with dual tracking barcodes: one for the initial consolidator and a secondary Postal Service Delivery Confirmation barcode. Once tendered, tracking updates on the primary consolidator's website may pause or show 'Delivered to USPS,' while the USPS tracking portal updates to 'Accepted at Destination Facility.'

Consumers should copy the partner carrier tracking number displayed on the initial tracking page and monitor the partner's direct portal for granular real-time delivery estimates.

How to Track a Package Tendered to a Delivery Service Provider

Step-by-step instructions for monitoring shipments transferred across partner carrier networks.

  1. Locate the Secondary Partner Tracking Number

    Look near the top or bottom of the primary carrier tracking page (UPS/FedEx/DHL) for an alternate tracking number, often labeled 'Postal Service Tracking ID.'

  2. Enter the ID into the USPS or Partner Portal

    Visit the official USPS.com tracking tool and paste the 20-to-34-digit barcode number to access direct postal route updates.

  3. Allow 24 to 48 Hours for Partner Intake Scans

    Understand that after bulk container handoff, local postal workers require 12 to 24 hours to scan individual parcel barcodes into the sorting system.

  4. Check Your Residential Mailbox First

    Because USPS handles the final mile of tendered shipments, couriers often place packages directly inside residential mailboxes rather than front porches.

  5. Contact Your Local Post Office if Stalled Past 72 Hours

    If tracking shows tendered but USPS has not scanned the item after 3 business days, call your local post station with the postal tracking number.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does tendered to delivery service provider mean?

It means the primary shipping company (like UPS, FedEx, or DHL) has handed over your package to a local partner—usually USPS—to complete the final delivery to your door.

Q2: Who will deliver my package after it is tendered?

In the United States, the vast majority of tendered packages are delivered by your regular United States Postal Service (USPS) mail carrier.

Q3: Why do shipping carriers tender packages to USPS?

USPS already visits every residential address and mailbox six days a week. Handing packages to USPS saves fuel, reduces delivery costs, and lowers shipping fees for retailers.

Q4: How long after being tendered will my package arrive?

Packages are typically delivered within 1 to 3 business days following the tender scan, depending on local post office processing speed.

Q5: Why does tracking say delivered when it was just tendered?

Some shipping portals mark 'Delivered' to signify delivery to the partner carrier, not your home. Look for the secondary USPS tracking number for actual home delivery progress.

Q6: Can I pick up my package directly from the transfer facility?

No. Packages are transferred in bulk sealed postal pallets and cannot be sorted individually for customer walk-in pickup at transfer depots.

Q7: What should I do if tracking stops updating after being tendered?

Wait 48 hours for the local post office to scan the package into their local manifest. If no scan appears after 4 days, file a missing mail search on USPS.com.

Q8: Does tendered mean the package is lost if there is no scan for 3 days?

No, a 3-day pause often reflects container staging at high-volume postal processing hubs. Packages move through bulk sorting bins before individual barcode scans register on tracking websites.
