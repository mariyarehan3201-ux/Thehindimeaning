Do You Need a License to Be a Medical Assistant?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 12, 2026

The healthcare sector continues to experience strong demand for skilled clinical support professionals, making medical assisting an attractive entry point into clinical medicine. Prospective students frequently ask whether state licensing is legally required to work as a medical assistant. In the vast majority of US states, medical assistants do not require a government-issued professional license; however, employers overwhelmingly require professional industry certification before hiring.

Licensure Versus Certification in Medical Assisting

In healthcare terminology, there is a fundamental legal distinction between licensure and certification. Licensure is a mandatory legal credential granted by a state government licensing board granting statutory authority to practice a regulated medical profession (such as registered nursing or medicine). Only a tiny handful of states—such as Washington State, which requires medical assistants to register and certify through the Department of Health—enforce formal state-level licensing.

In contrast, certification is a voluntary professional credential issued by accredited private certifying agencies that validate clinical and administrative competency. Because medical assistants operate as unlicensed assistive personnel under the direct supervision and delegated authority of a licensed physician, physician assistant, or nurse practitioner, the law permits clinics to hire uncertified assistants. However, healthcare employers virtually always mandate national certification.

Major National Certifications: CMA, RMA, CCMA, and NCMA

To qualify for competitive hospital and clinic positions, candidates pursue credentials from recognized credentialing bodies. The two most prestigious and universally recognized credentials are the Certified Medical Assistant (CMA), administered by the American Association of Medical Assistants (AAMA), and the Registered Medical Assistant (RMA), conferred by American Medical Technologists (AMT). Both credentials require graduation from a program accredited by CAAHEP or ABHES.

Two other widely accepted certifications are the Certified Clinical Medical Assistant (CCMA), issued by the National Healthcareer Association (NHA), and the National Certified Medical Assistant (NCMA), administered by the National Center for Competency Testing (NCCT). While curriculum prerequisites for NHA and NCCT exams are often more flexible—accommodating vocational diplomas and approved work apprenticeships—all four certifications demonstrate rigorous competency in phlebotomy, EKG administration, pharmacology, and patient triage.

Insurance Billing, Meaningful Use, and Career Advancement

A primary reason hospital networks and physician groups insist on hiring certified medical assistants is compliance with federal healthcare regulations and insurance reimbursement standards. Under Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) guidelines for Electronic Health Record (EHR) incentive programs, only 'credentialed medical assistants' or licensed healthcare professionals are legally authorized to enter computerized provider orders (CPOE) into medical charts.

Furthermore, certification directly influences earning potential and professional mobility. Certified medical assistants consistently command higher hourly wages, receive priority consideration for clinic supervisor roles, and qualify for employer tuition assistance. Additionally, clinical experience gained as a CMA serves as an invaluable clinical foundation for those later pursuing registered nursing (RN), physician assistant (PA) studies, or healthcare administration.

How to Become a Certified Medical Assistant in 5 Steps

Follow these five strategic steps to obtain medical assisting education, earn national certification, and secure clinical employment.

  1. Complete a High School Diploma or GED

    Ensure you possess a high school diploma or equivalent, which is mandatory for admission to accredited vocational and collegiate MA programs.

  2. Enroll in an Accredited Medical Assistant Program

    Select a CAAHEP or ABHES-accredited diploma, certificate, or associate degree program with hands-on lab training.

  3. Complete an In-Person Clinical Externship

    Perform 160 or more hours of unpaid hands-on clinical training at a regional hospital or outpatient medical practice.

  4. Pass a National Certification Exam (CMA or RMA)

    Register for and successfully pass the CMA (AAMA), RMA (AMT), or CCMA (NHA) examination upon graduation.

  5. Apply to Regional Healthcare Employers

    Highlight your clinical certification, EKG, phlebotomy, and EHR competencies when applying for ambulatory clinic roles.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is a medical assistant license required in every state?

No, most states do not license medical assistants. Washington State is one of the few exceptions requiring formal state registration and credentials.

Q2: Can you be a medical assistant without certification?

Legally yes in most states, but the vast majority of hospital networks and clinics require national certification (CMA or RMA) as a hiring condition.

Q3: How long does it take to become a medical assistant?

Certificate and diploma programs typically take 9 to 12 months, while an Associate of Applied Science (AAS) degree requires two full years.

Q4: Can a medical assistant give injections and draw blood?

Yes, under the delegation and supervision of a licensed physician, trained medical assistants routinely perform intramuscular injections and phlebotomy.

Q5: What is the difference between CMA and RMA?

Both are prestigious national credentials. The CMA is awarded by the AAMA, while the RMA is awarded by AMT; both are widely respected by employers.

Q6: Do medical assistant certifications expire?

Yes, CMA credentials must be recertified every 60 months through continuing education units (CEUs) or re-examination, while RMA requires annual renewal.

Q7: Can medical assistants work in emergency rooms?

Yes, medical assistants work as ER technicians assisting trauma teams, recording vital signs, performing EKGs, and transporting patients.

Q8: Can I become a medical assistant online?

Hybrid programs offer lecture classes online, but accredited programs always mandate in-person clinical laboratories and supervised on-site externships.
