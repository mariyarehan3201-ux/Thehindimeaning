How to Determine Who Owns a Private Road?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Determining who owns a private road is an essential legal inquiry for rural property buyers, adjoining landowners, and local utility providers. Unlike public streets funded by municipal tax dollars and maintained by city public works departments, private roads reside on privately owned real estate. Ownership may belong to a single adjacent homeowner, an incorporated Homeowners Association, or multiple parcel owners holding undivided shared interests governed by recorded access easements and maintenance covenants.

The Complex Legal Landscape of Private Road Ownership

Private roads present unique legal and financial considerations. While a road may appear open to traffic, its underlying real property is owned by private entities who are solely responsible for snow removal, pothole repair, resurfacing, and liability insurance. Establishing ownership is critical when road maintenance disputes erupt, when applying for residential mortgages (such as Fannie Mae or FHA loans that mandate formal road maintenance agreements), or when resolving boundary encroachments.

In property law, ownership of the physical land (the fee simple estate) is frequently separated from the legal right to travel across it (an easement). A road may cross Parcel A owned entirely by one homeowner, yet Parcels B, C, and D may possess express appurtenant easements granting permanent ingress and egress rights. Alternatively, a developer may have deeded the road tract to an association, or ownership may extend to the road centerline across all abutting properties.

Step-by-Step Title and County Records Investigation

Uncovering the true owner of a private road requires systematic investigation across municipal and county government archives. The process begins with the County Assessor GIS (Geographic Information System) mapping portal. By searching your address and panning to the roadway, you can see whether the road exists as a distinct, numbered tax parcel with an assigned owner or if adjacent property lot lines extend directly into the middle of the road.

If the GIS map shows an independent parcel number, consult the County Tax Collector records to identify who pays real property taxes on that strip of land. Next, visit the County Recorder of Deeds (or Register of Deeds) to examine historical subdivision plats. Plat maps illustrate the original developer intentions, showing whether the road was formally offered for public dedication (and whether the municipality rejected or accepted that dedication) or designated as a permanent private right-of-way.

Maintenance Liabilities and Mortgage Underwriting Hurdles

Identifying road ownership is inextricably linked to maintenance obligations. When snow falls or catastrophic storm run-off washes out a culvert, municipal road graders will not enter a private road. In states governed by common law without specific private road statutes, every user of an easement has an implied duty to contribute proportionally to its maintenance, but enforcing this without a recorded agreement often requires contentious court litigation.

Furthermore, major mortgage buyers such as Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and the VA strictly regulate lending on private roads. In most circumstances, lenders require a recorded Road Maintenance Agreement (RMA) signed by all participating landowners that defines how repairs are funded, how votes are tallied, and how emergency repairs are initiated. If title research reveals that road ownership is nebulous or lacks a recorded maintenance agreement, hiring a real estate attorney to draft an RMA is essential before buying or selling property.

How to Research and Verify Private Road Ownership

A step-by-step workflow for discovering legal ownership of a private roadway.

  1. Consult the County Assessor GIS Mapping Portal

    Search the road location on your county GIS map to verify whether the road is a dedicated tax parcel or split across adjacent lot lines.

  2. Review the Property Tax Collector Records

    If a distinct parcel number exists for the road, search tax records to identify the registered taxpayer and current tax billing address.

  3. Examine Historical Recorded Subdivision Plats

    Pull the original subdivision plat map from the County Recorder of Deeds to inspect road dedication language and easement widths.

  4. Inspect Adjacent Property Warranty Deeds

    Review the metes-and-bounds legal descriptions on surrounding deeds to determine if ownership extends to the road centerline.

  5. Order a Professional Title Search or Legal Opinion

    Retain a licensed title company or real estate attorney to run a comprehensive chain-of-title search for unrecorded easements.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can the public drive on a private road?

Generally no, a private road is private property restricted to owners and authorized visitors, though an owner must post clear private property signs to prevent prescriptive public claims.

Q2: Does the city pave or plow private roads?

No, municipal public works departments do not pave, grade, or plow private roads because public tax funds cannot be legally expended on private real estate.

Q3: What is an appurtenant access easement?

An appurtenant easement is a permanent legal right attached to a parcel of land that allows its owner to travel across an adjacent private road to reach public highways.

Q4: What happens if a private road owner refuses to maintain it?

If a recorded road maintenance agreement exists, neighbors can enforce its terms in civil court; without an agreement, state common law equitable contribution principles apply.

Q5: Can I get a mortgage on a home located on a private road?

Yes, but conventional and government-backed lenders (Fannie Mae, VA, FHA) typically require a legally recorded Road Maintenance Agreement signed by participating homeowners.

Q6: What does it mean if a road was dedicated but not accepted?

A developer may have offered the road to the municipality, but if the local council never formally accepted maintenance responsibility, the road remains private.

Q7: Who is legally liable if an accident occurs on a private road?

The fee simple owner or the governing Homeowners Association can be held liable for injuries caused by neglected road hazards unless comprehensive liability insurance is maintained.

Q8: How do adjacent owners divide private road maintenance costs?

Costs are typically divided equally among lots, or allocated proportionally based on road frontage or the distance each homeowner travels along the road.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Unraveling the ownership of a private road requires methodical research through county land records, GIS parcel layers, and title abstracts. Knowing who holds fee simple title and understanding the specific easements governing access ensures that property rights are protected, maintenance costs are equitably shared, and future real estate transactions proceed smoothly.
