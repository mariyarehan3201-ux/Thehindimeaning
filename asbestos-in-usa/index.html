Asbestos in the USA: History & Laws

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 23, 2026

The history, regulation, and modern legal status of asbestos in the USA represents one of the most complex chapters in American public health, industrial capitalism, and environmental jurisprudence. Once celebrated as an indispensable miracle mineral that powered 20th-century American industry.

Asbestos was woven into naval warships, sprayed onto steel skyscrapers, and blended into millions of suburban homes. However, this massive commercial deployment came at an unprecedented human cost, claiming hundreds of thousands of lives from preventable occupational illnesses and generating the longest-running corporate tort litigation in American legal history.

Today, widespread confusion exists regarding whether asbestos is genuinely banned in the United States. While many European nations and developed democracies enacted total asbestos bans in the 1990s and early 2000s, the United States maintained a patchwork of partial regulatory restrictions for decades. Only with the EPA's landmark March 2024 final rule under the Toxic Substances Control Act (TSCA) did the nation finally close the chapter on the ongoing commercial import and use of raw chrysotile asbestos.

Historical Timeline of Asbestos in the United States

The arc of asbestos in America traces a century of rapid industrial adoption, clinical discovery of disease, regulatory battles, and judicial landmark decisions. The table below delineates these critical historical eras.

Historical Era Key Regulatory / Industrial Event Impact on American Industry Public Health Significance
1940 - 1970 (Industrial Peak) WWII naval expansion & post-war housing boom Over 30 million tons of raw asbestos consumed across US Hundreds of thousands of workers exposed without warnings
1973 - 1978 (Initial EPA/CPSC Bans) Clean Air Act bans spray fireproofing; CPSC bans patching Halted spray-applied acoustic popcorn & structural fireproofing First federal limits on consumer product exposures
1986 (Enactment of AHERA) Asbestos Hazard Emergency Response Act Mandated inspections & management plans in all US schools Established national standards for building inspectors
1989 - 1991 (Overturned 1989 Ban) EPA Asbestos Ban and Phase-Out Rule (ABPR) Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals overturns total ban in 1991 Legal blow allowing continued import in chlor-alkali & brakes
2024 (Landmark Final TSCA Ban) EPA final rule banning chrysotile asbestos under TSCA Comprehensive prohibition on raw chrysotile import and use Complete phaseout of commercial chrysotile in the United States

The most consequential turning point in American asbestos history occurred in 1991. In 1989, under the Toxic Substances Control Act, the EPA issued the Asbestos Ban and Phase-Out Rule, which sought to prohibit 94% of all asbestos manufacturing and distribution over a seven-year timeline. However, the asbestos industry filed suit in Corrosion Proof Fittings v. EPA. In 1991, the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals overturned the ban on procedural grounds, ruling that the EPA failed to demonstrate the ban was the least burdensome regulation under the statute.

As a result of this 1991 judicial ruling, asbestos remained legal in the United States for dozens of specialized commercial uses for over thirty years. Companies continued importing raw chrysotile for use in chlor-alkali chemical diaphragm cells to produce chlorine and bleach, as well as importing aftermarket automotive brake pads, oilfield gaskets, and sheet gasketing.

The Landmark 2024 EPA TSCA Chrysotile Prohibition

In March 2024, utilizing newly strengthened regulatory authorities under the 2016 Frank R. Lautenberg Chemical Safety for the 21st Century Act, the EPA finalized a comprehensive rule banning chrysotile asbestos. The table below outlines the phaseout timelines established by this landmark rule.

Commercial Industrial Sector Scope of Historical Use EPA TSCA Phaseout Compliance Timeline
Chlor-Alkali Chemical Industry Chrysotile diaphragm cells producing chlorine & caustic soda Phased transition: immediate ban on imports; 5 to 12 year facility conversion
Sheet Gaskets for Chemical Processing Compressed chrysotile sheet gaskets in chemical plants 2-year total phaseout; transition to non-asbestos materials
Oilfield Brake Blocks & Rig Linings Friction linings on heavy petroleum drilling rigs 6-month phaseout; immediate transition to synthetic friction pads
Aftermarket Automotive Brakes & Linings Imported commercial friction brake pads & linings 6-month ban on commercial importation and distribution
Commercial Sheet Gaskets (Nuclear) Specialized gaskets in nuclear power generation plants 5-year phaseout period to allow safety qualification of substitutes

The 2024 TSCA rule represents the first comprehensive chemical prohibition enacted under the modernized Lautenberg Act. By banning the ongoing import and processing of chrysotile—the only form of asbestos imported into the United States in recent decades—the rule effectively eliminates the active commercial use of raw asbestos in America.

However, the 2024 ban applies to ongoing commercial manufacture and importation. It does not—and cannot—remove the millions of tons of legacy asbestos already embedded in older American infrastructure. Pre-1990 homes, schools, hospitals, commercial office towers, and underground municipal utility mains remain massive reservoirs of legacy asbestos that must be managed through continuous Operations and Maintenance programs and certified environmental abatement.

How Property Owners Navigate Legacy Asbestos in the USA

Standard roadmap for property owners and managers handling legacy asbestos under current US law.

  1. Determine Era of Construction

    Check building permits and deeds. Structures built prior to 1990 must be treated as potentially containing regulated asbestos materials.

  2. Commission EPA AHERA Accredited Surveys

    Retain independent state-licensed inspectors to survey building components prior to initiating any renovation, repair, or demolition.

  3. Implement an Operations & Maintenance (O&M) Plan

    If asbestos materials are intact, author an O&M plan to monitor material condition periodically and restrict maintenance access.

  4. Adhere to Federal NESHAP Demolition Standards

    File mandatory 10-day state/county air quality notifications before performing demolition or major renovations exceeding 160 sq ft.

  5. Hire Exclusively Licensed Abatement Firms

    Contract licensed environmental remediation firms to remove hazardous materials under negative-air containment and verified air clearance.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is asbestos completely banned in the United States?

In March 2024, the EPA finalized a landmark rule banning the ongoing import and use of chrysotile asbestos. However, millions of tons of legacy asbestos remain embedded in older homes and buildings.

Q2: Why wasn't asbestos banned earlier in the USA?

The EPA attempted a total ban in 1989, but a federal appeals court overturned the rule in 1991 in Corrosion Proof Fittings v. EPA, ruling the agency did not use the least burdensome regulation.

Q3: What was the last commercial industry using asbestos in the US?

The chlor-alkali chemical manufacturing industry was the primary remaining user, utilizing imported raw chrysotile in diaphragm cells to produce chlorine and caustic soda until the 2024 ban.

Q4: When did the last asbestos mine close in the USA?

The last commercial asbestos mine in the United States, the KCAC Mine in San Benito County, California, permanently closed in 2002.

Q5: What year of home construction in the US is safe from asbestos?

Homes built after 1990 are generally considered safe from asbestos, though older pre-ban drywall compounds and adhesives were occasionally installed into the mid-1980s.

Q6: What federal agency regulates asbestos in the workplace?

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) enforces worker exposure limits and containment standards under 29 CFR 1926.1101 and 29 CFR 1910.1001.

Q7: Can I sell a house in the USA that contains asbestos?

Yes. You can legally sell a home containing asbestos, but state real estate disclosure laws mandate disclosing all known asbestos materials to prospective buyers in writing.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Asbestos in the USA has transitioned from an era of unchecked industrial exploitation to decades of regulatory gridlock, culminating in the historic 2024 EPA comprehensive ban under TSCA. While raw asbestos is no longer imported for manufacturing, the legacy of asbestos remains an everyday reality for millions of Americans living and working in vintage architecture. Continued vigilance, professional accredited testing, and strict occupational safety protocols remain vital for managing America's built environment.
