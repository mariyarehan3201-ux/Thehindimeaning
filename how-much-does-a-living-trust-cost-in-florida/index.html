How Much Does a Living Trust Cost in Florida?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a living trust cost in Florida requires examining attorney qualifications, Florida's stringent homestead laws, and estate complexity. On average, a comprehensive revocable living trust package prepared by a Florida estate planning attorney costs between $1,500 and $3,500 for an individual, and $2,000 to $5,000 for a married couple. High-net-worth plans involving irrevocable trusts or multi-generational dynasty planning range from $5,000 to $12,000+.

Estate Planning Attorney Rates Across Florida Regions

Legal fees for living trusts fluctuate based on regional overhead and attorney credentials. In South Florida (Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach counties) and affluent coastal hubs like Naples and Sarasota, estate planning attorneys bill between $350 and $650 per hour. Flat-fee packages for living trusts in these regions typically range from $2,500 to $5,500. Attorneys board-certified in Wills, Trusts, and Estates by The Florida Bar command top-tier pricing.

In Central and North Florida—including Orlando, Tampa, St. Petersburg, Jacksonville, and Tallahassee—rates are slightly more affordable, typically averaging $1,500 to $3,200 for individuals and $2,000 to $4,200 for couples. Online DIY software kits cost $100 to $300, but using automated forms in Florida carries severe risks due to the state's complex constitutional homestead rules and strict probate code.

Florida Homestead Protection and Lady Bird Deeds

A unique legal consideration in Florida estate planning is Article X, Section 4 of the Florida Constitution, which provides virtually unlimited asset protection for primary residences (homestead property) against unsecured creditors. However, Florida law restricts how homestead property can be devised if the owner is survived by a spouse or minor child. If a living trust is drafted improperly, conveying homestead real estate into the trust can inadvertently forfeit homestead tax exemptions or violate constitutional descent rules.

To navigate these complexities, Florida attorneys frequently utilize an Enhanced Life Estate Deed, widely known as a 'Lady Bird Deed.' A Lady Bird deed allows a homeowner to maintain total control of their property during life—including the right to sell, mortgage, or lease it without beneficiary permission—while automatically transferring title to the trust or designated beneficiaries upon death outside of probate, avoiding doc stamps and saving thousands in fees.

The True Financial Value: Bypassing Costly Florida Probate

The primary motivation for establishing a living trust in Florida is avoiding the time and expense of formal probate administration in Circuit Court. Florida probate is governed by Florida Statute Section 733.6171, which establishes statutory attorney fees presumed 'reasonable' for formal administration. Under this statutory schedule, the attorney is entitled to 3% of the first $1 million of the estate, 2.5% of the next $2 million, and 2% on amounts up to $5 million.

Under this statutory formula, probating a $500,000 estate incurs $15,000 in attorney fees alone, plus $400 in court filing fees and hundreds more in publication and accounting expenses. Furthermore, Florida formal probate takes 9 to 18 months, during which family assets remain tied up in court. Spending $2,500 on a living trust eliminates statutory probate fees entirely, saving families tens of thousands of dollars.

How to Establish a Revocable Living Trust in Florida

Follow these five critical steps to create and fund a valid Florida living trust.

  1. Assemble an Asset and Account Ledger

    Document all Florida real estate, brokerage portfolios, bank deposits, vehicles, and business LLC interests.

  2. Consult a Florida Estate Planning Attorney

    Hire an attorney familiar with Florida homestead rules, elective share laws, and Lady Bird deeds to customize your trust agreement.

  3. Execute the Trust Agreement with Proper Formalities

    Sign your trust in the presence of two independent adult witnesses and an authorized Florida notary public.

  4. Record Lady Bird or Warranty Deeds for Real Estate

    Record your deed with the County Clerk of the Circuit Court to officially transfer real property into trust ownership.

  5. Re-Title Bank Accounts and Non-Retirement Portfolios

    Present a Certificate of Trust to financial institutions to re-title accounts and designate successor beneficiaries.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a living trust cost in Florida?

A comprehensive living trust package prepared by a Florida attorney averages $1,500 to $3,500 for individuals and $2,000 to $5,000 for couples.

Q2: Why do you need a living trust in Florida?

To avoid costly, public, and lengthy Florida probate court proceedings (which legally cost 3% of the gross estate in statutory attorney fees).

Q3: What is a Lady Bird deed in Florida?

An Enhanced Life Estate Deed that transfers real estate to beneficiaries or a trust upon death without losing lifetime control or triggering doc stamp taxes.

Q4: Can a living trust hold Florida homestead property?

Yes, but it must be drafted meticulously to preserve constitutional homestead tax exemptions and adhere to spousal/minor descent laws.

Q5: Does a living trust protect assets from Florida nursing homes?

No, a revocable trust is an available resource for Medicaid; long-term care Medicaid planning requires specialized irrevocable trusts.

Q6: Does a living trust avoid Florida probate completely?

Yes, provided all assets and real estate are properly re-titled into the trust's name prior to the grantor's death.

Q7: Who should be the trustee of my Florida living trust?

Most grantors act as their own primary trustee during life, appointing adult children, a trusted relative, or a trust company as successor trustee.

Q8: How much are court filing fees for probate in Florida?

Formal probate administration filing fees in Florida Circuit Courts typically range from $400 to $450 depending on the county.
