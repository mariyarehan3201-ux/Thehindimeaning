How to Get a Class A License in MN?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Acquiring a Class A Commercial Driver's License in Minnesota represents the pinnacle of commercial vehicle licensing, permitting drivers to pilot heavy tractor-trailers, livestock haulers, flatbeds, and tandem combination rigs across state and interstate freight corridors. Overseen by Minnesota Driver and Vehicle Services alongside strict federal Entry-Level Driver Training mandates, obtaining this credential requires in-depth mastery of pneumatic braking systems, mechanical fifth-wheel coupling, closed-course precision backing, and comprehensive traffic navigation. For those aspiring to enter commercial freight hauling, following Minnesota statutory protocols guarantees rigorous preparation and compliant credentialing.

Class A Commercial Scope, Qualifications, and Medical Mandates

A Minnesota Class A commercial driver's license authorizes the operation of any vehicle combination with a Gross Combination Weight Rating of 26,001 or more pounds, provided the Gross Vehicle Weight Rating of the towed unit exceeds 10,000 pounds. This distinction separates Class A operators from Class B straight truck drivers. With appropriate endorsements, a Class A credential also legally clears drivers to operate Class B and Class C commercial vehicles, making it the most versatile and valuable driving asset in the transportation sector.

To qualify for a Minnesota Class A CDL, candidates must be at least 18 years of age for intrastate freight transit within Minnesota borders or 21 years of age for interstate commerce, hazardous material transport, or passenger transit. Applicants must hold a valid Minnesota Class D driver's license free from disqualifying suspensions or revocations. In addition, drivers must obtain an active Department of Transportation Medical Examiner's Certificate from a healthcare professional listed on the National Registry of Certified Medical Examiners. This medical card must be formally registered and self-certified with Minnesota DVS prior to practical testing.

Written Knowledge Examinations and Commercial Learner's Permit

Before stepping behind the wheel of a tractor-trailer, applicants must pass a series of proctored, computer-based written examinations at a Minnesota DVS exam station to earn a Commercial Learner's Permit with Class A privileges. The mandatory written testing suite consists of three core examinations: the General Knowledge test (50 questions), the Air Brakes test (25 questions), and the Combination Vehicles test (20 questions). Applicants seeking endorsements such as Double/Triple Trailers (T), Tank Vehicles (N), or Hazardous Materials (H) may complete those supplementary tests simultaneously.

Achieving a score of 80 percent or higher on each test qualifies the applicant for the Class A Commercial Learner's Permit upon payment of the $10.50 permit fee. Under Minnesota and federal statutes, the CLP must be held for a minimum of 14 days before the applicant is eligible to attempt the state skills road examination. While holding the CLP, the student driver may operate a commercial combination vehicle on public roadways only when accompanied by a fully licensed Class A commercial driver seated in the front passenger seat.

During the permit holding window, applicants must complete their federally mandated Entry-Level Driver Training through an institution verified on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry. This comprehensive training program combines classroom theory covering federal hours of service, logbooks, load securement, and vehicle maintenance, alongside intensive behind-the-wheel range practice focusing on vehicle control maneuvers and public roadway navigation.

Minnesota DVS Class A Road Skills Testing and Final Licensure

The practical Class A road skills evaluation in Minnesota is an exhaustive, three-stage examination conducted by certified state examiners. Candidates must supply their own representative Class A combination vehicle, which must be fully registered, insured, and pass an initial safety inspection by the examiner before testing begins. The first phase is the Pre-Trip Inspection, where the applicant must verbally inspect every major mechanical assembly, including the engine compartment, steering box, suspension linkages, brake drums, tires, coupling fifth-wheel jaws, air lines, and lighting.

Crucially, the applicant must perform a flawless in-cab air brake test, verifying low air warnings and emergency spring brake pop-outs. A single procedural error during the air brake test results in immediate disqualification. The second phase evaluates Basic Vehicle Control Maneuvers on a closed course, requiring precise execution of straight-line backing, forward offset lane positioning, and traditional 90-degree alley docking within marked boundary boundaries. The third and final phase tests On-Road Driving, assessing safe navigation through intersections, railroad crossings, merging highways, turning radii, and defensive speed management.

After successfully passing all three testing segments, the examiner validates the results electronically in the state database. The applicant then visits a Minnesota deputy registrar office, submits their identity and residency credentials, completes self-certification paperwork, and pays the final licensing fees to receive a temporary commercial license while the permanent Class A card is processed and mailed.

How to Secure a Class A CDL in Minnesota

A comprehensive checklist for fulfilling FMCSA ELDT rules, passing Minnesota DVS tests, and obtaining a Class A commercial combination license.

  1. Verify Age and Health Prerequisites

    Confirm you are at least 18 years old for intrastate driving or 21 for interstate driving, hold an active Minnesota Class D license, and obtain an official DOT medical card from an accredited medical examiner.

  2. Study the Minnesota DVS Commercial Driver Manual

    Focus intensively on the General Knowledge, Air Brakes, and Combination Vehicles chapters to prepare for the written permit examinations.

  3. Pass Class A Written Exams and Obtain Your CLP

    Visit a Minnesota DVS testing center, complete the General Knowledge, Air Brakes, and Combination tests with an 80% passing grade, and obtain your Class A Commercial Learner's Permit.

  4. Complete Registered Entry-Level Driver Training

    Enroll in an FMCSA-approved commercial driving school to finish mandatory theory and behind-the-wheel range and public road training in a Class A combination rig.

  5. Master Pre-Trip Inspections and Backing Skills

    Practice comprehensive component inspections, in-cab air brake leak-down checks, straight-line backing, offset backing, and 90-degree alley dock maneuvers.

  6. Pass the Minnesota DVS Three-Part Skills Test

    Schedule your state exam after the mandatory 14-day permit period, successfully pass the Pre-Trip, Basic Control, and On-Road tests, and finalize your paperwork at a deputy registrar office.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the difference between a Class A and Class B license in Minnesota?

A Class A CDL allows you to drive combination vehicles towing a trailer weighing more than 10,000 pounds with a total combination rating over 26,000 pounds, whereas a Class B CDL is restricted to single heavy vehicles or vehicles towing trailers weighing 10,000 pounds or less.

Q2: Can an 18-year-old get a Class A license in Minnesota?

Yes, an 18-year-old can obtain a Minnesota Class A CDL, but the credential will carry an intrastate restriction (K restriction), limiting commercial driving operations strictly within Minnesota state borders until reaching age 21.

Q3: What happens if you fail the air brake check during the Minnesota road test?

Failing the in-cab air brake inspection constitutes an automatic failure of the entire skills exam. The test is terminated immediately because proper air brake operation is critical to highway safety.

Q4: How much does truck driving school cost in Minnesota for a Class A CDL?

Tuition for FMCSA-approved Class A CDL training programs in Minnesota typically ranges between 4,000 and 7,500 dollars, depending on whether the program is offered through a private commercial academy or a public technical college.

Q5: Do you have to take the combination vehicle test for a Class A license?

Yes, the Combination Vehicles written examination is mandatory for all Class A applicants because Class A licensing specifically pertains to operating tractor-trailers and combination truck configurations.

Q6: How many questions are on the Minnesota Class A written test?

To earn a Class A permit, you must take three tests totaling 95 questions: General Knowledge (50 questions), Air Brakes (25 questions), and Combination Vehicles (20 questions), requiring an 80% passing score on each.

Q7: Can I drive out of state with a Minnesota commercial learner permit?

Yes, provided you are at least 21 years old and accompanied by a licensed commercial driver who possesses the appropriate class and endorsements for the vehicle, you may operate across state lines during training.

Q8: How long does a Minnesota Class A CDL remain valid?

A Minnesota Class A commercial driver's license is valid for four years and expires on your birthdate. You must maintain continuous, valid DOT medical self-certification to keep the license in good standing.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Securing a Class A commercial driving license in Minnesota demands technical precision, dedication to safety regulations, and hours of hands-on practice backing and maneuvering heavy combination equipment. By thoroughly mastering the Minnesota Commercial Driver's Manual, excelling in certified ELDT training, and executing a flawless pre-trip air brake inspection, applicants can successfully pass their state exams and embark on high-demand careers across the freight transportation industry.
