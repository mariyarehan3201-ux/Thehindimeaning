How Much Is a 75 Carat Diamond Worth?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Understanding how much a 75 carat diamond is worth requires exploring the highest echelon of mineralogy, royal treasures, and world-record auction archives. Weighing fifteen full grams and possessing a physical presence larger than a walnut, a 75-carat natural diamond is an extraordinary geological wonder. Depending on its color saturation, crystalline clarity, cut architecture, and Type IIa chemical classification, a natural 75-carat diamond commands between $3.5 million for commercial qualities to well over $25 million for flawless, museum-grade masterpieces.

Rarity Factors and Landmark Auction Records

In the international gem trade, a 75-carat diamond exists beyond conventional retail markets. Finding a rough diamond crystal capable of yielding an exquisite 75-carat polished gemstone occurs only a few times each decade across all diamond mines on Earth. Because rough weight loss during faceting often exceeds fifty to sixty percent, recovering a finished 75-carat gem requires unearthing a rare rough crystal weighing over one hundred fifty to two hundred carats.

Auction history illustrates the monumental financial scale of stones in this category. In April 2013, Christie in New York sold the historic 75.36-carat D-color, Internally Flawless briolette diamond for $11.15 million, establishing a world-record price for a briolette diamond at over $148,000 per carat. For top-tier stones exhibiting natural fancy colored saturation—such as deep fancy vivid yellow, intense pink, or pure blue—valuations can soar to between $30 million and $80 million.

Type IIa Structural Purity and Famous Historic Precedents

In diamonds of 75 carats and larger, gemological institutions like the Gemological Institute of America (GIA) conduct infrared spectroscopy to identify atomic purity. Less than two percent of diamonds qualify as Type IIa, possessing zero measurable nitrogen atoms in the crystal matrix. This structural perfection gives the stone a distinctive optical transparency often likened to liquid ice, reminiscent of the legendary Golconda gems that adorned Mughal emperors and European monarchs.

A famous historical touchstone in this weight class is the celebrated 75.52-carat Star of South Africa (Dudley Diamond), a magnificent pear-shaped Type IIa diamond discovered in 1869 that sparked the great South African diamond rush. Stones that carry unbroken historical provenance, royal custody logs, or documented museum exhibitions command substantial cultural premiums that exceed raw gemological formulas.

Vaulting, Security, and Private Treaty Liquidation

Holding an asset worth tens of millions of dollars requires institutional-grade security and asset protection. A 75-carat diamond is virtually never kept in a residential setting. Owners store these historic assets inside subterranean freeport vaults in Geneva, Zurich, or Singapore, which offer bonded tax-free transit zones and armed military-grade perimeter defense. Annual vaulting fees and Lloyd of London specie insurance policies typically range between $75,000 and $200,000 per year.

Liquidating a 75-carat diamond takes place through discreet private treaty transactions handled by senior directors at Christie or Sotheby, or via direct private acquisition by heritage jewelers like Graff, Harry Winston, or Mouawad. Bidders undergo thorough KYC (Know Your Customer) and anti-money laundering checks, providing multimillion-dollar banking letters of credit before private salon viewings are scheduled.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a 75 carat diamond weigh in grams?

A 75-carat diamond weighs exactly 15 grams (since one carat equals 0.20 grams), equivalent to the weight of three standard nickels.

Q2: How big is a 75 carat diamond?

Depending on the shape, a 75-carat diamond measures approximately 26 to 28 millimeters across, easily rivaling the size of a walnut.

Q3: What did the 75 carat briolette diamond sell for?

The 75.36-carat D-color Internally Flawless briolette diamond sold at Christie in New York for $11.15 million in April 2013.

Q4: Can a 75 carat diamond be worn as a ring?

While theoretically possible, a 75-carat diamond is too heavy and massive for practical ring wear; it is typically set as an imperial pendant necklace or brooch.

Q5: What is a 75 carat Type IIa diamond?

A Type IIa diamond contains no measurable nitrogen impurities, giving it the purest optical clarity and exceptional brilliance found in nature.

Q6: How much does it cost to insure a 75 carat diamond?

Annual specie insurance on a 75-carat diamond valued at $15 million typically costs between $75,000 and $150,000 per year.

Q7: Do lab-grown 75 carat diamonds exist?

A few specialized scientific laboratories have grown massive diamond crystals for optical and laser research, but commercial 75-carat jewelry lab diamonds do not exist.

Q8: Who owns 75 carat diamonds?

Owners include royal families, sovereign wealth funds, high-jewelry luxury maisons, national museums, and private billionaire collectors.
