How Much to Rent a Skid Steer for a Day?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Renting a skid steer loader for a day typically costs between $200 and $450 for a standard wheeled machine and between $300 and $600 for a heavy-duty compact track loader (rubber tracks). Equipment rental giants (such as Sunbelt Rentals, United Rentals, and The Home Depot) structure single-day rentals around an 8-hour engine meter run time within a 24-hour possession window. Beyond the base rental rate, overall daily expenses are influenced by delivery drop-off and pickup fees ($150 to $300 round-trip), mandatory equipment damage waivers (12 to 16 percent), fuel replacement, and specialized hydraulic attachments.

Wheeled Skid Steers vs. Compact Track Loaders (CTL)

The primary mechanical distinction when selecting a skid steer is choosing between wheels (skid steer loader or SSL) and rubber tracks (compact track loader or CTL). Wheeled skid steers (like a Bobcat S550 or Cat 242D) are ideal for hard, dry surfaces, asphalt parking lots, and packed gravel. Wheeled machines rent at the lower price bracket, typically $200 to $350 per day, offering faster travel speeds and lower wear on tires.

Compact track loaders (such as a Kubota SVL75 or Bobcat T66) utilize continuous rubber tracks that distribute machine operating weight across a wide ground footprint. This creates low ground pressure, allowing the machine to navigate wet clay, soft dirt, muddy yards, and sloped terrain without sinking or tearing up landscaping. Because track undercarriages have higher maintenance and rubber track replacement expenses, track loaders rent at a 20 to 30 percent premium, averaging $320 to $580 per day.

Logistics: Delivery Transport, Damage Waivers, and Fuel

When budgeting for equipment rental, you must account for transporting an 8,000-pound machine. If you do not own a heavy three-quarter-ton or one-ton truck paired with a 10,000-pound GVWR tandem-axle equipment trailer with electronic trailer brakes, you must pay the rental yard for delivery and pickup. Commercial transport flatbeds typically charge $150 to $300 round-trip depending on transit distance from the local equipment branch.

Additionally, rental invoices automatically append an optional or mandatory Loss Damage Waiver (LDW), which adds 12 to 16 percent to the rental fee. The waiver shields the renter from catastrophic physical machinery theft or rollover damage, capping liability at a $500 to $1,500 deductible. Finally, skid steers run on off-road or ultra-low sulfur diesel; returning the machine with an empty fuel tank triggers high rental yard refueling fees of $6.00 to $9.00 per gallon.

Operating Meter Hours, Cleaning Charges, and Safety Protocols

A standard single-day rental contract permits up to 8 running engine meter hours recorded on the digital hour meter. If you operate the machine for 12 hours during a grueling weekend grading project, the rental yard will bill an overtime rate of 1/8th the daily rental rate for each additional meter hour recorded. Weekend specials frequently allow renters to pick up machines on Friday evening and return Monday morning for a 1.5-day rate, permitting 12 to 16 meter hours.

Before returning the equipment, take 20 minutes to wash down the tracks and cab. Rental yards assess a cleaning fee of $75 to $200 if a compact track loader is returned choked with dried, caked clay or concrete slurry. From a safety perspective, always wear the lap bar safety harness, keep the bucket low to the ground while driving across slopes to maintain a low center of gravity, and check for underground utilities via 811 before excavating.

How to Rent and Operate a Skid Steer for a Single Day

Follow these five straightforward steps to book equipment, manage logistics, and operate safely.

  1. Determine Soil Conditions and Material Weight

    Choose a wheeled skid steer for hard paved driveways or a tracked loader (CTL) for muddy, wet, or hilly backyard turf.

  2. Verify Towing Capabilities or Schedule Delivery

    Confirm your truck and trailer can legally handle an 8,000-pound load, or schedule professional flatbed delivery.

  3. Call 811 for Utility Location Markings 72 Hours Prior

    Ensure municipal gas, water, electric, and fiber optic lines are spray-marked before digging on site.

  4. Inspect the Machine and Photograph Current Hour Meter

    Take photos of existing scratches, track wear, fuel level, and starting engine hours before signing off with the delivery driver.

  5. Refuel with Ultra-Low Sulfur Diesel Before Return

    Fill the fuel tank at a local service station prior to pickup to avoid expensive $7/gal rental refueling surcharges.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to rent a Bobcat for a day?

Renting a genuine Bobcat skid steer costs between $200 and $400 for wheeled units and $300 to $550 for tracked models.

Q2: How many hours of use are included in a one-day rental?

A standard 1-day rental includes up to 8 engine run meter hours over a 24-hour possession period, with overtime billed per hour.

Q3: Do you need a special license to rent a skid steer?

No commercial driver's license (CDL) or special heavy equipment license is required for private individuals renting for homeowner use.

Q4: Is a track loader better than a wheeled skid steer?

Track loaders provide superior traction on mud, sand, and slopes without tearing up grass, while wheeled loaders are faster on concrete and asphalt.

Q5: How much does it cost to rent skid steer attachments?

Basic attachments like pallet forks cost $40 to $70 per day, while hydraulic augers or concrete breakers cost $90 to $300 per day.

Q6: Can a skid steer fit through a standard backyard gate?

Full-size skid steers are 60 to 72 inches wide; for residential backyard gates (36 to 48 inches), rent a mini stand-on walk-behind loader.

Q7: How much does equipment delivery cost?

Professional delivery and pickup service typically costs between $150 and $300 round-trip depending on your distance from the rental yard.

Q8: What should you do if the machine breaks down on the job?

Call the rental branch immediately; commercial rental yards dispatch mobile field mechanics to repair or swap out downed machinery quickly.
