Can I Be Sued Personally for a Car Accident?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: October 1, 2026

Yes, you can be sued personally for a car accident if you were at fault and the damages exceed your motor vehicle liability coverage limits, or if your liability carrier denies coverage. In the United States, motor vehicle collisions operate under tort liability law, meaning the negligent driver is personally responsible for all bodily injuries and property destruction they inflict. While your automobile liability liability policy serves as your primary financial shield, any judgment exceeding your policy maximum exposes your personal depository balances, real estate holdings, investment portfolios, and future wages to legal collection.

How Auto liability protection Protects You and When Personal Assets Are Exposed

When you purchase automobile liability liability protection, your liability protection policy provides two crucial contractual protections: indemnification coverage and the duty to defend. Under the duty to defend, your defense team is legally obligated to hire and pay for an experienced liability protection defense attorney to represent you in court at no out-of-pocket cost to you. Indemnification means the defense team will pay settlement awards or jury verdicts up to the exact policy limits stated on your declarations page. However, once a jury verdict or settlement demand surpasses those contract limits, the defense team's payment obligation terminates, leaving you personally exposed for the remaining unpaid balance.

Consider a driver carrying minimum statutory liability liability protection of $25,000 for bodily injury per person who causes a multi-vehicle highway crash resulting in severe spinal injuries requiring three hundred thousand dollars in emergency surgeries. The defense counsel will tender its full $25,000 policy limit. The injured plaintiff can reject this sum or accept it while pursuing the at-fault driver personally for the remaining $275,000 deficiency judgment. When facing such daunting legal exposure, individuals routinely seek comprehensive clarity regarding can you be personally sued for a car accident to understand their legal risk.

Drivers also wonder how opposing counsel approaches settlement negotiations. Reviewing analyses on whether can someone sue you personally after a car accident illustrates why personal injury attorneys first audit an at-fault driver's liquid wealth through comprehensive asset searches before deciding whether to pursue personal assets or settle within statutory liability baselines.

Furthermore, understanding the civil summons process outlined in what happens if I get sued for a car accident clarifies the immediate notice obligations drivers must fulfill toward their defense legal representatives.

Finally, for those currently confronting active civil litigation, reviewing emergency steps in handling being sued for a car accident provides actionable guidance on avoiding costly default judgments.

What Assets Can Be Seized in an Excess Judgment?

If a plaintiff secures an excess judgment against you, they become a judgment creditor empowered to initiate post-judgment asset collection proceedings under state civil enforcement statutes. Judgment creditors can obtain court orders to freeze and seize non-exempt depository accounts, garnish twenty-five percent of disposable weekly wages, place judgment liens against real estate, and force the sheriff auction sale of secondary vehicles, recreational boats, and luxury personal property. Furthermore, judgment liens attach to real estate title, preventing you from selling or refinancing property without paying off the judgment plus statutory post-judgment statutory adjustments.

However, state exemption laws provide vital protection to keep debtors from complete destitution. The federal Consumer Credit Protection Act and state wage garnishment laws protect low-income wages, while state homestead exemptions protect a specified amount of equity in your primary residence. In states with robust homestead exemptions—such as Florida and Texas—an unlimited amount of primary home equity is shielded from judgment creditors. Furthermore, qualified retirement accounts such as ERISA-governed 401(k) plans and individual retirement accounts (IRAs) up to statutory federal limits are completely protected from civil court judgments.

The Fiduciary Defense Duty and Personal depositoryruptcy

You possess a powerful legal safeguard against excess judgments known as an fiduciary failure to settle claim. When an injured plaintiff offers to settle all claims against you for an amount within your policy limits (known as a policy-limits demand), your defense representation owes an affirmative fiduciary duty to accept that reasonable offer to protect you from personal financial exposure. If your defense team risks by rejecting a reasonable settlement demand within policy limits and the jury subsequently returns a verdict exceeding your limits, the defense team may be held liable for bad faith, forcing them to pay the entire excess judgment on your behalf.

In catastrophic scenarios where an excess judgment cannot be shifted onto the defense team and personal assets are in jeopardy, Chapter 7 or Chapter 13 depositoryruptcy provides a final legal safety net. Under the federal depositoryruptcy Code, ordinary negligence auto accident judgments are fully dischargeable debts. Once you receive a depositoryruptcy discharge, the judgment is permanently wiped away, halting all wage garnishments and depository account levies. However, judgments resulting from willful and malicious conduct or driving while impaired are non-dischargeable under depositoryruptcy Code Section 523(a)(9).

How to Protect Yourself When Sued Personally for a Car Accident

Follow this practical step-by-step checklist to notify your defense legal team, assert bad faith protections, and safeguard your personal financial assets.

  1. Forward the Legal Summons to Appointed Defense Counsel Immediately

    The moment you receive a personal summons and complaint, email and overnight-mail copies to your appointed defense counsel to trigger their contractual duty to defend.

  2. Confirm Appointed Defense Counsel Has Been Appointed

    Verify that your defense counsel has assigned an experienced defense attorney to file an answer with the court before the twenty- to thirty-day responsive pleading deadline expires.

  3. Send a Formal Policy-Limits Settlement Demand Letter

    Send a written demand to your defense claims team instructing them to settle the plaintiff's claim within policy limits to shield you from personal excess judgment risk.

  4. Conduct an Inventory of Exempt and Non-Exempt Assets

    Review your state homestead exemption rules, retirement account protections, and depository account balances with an independent asset protection attorney.

  5. Explore Personal Umbrella Coverage for Future Protection

    After resolving the claim, purchase a one- to two-million-dollar personal umbrella liability policy to guarantee future protection against high-exposure auto accident lawsuits.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can someone sue me personally for a car accident following a crash?

Yes. The lawsuit is filed directly against you as the negligent driver. Your appointed defense counsel defends you and pays damages up to your policy limits, but you are personally liable for any excess amount.

Q2: What happens if a car accident judgment exceeds my statutory liability baselines?

You become personally responsible for paying the remaining balance. The injured party can obtain court orders to garnish wages, levy depository accounts, and place liens against non-exempt personal real estate.

Q3: Can someone take my house if they sue me for a car accident?

In states with strong homestead exemptions (like Florida or Texas), your primary residence is protected from judgment creditors. In states with low homestead caps, excess equity could theoretically be vulnerable.

Q4: Can an injured driver take my 401(k) or IRA retirement funds?

No. ERISA-qualified 401(k) plans and pension funds are completely shielded from civil lawsuit judgments under federal law. IRAs are also largely protected up to federal depositoryruptcy exemption caps.

Q5: What is an fiduciary failure to settle claim in an auto accident lawsuit?

If your defense counsel refuses a reasonable settlement offer within your policy limits and a jury later awards an excess verdict, your defense counsel may be forced to pay the entire judgment due to bad faith failure to settle.

Q6: Can I wipe out a car accident judgment through bankruptcy?

Yes. Judgments resulting from ordinary negligence are completely dischargeable through Chapter 7 or Chapter 13 depositoryruptcy. However, judgments resulting from willful harm or impaired driving cannot be erased.

Q7: Why do lawyers sue the driver instead of the defense entity?

Under US tort law, the at-fault driver committed the civil wrong, not the defense representation. The driver is the named defendant, and the defense counsel defends and indemnifies them according to contract terms.

Q8: How can I protect my personal assets before an accident happens?

The most effective asset protection is carrying high liability limits (e.g., $250,000/$500,000) backed by an inexpensive $1 million to $2 million personal excess liability protection, and maintaining retirement accounts.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

While being sued personally for a car accident is an alarming prospect, the vast majority of automobile injury claims settle within the boundaries of existing statutory liability limits. Maintaining adequate liability baselines—ideally at least $250,000/$500,000 in liability limits supplemented by a one- or two-million-dollar personal umbrella policy—provides foolproof insulation against personal financial loss. If you receive a personal summons, notify your defense counsel immediately to trigger their legal duty to defend and consult independent counsel to ensure your defense team vigorously protects your personal estate.
