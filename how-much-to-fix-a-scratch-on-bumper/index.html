How Much to Fix a Scratch on Bumper?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Fixing a scratch on a car bumper typically costs between $150 and $800 depending on scratch depth, bumper material, and whether the repair is performed via localized mobile blending or full bumper removal and repainting. Light surface clearcoat scuffs and paint transfer from parking curbs can often be machine buffed and compounded for $100 to $200. However, deep gouges that penetrate into the black plastic substrate or crack the underlying polyurethane bumper cover demand plastic filler, flexible primer, computerized color matching, and full clearcoating ranging from $450 to $900.

Determining Scratch Depth: Clearcoat Scuffs vs. Deep Plastic Gouges

Evaluating bumper scratch severity starts with the fingernail test. If you gently slide your fingernail across the scratch and it glides smoothly without catching, the damage is confined to the microscopic polyurethane clearcoat layer. These superficial scuffs are the easiest and most economical to eliminate, requiring professional multi-stage rotary polishing with diminishing abrasive compounds or wet sanding with 2500-grit paper.

If your fingernail distinctly catches in the groove or you observe exposed black or grey plastic (thermoplastic olefin or TPO), the scratch has penetrated through the color basecoat and primer into the bumper substrate. These deep gouges cannot simply be polished out. They require plastic surface adhesion promoters, flexible two-part epoxy filler to reconstruct damaged contours, primer sealer, and a fresh application of basecoat and clearcoat.

Repair Methods: Mobile Smart Repair vs. Full Auto Body Shop Respray

Automotive consumers have two primary commercial avenues for resolving bumper scratches: mobile smart repair technicians and traditional brick-and-mortar collision centers. Mobile bumper specialists operate self-contained vans equipped with computerized paint-mixing systems and localized spray equipment. They repair bumper scuffs right in your driveway, feathering the paint within a 12- to 18-inch blend zone for $200 to $400. This is convenient and fast, taking only two to three hours.

Traditional auto body shops typically decline spot blending because clearcoat edges on bumper covers can degrade after several years of UV exposure and pressure washing. Instead, body shops remove the entire bumper assembly, disconnect parking proximity sensors and blind-spot radar modules, strip badges and grilles, sand the whole bumper cover, spray paint in a climate-controlled downdraft booth, and bake the finish. While superior in durability and seamlessness, full shop repainting costs $500 to $900.

ADAS Sensor Recalibration and Insurance Considerations

A rapidly growing factor in modern bumper scratch repair is the integration of Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS). Late-model vehicles feature ultrasonic parking sensors, radar units for adaptive cruise control, and cross-traffic cameras embedded directly behind bumper plastic. If a body shop removes the bumper cover or applies excessive paint film thickness over radar paths, the vehicle often requires computerized ADAS recalibration, adding $250 to $600 to the repair invoice.

When evaluating whether to file an insurance claim, compare your collision or comprehensive deductible against the repair estimate. A $350 mobile bumper fix should always be paid out-of-pocket to avoid claims history dings. However, if an accident involves deep bumper gouges, dented quarter panels, and damaged radar sensors exceeding $1,500, utilizing your collision insurance coverage is usually warranted.

How to Evaluate and Repair a Scratched Car Bumper

Follow these five straightforward steps to determine scratch depth and choose the most economical repair.

  1. Perform the Fingernail and Water Wetness Test

    Spray clean water over the scratch; if the mark temporarily disappears while wet, it is superficial and can be buffed out.

  2. Remove Superficial Paint Transfer with Rubbing Compound

    Use a microfiber cloth with mild rubbing compound or isopropyl alcohol to lift exterior paint scuffed from posts or other cars.

  3. Identify ADAS Sensors Embedded in the Bumper Cover

    Check whether the scratch is within 6 inches of radar modules or sonar sensors that might require recalibration if removed.

  4. Obtain Quotes from Both Mobile Technicians and Body Shops

    Compare localized smart repair estimates against full-panel respray bids to balance budget against perfection.

  5. Confirm UV-Resistant 2K Clearcoat is Used

    Ensure whichever professional you hire utilizes two-part catalyzed polyurethane clearcoat rather than temporary aerosol lacquer.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can a bumper scratch be buffed out completely?

If the scratch is confined to the clearcoat and does not catch your fingernail, it can almost always be completely buffed out with compound.

Q2: How much does a mobile bumper repair cost?

Mobile bumper scratch repair technicians generally charge between $175 and $375 for localized blending and repainting in your driveway.

Q3: Do plastic bumpers rust if scratched?

No, plastic bumper covers do not rust; however, deep gouges can cause surrounding paint to crack and peel if left unsealed.

Q4: Why do modern bumpers cost more to repaint?

Modern bumpers house delicate ADAS radar units, parking sonar sensors, and cameras that require careful removal and computerized recalibration.

Q5: Can I use touch-up paint on a bumper scratch?

Yes, touch-up paint prevents peeling and hides dark plastic gouges from 5 feet away, but it will not look smooth like professional spray refinishing.

Q6: How long does a bumper paint repair take at a body shop?

A traditional auto body shop typically needs 2 to 3 days to remove, prep, paint, bake, reassemble, and recalibrate sensors on a bumper.

Q7: Does bumper repainting match the original body color?

Yes, professional shops use computerized spectrophotometer scanners and spray test cards to ensure the newly painted bumper matches surrounding fenders.

Q8: Should I replace or repair a scratched bumper?

If the plastic cover is merely scratched or gouged, repair is far cheaper ($250-$600); replacement is only required if the plastic is torn or mounting tabs are snapped.
