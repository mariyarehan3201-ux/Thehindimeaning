Ontario Regulation 278/05 and the Ministry of Labour Standards

Hazardous material abatement in Kingston and across the province of Ontario is governed by the Occupational Health and Safety Act under Ontario Regulation 278/05 (O. Reg. 278/05: Designated Substance — Asbestos on Construction Projects and in Buildings and Repair Operations). This comprehensive provincial statute establishes explicit standards for classifying abatement operations, worker training certifications, containment enclosures, and post-abatement air clearance. Reviewing regional abatement guidelines across nearby American jurisdictions, such as asbestos removal in Rochester MN, demonstrates that Ontario enforces equally strict negative-air engineering standards.

Under O. Reg. 278/05, remediation operations are categorized into three distinct operational classifications based on friability, material type, and the degree of fiber disturbance. Type 1 operations involve minor disturbance of non-friable materials using hand tools. Type 2 operations govern intermediate repairs, glovebag stripping on pipes, and small-scale enclosure maintenance. Type 3 operations govern high-risk activities, including the removal of friable acoustic plaster, sprayed-on structural fireproofing, or large-scale thermal system insulation, requiring full negative-pressure containment and three-stage decontamination facilities.

Designated Substance Reports (DSR) and Municipal Permitting

Under Section 30 of the Ontario Occupational Health and Safety Act, building owners are legally required to provide a Designated Substance Report (DSR) to all prospective constructors and contractors before soliciting tenders or commencing any construction, renovation, or demolition project. The City of Kingston Building Services Department strictly enforces this provincial mandate, requiring property owners to attach a verified DSR to all commercial and residential building permit applications.

Waste generated during Kingston remediation projects is governed by Ontario Ministry of the Environment, Conservation and Parks (MECP) Regulation 347. Regulated mineral waste must be saturated with water, sealed in double six-mil yellow polyethylene bags labeled with designated substance warnings, and hauled by MECP-licensed waste management carriers. Waste cannot be dumped at standard Kingston waste transfer stations; it must be transported under manifest to approved landfills authorized to receive asbestos, such as the Moose Creek Landfill or local designated cells.

How to Plan an Asbestos Removal Project in Kingston, Ontario

Follow these five municipal steps to satisfy Ontario Regulation 278/05 rules, secure City of Kingston permits, and execute certified abatement.

  1. Commission a Designated Substance Report (DSR)

    Retain a qualified environmental consultant in eastern Ontario to inspect the building, sample suspect components, and compile an official DSR.

  2. Submit the DSR with City of Kingston Building Permits

    Attach your completed Designated Substance Report to your municipal building or demolition permit application submitted to City of Kingston Building Services.

  3. Retain a Ministry of Labour Certified 253W/253S Contractor

    Hire an abatement firm employing technicians holding active Ontario Asbestos Abatement Worker (253W) and Supervisor (253S) provincial credentials.

  4. Execute Abatement Under Strict Type 1, 2, or 3 Controls

    Ensure the contractor establishes appropriate containment enclosures, HEPA negative air filtration, and wet stripping methods required for the operation type.

  5. Obtain Final Air Clearance and MECP Waste Manifests

    Require third-party phase contrast microscopy air clearance testing and archive all Ontario MECP Regulation 347 waste shipment manifests.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What regulation governs asbestos removal in Kingston, Ontario?

Asbestos removal in Kingston is governed provincially by Ontario Regulation 278/05 under the Occupational Health and Safety Act enforced by the Ministry of Labour.

Q2: What is a Designated Substance Report (DSR) in Ontario?

A DSR is a legally mandated building survey report that identifies all hazardous substances, including asbestos and lead, prior to any renovation or demolition.

Q3: Does the City of Kingston require a DSR for building permits?

Yes. The City of Kingston Building Services Department requires a valid Designated Substance Report before approving building or demolition permit applications.

Q4: What are Type 1, 2, and 3 asbestos operations in Ontario?

Type 1 covers minor low-risk disturbance of non-friable materials; Type 2 covers intermediate glovebag work; Type 3 covers high-risk friable removal requiring negative air.

Q5: Where is asbestos waste disposed of in the Kingston area?

Asbestos waste must be double-bagged in six-mil poly bags, transported by an MECP-licensed hauler under manifest, and buried at an approved provincial landfill cell.

Q6: Can Kingston homeowners remove asbestos from their own home?

While private homeowners working on their own single-family residence have limited exemptions, provincial disposal laws and extreme health risks make hiring certified firms advised.

Q7: How much does asbestos removal cost in Kingston, Ontario?

Residential removal in Kingston typically costs between $1,500 and $4,500 for small pipe or floor projects, and $5,000 to $10,000 or more for full-house ceiling abatement.

Q8: What qualification must asbestos workers have in Ontario?

Workers performing Type 2 and Type 3 abatement must complete specialized training and hold Ministry of Labour 253W (Worker) or 253S (Supervisor) certification.
