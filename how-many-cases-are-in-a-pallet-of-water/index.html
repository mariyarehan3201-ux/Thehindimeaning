How Many Cases Are in a Pallet of Water?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how many cases are in a pallet of water depends primarily on bottle case pack sizing (24-pack, 32-pack, or 40-pack) and bottle fluid volume (16.9 oz / 500 mL). On a standard 48x40-inch GMA wooden pallet, a pallet of commercial bottled water typically contains between 48 and 84 cases. For standard 24-count cases of 16.9-ounce bottles, a full pallet contains exactly 72 or 84 cases, while retail club-store 40-count cases (such as Costco Kirkland or Sam's Club Member's Mark) are stacked in tiers of 48 cases per pallet.

Standard Pallet Configuration: Case Packing and Tier Stacking

In commercial beverage warehousing and logistics, bottled water is shipped on standard Grocery Manufacturers Association (GMA) 48-inch by 40-inch four-way wooden or plastic pallets. The total case count on a pallet is determined by two factors: the 'Ti' (the number of cases packed on one flat horizontal layer) and the 'Hi' (the vertical number of layers stacked high on the pallet).

For standard retail 24-packs of 16.9 fl oz (500 mL) water bottles, beverage distributors typically configure pallets with a Ti of 12 or 14 cases per layer, stacked 6 layers high (a 12x6 Ti/Hi configuration equals 72 cases; a 14x6 Ti/Hi configuration equals 84 cases). In major supermarket distribution centers, 78 or 84 cases represent the most common operational shipping density.

Weight Limits: The Real Reason Water Pallets Aren't Stacked Higher

People often wonder why water pallets are only stacked 4 to 5 feet high when freight trailers have 9 feet of vertical clearance. The limiting factor is not cargo volume; it is weight. Water is an exceptionally heavy freight commodity, weighing 8.34 pounds per gallon (or approximately 1.04 pounds per 16.9-ounce bottle when factoring in the plastic bottle, cap, and shrink film).

A standard 24-pack case of water weighs approximately 27 to 28 pounds. Multiplying 84 cases by 28 pounds yields 2,352 pounds, plus the 45-pound tare weight of the wooden pallet itself, bringing the total gross pallet weight to approximately 2,400 pounds (1.2 short tons). Federal Department of Transportation (DOT) highway weight limits restrict a standard 53-foot semi-truck to a maximum gross vehicle weight of 80,000 pounds (allowing roughly 44,000 to 45,000 pounds of cargo). Consequently, a truck can only carry 18 to 20 pallets of water before 'weighing out' legally, regardless of remaining trailer floor space.

Unloading and Storage: Pallet Jacks, Forklifts, and Floor Load Capacities

Receiving a full pallet of water requires appropriate material handling equipment. Because a full pallet weighs between 2,050 and 2,400 pounds, it cannot be safely unloaded by hand truck or moved across residential floors. Unloading from a commercial freight truck requires a dock-height warehouse bay, a mechanical forklift, or a heavy-duty hydraulic pallet jack on a truck liftgate.

Furthermore, structural floor load ratings must be considered before setting a water pallet down inside a commercial office, retail stockroom, or residential garage. Storing a 2,400-pound pallet on a standard residential wooden suspended subfloor creates an intense point load of roughly 180 pounds per square foot, risking structural joist sagging. Water pallets should always be staged on ground-level concrete slabs or heavy-duty steel warehouse pallet racking.

How to Safely Receive and Store a Pallet of Water

Follow these five tactical steps to safely handle, inspect, and store bulk bottled water pallets.

  1. Verify Delivery Truck Liftgate and Pallet Jack Access

    Confirm the freight carrier provides a liftgate truck and manual pallet jack if your delivery site lacks a raised loading dock.

  2. Inspect Exterior Stretch Film and Corner Boards

    Check that the industrial shrink wrap is intact and cases have not shifted, tilted, or crushed lower bottles in transit.

  3. Stage Pallets Exclusively on Reinforced Concrete Floors

    Place the 2,400-pound load onto solid concrete slabs rather than residential wood subfloors or light wooden decks.

  4. Store in a Climate-Controlled Area Away from Direct Sunlight

    Keep water pallets indoors below 85°F away from UV sunlight to prevent algae growth and plastic chemical leaching.

  5. Keep Stored Water Isolated from Volatile Chemicals

    Never store water pallets near gasoline cans, paint thinners, or pesticides; thin PET plastic bottles breathe and absorb volatile vapors.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How many cases of water are on a standard pallet?

A standard pallet typically holds 72 to 84 cases of 24-pack water bottles, or 48 cases of 40-pack wholesale club cases.

Q2: How much does a full pallet of water weigh?

A full pallet of water weighs between 2,050 and 2,400 pounds depending on case count and bottle size.

Q3: How many cases of water are on a Costco pallet?

Costco Kirkland Signature 40-pack pallets typically hold 48 cases, totaling 1,920 bottles and weighing roughly 2,300 pounds.

Q4: How many pallets of water fit on a semi-truck?

A 53-foot semi-truck carries 18 to 20 pallets of water before reaching the federal 45,000-pound maximum highway cargo weight limit.

Q5: Can you double-stack water pallets in a warehouse?

Yes, if pallets are in pristine condition with heavy corner protectors, they can be double-stacked, though single-stacking in pallet racking is safest.

Q6: How many individual bottles are in a pallet of water?

A pallet of 72 cases of 24-packs contains 1,728 bottles; an 84-case pallet contains 2,016 individual bottles.

Q7: What are the dimensions of a water pallet?

It uses a standard 48-inch by 40-inch GMA pallet, standing approximately 50 to 58 inches tall when fully loaded.

Q8: How long can a pallet of bottled water be stored?

Commercially sealed bottled water has an indefinite shelf life if kept in a cool, dark, chemical-free environment; manufacturers recommend 2 years.
