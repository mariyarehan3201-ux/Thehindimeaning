How Much Does It Cost to Add a Fireplace?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does it cost to add a fireplace requires evaluating the fuel source (electric, direct-vent gas, wood-burning, or ethanol), structural framing, and chimney/venting requirements. On average, adding a new fireplace costs between $1,500 and $8,000 installed. A modern electric fireplace insert costs $600 to $2,500 installed, a direct-vent gas fireplace ranges from $2,500 to $6,500, and a traditional masonry wood-burning fireplace with a brick chimney costs $9,000 to $25,000+.

Fuel Source Comparison: Electric vs. Gas vs. Wood-Burning

The choice of fuel source represents the primary determinant of both equipment costs and structural installation complexity. Electric fireplaces are the most affordable and versatile option ($400 to $1,800 for hardware; $800 to $2,500 installed). Operating on standard 120V household current, electric units require zero exterior venting, chimneys, or gas lines, producing realistic LED flames and 4,500 to 5,000 BTUs of supplemental zone heating.

Direct-vent gas fireplaces are the most popular choice for modern living rooms, priced at $2,500 to $6,500 installed. Utilizing natural gas or propane, direct-vent units draw combustion air from outside and vent exhaust gases through a coaxial pipe penetrating an exterior wall. Traditional site-built masonry wood-burning fireplaces require a reinforced concrete foundation footing and a vertical brick/mortar chimney, costing $10,000 to $25,000+.

Venting Infrastructure: Direct-Vent Pipe vs. Full Masonry Chimneys

Venting represents the primary technical and structural expense when adding a combustion fireplace. Direct-vent gas fireplaces utilize a rigid coaxial double-wall pipe: the outer pipe pulls fresh combustion air into the sealed firebox, while the inner pipe expels combustion fumes outside. Because direct-vent pipes can vent horizontally through an exterior wall between floor joists, installation costs only $600 to $1,500 for venting labor and materials.

In contrast, wood-burning fireplaces require vertical draft chimneys extending at least 3 feet above the roofline. Installing an insulated double-wall stainless steel chimney pipe (Class A pipe) through two stories and an attic costs $1,500 to $3,500. Constructing a traditional brick masonry chimney requires excavating a massive concrete footing beneath the foundation and hand-laying bricks with clay flue tiles, adding $6,000 to $15,000 in specialized masonry labor.

Surround Finishes: Stone Veneer, Shiplap, and Custom Mantels

The aesthetic centerpiece of any fireplace build is the exterior decorative surround. A bare drywall fireplace chase looks unfinished; dressing the bump-out transforms the living room. Installing modern floor-to-ceiling shiplap with a floating timber mantel costs $800 to $2,000. Applying manufactured stone veneer (cultured stone) or porcelain large-format tile costs $1,500 to $4,500 installed.

High-end natural stone slabs—such as Carrara marble, soapstone, or granite surrounds with custom hand-carved hardwood mantels—range from $3,000 to $7,500+. Beyond aesthetic beauty, fireplaces offer an exceptional return on investment. According to the National Association of Realtors (NAR), adding a working fireplace increases home appraisal values by $4,000 to $12,000, recovering 65% to 85% of construction expenditures upon resale.

How to Add a Fireplace to Your Home

Follow these five tactical steps to plan and install a new fireplace.

  1. Choose the Best Fuel Source for Your Lifestyle

    Select electric for easy zero-clearance installs, direct-vent gas for push-button real heat, or wood for classic crackling ambiance.

  2. Verify Clearance to Combustibles and Local Codes

    Review manufacturer installation manuals for mandatory floor protection, mantle clearances, and non-combustible zone rules.

  3. Hire a Licensed Plumber for Gas Line Extension

    Have a licensed professional plumber extend a dedicated black iron gas pipe and conduct pressure tests.

  4. Frame the Enclosure Bump-Out Chase Wall

    Frame the 2x4 stud wall around the firebox unit, leaving appropriate clearances for ventilation pipes and electrical wiring.

  5. Install Decorative Non-Combustible Facing and Mantel

    Finish the facade with cultured stone veneer, tile, or brick, and anchor a secure floating timber mantel shelf.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to add a fireplace to an existing house?

Adding a fireplace typically costs between $1,500 and $8,000, with electric units costing $1,500, gas units averaging $4,500, and wood units reaching $10,000+.

Q2: What is the cheapest type of fireplace to add?

An electric fireplace is the cheapest, costing $600 to $2,500 installed because it requires zero venting, gas lines, or chimneys.

Q3: Can you put a gas fireplace where there is no chimney?

Yes, direct-vent gas fireplaces do not require chimneys; they vent directly through an exterior side wall using horizontal coaxial piping.

Q4: Does adding a fireplace increase home value?

Yes, adding a working fireplace increases home resale value by $4,000 to $12,000 and consistently ranks among top buyer wish-list features.

Q5: How much does it cost to run a gas line for a fireplace?

A licensed plumber typically charges between $400 and $1,200 to extend a natural gas or propane line to a new fireplace location.

Q6: What is the difference between a fireplace insert and a zero-clearance fireplace?

An insert fits inside an existing open masonry firebox; a zero-clearance fireplace is a self-contained unit framed into new wood wall studs.

Q7: Do you need a permit to add a fireplace?

Yes, building permits and mechanical/plumbing permits are required to verify fire safety clearances, gas pressure, and venting.

Q8: How much heat does a gas fireplace produce?

A direct-vent gas fireplace produces 20,000 to 40,000 BTUs per hour, enough to heat 1,000 to 1,800 square feet of living space.
