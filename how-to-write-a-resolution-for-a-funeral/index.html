How to Write a Resolution for a Funeral?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Writing a formal funeral resolution is a solemn honor that allows civic organizations, professional associations, academic institutions, and fraternal societies to formally commemorate the character, contributions, and enduring legacy of a deceased member. Unlike a personal eulogy, which is an informal, intimate speech delivered by a family member or friend, a funeral resolution is an official, structured legalistic document that becomes a permanent part of the organization's official archives, with an embossed presentation copy delivered directly to the grieving family.

The Traditional Four-Part Anatomy of a Funeral Resolution

A formal memorial resolution follows a standardized four-part parliamentary structure that has been observed by civic boards, academic faculties, and fraternal assemblies for generations. Every section performs a specific ceremonial and commemorative function, moving from formal identification to character accolades, condolences, and permanent archival preservation.

The document begins with the Title and Introduction, establishing the name of the deceased and the organization issuing the document. This is followed by the 'Whereas' clauses—a sequence of formal preamble statements that recount the deceased's character, public service, professional accomplishments, and positive influence on the community. The resolution culminates in the 'Therefore Be It Resolved' clauses, which offer heartfelt institutional condolences to the surviving family and declare how the organization will permanently honor the member's memory.

The table below provides a detailed structural breakdown of the four core sections of a formal funeral resolution, highlighting their specific language formulas and objectives.

Resolution Section Standard Language Marker Primary Functional Objective Typical Clause Count
Title & Preamble 'Resolution in Loving Memory of...' Formally identifies subject and governing body 1 Introductory paragraph
Whereas Clauses 'Whereas, [Name] distinguished themselves...' Recounts public service, character, & achievements 3 to 6 distinct clauses
Resolved Clauses 'Therefore, Be It Resolved that we extend...' Extends formal institutional sympathy & love 1 to 2 concluding clauses
Attestation & Signatures 'Duly adopted on this [Date] day of [Month]...' Validates document for official archives Executive signatures & official seal
Presentation Copy High-grade archival parchment in leather folder Permanent family keepsake heirloom Delivered to family during service

Drafting Impactful 'Whereas' and 'Resolved' Clauses

When drafting 'Whereas' clauses, writers should focus on concrete biographical contributions rather than vague platitudes. Each clause begins with the capitalized word 'WHEREAS,' followed by a comma, and articulates a specific facet of the individual's life—their professional dedication, community mentorship, volunteer leadership, or personal integrity.

For example: 'WHEREAS, our esteemed colleague dedicated over thirty years of tireless leadership to mentoring young professionals and expanding community educational access...' Each statement builds upon the last. The resolution transitions into the active declarations using the phrase: 'THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that we, the officers and members of [Organization], extend our deepest condolences and heartfelt sympathy to the bereaved family; and BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that a copy of this memorial resolution be entered into the permanent records of our institution and a presentation copy be conveyed to the family.'

Tone & Style Solemn, ceremonial, structured, parliamentary Intimate, emotional, storytelling, conversational

Archival Status Permanently recorded in institutional archives Personal family memory

Presentation Protocol and Reading Etiquette at the Service

Delivering a funeral resolution at a memorial service requires strict adherence to ceremonial etiquette. The presiding officer or designated reader should coordinate in advance with the funeral director and family to ensure the resolution is placed at the proper moment in the printed order of service (typically following the formal obituary reading and preceding the eulogies).

The reader should step to the lectern, maintain a steady and dignified vocal cadence, and read the document clearly without rushing. After reading the final 'Be It Further Resolved' clause and the attestation, the reader folds or closes the presentation folder, walks over to the front family row, and presents the document to the primary next of kin with a respectful nod and quiet words of sympathy.

How to Draft a Formal Funeral Resolution

Follow this step-by-step roadmap to gather biographical milestones, draft structured Whereas clauses, format presentation parchment, and deliver the resolution.

  1. Gather Verified Service and Character Milestones

    Collect specific details regarding the deceased member's organizational roles, years of service, volunteer projects, and awards.

  2. Draft 3 to 5 Structured 'Whereas' Clauses

    Write concise, dignity-filled clauses highlighting the member's professional dedication, personal ethics, and institutional contributions.

  3. Compose the Concluding 'Be It Resolved' Statements

    Draft statements extending official institutional condolences, pledging to preserve their memory, and ordering permanent archival filing.

  4. Print on Heavy Archival Linen or Parchment Paper

    Typeset the document with elegant margins, apply the organization's embossed seal, and obtain signatures from presiding executive officers.

  5. Read Dignifiedly at Service and Present to Family

    Coordinate with the service director, read clearly from the podium, and hand-deliver the framed or bound presentation copy to the next of kin.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the difference between a funeral resolution and a eulogy?

A funeral resolution is an official, structured parliamentary document issued by an organization or civic society that becomes part of permanent archives. A eulogy is a personal, emotional speech delivered by a family member or friend.

Q2: Who typically writes a funeral resolution?

Funeral resolutions are typically drafted by an appointed committee, secretary, or executive officer of a civic group, professional association, alumni society, or fraternal lodge where the deceased was an active member.

Q3: How long should a funeral resolution be?

A standard funeral resolution fits comfortably on a single sheet of archival paper (approximately 250 to 450 words) and takes approximately two to three minutes to read aloud at a service.

Q4: What does 'Whereas' mean in a funeral resolution?

'Whereas' translates to 'considering that' or 'in view of the fact that.' It serves as the parliamentary preamble explaining the reasons, achievements, and character traits that justify the honoring resolution.

Q5: Can anyone write a funeral resolution?

While anyone can write a memorial document, a true resolution represents a collective organization, committee, board, or society, speaking with institutional authority rather than as an isolated individual.

Q6: What happens to the resolution after the funeral?

One official copy is permanently preserved in the organization's official minute books or historical archives, while a handsomely bound or framed presentation copy is given to the deceased's family as a keepsake.

Q7: How many 'Whereas' clauses should be included?

Most well-crafted resolutions contain between three and five 'Whereas' clauses, providing ample space to honor character, service, and achievements without becoming overly lengthy.

Q8: What kind of paper should a funeral resolution be printed on?

Resolutions should be printed on heavy, 32-lb acid-free archival parchment or linen paper in ivory or cream, enclosed inside a formal presentation folder or elegant certificate binder.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

A funeral resolution is an enduring tribute that permanently enshrines an individual's character and service within an organization's history. By adhering to the time-honored structure of 'Whereas' and 'Resolved' clauses, highlighting verified civic and professional milestones, printing the document on archival parchment with official executive signatures, and delivering it with solemn dignity, you offer the bereaved family a treasured memorial keepsake that honors their loved one's legacy.
