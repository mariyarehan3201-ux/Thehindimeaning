Maryland Asbestos: MDE Regulations & Laws

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 23, 2026

Maryland asbestos regulations are administered primarily by the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) alongside the Maryland Occupational Safety and Health (MOSH) administration. Due to Maryland's extensive industrial, maritime, and shipbuilding heritage along the Chesapeake Bay and Port of Baltimore, historical asbestos usage was widespread across power plants, steel mills, and naval yards. Understanding state licensing, mandatory pre-renovation survey protocols, and strict disposal regulations is vital for property owners, contractors, and industrial workers.

Regulatory Framework of Asbestos Management in Maryland

In Maryland, asbestos oversight is governed by the Code of Maryland Regulations (COMAR 26.11.21), enforced by the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE). This state regulatory framework works in tandem with federal National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants (NESHAP) under 40 CFR Part 61. MDE mandates that prior to any commercial demolition or structural renovation involving specified threshold quantities, property owners must conduct a thorough asbestos survey performed by a certified building inspector.

The state establishes stringent licensing criteria for abatement contractors, project designers, and air sampling professionals. Any contractor undertaking an asbestos abatement project involving more than 160 square feet or 260 linear feet of friable asbestos must submit an official written notification to MDE at least 10 working days prior to initiating containment setup or disturbance activities.

Historical Asbestos Exposure in Maryland Industry

Maryland has a rich maritime and heavy manufacturing past, which unfortunately resulted in significant occupational asbestos exposure throughout the twentieth century. Facilities such as Bethlehem Steel's Sparrows Point plant, the Baltimore Naval Shipyard, Key Highway Shipyard, and numerous electrical power generation stations utilized tons of thermal insulation, pipe lagging, and refractory block containing amosite, crocidolite, and chrysotile asbestos.

Generations of pipefitters, machinists, boilermakers, and longshoremen in the Baltimore metropolitan area experienced severe secondary and primary exposure. Because asbestos-related pulmonary illnesses possess a latency window of twenty to fifty years, Maryland courts and clinical centers—such as Johns Hopkins Medicine and the University of Maryland Marlene and Stewart Greenebaum Comprehensive Cancer Center—continue to diagnose and manage substantial caseloads of malignant pleural mesothelioma and asbestosis.

MDE Asbestos Disposal and Landfill Requirements

Disposal of asbestos waste generated within Maryland is strictly monitored to prevent fugitive fiber dispersion. Friable asbestos waste must be thoroughly wetted, sealed inside two six-mil leak-tight polyethylene bags or rigid leak-proof drums, and clearly labeled with OSHA-mandated warning placards and generator identification tags. Transporters must be licensed hazardous material carriers possessing valid MDE waste transport permits.

Not all Maryland municipal landfills accept asbestos waste. Materials must be routed to approved municipal solid waste (MSW) landfills permitted under COMAR to accept asbestos-containing material, where it must be buried under at least six inches of non-asbestos cover material within 24 hours of arrival to maintain sanitary and air quality integrity.

How to Comply with MDE Asbestos Requirements for Renovation

  1. Schedule a Certified Asbestos Inspection

    Retain an MDE-accredited asbestos building inspector to survey the property and sample all suspect materials prior to demolition or renovation.

  2. Review the Comprehensive Survey Report

    Examine the laboratory results to determine whether identified asbestos materials are friable and exceed state regulatory quantity thresholds.

  3. Submit 10-Day Advance Notification to MDE

    For projects meeting NESHAP thresholds, submit an official notification form to the Maryland Department of the Environment at least 10 business days prior.

  4. Contract an MDE-Licensed Abatement Firm

    Hire an abatement company holding an active MDE contractor license to establish negative pressure containment and execute compliant removal.

  5. Secure Independent Air Clearance Certification

    Ensure an independent industrial hygiene firm conducts phase contrast microscopy (PCM) clearance testing before dismantling containment barriers.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Who enforces asbestos laws in the state of Maryland?

The Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) enforces environmental and disposal rules, while MOSH enforces workplace safety regulations.

Q2: Do homeowners in Maryland need a permit to remove asbestos?

Single-family homeowners performing work on their own private residence are technically exempt from MDE licensing, but professional abatement is strongly advised.

Q3: What is the 10-day notification rule in Maryland?

Contractors must notify MDE in writing at least 10 working days before starting any demolition or major asbestos abatement project.

Q4: Where can asbestos waste be disposed of in Maryland?

Asbestos waste can only be disposed of in designated MDE-permitted landfills equipped to receive and immediately bury regulated asbestos-containing material.

Q5: What Maryland industries had the highest asbestos exposure?

Shipbuilding along the Port of Baltimore, steel manufacturing at Sparrows Point, chemical refineries, and commercial steam power generation plants.

Q6: How do I verify if a Maryland asbestos contractor is licensed?

You can check the active contractor licensing database maintained on the official Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) website.

Q7: Does Baltimore City have separate asbestos requirements?

Baltimore City enforces local building permitting codes that require verified MDE inspection clearance prior to the release of demolition permits.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Compliance with Maryland asbestos regulations protects both project personnel and community air quality. Whether renovating an older historic rowhome in Baltimore or overseeing an extensive commercial demolition project along the Interstate 95 corridor, engaging MDE-certified inspectors and licensed abatement professionals guarantees adherence to state laws and preserves human health.
