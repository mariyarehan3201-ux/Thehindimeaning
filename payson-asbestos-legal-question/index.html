Historical Asbestos Mining and Processing in Gila County

Gila County holds a historic and tragic distinction in American industrial history as the epicenter of Arizona's commercial asbestos mining industry. From the early 1900s through the 1980s, extensive deposits of long-fiber, low-iron chrysotile asbestos were mined throughout the Salt River Canyon, Sierra Ancha mountains, and near Globe and Payson. The asbestos mined in Gila County was prized for military naval electrical insulation, submarine filter media, and high-temperature aerospace applications because it lacked conductive iron impurities.

Mining operations, underground tunneling, and dry crushing at regional milling plants generated massive, uncontained plumes of toxic airborne fibers. Miners, drillers, crushers, and transport truck drivers worked without respirators, inhaling microscopic fibers that caused widespread asbestosis and mesothelioma. Furthermore, crushed asbestos tailings were routinely spread across local roads, driveways, and construction sites as fill material, resulting in persistent community and environmental contamination throughout the region.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is Arizona's statute of limitations for an asbestos lawsuit in Payson?

Arizona law allows two years from the date of formal diagnosis or two years from the date of death to file an asbestos lawsuit.

Q2: Why is Gila County historically significant in asbestos exposure?

Gila County was the center of Arizona's chrysotile asbestos mining industry, with extensive mines in the Salt River Canyon and Sierra Ancha mountains.

Q3: Can miners and millers in the Payson area recover compensation?

Yes, former miners, mill workers, and their surviving families can recover funds from asbestos bankruptcy trusts and civil lawsuits.

Q4: Did road construction in Payson cause environmental asbestos exposure?

Yes, asbestos mine tailings were historically used as road aggregate and construction fill throughout Gila County, causing community exposure.

Q5: Where do Payson residents go for specialized mesothelioma treatment?

Patients typically travel to Phoenix or Tucson to receive specialized care at Banner MD Anderson Cancer Center or the University of Arizona Cancer Center.

Q6: Can family members in Payson sue for secondary asbestos exposure?

Yes, family members who inhaled asbestos fibers from laundering miners' or workers' clothing can pursue take-home exposure claims.

Q7: Do I have to travel to court if I file an asbestos lawsuit in Arizona?

No, asbestos attorneys handle all legal proceedings and will travel to your home in Payson to conduct necessary depositions.

Q8: How much does it cost to hire an asbestos attorney in Arizona?

Attorneys handle asbestos cases on contingency, meaning there are no upfront expenses and legal fees are paid only from successful recoveries.
