How Much Does It Cost to Start a Trampoline Park?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Starting a commercial indoor trampoline park typically costs between $1.2 million and $3.5 million for an independent facility, while launching an established franchise brand (such as Urban Air, Sky Zone, or Altitude Trampoline Park) generally ranges from $1.8 million to over $4.5 million. The substantial capital expenditure is driven by massive commercial real estate leasing (requiring 20,000 to 40,000 square feet with 18 to 22-foot clear ceiling heights), custom engineered trampoline court manufacturing, high-volume HVAC climate infrastructure, amusement liability insurance, and multi-attraction entertainment builds.

Capital Investment Breakdown and Facility Specifications

The physical buildout of an indoor trampoline park represents the bulk of initial capital outlays. Commercial trampoline parks require expansive industrial warehouse or converted big-box retail spaces spanning 25,000 to 35,000 square feet. Crucially, the facility must feature minimum clear ceiling heights of 18 to 22 feet measured beneath steel roof joists and fire sprinkler heads to allow safe aerial bouncing without overhead head impact risks.

Custom manufacturing and installation of continuous interconnected trampoline court systems, heavy-gauge structural steel support frameworks, safety containment netting, and impact-absorbing vinyl-wrapped foam margin pads cost between $500,000 and $1,200,000. In modern family entertainment centers (FECs), open jumping courts are supplemented with high-flying attractions such as foam pits, giant stunt airbags, dodgeball arenas, interactive slam-dunk basketball lanes, and multi-tier ninja warrior obstacle courses.

Franchise Model vs Independent Park Development

Aspiring park operators must decide whether to build an independent brand from scratch or partner with an established national franchise system like Sky Zone, Urban Air Adventure Park, Launch, or Altitude. Purchasing a franchise requires paying an upfront franchise fee of $50,000 to $75,000, alongside ongoing monthly royalty fees of 5 to 7 percent of gross revenues and mandatory marketing ad contributions of 1 to 2 percent. However, franchisees benefit from proven attraction engineering, proprietary safety protocols, national brand recognition, and established wholesale supply chain pricing.

Developing an independent trampoline park eliminates recurring franchise royalties, potentially saving $100,000 to $250,000 annually in bottom-line operational cash flow. However, independent founders must shoulder the complex burdens of direct equipment manufacturing contracts, architectural engineering approvals, custom brand development, local liability waiver legal drafting, and building local brand awareness from scratch without corporate operational handholding.

Liability Insurance, Safety Standards, and Revenue Models

Commercial general liability insurance represents one of the single most formidable ongoing operating expenses in the trampoline park industry. Because amusement trampolines carry inherent risks of fractures, sprains, and spinal injuries, insurance premiums range from $60,000 to over $150,000 annually. Underwriters mandate rigorous adherence to ASTM International standards (specifically ASTM F2970 for trampoline court design and operation), daily documented safety inspections, certified court monitors stationed at every attraction, and legally binding digital liability waiver kiosks.

To maximize profitability, modern trampoline parks diversify far beyond general open-jump admission tickets ($18 to $30 per hour). High-margin ancillary revenue streams are essential to building a lucrative park. Birthday party packages ($250 to $650 per party booking) represent up to forty percent of total park gross revenues, accompanied by branded grip socks sales ($3 to $5 per pair with a 90% profit margin), cafe concessions, recurring monthly VIP membership passes, and corporate group team-building rentals.

How to Plan and Launch a Commercial Trampoline Park

Follow these five fundamental phases to secure commercial financing, lease real estate, and open an indoor adventure park.

  1. Draft a Comprehensive Feasibility and Business Plan

    Analyze local demographics within a 15-mile radius, verifying a dense population of families with children aged 4 to 17.

  2. Secure Commercial Real Estate with Clear Height Clearance

    Negotiate a commercial lease for 25,000 to 40,000 square feet featuring at least 18 to 22 feet of unobstructed vertical ceiling clearance.

  3. Contract with an ASTM-Compliant Equipment Manufacturer

    Commission custom engineered court systems, foam pits, ninja courses, and stunt airbags complying strictly with ASTM F2970 standards.

  4. Procure Amusement General Liability Insurance and Permits

    Pass rigorous municipal building inspections, fire marshal occupancy checks, and bind a comprehensive amusement liability insurance policy.

  5. Recruit Staff and Install Digital Waiver Station Systems

    Hire and train energetic court monitors in CPR and first aid; install self-service touch-screen kiosks for mandatory electronic parental waivers.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How profitable is a commercial trampoline park?

A successful trampoline park can generate $1.5 million to $3.5 million in annual revenue, delivering EBITDA profit margins of 20 to 35 percent.

Q2: What ceiling height is required for a trampoline park?

Facilities must have a minimum clear ceiling height of 18 feet, though 20 to 24 feet is strongly preferred for high-flying stunt attractions.

Q3: How much is liability insurance for a trampoline park?

Commercial general liability insurance typically costs between $60,000 and $150,000 annually due to the physical injury risks inherent to the industry.

Q4: What are the most profitable attractions in a trampoline park?

Birthday party room packages and branded grip socks generate the highest profit margins, followed by ninja obstacle courses and dodgeball.

Q5: How many employees are needed to run a trampoline park?

An average park employs 30 to 60 part-time court monitors and cashiers, managed by two to four full-time general and assistant managers.

Q6: How much does a commercial trampoline court system cost?

A complete custom-engineered interconnected trampoline court system typically costs between $400,000 and $900,000 installed.

Q7: Can adults jump at commercial trampoline parks?

Yes, modern parks cater to both children and adults, hosting adult dodgeball leagues, fitness classes, and corporate team-building events.

Q8: How long does it take to build a trampoline park?

From site selection and lease signing through construction and grand opening, development typically takes between eight and fourteen months.
