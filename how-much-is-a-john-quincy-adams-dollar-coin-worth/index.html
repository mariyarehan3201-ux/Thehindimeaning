How Much Is a John Quincy Adams Dollar Coin Worth?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

If you have come across a golden coin bearing the portrait of the sixth United States president, finding out how much is a John Quincy Adams dollar coin worth will help you determine its true numismatic value. Released by the United States Mint in 2008 as part of the Presidential $1 Coin Program, standard circulated specimens from the Philadelphia (P) and Denver (D) mints are worth exactly face value—$1.00. However, pristine uncirculated mint-state examples sell for $2.50 to $6.00, proof strikes command $4.00 to $10.00, and rare minting errors such as missing edge lettering can fetch between $40 and $150 or more.

Mintage Figures, Circulated Values, and Composition

The John Quincy Adams Presidential $1 Coin was officially released into circulation on May 15, 2008, as the sixth issue in the Presidential Dollar series. The United States Mint struck substantial quantities to facilitate everyday commercial transactions: exactly 57,540,000 coins were produced at the Philadelphia Mint (bearing a small P mint mark on the edge) and 57,720,000 coins were struck at the Denver Mint (bearing a D mint mark). Because over 115 million coins were minted for general circulation, ordinary pocket-change pieces that exhibit minor handling scuffs, scratches, or tarnished toning are worth only their face value of $1.00.

A common misconception among the public is that golden presidential dollars contain real gold bullion. In reality, these coins contain zero gold content. They are struck from a core of 100 percent pure copper, clad with a special manganese-brass outer alloy consisting of 77 percent copper, 12 percent zinc, 7 percent manganese, and 4 percent nickel. This metallic composition imparts a lustrous golden hue designed to prevent corrosion and trigger electromagnetic vending machine sensors without the prohibitive expense of precious metals.

Valuable Mint Errors: Missing and Inverted Edge Lettering

The most significant factor that elevates the value of a 2008 John Quincy Adams dollar is the presence of legitimate mint production errors. With the Presidential Dollar series, the U.S. Mint revived the historical practice of edge incuse lettering. After coins were struck in the main coining press, they passed through a separate edge-lettering machine (the Schuler edging mill) where the year of issuance (2008), mint mark (P or D), national motto E PLURIBUS UNUM, and IN GOD WE TRUST were incised along the coin perimeter rim.

Occasionally, planchets bypassed the edging machine entirely due to mechanical feed jams, entering circulation with completely smooth, plain rims devoid of dates or mottoes. Known to collectors as the Missing Edge Lettering (MEL) error, authentic certified John Quincy Adams smooth edge dollars command between $50 and $150 depending on condition. Conversely, inverted edge lettering (where the edge words read upside-down when looking at the presidential portrait) is not an error; the Mint officially confirmed that edge orientation is completely random (50/50 distribution) during bulk machine processing.

Collector Demand, Grading Standards, and Preservation

For standard, non-error coins, condition is paramount. Professional numismatic grading services like the Professional Coin Grading Service (PCGS) and Numismatic Guaranty Company (NGC) evaluate coins on the Sheldon 70-point scale. A John Quincy Adams dollar graded MS65 (Gem Uncirculated) retails for approximately $3.00 to $6.00, but an exceptional specimen achieving an elusive MS68 grade can fetch $50 to $100 from competitive collectors assembling complete registry sets.

Preserving manganese-brass presidential dollars requires specialized coin care. The chemical composition of the outer cladding is notorious for reacting rapidly to airborne moisture and skin oils from bare fingertips, forming dark brown, oily-looking surface oxidation within months. To maintain pristine mint luster and protect collector value, handle coins only by their outer rims while wearing lint-free cotton gloves, and store valuable specimens in airtight acrylic coin capsules or archival inert Mylar flips.

How to Identify and Evaluate a John Quincy Adams Dollar Coin

Follow these five numismatic steps to determine the mint mark, condition, and potential error value of your dollar coin.

  1. Inspect the Coin Rim for Edge Lettering

    Rotate the coin on its side under bright lighting to locate the incised date (2008), mint mark (P or D), and national mottoes.

  2. Check for Plain Edge Error Varieties

    If the outer rim is completely smooth with no letters, numbers, or stars, you have a valuable Missing Edge Lettering mint error.

  3. Identify the Mint Facility Mark

    Locate the small P (Philadelphia), D (Denver), or S (San Francisco proof) stamped directly between the date and motto on the rim.

  4. Assess Surface Wear and Toning

    Inspect high points on Adams cheeks, coat lapels, and hair curls under a 5x magnifier to verify whether luster remains unbroken.

  5. Protect in Airtight Storage Capsules

    Place uncirculated or error coins in airtight archival holders to prevent manganese-brass oxidation and preserving numismatic value.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is a John Quincy Adams dollar coin made of real gold?

No, it contains zero gold; it is composed of a pure copper core clad with a golden-colored manganese-brass alloy.

Q2: How much is an ordinary circulated John Quincy Adams dollar worth?

In ordinary circulated pocket-change condition, it is worth exactly its legal tender face value of $1.00.

Q3: Where is the mint mark on a John Quincy Adams dollar coin?

The mint mark (P, D, or S) is stamped on the outer edge rim of the coin alongside the year 2008 and national mottoes.

Q4: What makes a John Quincy Adams dollar coin valuable?

Uncirculated condition in high grades (MS67+), proof finishes, and missing edge lettering errors are the primary value drivers.

Q5: How much is a missing edge lettering Adams dollar worth?

A genuine missing edge lettering error coin typically sells for between $50 and $150 depending on condition and certification.

Q6: Can you spend a John Quincy Adams dollar coin in stores?

Yes, it is legal tender in the United States and can be spent at face value in any retail store or bank.

Q7: What does the S mint mark mean on a dollar coin?

The S mint mark designates coins produced at the San Francisco Mint specifically for collectors in official proof sets.

Q8: Why does my dollar coin have upside-down edge lettering?

Edge lettering was applied randomly during minting; upside-down lettering is found on 50% of all coins and carries no extra value.
