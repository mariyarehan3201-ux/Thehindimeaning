Defining Mayor Pro Tem: The Temporary Mayoral Designation

In municipal government administration, local civics, and parliamentary law, Mayor Pro Tem (an abbreviation of the Latin phrase Mayor Pro Tempore, translating literally to 'Mayor for the Time Being') is the official title given to the designated vice-mayor or city council member chosen to serve as acting mayor whenever the sitting mayor is absent, incapacitated, or unavailable.

In municipal governance structures across the United States, a city government cannot cease operation when the elected mayor is out of town on business, undergoing medical care, or recused due to a conflict of interest. The Mayor Pro Tem steps in to preside over city council meetings, sign municipal emergency proclamations, execute municipal ordinances, and represent the city at public ceremonial functions, preserving unbroken municipal governance.

Selection Methods: Council-Manager vs Strong-Mayor Governments

How an individual becomes Mayor Pro Tem depends entirely on the municipal government structure established by the city municipal charter. The two prevailing municipal structures in North America are the Council-Manager system and the Strong-Mayor system.

In a Council-Manager government (used by roughly 60 percent of American municipalities), the city council hires a professional City Manager to manage day-to-day administrative operations. The mayor is primarily a ceremonial leader and council member. In these cities, the city council votes among its own members at the beginning of each legislative year to appoint one council member as Mayor Pro Tem (often rotating the role annually, or awarding it to the council member who received the highest popular vote in the last general election). In a Strong-Mayor system, the Mayor Pro Tem is frequently the elected Vice Mayor or City Council President.

How Municipal City Councils Appoint and Operate with a Mayor Pro Tem

Follow this parliamentary sequence typical of municipal city council reorganizations.

  1. Convene the Annual Municipal Reorganization Meeting

    Following municipal elections, the city council convenes an organizational meeting to swear in newly elected council members.

  2. Open Nominations for Mayor Pro Tem

    The city clerk or presiding mayor calls for nominations from the council floor to fill the seat of Mayor Pro Tem.

  3. Conduct a Formal Roll Call Vote

    Council members cast public roll call votes; a nominee must receive a simple majority vote (e.g., 3 out of 5 council votes) to be confirmed.

  4. Administer the Oath of Office

    The city clerk administers the oath of office, swearing the council member to faithfully execute the duties of Mayor Pro Tem.

  5. Assume the Gavel When Mayor Is Absent

    Whenever the mayor steps away from the dais or is absent from town, the Mayor Pro Tem automatically assumes the center chair and wields the parliamentary gavel.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does mayor pro tem mean?

Mayor pro tem stands for Mayor Pro Tempore ('for the time being'), meaning the deputy mayor or council member who acts as mayor when the mayor is absent.

Q2: Is a mayor pro tem the same as a vice mayor?

Yes. In most city governments, 'Mayor Pro Tem' and 'Vice Mayor' are synonymous titles for the second-in-command municipal leader.

Q3: How is a mayor pro tem chosen?

In most cities, the city council votes among its own elected members to appoint one council member as Mayor Pro Tem, often on a rotating annual basis.

Q4: Does a mayor pro tem get paid more than a council member?

In most smaller cities, no; in larger metropolitan cities, they may receive a modest additional monthly stipend for added mayoral duties.

Q5: What powers does a mayor pro tem have?

When the mayor is absent, they have the legal authority to chair council meetings, sign city ordinances, and declare local states of emergency.

Q6: Can a mayor pro tem veto a bill?

Only in strong-mayor cities where the mayor holds veto power and the city charter explicitly grants acting mayoral veto power during an absence.

Q7: What happens if the mayor resigns or passes away?

The Mayor Pro Tem typically assumes the duties of acting mayor until a special election is held or the city council appoints a permanent replacement.

Q8: Does the mayor pro tem still vote on city council?

Yes. A Mayor Pro Tem remains an elected city council member and retains their full voting rights on all ordinances and municipal budgets.
