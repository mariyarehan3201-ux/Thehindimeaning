Other Driver Was Cited Meaning: Auto Insurance Guide

The Legal and Insurance Meaning of a Traffic Citation

Following an automobile collision, discovering that the other driver was cited meaning indicates that the responding law enforcement officer determined the other motorist violated a specific motor vehicle statute. When police officers arrive at a collision scene, they conduct an impartial preliminary investigation by interviewing drivers, examining vehicle damage geometry, measuring skid marks, and reviewing independent witness statements.

If the officer determines that a motorist's unlawful conduct—such as running a red light, failing to yield right-of-way, following too closely, or exceeding the posted speed limit—directly caused or contributed to the crash, they issue a formal uniform traffic citation (a ticket). While a police officer's traffic ticket is an administrative charge rather than a final civil judgment, it serves as powerful documentary evidence used by insurance adjusters and civil courts to assign legal fault.

How Insurance Adjusters Evaluate Police Citations

Insurance claims adjusters do not automatically pay claims simply because a citation was issued; however, an official traffic citation establishes a strong presumption of negligence. Under the legal doctrine of negligence per se, violating a public safety statute designed to prevent vehicular collisions serves as presumptive proof of negligent driving.

The table below illustrates how different types of police citations directly influence insurance liability determinations and settlement timelines.

Type of Police Citation Issued Specific Traffic Violation Impact on Insurance Fault Determination Typical Claim Resolution Speed
Right-of-Way Violation Running red light, ignoring stop sign Clear 100% liability assigned to cited driver; property damages approved rapidly. Fast (1 to 3 weeks for vehicle repair approval).
Speed & Distance Violation Following too closely (rear-end crash) Virtually indisputable fault; presumption rests against the trailing vehicle. Rapid (insurer accepts full responsibility quickly).
Distracted / Reckless Driving Texting while driving, reckless swerving High likelihood of full liability; opens potential for bad-faith claims if disputed. Moderate (requires corroborating phone records or dashcam).
Equipment / License Violation Driving with expired tags or no insurance Proves administrative non-compliance, but adjuster still analyzes driving mechanics. Variable (may trigger uninsured motorist claim).

Comparative Fault States vs. Contributory Negligence

The practical financial impact of the other driver being cited depends heavily on the tort liability laws of the state where the collision occurred. Different state jurisdictions apply contrasting rules when evaluating comparative driver fault.

The table below summarizes how state fault frameworks evaluate insurance claims when one driver receives an official citation.

Legal Fault Framework Governing State Principle Effect on Compensation When Other Driver Is Cited
Pure Comparative Fault (e.g., CA, FL, NY) Damages apportioned by exact fault percentage Even if you are found 10% responsible, you recover 90% of your damages from the cited driver.
Modified Comparative Fault (e.g., TX, OH, IL) 50% or 51% bar rule prevents recovery if mostly at fault As long as the cited driver is found 51% or more liable, you recover your medical and vehicle damages.
Contributory Negligence (e.g., VA, NC, MD, AL) Any victim fault (even 1%) completely bars recovery The other driver's citation is critical proof to demonstrate you had 0% fault in the accident.

In contributory negligence jurisdictions, securing an official certified copy of the accident report showing the other driver received a moving violation ticket is essential for preventing the adverse insurer from denying the entire claim.

Furthermore, what happens in traffic court can influence your civil injury settlement. If the cited driver pays the ticket by mail or pleads guilty in municipal court, their formal admission of guilt can often be entered into the civil record as an admission against interest, preventing them from subsequently fabricating a contradictory version of events.

How to Obtain and Use an Accident Police Report Showing a Driver Citation

A step-by-step practical checklist for securing police collision reports and leveraging citations in insurance claims.

  1. Collect the Police Incident Report Number

    Before leaving the crash scene, obtain the responding officer's name, badge number, and incident report receipt card.

  2. Order the Official Certified Crash Report

    Contact the local police department records bureau five to ten business days post-accident or download the report via state online portals.

  3. Inspect the Contributing Factors Section

    Check boxes detailing driver citations, moving violation statute codes, and the officer's narrative description of the crash sequence.

  4. Forward the Report to Both Insurance Adjusters

    Transmit digital PDF copies of the report to your claims adjuster and the at-fault driver's insurance carrier to expedite liability acceptance.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does the other driver being cited mean their insurance must pay?

In the vast majority of cases yes; while insurers conduct independent reviews, an official citation creates strong proof of fault that leads to claim approval.

Q2: Can an insurance company deny a claim if their driver got a ticket?

Yes, an insurance company can attempt to dispute liability if their driver contests the ticket in court or if they argue you shared partial comparative fault.

Q3: What if the other driver goes to court and beats the ticket?

If the traffic ticket is dismissed due to technicalities, civil insurance adjusters still evaluate physical evidence, but your negotiation leverage may be slightly weakened.

Q4: Where do I find if the other driver was cited on the report?

Look in the 'Violations', 'Citations Issued', or 'Contributing Circumstances' section on the second or third page of the standard state crash report.

Q5: Should I notify my own insurance if the other driver was cited?

Yes, standard auto insurance policies require prompt notification of all accidents, regardless of who was at fault.

Q6: What if both drivers were cited by police?

If both drivers received tickets, insurance adjusters will negotiate shared comparative fault percentages, reducing payouts accordingly.

Q7: Does a police citation prove fault in a civil lawsuit?

A citation is strong evidence, but in civil court, the jury or judge makes the final independent legal determination of liability based on all evidence.

Q8: How long does it take for a police crash report to be ready?

Most municipal and state police departments process and release official accident reports within three to ten business days.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Discovering that the other driver was cited provides substantial leverage when resolving property damage and bodily injury claims with insurance carriers. While insurance adjusters conduct independent reviews, a formal police citation creates a powerful legal presumption of negligence. Securing the official police report promptly ensures your rights are protected throughout the claims process.
