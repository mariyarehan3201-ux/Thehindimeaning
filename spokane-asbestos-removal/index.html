Spokane Asbestos Removal

Informational

Managing Spokane asbestos removal requires strict adherence to Washington state environmental statutes and the specialized regulatory directives of the Spokane Regional Clean Air Agency (SRCAA). Homeowners, commercial landlords, and demolition contractors operating throughout Spokane County must follow rigorous pre-renovation survey protocols, submit mandatory project notifications, and engage certified abatement professionals.

Spokane Regional Clean Air Agency (SRCAA) Jurisdiction and Rules

Unlike many regions where asbestos enforcement is solely managed at the state level, Spokane County is governed by the Spokane Regional Clean Air Agency under Regulation I, Article VIII. Under SRCAA rules, any property owner or contractor undertaking demolition, remodeling, or maintenance that disturbs suspect building materials must commission an asbestos survey performed by an AHERA-certified building inspector before commencing work.

A mandatory Notice of Intent filing must be submitted to SRCAA along with required filing fees prior to initiating any asbestos project. For standard non-emergency projects, a ten-calendar-day waiting period is strictly enforced. Initiating work without an approved SRCAA notification or failing to conduct an AHERA survey can result in substantial civil financial penalties exceeding $10,000 per violation day.

Certified Abatement Practices and Regional Contractor Pricing

Hiring a Washington Department of Labor & Industries (L&I) certified asbestos abatement contractor ensures that toxic materials are extracted under strict containment. Similar to regulatory protocols enforced on projects like asbestos removal long island contracts, Spokane remediation teams seal work spaces with poly sheeting, run HEPA negative pressure air machines, and perform aggressive post-abatement air clearance testing before issuing clean re-occupancy certificates.

Contractor rates across the Inland Northwest reflect local labor markets and hazardous disposal transport fees. Removing friable furnace duct wrap or acoustic popcorn ceiling texture typically costs between $6.00 and $12.00 per square foot, while non-friable exterior siding or vinyl floor tile removal ranges from $3.50 to $7.50 per square foot.

Disposal Facilities and Regional Mineral Health Awareness

Proper containment of chrysotile asbestos removal waste requires disposal at permitted regional facilities. Within Spokane County, asbestos waste cannot be brought to regular transfer stations. All double-bagged, leak-tight 6-mil plastic packages must be accompanied by an official SRCAA Asbestos Waste Shipment Record and transported to the Graham Road Landfill in Medical Lake.

Public awareness regarding asbestos health risks is exceptionally high throughout Eastern Washington due to the historical transport of vermiculite attic insulation from nearby Libby, Montana. Spokane homeowners discovering vermiculite Zonolite insulation in attics should treat it as hazardous and engage certified contractors who can access the national Zonolite Attic Insulation Trust for partial financial reimbursement.

How to Plan and Execute Asbestos Removal in Spokane WA

Follow these five regulatory steps to survey, permit, and remove asbestos in accordance with SRCAA regulations.

  1. Schedule an AHERA-Certified Building Survey

    Hire an independent Washington-certified AHERA building inspector to collect bulk samples and create an official survey report.

  2. Submit SRCAA Notice of Intent Application

    File the online Notice of Intent with the Spokane Regional Clean Air Agency and pay the required project notification fee.

  3. Observe the Mandatory 10-Day Waiting Period

    Allow the legally required ten calendar days for agency review before touching or disturbing any building materials on site.

  4. Execute Professional Abatement Under Containment

    Have a certified Washington L&I abatement company construct negative pressure enclosures and extract the hazardous materials wet.

  5. Transport Waste to Graham Road Landfill

    Deliver double-bagged waste accompanied by an official Waste Shipment Record to the authorized Graham Road landfill in Medical Lake.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Who regulates asbestos removal projects in Spokane County?

The Spokane Regional Clean Air Agency (SRCAA) and the Washington State Department of Labor & Industries (L&I) jointly regulate asbestos in Spokane County.

Q2: Is an AHERA survey mandatory before residential remodeling in Spokane?

Yes, SRCAA regulations mandate a certified AHERA asbestos survey prior to any renovation, remodel, or demolition that disturbs suspect materials.

Q3: How long is the notification waiting period before work can begin?

SRCAA enforces a strict ten-calendar-day waiting period after filing the official Notice of Intent before any physical abatement can start.

Q4: Can homeowners remove asbestos from their own home in Spokane?

Owner-occupants of single-family residences can perform removal, but they must still file an SRCAA notification and comply with strict wet disposal rules.

Q5: Where can asbestos waste be legally disposed of in Spokane?

All regulated asbestos waste must be delivered with a Waste Shipment Record to the Graham Road Landfill located in Medical Lake, Washington.

Q6: How much does an AHERA asbestos survey cost in Spokane?

A targeted residential pre-renovation survey typically costs $350 to $650, while whole-house pre-demolition surveys range from $750 to $1,500.

Q7: What building materials in Spokane homes most often contain asbestos?

Common materials include popcorn ceiling texture, gravity heating duct insulation, sheet vinyl backing, 9x9 floor tiles, and vermiculite attic insulation.

Q8: What are the penalties for unpermitted asbestos removal in Spokane?

SRCAA issues civil financial penalties that can exceed $10,000 per violation day for unpermitted removal, missing surveys, or illegal open dumping.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Successfully executing an asbestos removal project in Spokane requires close coordination with the Spokane Regional Clean Air Agency, securing an accredited AHERA survey, and utilizing state-licensed L&I contractors. By respecting mandatory waiting periods, securing official clearance documentation, and following proper disposal pathways, property owners protect community health and avoid costly environmental citations.
