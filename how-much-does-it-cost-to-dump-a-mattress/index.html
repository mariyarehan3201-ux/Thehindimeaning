How Much Does It Cost to Dump a Mattress?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Discarding an old mattress can prove surprisingly challenging due to municipal waste restrictions, bulky transport logistics, and environmental recycling regulations. If you are preparing to dispose of a worn mattress and matching box spring, typical costs range from completely free through municipal bulk collection up to $150 or more for on-demand private junk hauling services. Dropping off a mattress yourself at a local municipal landfill or transfer station generally incurs a gate fee between $15 and $60 per piece.

Municipal Landfill Gate Fees and Transfer Station Rates

Taking your mattress directly to a county landfill or regional solid waste transfer station represents the most common self-haul disposal route. Landfills charge either a flat per-unit fee or bill based on vehicle gross weight at the scale house, often subject to a minimum weight assessment of $25 to $50. In many jurisdictions, landfills assess a separate special handling surcharge of $15 to $35 specifically for mattresses because internal steel coil springs frequently snag, tangle, and severely damage heavy landfill compactors and shredder blades.

To circumvent landfill equipment jams, an increasing number of states and municipalities mandate that discarded bedding be routed to specialized mattress recycling facilities. States participating in Mattress Recycling Council programs (such as California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon) permit residents to drop off clean, unsoiled mattresses at designated recycling collection hubs at zero out-of-pocket cost, as disposal expenses are pre-funded by statutory recycling fees collected at point-of-sale.

Private Junk Hauling and Curbside Collection Alternatives

For homeowners and apartment dwellers lacking a pickup truck or the physical strength to maneuver a bulky king mattress down stairwells, professional junk removal services provide effortless curbside or in-home removal. National haulers such as 1-800-Got-Junk, College Hunks Hauling Junk, and local independent trash haulers charge between $75 and $160 for a single mattress pickup, with rates scaling down per item if bundled with other household junk.

Alternatively, many municipal sanitation departments offer residents scheduled bulky item pickups once or twice per year at nominal rates or free with residential utility bills. However, strict municipal rules often dictate that mattresses must be fully encased in heavy plastic zippered disposal bags before collection to safeguard sanitation workers against potential bedbug infestations, biological contaminants, and wet weather saturation.

Environmental Recycling Benefits and Illegal Dumping Penalties

Over twenty million mattresses enter North American landfills annually, consuming significant municipal airspace because their porous polyurethane foam and resilient steel spring frameworks resist natural compaction. Responsible recycling diverts over seventy-five percent of mattress materials by weight: steel springs are melted down for scrap metal, polyurethane foam is shredded into carpet underlayment, and natural cotton and wood framing are converted into industrial boiler fuel and mulch.

Temptations to bypass legitimate disposal fees by dumping mattresses in commercial dumpsters, vacant lots, or rural road shoulders carry harsh financial and criminal penalties. Municipal codes treat mattress abandonment as misdemeanor illegal dumping, with statutory fines ranging from $500 to over $2,500 along with mandatory community service and restitution for cleanup expenses. Utilizing certified municipal programs remains the safe, ethical, and legal standard.

How to Responsibly Dispose of an Old Mattress in Five Steps

Follow this practical guide to safely, affordably, and compliantly dispose of or recycle an unwanted mattress.

  1. Inspect Condition for Donation Suitability

    Check if the mattress is free of stains, rips, structural sagging, and odors; if so, contact local charities or shelters for free pickup.

  2. Check New Mattress Retailer Haul-Away Options

    If purchasing a new mattress, verify whether the retailer offers complimentary or low-cost haul-away service under state recycling laws.

  3. Schedule a Municipal Bulk Trash Collection

    Contact your city waste department or check your utility account to schedule an authorized bulk collection day or claim a disposal voucher.

  4. Encase Mattress in Sealed Plastic Disposal Wrap

    Purchase an inexpensive polyethylene mattress bag from a hardware store to encase the mattress securely against moisture and pests.

  5. Transport to Landfill or Hire Certified Haulers

    Load the wrapped mattress into a truck bed tied down safely for landfill drop-off, or schedule a certified junk hauling service.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I throw a mattress in a regular apartment dumpster?

No, property managers strictly prohibit placing mattresses in communal trash dumpsters, and doing so will result in substantial lease violation fines.

Q2: Why do landfills charge extra to dump a mattress?

Landfills assess surcharges because steel mattress springs wrap around heavy compactor wheels and shredder teeth, causing catastrophic mechanical breakdowns.

Q3: Does Goodwill or Salvation Army accept used mattresses?

Most charitable thrift stores do not accept used mattresses due to sanitary regulations and bedbug concerns, unless the mattress is brand new in factory packaging.

Q4: How much does a mattress disposal bag cost?

Heavy-duty plastic mattress disposal bags cost between $6 and $15 at home improvement centers or moving supply retailers.

Q5: Can I burn an old mattress in a backyard fire pit?

No, burning mattresses is illegal in nearly all jurisdictions; burning polyurethane foam and synthetic textiles releases toxic cyanide and dioxin fumes.

Q6: What parts of a mattress can be recycled?

Up to 80% of a mattress can be recycled, including steel innersprings, polyurethane foam, cotton batting, and structural timber frames.

Q7: Is dumping a mattress on the side of the road illegal?

Yes, dumping a mattress on public or private property without authorization is illegal dumping punishable by fines between $500 and $2,500.

Q8: Can I dismantle a mattress myself to save money?

Yes, you can cut open the fabric covering, pull out the polyurethane foam for trash, and take the steel spring frame to a scrap metal recycler for cash.
