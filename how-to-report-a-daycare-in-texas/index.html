How to Report a Daycare in Texas?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Entrusting the safety, emotional well-being, and daily care of your infant or toddler to a professional childcare facility is one of the most vulnerable decisions any parent makes. When you observe severe staff-to-child ratio violations, hazardous structural conditions, unsanitary feeding practices, unlicensed operations, or suspect physical abuse and neglect, taking swift, decisive legal action is paramount to protect vulnerable children. In the Lone Star State, childcare facilities are strictly monitored and enforced by the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC) Child Care Regulation (CCR) division.

The Texas Child Care Regulatory Authority: HHSC and DFPS

To file an effective, impactful complaint against a Texas childcare facility, you must understand the state's jurisdictional division between regulatory licensing enforcement and emergency child abuse investigations. All licensed childcare centers, registered family child care homes, and licensed before-and-after school programs in Texas are legally regulated by Child Care Regulation (CCR), a specialized regulatory division operating under the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC) governed by Chapter 42 of the Texas Human Resources Code.

CCR enforces the Texas Minimum Standards for Child-Care Centers, an exhaustive regulatory framework governing strict adult-to-child staffing ratios, background check clearances, playground impact cushioning, nutritional standards, and medication administration logs. However, if your report involves active, imminent physical abuse, sexual abuse, severe neglect, or immediate endangerment of a child, the investigation is spearheaded simultaneously by the Texas Department of Family and Protective Services (DFPS) Statewide Intake and local municipal law enforcement agencies.

The Legal Standards: Texas Staff-to-Child Ratios and Violations

The most common and dangerous regulatory failure in Texas daycares centers on illegal staff-to-child ratios. When centers cut staffing to maximize corporate profits, chaotic environments emerge where accidents, choking, and altercations occur unseen. Texas Minimum Standards (40 TAC § 746.1601) establish strict statutory caps on how many children a single caregiver may supervise based on the chronological age of the children in the classroom.

For infants (ages 0 to 11 months), Texas law mandates a strict 1:4 ratio with a maximum group size of ten infants. For toddlers aged twelve to seventeen months, the ratio is 1:5; for eighteen to twenty-three months, it is 1:9. For two-year-olds, the ratio is 1:11, expanding to 1:15 for three-year-olds, 1:18 for four-year-olds, and 1:22 for five-year-olds. If you walk into your child's infant nursery and observe a single caregiver alone with eight infants, the facility is committing an egregious Level 1 regulatory violation that warrants an immediate complaint.

How to File: Hotlines, Evidence Gathering, and Anonymity

When reporting a daycare, you have the legal right to submit your complaint completely anonymously under Texas law. However, providing your contact information to the state investigator is highly beneficial, as it allows the inspector to call you for follow-up details and provide you with a written copy of the final investigative findings. Your identity as a reporting source is strictly protected under Texas confidentiality statutes and will never be disclosed to the daycare owner, director, or staff.

To file your complaint, you can call the Texas Child Care Regulation Hotline at 1-800-862-5252, contact your regional municipal HHSC field office, or submit a detailed electronic report through the Texas Child Care Licensing Online Complaint portal. Compile concrete, factual evidence: document exact dates, specific times, names of teachers present, classroom room numbers, photographs of physical hazards (such as broken fencing or contaminated food), and written journals of unexplained bruises or injuries. Once filed, state law mandates that an inspector conduct an unannounced, surprise physical inspection of the facility within one to five business days.

How to File a Regulatory Complaint Against a Texas Daycare

A complete step-by-step procedure to document violations, submit formal reports to HHSC, and monitor state inspection investigations.

  1. Document the Specific Incident and Violations

    Record the exact date, time, classroom name, employee names, and nature of the violation (e.g., illegal infant ratios, unattended children, injuries).

  2. Gather Corroborating Physical Evidence

    Take clear photos of physical injuries, photograph broken facility equipment or hazards, and screenshot conflicting parent-app communication messages.

  3. Submit Complaint to Texas Child Care Regulation (CCR)

    Call the official state hotline at 1-800-862-5252 or submit a detailed digital report through the Texas HHS Child Care Online Complaint system.

  4. Report Serious Abuse to DFPS and Local Police

    If physical abuse, sexual trauma, or acute child endangerment is suspected, call the Texas Abuse Hotline at 1-800-252-5400 and dial 911 immediately.

  5. Track the State Inspection and Review Public Findings

    Monitor your assigned state case number; once the unannounced inspection concludes, review the public deficiency report on the Texas HHS website.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I report a Texas daycare anonymously?

Yes. Texas law allows you to file complaints completely anonymously; if you choose to give your name to investigators, it is strictly confidential.

Q2: What is the phone number to report a daycare in Texas?

For licensing and ratio violations, call the HHSC Child Care Regulation Hotline at 1-800-862-5252; for severe abuse/neglect, call 1-800-252-5400.

Q3: How quickly does Texas investigate a daycare complaint?

State investigators conduct an unannounced surprise site inspection within 24 hours for Priority 1 safety threats, and within 5 business days for standard violations.

Q4: What is the legal ratio for infants in a Texas daycare?

Texas Minimum Standards strictly require 1 caregiver for every 4 infants (1:4 ratio), with an absolute maximum classroom group size of 10 infants.

Q5: How can I check a Texas daycare's past inspection records?

Visit the Texas Health and Human Services 'Search Texas Child Care' public portal to view any licensed center's 3-year history of violations and inspections.

Q6: What happens if a daycare is caught with illegal ratios?

The state issues an official citation/deficiency, places the center on probation, increases surprise inspection frequency, and can revoke their license for repeat offenses.

Q7: Is it illegal to operate an unlicensed daycare in Texas?

Yes. Caring for four or more unrelated children on a regular basis without a state license or registration is a Class B misdemeanor in Texas.

Q8: Can a daycare retaliate against my family for reporting them?

While centers may attempt to expel children, filing false criminal reports or retaliatory actions can expose the facility to severe civil lawsuits and state penalties.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Reporting a non-compliant or abusive childcare center is an essential civic and parental responsibility that protects not only your own child, but dozens of defenseless children who cannot speak for themselves. By understanding the Texas Minimum Standards, documenting factual evidence, and engaging the HHSC Child Care Regulation division, you hold commercial daycare operators accountable and ensure that every child learns and plays in a secure, nurturing environment.
