Royal History and Enduring Symbolism of the Ruby

Long before white diamonds gained dominance in the twentieth century, rubies reigned as the ultimate symbol of love and sovereignty. In ancient Sanskrit, ruby was named Ratnaraj, meaning King of Precious Stones. Monarchs throughout European and Asian history set rubies into coronation crowns and royal betrothal rings to signify divine favor and invincible courage.

A ruby engagement ring carries profound emotional resonance. The vivid red color represents the beating heart, blood-deep commitment, and inextinguishable romantic desire. Couples who exchange ruby rings celebrate a love that is bold, adventurous, and resilient—refusing to settle for ordinary conventions in favor of a vibrant, deeply meaningful partnership.

Metaphysical Power, Prosperity, and Protection

Beyond romantic devotion, rubies have carried powerful protective lore across cultures. Ancient warriors wore rubies into battle to ensure invulnerability, while medieval European nobility believed that wearing a ruby on the left hand close to the heart brought peace, preserved wealth, and warned wearers of impending danger by darkening slightly in color.

In spiritual traditions, ruby stimulates the root chakra, anchoring life force energy and infusing the wearer with zest, motivation, and physical vitality. When placed in an engagement ring, the ruby serves as an energetic shield against negativity, jealousy, and relational burnout, continually refueling mutual attraction and shared enthusiasm over decades of marriage.

How to Choose and Care for a Ruby Engagement Ring

Follow this expert buying and maintenance guide to select an authentic, high-value ruby ring.

  1. Prioritize Rich Color Over Flawless Clarity

    Unlike diamonds where clarity leads value, ruby value is dictated by rich red color saturation without excessive brown or orange tones.

  2. Inspect Inclusions as Proof of Natural Origin

    Natural rubies almost always contain rutile silk and mineral crystals; an entirely flawless cheap ruby is almost certainly synthetic glass or flame-fusion corundum.

  3. Avoid Lead-Glass Filled Rubies

    Ensure your gem certificate specifies traditional heat treatment only; lead-glass filled composite rubies degrade quickly when exposed to household lemon juice or cleaners.

  4. Choose a Secure Setting Style

    Set your ruby in durable 14k or 18k gold prongs, a halo of pavé diamonds, or a modern bezel setting that guards the girdle against accidental impacts.

  5. Clean Regularly with Warm Soapy Water

    Maintain peak red brilliance by soaking the ring monthly in warm water with mild dish soap, scrubbing gently with an ultra-soft toothbrush.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does a ruby engagement ring mean?

A ruby engagement ring symbolizes passionate romantic love, royal heritage, fierce fidelity, courage, and lifelong good fortune.

Q2: Are rubies good for everyday wear?

Yes. Measuring 9.0 on the Mohs scale, rubies are second only to diamonds in hardness, making them outstanding for daily engagement wear.

Q3: What is pigeon blood ruby?

Pigeon blood ruby refers to the most prized ruby color: a vivid, intensely saturated red with a subtle bluish undertone and strong fluorescence.

Q4: Are ruby engagement rings more expensive than diamonds?

Top-quality untreated natural rubies can cost significantly more per carat than white diamonds due to their extreme scarcity.

Q5: Can rubies get wet?

Yes. Natural corundum rubies are completely water-safe and can be cleaned regularly with warm soapy water.

Q6: What does giving a ruby ring to someone mean?

Giving a ruby ring represents giving one whole heart, pledging eternal devotion, and promising passionate loyalty.

Q7: What metal looks best with a ruby engagement ring?

Yellow gold enhances ruby rich warmth for a vintage royal look, while platinum and white gold provide a dramatic, modern contrast.

Q8: How do you tell if a ruby is real or fake?

Real rubies feel heavy, scratch glass, show natural microscopic inclusions, and emit an intense red glow under long-wave ultraviolet light.
