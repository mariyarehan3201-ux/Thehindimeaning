Asbestos Arkansas

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

In the state of Arkansas, asbestos management and environmental remediation are governed by strict statutory frameworks enforced primarily by the Arkansas Division of Environmental Quality under the Arkansas Department of Energy and Environment. Codified within the Arkansas Pollution Control and Ecology Commission Regulation Number 21, these comprehensive mandates oversee the inspection, demolition, renovation, packaging, and permanent landfill disposal of regulated asbestos-containing materials. Whether managing commercial property renovations in Little Rock, industrial plant retrofits in Fort Smith, or agricultural facility overhauls in Northwest Arkansas, compliance protects public health and legal liability.

Arkansas Division of Environmental Quality and Regulation Number 21 Mandates

Arkansas environmental protection statutes mandate that any commercial, institutional, or multi-family residential demolition or significant renovation must comply with Arkansas Pollution Control and Ecology Commission (APC&EC) Regulation No. 21. Regulated under delegated authority from the United States Environmental Protection Agency National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants (NESHAP), Regulation 21 establishes rigorous standards for training, licensing, certification, notification, and work practices across all asbestos projects throughout the state.

Under Regulation 21, building owners and environmental contractors must submit an official Notice of Intent (NOI) to the Arkansas Division of Environmental Quality (DEQ) Office of Air Quality at least ten working days prior to initiating any demolition activity, regardless of whether asbestos is known to be present. For renovation projects involving regulated asbestos-containing material (RACM), the 10-day notification threshold is triggered whenever disturbed quantities meet or exceed 160 square feet, 260 linear feet, or 35 cubic feet. Submitting the NOI requires payment of state administrative review fees, detailed project descriptions, licensed contractor credentials, and verified disposal site designations.

Arkansas Professional Certification Categories and Training Protocols

To legally perform asbestos-related disciplines in Arkansas, individuals and environmental contracting firms must hold active credentials issued by the DEQ. Arkansas recognizes several distinct certification categories: Asbestos Abatement Contractor, Asbestos Supervisor, Asbestos Abatement Worker, Asbestos Inspector, Asbestos Management Planner, and Asbestos Project Designer. Each category requires initial comprehensive course training through an EPA-accredited training provider, passing a rigorous examination, and completing annual refresher coursework to maintain active status.

A certified Arkansas Asbestos Inspector must conduct an exhaustive, non-destructive survey of all accessible structural components before any architectural renovation or demolition commences. The inspector collects bulk material samples following EPA AHERA protocols and submits them to an accredited Polarized Light Microscopy (PLM) or Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM) laboratory. If materials contain greater than one percent asbestos by weight, a certified Project Designer or licensed Contractor develops a project containment plan adhering to Arkansas engineering standards, including HEPA negative air filtration and three-stage worker decontamination chambers.

Waste Packaging, Permitted Class 1 Landfills, and Geological Considerations

Disposal of regulated asbestos waste generated in Arkansas must strictly comply with APC&EC Regulation No. 22 (Solid Waste Management) and Regulation 21. Regulated debris must be thoroughly saturated with surfactant-treated water, double-bagged in 6-mil polyethylene bags or sealed within leak-tight drums, labeled with OSHA warning statements, and transported exclusively by licensed haulers carrying an Arkansas Waste Shipment Record. Debris may only be deposited in permitted Class 1 municipal solid waste landfills that have explicit authorization from DEQ to maintain dedicated asbestos monofill cells.

Arkansas industrial history also encompasses unique exposure risks in timber processing mills, legacy bauxite mining and refining operations in Saline County, and electric utility generation plants along the Arkansas River. Industrial facility managers must maintain accurate historical asbestos inventories for boilers, steam piping, refractory brick, and cooling tower assemblies to ensure compliance during scheduled turnarounds and decommissioning.

How to Comply with Arkansas Asbestos Abatement Regulations

A structured workflow for property owners and contractors executing compliant asbestos inspections, notifications, and remediation under Arkansas DEQ Regulation 21.

  1. Commission an Arkansas-Certified Asbestos Building Survey

    Retain a DEQ-certified Asbestos Building Inspector to thoroughly survey the facility and collect representative bulk material samples before any renovation or demolition begins.

  2. Review Laboratory Results and Quantify RACM Thresholds

    Examine PLM laboratory reports to determine if materials contain over 1% asbestos and calculate whether project volume exceeds 160 square feet or 260 linear feet.

  3. Submit Arkansas DEQ Notice of Intent (NOI) Ten Days Prior

    Complete and file the official Regulation 21 Notice of Intent with the DEQ Office of Air Quality at least ten working days before starting work, accompanied by mandatory state review fees.

  4. Execute Containment Remediation with Licensed Contractors

    Deploy an Arkansas-licensed asbestos abatement contractor to erect negative-pressure enclosures, utilize wet scraping methods, and package waste in double 6-mil poly bags.

  5. Transport Waste to Permitted Arkansas Class 1 Landfills

    Transport packaged waste with a completed Waste Shipment Record directly to an authorized Arkansas Class 1 landfill, archiving countersigned disposal slips for permanent records.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What state agency regulates asbestos in Arkansas?

The Arkansas Division of Environmental Quality (DEQ), operating under the Arkansas Department of Energy and Environment, enforces state asbestos regulations under APC&EC Regulation No. 21.

Q2: When is an asbestos notification required in Arkansas?

A Notice of Intent must be filed with DEQ at least 10 working days prior to any commercial demolition, or any renovation involving at least 160 square feet, 260 linear feet, or 35 cubic feet of regulated asbestos material.

Q3: Are single-family homes in Arkansas exempt from asbestos regulations?

While single-family residential owner-occupants performing their own renovations have limited exemptions under federal NESHAP, demolishing a home or hiring contractors often invokes municipal codes and DEQ disposal rules.

Q4: What training is required to become an asbestos worker in Arkansas?

An asbestos abatement worker in Arkansas must complete an EPA-accredited 32-hour initial training course, pass a written exam, apply for a DEQ certification card, and complete an 8-hour refresher annually.

Q5: Where can asbestos waste be disposed of in Arkansas?

Asbestos waste must be deposited exclusively at permitted Class 1 municipal solid waste landfills in Arkansas that are specifically licensed by DEQ to operate dedicated asbestos disposal cells.

Q6: What is Arkansas Regulation Number 21?

Regulation Number 21 is the comprehensive Arkansas state administrative code that defines work practices, contractor licensing, notification rules, and disposal mandates for asbestos abatement.

Q7: How long must asbestos records be kept in Arkansas?

Arkansas regulations and federal standards mandate retaining asbestos survey reports, air monitoring logs, and waste shipment records for a minimum of thirty years to protect against latent liabilities.

Q8: What are the penalties for illegal asbestos removal in Arkansas?

DEQ can issue formal administrative orders with civil monetary penalties reaching up to ten thousand dollars per day per violation, alongside stop-work orders and potential criminal enforcement.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Managing asbestos in Arkansas requires strict alignment with APC&amp;EC Regulation No. 21, proactive communication with the Division of Environmental Quality, and engagement of certified environmental specialists. By honoring mandatory 10-day notification windows, implementing verified engineering containment, and securing permanent disposal manifests, property owners preserve ecological integrity, ensure worker safety, and eliminate severe administrative liabilities.
