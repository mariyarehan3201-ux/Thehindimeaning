How to Get a Possession Charge Dismissed?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Being arrested and charged with unlawful possession of a controlled substance, prescription medication, or prohibited contraband carries serious legal penalties, including felony convictions, prison sentences, mandatory driver's license suspensions, and a permanent criminal record. However, possessing contraband is an evidentiary element that prosecutors must legally prove under strict constitutional standards. From exposing illegal police searches and traffic stops under the Fourth Amendment to demonstrating a lack of knowledge or actual control over items found in shared vehicles or homes, proven defense strategies routinely result in dismissed possession charges.

Challenging Fourth Amendment Search and Seizure Violations

The vast majority of controlled substance possession charges originate from police encounters during roadside traffic stops, pedestrian stop-and-frisk interactions, or residential search warrant executions. Under the Fourth Amendment of the United States Constitution, every individual possesses the fundamental right to be secure in their person, houses, papers, and effects against unreasonable searches and seizures. Unless police officers obtain a valid search warrant issued by a neutral magistrate upon probable cause, or an established legal exception applies, any physical search is presumed unconstitutional.

Common exceptions claimed by law enforcement include consent, search incident to lawful arrest, the automobile exception, inventory searches, and plain view observations. A seasoned criminal defense attorney scrutinizes the arresting officer's conduct to expose unconstitutional overreach. For example, if an officer prolongs a standard traffic stop beyond the time required to complete the mission of issuing a citation—specifically to summon a drug-detection canine without independent reasonable suspicion—the prolonged detention violates Supreme Court precedent established in Rodriguez v. United States. When the defense files a Motion to Suppress Evidence and prevails, all recovered contraband is excluded from the trial, forcing an immediate dismissal.

Actual Versus Constructive Possession Defense Strategies

In criminal law, possession is divided into two distinct legal concepts: 'actual possession' and 'constructive possession.' Actual possession requires the state to prove that the illicit substance was discovered on the defendant's physical person—such as inside a pocket, taped to the body, or held in the hand. Conversely, constructive possession applies when contraband is found in a general area over which multiple people have access, such as the glove compartment of a vehicle carrying passengers, the trunk of a borrowed car, beneath a couch in a shared apartment, or inside common kitchen cabinets.

To secure a conviction under a constructive possession theory, prosecutors must prove two distinct elements beyond a reasonable doubt: first, that the defendant had actual dominion and control over the specific location where the contraband was found, and second, that the defendant had subjective, knowing awareness of the contraband's presence and illicit nature. Mere proximity to drugs is completely insufficient under state and federal law to sustain a conviction. If an individual is riding as a passenger in a friend's vehicle where drugs are hidden beneath a seat, or living with roommates where contraband is found in a shared living room, a skilled attorney can establish reasonable doubt regarding knowledge and ownership, compelling a dismissal.

Crime Lab Chemical Testing, Chain of Custody, and Diversion Programs

Another powerful avenue for dismissing possession charges lies in the scientific analysis of the seized material. Roadside chemical field test kits used by police officers are notorious for producing false positives, misidentifying household items like soap, candy, baking soda, and breath mints as illicit narcotics. The prosecution cannot proceed to trial based solely on a roadside field test; they must submit the physical evidence to an accredited state crime laboratory for definitive Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (GC-MS) analysis. If the state crime lab report reveals that the substance is not a regulated drug, or if the net chemical weight falls below statutory thresholds, the case must be dismissed.

Furthermore, prosecutors must maintain an unbroken, verifiable chain of custody for the seized substance from the moment of arrest to courtroom presentation. If evidence room logs reveal missing tamper-evident seals, unaccounted storage gaps, or discrepancies in weight measurements, the physical evidence can be excluded. Finally, many jurisdictions offer first-time offenders access to Pre-Trial Intervention (PTI), Drug Court, or deferred adjudication programs. Participants complete counseling, submit to clean drug screenings, and perform community service in exchange for an agreed, automatic dismissal of all criminal charges with prejudice.

How to Fight and Dismiss a Criminal Possession Charge

A comprehensive procedural guide to examining evidence, challenging searches, and securing a dismissal of possession charges.

  1. Retain Experienced Criminal Defense Counsel

    Hire a defense attorney immediately to assert your rights, handle arraignment proceedings, and prevent self-incriminating statements to investigators.

  2. Examine the Constitutionality of the Search

    Analyze whether law enforcement officers possessed a valid search warrant, lawful consent, or legitimate probable cause under Fourth Amendment standards.

  3. Assert a Constructive Possession Defense

    If the contraband was located in a shared vehicle, apartment, or common container, demonstrate that you lacked exclusive control or knowledge of its presence.

  4. Demand Certified Crime Laboratory Testing

    Challenge roadside field test kits and compel prosecutors to produce certified forensic GC-MS laboratory reports confirming the exact chemical identity.

  5. File Pre-Trial Motions to Suppress Evidence

    Submit formal legal motions to suppress unconstitutionally seized contraband, arguing for full exclusion of physical evidence before trial.

  6. Apply for Pre-Trial Diversion or Drug Court

    If suppression is unavailable, enroll in a diversionary or deferred prosecution program that guarantees complete charge dismissal upon program completion.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can a possession charge be dismissed if the drugs were not on my body?

Yes, when drugs are found in shared locations like car consoles or apartments, prosecutors must prove constructive possession. Establishing that you did not know the drugs were there or did not control them often leads to dismissal.

Q2: What happens if police conducted an illegal search without a warrant?

If the court rules that law enforcement conducted an unconstitutional search violating the Fourth Amendment, all seized contraband is suppressed, leaving the state with no evidence and forcing a dismissal.

Q3: Are roadside drug test kits reliable in court?

No, roadside field tests are presumptive screening tools prone to false positives. Courts require formal forensic testing from an accredited state crime laboratory to establish chemical identity.

Q4: What is pre-trial diversion for drug possession?

Pre-trial diversion is a court program for eligible first-time offenders. If you complete substance education, community service, and random screenings, the prosecutor completely dismisses the charge.

Q5: Can a possession charge be dropped if someone else claims the drugs?

If a co-defendant or third party provides a credible, sworn affidavit admitting sole ownership and possession of the contraband, prosecutors will frequently drop charges against innocent bystanders.

Q6: Does an unlawful dog sniff result in a dismissed possession charge?

Yes, under the Supreme Court's Rodriguez ruling, if officers prolong a traffic stop without independent reasonable suspicion to wait for a K-9 drug dog, the search is illegal and evidence is suppressed.

Q7: Will a dismissed drug charge show up on my background check?

While the arrest record may initially appear, a dismissed charge does not count as a conviction. Most states permit filing for an expungement or record sealing to permanently delete the arrest.

Q8: How long does it take to get a possession charge dismissed?

Dismissing a possession charge generally takes between two and six months, depending on crime lab testing turnaround times, pre-trial motion hearings, or the duration of a diversion program.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Facing a criminal charge for contraband or drug possession does not mean a conviction is inevitable. By identifying unconstitutional police searches, raising powerful constructive possession defenses in shared environments, demanding verified forensic laboratory testing, and utilizing diversionary dismissal programs, defendants can successfully overcome possession charges and safeguard their personal freedom and professional futures.
