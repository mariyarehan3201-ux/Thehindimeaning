How Much Is a Home Inspection in Ohio?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

When buying a residential property in the Buckeye State, knowing how much is a home inspection in Ohio is a critical step in your due diligence budget. In Ohio, a comprehensive general home inspection for an average single-family property under 2,000 square feet typically costs between $325 and $475. Larger homes measuring 2,500 to 4,000 square feet, historical properties, and multi-family units range from $500 to $750. In addition, essential ancillary evaluations common to Ohio—including continuous radon monitoring, wood-destroying insect (termite) inspections, and sewer lateral scopes—often bring total inspection expenditures to $600 to $950.

State Licensing Standards and General Inspection Fees

Under Ohio Revised Code Chapter 4764, all home inspectors operating in Ohio must be licensed through the Ohio Division of Real Estate and Professional Licensing. To achieve and maintain licensure, Ohio inspectors must complete 80 hours of pre-licensing education, execute parallel supervised inspections, pass the National Home Inspector Examination (NHIE), maintain extensive errors and omissions insurance, and undergo regular background checks. This strict state regulatory standard ensures that your $350 to $500 investment delivers a standardized, objective evaluation of all accessible structural and mechanical systems.

A standard Ohio residential home inspection covers hundreds of critical checkpoints over a three- to four-hour physical walkthrough. The inspector assesses the roof covering, flashings, gutters, foundation walls, attic insulation, electrical panels and branch wiring, plumbing distribution lines, water heaters, and heating and cooling (HVAC) systems. Given Ohio freezing winters and humid summers, inspectors pay special attention to furnace heat exchanger integrity, exterior drainage grading, and basement foundation walls to identify signs of hydrostatic pressure or frost heave.

Crucial Ohio Ancillary Add-Ons: Radon, Pests, and Sewer Scopes

Ohio unique geology and aged urban infrastructure make ancillary specialty testing highly advisable during real estate transactions. According to the Ohio Department of Health, elevated levels of radioactive radon gas are prevalent across the state, with more than one in three Ohio homes testing above the EPA action guideline of 4.0 picocuries per liter (pCi/L). Adding an electronic continuous radon monitor test costs $100 to $175 when bundled with a general inspection, providing critical leverage to request a seller-funded mitigation system ($1,000 to $1,800).

Older municipalities like Cleveland, Columbus, Cincinnati, Akron, and Dayton feature historic clay and cast-iron sewer lines prone to tree root intrusion and pipe collapse. A sewer scope inspection involves inserting a high-definition fiber-optic camera through the main waste pipe to the municipal sewer main, costing $150 to $250. Wood-Destroying Insect (WDI) termite inspections—mandatory for VA and FHA mortgage financing—cost $75 to $125. When bundled together, home inspection companies typically offer complete buyer protection packages for $650 to $950.

Negotiation Leverage, Repair Credits, and Inspection Contingencies

A home inspection is not merely a diagnostic safety health check; it functions as your primary financial bargaining instrument during the Ohio standard purchase contract due diligence window. Under standard Ohio Association of Realtors purchase agreements, buyers possess a contractual inspection period—typically 10 to 14 calendar days—to inspect the home, request repairs, demand monetary escrow credits, or terminate the purchase contract with a full refund of earnest money deposits.

Uncovering major deferred capital maintenance items—such as a cracked heat exchanger in an aging furnace ($3,500 to $6,000), an end-of-life asphalt shingle roof ($8,000 to $15,000), or horizontal foundation shear cracking requiring steel I-beam reinforcement ($5,000 to $12,000)—frequently saves buyers thousands of dollars. Presenting an objective, licensed inspector report alongside licensed contractor repair estimates allows your real estate agent to negotiate substantial purchase price reductions before closing.

How to Maximize Your Ohio Home Inspection Experience

Follow these five homeowner steps to prepare for your Ohio property inspection and protect your financial investment.

  1. Verify Ohio State Inspector Licensure

    Confirm that your inspector holds an active license from the Ohio Division of Real Estate and carries errors and omissions insurance.

  2. Order High-Risk Ancillary Tests

    Bundle a 48-hour continuous radon monitor and sewer lateral camera scope, especially for homes built before 1990.

  3. Attend the Final Inspection Walkthrough

    Arrive for the final 60 minutes of the inspection to walk the property with the inspector and observe functional shutoffs.

  4. Distinguish Major Defects from Cosmetic Wear

    Focus your negotiation requests on major structural, safety, electrical, and HVAC defects rather than minor cosmetic imperfections.

  5. Act Within Your Contractual Contingency Window

    Submit your written repair requests and signed inspection addenda to the seller before your contractual deadline expires.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is a home inspection legally required in Ohio?

A home inspection is not legally required by the state, but it is strongly recommended, and mortgage lenders often require specific pest/WDI inspections.

Q2: Are home inspectors licensed in Ohio?

Yes, Ohio requires all professional home inspectors to be licensed through the Ohio Division of Real Estate and Professional Licensing.

Q3: Why is radon testing so important in Ohio?

Ohio has some of the highest radon levels in the United States, with over 35% of tested homes exceeding the EPA action guideline of 4.0 pCi/L.

Q4: How much does a sewer scope cost in Ohio?

A sewer scope inspection in Ohio typically costs between $150 and $250 when added to a standard home inspection.

Q5: Who pays for the home inspection in Ohio?

The home buyer pays for the home inspection and any optional ancillary testing during their contractual contingency period.

Q6: How long does an Ohio home inspection take?

A standard single-family home inspection takes between two and a half and four hours depending on square footage, age, and crawlspaces.

Q7: Can a buyer back out of a contract after an Ohio inspection?

Yes, if the purchase contract contains an active inspection contingency, the buyer can terminate and recover earnest money within the deadline.

Q8: What is a WDI inspection in Ohio?

A Wood Destroying Insect inspection evaluates properties for active termites, carpenter ants, and wood-boring beetles, costing $75 to $125.
