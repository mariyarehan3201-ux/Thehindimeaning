What Does Deferred Mean on a VA Claim?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

When you open a Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) disability rating decision letter or check your status on VA.gov and see a medical condition labeled "Deferred," it means the VA has not yet made a final decision to grant or deny that specific disability. A deferred status is neither an approval nor a rejection; rather, it is a formal administrative pause indicating that the Veterans Benefits Administration requires additional medical evidence, an updated Compensation and Pension (C&P) examination, or military personnel records before rendering a legally sound rating decision.

The Administrative Mechanics of a Partial VA Rating Decision

When a military veteran submits a comprehensive VA disability claim encompassing multiple medical conditions—such as tinnitus, knee osteoarthritis, lumbar spine strain, and post-traumatic stress disorder—the VA does not hold back the entire claim package until every single condition is fully developed. Instead, under modern VA claims processing modernization directives, the rating authority issues a "Partial Rating Decision."

In a partial decision, the VA rates and awards monthly disability compensation for all conditions that possess sufficient clinical evidence, service connection nexus letters, and completed C&P exams. Any remaining conditions that lack complete medical records, require a secondary medical opinion, or need clarification regarding in-service exposure (such as toxic burn pits under the PACT Act) are marked as "Deferred." This ensures that veterans begin receiving monthly financial benefits and healthcare access for granted disabilities immediately, rather than waiting months for secondary investigations to conclude.

Common Reasons the VA Defers a Disability Claim

A claim condition is deferred when the VA Rating Veterans Service Representative (RVSR) encounters an informational gap that prevents them from assigning a legal diagnostic code or disability percentage under the 38 CFR Part 4 Schedule for Rating Disabilities. The most common trigger is an incomplete or contradictory Compensation and Pension (C&P) exam. If an examiner failed to record range-of-motion measurements with a goniometer or neglected to provide an etiology nexus opinion, the rater sends the file back for a medical addendum.

Another frequent catalyst for deferral is the need to obtain historical government records. The VA has a statutory "Duty to Assist" under federal law, obligating them to obtain military treatment records, personnel service records, line-of-duty determinations, or Social Security Administration disability files. If those archives take months to respond, the condition is deferred while the VA continues pursuing the records on the veteran's behalf.

Impact on Disability Compensation and Backpay

The most reassuring aspect of a deferred VA claim is that it does not harm your effective filing date. In VA disability jurisprudence, monthly financial compensation is paid retroactively back to your original date of claim submission or intent to file (ITF) date. If a condition is deferred for six months while the VA schedules an additional specialist exam and is subsequently granted at 30 percent, the veteran receives a lump-sum retroactive backpay deposit covering all six months of delay.

Furthermore, receiving an initial partial grant alongside deferred claims provides immediate relief. A veteran awarded 50 percent for PTSD while back strain is deferred will immediately begin receiving monthly 50-percent compensation checks on the first of the subsequent month. When the deferred back strain is eventually granted, the VA recalculates the combined disability rating using non-linear VA math and adjusts the monthly payout upward, issuing retroactive backpay for the difference.

How to Manage and Track a Deferred VA Disability Claim

Follow these tactical steps to assist the VA, track developmental progress, and secure a favorable decision on a deferred claim.

  1. Read Your VA Decision Letter Narrative Closely

    Turn to the narrative explanation section of your rating letter to read the exact legal reason why the condition was deferred.

  2. Check VA.gov for Scheduled C&P Exam Notifications

    Log into your VA.gov account and check for messages from third-party exam contractors like QTC, VES, or Optum Serve.

  3. Attend All Scheduled In-Person or Telehealth Exams

    Never skip a follow-up examination; failing to attend a C&P exam for a deferred condition will result in an immediate denial.

  4. Submit Missing Private Treatment Records

    Upload recent medical treatment notes, MRI scans, or private doctor nexus letters through the VA.gov Evidence Submission portal.

  5. Consult with an Accredited Veterans Service Officer (VSO)

    Work with an accredited VSO (VFW, DAV, American Legion) who can access the internal VA VBMS portal to track examiner notes.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does deferred mean my VA claim was denied?

No. Deferred means the VA has not made a decision yet because they need more evidence, records, or a medical exam before deciding.

Q2: Do I get backpay for deferred VA claims?

Yes. If your deferred condition is eventually approved, you will receive retroactive backpay dating back to your original claim or Intent to File date.

Q3: How long does a deferred VA claim take to resolve?

Most deferred claims take between 30 to 90 days to resolve, depending on how quickly new C&P exams and medical records are received.

Q4: Will I have to do another C&P exam for a deferred claim?

Often yes. The VA frequently defers claims specifically to order an in-person exam, a specialist consultation, or a records-only ACE review.

Q5: Can I get paid for other claims while one is deferred?

Yes. The VA issues a partial rating decision, paying you immediately for approved conditions while the deferred condition continues processing.

Q6: What is an ACE exam in a deferred claim?

An Acceptable Clinical Evidence (ACE) exam is a records-only review where a medical professional reviews your file without requiring an in-person visit.

Q7: What should I do if my VA claim is deferred?

Review your decision letter to see what the VA needs, watch your mail/phone for C&P exam scheduling, and submit any requested medical evidence.

Q8: Can a deferred claim be closed without warning?

No. The VA has a legal Duty to Assist and must notify you of any required steps before closing or issuing a final decision on the claim.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Seeing "Deferred" on a VA rating decision is not a denial; it is an active administrative step ensuring your claim receives complete developmental review under the law. By issuing partial decisions for ready conditions while gathering medical opinions for deferred ones, the VA ensures veterans receive immediate benefits while protecting their right to thorough evidence gathering and retroactive backpay.
