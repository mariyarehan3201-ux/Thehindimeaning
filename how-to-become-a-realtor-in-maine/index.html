How to Become a Realtor in Maine?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Embarking on a career as a licensed real estate professional and Realtor in Maine allows you to guide buyers and sellers through one of the country's most scenic and distinctive property markets. Overseen by the Maine Real Estate Commission (MREC) under the Department of Professional and Financial Regulation, the licensing structure in Maine follows a unique tiered progression starting with the Sales Agent license. This complete guide details the prerequisite education, Pearson VUE examinations, agency affiliation, and Realtor designation requirements.

The Maine Real Estate Commission's Tiered Licensing Framework

To practice real estate in Maine, candidates must understand the licensing hierarchy regulated by the Maine Real Estate Commission (MREC). Unlike states that issue a permanent general salesperson license immediately, Maine utilizes a progressive, tiered licensing structure designed to ensure comprehensive mentorship during an agent's foundational career stages. The initial license issued to all newcomers is the Sales Agent License.

The Sales Agent license is an entry-level, non-renewable credential valid for exactly two years. During this 24-month period, the sales agent must complete the subsequent 60-hour Associate Broker course and upgrade to an Associate Broker License before their initial license expires. The Associate Broker credential represents a renewable, mid-level license that permits unsupervised real estate practice. After gaining two years of active experience as an Associate Broker, a licensee can complete the Designated Broker course to qualify as a full agency Designated Broker, authorized to manage an independent real estate firm.

Pre-Licensing Coursework, Pearson VUE Exam, and Brokerage Affiliation

The journey begins with enrolling in the mandatory 55-hour Sales Agent Course approved by MREC. Delivered by state-certified real estate schools online or in classroom settings, the curriculum covers property ownership rights, land use controls, contract law, real estate finance, agency relationships, valuation, and Maine-specific real estate statutes. Students must achieve a final grade of at least 75 percent on the school's comprehensive proctored course examination to receive their official course completion certificate.

Armed with course clearance, candidates schedule their state licensing exam administered by Pearson VUE. The examination consists of a general national section and a Maine law section. Candidates must achieve a scaled passing score of at least 75 percent on both portions. Following examination success, the candidate must affiliate with a licensed Maine real estate agency. The Designated Broker of the agency must sign the licensing application, confirming active supervision, before the state formally issues the Sales Agent license.

Joining the National Association of Realtors and Building Your Practice

While holding a Maine Sales Agent license authorizes you to practice legally as a real estate agent, the title "Realtor" is a trademarked professional designation reserved exclusively for members of the National Association of Realtors (NAR). After joining your sponsoring brokerage, you will join your local board of Realtors (such as the Greater Portland Board of Realtors or Mid-Coast Board of Realtors), the Maine Association of Realtors (MAR), and NAR. Membership grants access to the Maine Real Estate Information System (Maine Listings MLS) and requires upholding the strict NAR Code of Ethics.

Launching a thriving real estate practice in Maine requires embracing the state's diverse geographical markets. From urban condos in Portland and coastal vacation homes in Bar Harbor to rural acreage in Aroostook County, successful agents develop localized property marketing plans. Setting up lead generation pipelines, mastering comparative market analysis (CMA) reports, investing in professional architectural photography, and holding open houses establish consistent client relationships and drive referral business.

How to Obtain Your Maine Real Estate Sales Agent License

Follow these steps to complete required coursework, pass the Pearson VUE exam, and activate your Maine real estate license.

  1. Confirm Minimum Statutory Qualifications

    Verify you are at least 18 years old and possess a high school diploma or certified equivalent GED certificate.

  2. Complete the 55-Hour Maine Sales Agent Course

    Enroll in an MREC-approved real estate school and pass the course final exam with a score of 75 percent or higher.

  3. Pass the Pearson VUE State Licensing Examination

    Schedule and pass both the national and Maine-specific portions of the Pearson VUE exam within one year of finishing your course.

  4. Complete Fingerprint-Based Background Check

    Schedule biometric fingerprinting through the state-approved vendor for criminal background processing by the Maine State Police.

  5. Affiliate with a Licensed Maine Real Estate Brokerage

    Select a real estate agency whose Designated Broker agrees to sign your application and provide active professional supervision.

  6. Submit Application to MREC and Join NAR

    Submit your official application and one hundred dollar fee to MREC, then join your local Realtor board to access the MLS.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the initial real estate license called in Maine?

The entry-level license in Maine is the Sales Agent license, which is a non-renewable credential valid for two years.

Q2: What happens if a Maine sales agent does not upgrade within two years?

If a sales agent fails to complete the 60-hour Associate Broker course within two years, their license lapses and they must restart the process.

Q3: How much does it cost to get a real estate license in Maine?

Total initial startup costs typically range from eight hundred to one thousand two hundred dollars, covering courses, exams, fees, and fingerprinting.

Q4: What score is required to pass the Maine real estate exam?

Candidates must achieve a minimum scaled score of 75 percent on both the national and Maine state law portions of the exam.

Q5: Is Maine real estate licensing reciprocal with other states?

Yes, Maine offers reciprocity to actively licensed agents from other jurisdictions who pass the Maine-specific law examination.

Q6: What is the difference between a real estate agent and a Realtor?

A real estate agent is state-licensed, while a Realtor is a licensed agent who is a dues-paying member of the National Association of Realtors.

Q7: Can I practice real estate part-time in Maine?

Yes, Maine law permits part-time real estate practice, provided your supervising Designated Broker permits part-time agent affiliation.

Q8: What MLS system is used in Maine?

Maine operates a centralized statewide Multiple Listing Service known as Maine Listings (Maine Real Estate Information System).

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Becoming a licensed real estate agent and Realtor in Maine offers immense personal autonomy and financial rewards in a state celebrated for its natural beauty and quality of life. By mastering the 55-hour Sales Agent course, passing the Pearson VUE exam, partnering with a supportive Designated Broker, and advancing to your Associate Broker license, you establish a resilient, highly respected real estate career in the Pine Tree State.
