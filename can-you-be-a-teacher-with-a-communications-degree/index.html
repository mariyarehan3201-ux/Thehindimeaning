Can You Be a Teacher with a Comm Degree?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: October 1, 2026

Knowing whether you can be a teacher with a communications degree opens exciting career opportunities for graduates looking to transition into elementary, secondary, or post-secondary education. While traditional public school teaching certification requires specialized pedagogical training, almost every state provides alternative teacher certification pathways that welcome communications majors. Your strong verbal skills, media literacy, and interpersonal training make you a compelling candidate for the classroom.

Alternative Teacher Certification Pathways for Non-Education Majors

Public school systems throughout the nation are facing widespread teacher shortages across critical subject disciplines. To recruit talented professionals into the classroom, state departments of education have developed alternative teacher certification routes. Rather than returning to college for a four-year undergraduate education degree, individuals holding a Bachelor of Arts or Bachelor of Science in Communications can complete an accelerated post-baccalaureate educator preparation program (EPP) in nine to twelve months while earning a full teacher salary. Similar to non-traditional credentialing explored in how to become a PE teacher without a degree guides, state alternative routes bridge academic degrees into professional teaching credentials.

To qualify for an alternative certification program, communications graduates must possess a bachelor degree from a regionally accredited college or university with a minimum cumulative grade point average (typically 2.50 to 3.00 on a 4.0 scale). Candidates must also pass their state-required basic skills exam (such as the Praxis Core) and specific subject area knowledge assessments. Communications coursework in public speaking, mass media, rhetoric, and interpersonal communication aligns directly with secondary English Language Arts, Speech, Journalism, and Theater teaching endorsements.

Translating Communications Coursework to Classroom Subjects

A communications degree provides versatile, transferable subject knowledge. At the high school level, state licensing boards frequently endorse communications majors to teach English Language Arts (ELA), Speech and Debate, Journalism, Creative Writing, and Media Literacy. In many districts, communications graduates spearhead student news broadcasts, yearbook publications, and forensic debate teams, positioning them as valuable assets for school administration. Reviewing health-related degree transitions like can you be a NICU nurse with an associate degree illustrates how specialized coursework unlocks high-demand public sector roles.

Furthermore, communications graduates excel in classroom management because their academic training emphasizes verbal articulation, nonverbal cues, and conflict resolution. In elementary education settings, where teachers must deliver integrated curriculum across reading, writing, social studies, and math, communications graduates use storytelling and interactive presentation techniques to maintain student engagement.

Comparing Degree Adaptability and Post-Secondary Instruction

Just as bachelor degree holders assess diverse career trajectories through what can you do with a bachelor degree in nursing guides, communications graduates enjoy broad occupational versatility across public and private educational environments. Outside traditional public K-12 classrooms, private academies, parochial schools, and charter school networks operate outside state civil service mandates, allowing them to hire communications graduates immediately without state credentials.

For those interested in teaching at the community college or university level, earning a Master of Arts or Master of Science in Communication (with at least eighteen graduate credit hours in the discipline) qualifies graduates to serve as adjunct professors or full-time lecturers in introductory public speaking and mass communications. How to Transition from a Communications Degree into Teaching

Step-by-step instructions for enrolling in alternative certification and becoming a licensed classroom teacher.

  1. Step 1: Obtain Official College Academic Transcripts

    Order official transcripts from your undergraduate institution to verify bachelor degree conferral and confirm your GPA meets state requirements.

  2. Step 2: Research State-Approved Alternative Certification Programs

    Select a state-accredited educator preparation program (EPP) that offers pathways in English Language Arts, Speech, or Elementary Education.

  3. Step 3: Pass Required Subject Content Exams

    Register for and pass the state-mandated Praxis or state-specific content exam in your chosen subject area to demonstrate subject competency.

  4. Step 4: Secure an Intern or Probationary Teaching Position

    Apply for open teaching positions in local school districts with your statement of eligibility, securing a paid classroom teaching role.

  5. Step 5: Complete Field Mentorship and Earn Standard Certification

    Complete one academic year of supervised classroom teaching and coursework modules to receive your standard, renewable state teaching license.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can you teach high school English with a communications degree?

Yes, communications graduates frequently qualify for English Language Arts (ELA) teaching endorsements by passing their state subject content exam.

Q2: What subjects can a communications major teach?

Communications majors can teach English Language Arts, Speech and Debate, Journalism, Media Literacy, Theater Arts, and Elementary Education.

Q3: Do you have to go back to college for four years to teach?

No, alternative certification programs allow non-education degree holders to earn their state teaching license in nine to twelve months while working as teachers.

Q4: Can I teach in private schools without a teaching certificate?

Yes, private and independent schools are not bound by state licensing mandates and frequently hire communications graduates directly.

Q5: Can I teach at a community college with a bachelor's in communications?

Community colleges require a master degree with at least eighteen graduate credit hours in the specific discipline being taught.

Q6: Are teachers with communications degrees paid the same as education majors?

Yes, public school salary schedules are determined strictly by degree level (Bachelor vs Master) and years of accredited teaching service.

Q7: What exams are required for alternative teacher certification?

Candidates typically take a basic academic skills test (like Praxis Core) and a specific subject matter competency exam (like Praxis II).

Q8: Do substitute teaching hours count toward teacher certification?

Many state programs accept long-term substitute teaching experience toward required classroom practicum hours, accelerating licensing.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

You can readily become a teacher with a communications degree. State alternative certification programs allow you to step into the classroom as a salaried teacher of record while completing pedagogical training in under a year. Your skills in public speaking, media literacy, active listening, and conflict management give you a distinct advantage in classroom leadership. Choose your preferred subject endorsement, complete your state background checks and exams, and launch a rewarding teaching career that inspires the next generation.
