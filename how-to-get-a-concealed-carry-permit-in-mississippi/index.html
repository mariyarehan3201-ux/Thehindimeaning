How to Get a Concealed Carry Permit in Mississippi?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Mississippi provides its citizens with some of the most robust Second Amendment protections in the United States, allowing permitless carry for loaded handguns in holsters, purses, and motor vehicles. Despite this legal baseline, the state maintains a comprehensive two-tier licensing system overseen by the Mississippi Department of Public Safety Firearms Permit Unit. Citizens can choose between a standard Firearms Permit and the highly sought-after Enhanced Firearms Permit. Earning the enhanced endorsement dramatically broadens legal carry rights into restricted public spaces such as university campuses, state government buildings, and courthouses.

Mississippi Concealed Carry Framework: Standard Versus Enhanced Endorsements

Under Mississippi Code Annotated Section 45-9-101, the state establishes a concealed carry licensing process that serves as the foundation for state and multi-state handgun carry. While Mississippi enacted permitless carry in 2016 for handguns carried in sheaths, holsters, backpacks, and handbags, possessing a state-issued credential provides essential benefits. Beyond establishing multi-state reciprocity with over thirty states, Mississippi permits clearly define legal boundaries for carry in sensitive geographical locations.

The cornerstone of Mississippi firearm policy is the distinction between a Standard Firearms Permit and an Enhanced Firearms Permit. The standard permit requires background vetting and fingerprinting but does not mandate formal firearms training. In contrast, the Enhanced Endorsement, established under Mississippi Code Section 97-37-7, requires the applicant to complete an eight-hour certified firearms training course led by an approved instructor. Upon completion, an 'Enhanced' or 'IC' (Instructor Certified) sticker or notation is placed on the permit card, legally overriding many conventional location prohibitions and authorizing carry in locations where standard carry is strictly banned.

Eligibility Criteria and the Enhanced Firearms Training Course

To qualify for a Mississippi concealed carry permit, an applicant must be a resident of Mississippi for at least twelve months prior to application (waivable for active-duty military or holders of valid out-of-state permits), at least twenty-one years of age (or eighteen years of age for active-duty military personnel), and free from felony convictions or active indictments. Applicants must not suffer from physical or mental infirmities that prevent safe firearm operation, have no history of chronic alcohol or drug dependency within the preceding three years, and have no domestic violence convictions.

To obtain the coveted Enhanced Endorsement, applicants must complete an eight-hour training program taught by a certified Mississippi Law Enforcement Officers Training Program instructor, NRA certified instructor, or United States military firearms instructor. The curriculum mandates classroom instruction covering Mississippi deadly force statutes, justifiable homicide laws, defensive mindset, and weapon retention. Furthermore, candidates must participate in live-fire range qualification, firing a minimum of fifty rounds at designated distances while demonstrating safe handling, loading, malfunction clearing, and accuracy.

Application Steps, DPS Stations, and Renewal Procedures

The Mississippi Department of Public Safety manages all concealed carry permit applications through regional Mississippi Highway Safety Patrol troop substations and designated Driver Service Stations across the state. First-time applicants must apply in person. The applicant must bring a completed notarized application form, a valid Mississippi driver's license reflecting their current physical address, and the original certified training completion certificate if applying for the enhanced status.

At the DPS station, technicians take the applicant's official digital photograph and capture electronic fingerprints for state and FBI criminal history record checks. The standard application fee is eighty dollars for individuals under sixty-five, or twenty-four dollars for seniors aged sixty-five and older. Processing times generally range from thirty to forty-five days. The resulting permit is valid for five years. Renewals can be completed online or at regional driver service kiosks for a reduced fee of fifty dollars.

How to Apply for a Mississippi Concealed Carry Permit

A step-by-step roadmap to earning a Mississippi Standard or Enhanced Concealed Firearms Permit through the Department of Public Safety.

  1. Verify Residency and Age Qualifications

    Confirm you have resided in Mississippi for at least 12 months (or qualify for a military/relocation waiver), are 21 years old (or 18 for military), and have no felony records.

  2. Complete an Enhanced Firearms Training Course

    For the enhanced permit, enroll in an 8-hour certified safety course that includes classroom instruction on Mississippi self-defense law and a 50-round live-fire qualification.

  3. Complete and Notarize the Official DPS Application

    Download the Mississippi Firearms Permit application from the DPS website and have your signature notarized prior to your station visit.

  4. Visit a Regional Highway Safety Patrol Substation

    Present your notarized application, Mississippi driver's license, training certificate, and required fee at a local Mississippi Highway Patrol driver service location.

  5. Submit Digital Fingerprints and Photographs

    Allow DPS staff to capture your biometric fingerprints and take an official passport-style photograph for your permit credentials.

  6. Receive Your Five-Year Permit by Mail

    Wait approximately 30 to 45 days for background checks to conclude; your laminated permit card with the enhanced sticker will be delivered to your mailing address.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Do you need a permit to carry a gun in Mississippi?

No, Mississippi allows permitless concealed carry for handguns carried in holsters, purses, or motor vehicles. However, a permit is required for multi-state reciprocity and to carry in restricted locations.

Q2: What does an enhanced concealed carry permit mean in Mississippi?

An Enhanced Permit indicates that you completed an approved eight-hour training course with live fire. Under Mississippi Code Section 97-37-7, it legally authorizes you to carry in locations normally restricted, including college campuses and government meeting rooms.

Q3: How much does a Mississippi concealed carry permit cost?

The initial state application fee is 80 dollars for applicants under 65 years old and 24 dollars for seniors 65 and older. Five-year renewals cost 50 dollars.

Q4: Where can you NOT carry even with an enhanced permit in Mississippi?

Even with an enhanced permit, firearms remain strictly prohibited in courtrooms during judicial proceedings, active jury rooms, federal facilities, detention centers, and private establishments with posted armed security guards.

Q5: How long is a Mississippi firearms permit valid?

A Mississippi concealed carry permit is valid for five years from the date of issuance. You will receive a renewal notice prior to expiration.

Q6: Does Mississippi require firearms training for a standard permit?

No, the standard Mississippi concealed carry permit does not require firearms training. Only the Enhanced Endorsement requires completion of the eight-hour certified training course.

Q7: Can veterans get the enhanced endorsement without taking a class?

Honorably discharged military veterans or active-duty personnel with documented small arms qualification can often submit their military training records to satisfy the enhanced training requirement.

Q8: How long does it take to receive a Mississippi CCW permit?

State law allows the Mississippi Department of Public Safety up to 45 days to process applications and background checks, though many permits are issued within three to four weeks.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Securing a Mississippi concealed carry permit, particularly with the Enhanced Endorsement, represents the gold standard in lawful self-defense for Magnolia State residents. By taking the eight-hour certified training course, mastering Mississippi statutory limits on force, and completing DPS background processing, gun owners gain exceptional legal latitude to protect themselves across educational institutions, public buildings, and reciprocal states nationwide.
