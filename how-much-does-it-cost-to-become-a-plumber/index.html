How Much Does It Cost to Become a Plumber?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does it cost to become a plumber requires evaluating the training path chosen: a paid union/non-union apprenticeship vs. vocational trade school, alongside essential hand tools, PPE gear, and state journeyman licensing fees. On average, becoming a licensed plumber costs between $1,500 and $15,000. Going the union apprenticeship route (UA) costs as little as $1,000 to $2,500 out-of-pocket while earning a full-time paycheck, whereas private trade schools charge $5,000 to $16,000 in upfront tuition.

Apprenticeship vs. Vocational Trade School Pathways

The plumbing profession offers one of the few high-earning career paths where individuals 'earn while they learn' without accumulating student loan debt. The premier pathway is a registered 4 to 5-year plumbing apprenticeship sponsored by the United Association (UA union) or the Plumbing-Heating-Cooling Contractors Association (PHCC). In an apprenticeship, employers cover 100% of classroom instructional tuition.

Apprentices work full-time under master plumbers, earning 45% to 55% of journeyman wages to start ($18 to $24 per hour), with guaranteed annual wage increases. The apprentice only pays for basic hand tools, work boots, and annual union dues ($800 to $2,000 total). Alternatively, attending a 6 to 12-month private plumbing trade school or technical college costs $5,000 to $16,000 in tuition. While trade schools provide immediate shop training, graduates must still complete a 3 to 4-year field apprenticeship before sitting for licensing exams.

Initial Tool Kits, Work Gear, and PPE Expenses

Every apprentice plumber must invest in personal hand tools and durable personal protective equipment (PPE). Plumbing contractors provide heavy machinery, threaders, drain cameras, and pro-press power tools, but mechanics must supply their own hand tools. An entry-level apprentice tool set costs between $600 and $1,500.

Essential tools include 10-inch and 14-inch pipe wrenches, tongue-and-groove channel lock pliers (models 420 and 440), tubing cutters, PEX crimpers, torpedo levels, cast iron soil pipe cutters, copper deburring tools, and a heavy-duty tool bag (like Veto Pro Pac). Sturdy steel-toe or composite-toe waterproof work boots (such as Red Wing or Thorogood) cost $150 to $300, and mandatory safety glasses, cut-resistant gloves, and hard hats add $100 to $200.

Journeyman and Master Plumber Licensing Exams

Upon completing 4 to 5 years (typically 8,000 hours of documented on-the-job training and 500 to 900 hours of classroom code study), apprentices are eligible to take the State Journeyman Plumber Examination. State licensing exam application fees range from $100 to $250, and the multi-hour exam tests knowledge of the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), isometric isometric pipe sizing, drainage venting, and backflow prevention.

State journeyman license fees cost $75 to $200 annually. After practicing as a licensed journeyman for 2 to 3 years, plumbers can sit for the Master Plumber or Plumbing Contractor exam ($200 to $400), which authorizes them to pull municipal permits, start their own plumbing business, and supervise apprentices. Plumbers in business for themselves must also budget for contractor surety bonds ($250 to $600/yr) and commercial general liability insurance ($1,500 to $3,500/yr).

How to Become a Licensed Plumber

Follow these five career steps to start your plumbing journey and earn your journeyman license.

  1. Apply for a Local Plumbing Apprenticeship

    Submit applications to local United Association (UA) union training halls or non-union PHCC plumbing contractors.

  2. Register with the State Licensing Board as an Apprentice

    File your apprentice registration paperwork with your state department of labor or licensing agency ($25 to $75 fee).

  3. Acquire Quality Hand Tools and Safety Boots

    Invest in Ridgid pipe wrenches, Channellock pliers, and OSHA-compliant steel-toe waterproof work boots.

  4. Complete 8,000 Hours of Supervised On-the-Job Training

    Log your daily field work hours across commercial, residential rough-in, and service plumbing tasks over 4 to 5 years.

  5. Pass the State Journeyman Plumber Exam

    Study the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and pass the state written and practical exam.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to become a plumber through an apprenticeship?

An apprenticeship typically costs between $1,000 and $2,500 total for basic hand tools, safety boots, and registration, while you earn full-time wages.

Q2: How much does plumbing trade school cost?

Plumbing trade schools and vocational colleges charge between $5,000 and $16,000 in tuition for 6 to 12-month pre-apprenticeship programs.

Q3: How much do apprentice plumbers make?

Apprentice plumbers typically earn between $18 and $26 per hour to start, with automatic wage increases each year reaching $35 to $50+/hr as a journeyman.

Q4: How long does it take to become a licensed plumber?

It takes 4 to 5 years (roughly 8,000 documented on-the-job training hours and 600+ classroom hours) to become a licensed journeyman.

Q5: Is plumbing hard to learn?

Plumbing requires mechanical aptitude, physical stamina, basic math for pipe slope calculations, and understanding complex plumbing code rules.

Q6: How much does a master plumber make?

Licensed master plumbers and commercial plumbing contractors typically earn $85,000 to $140,000+ annually.

Q7: Do plumbers have student loan debt?

Most plumbers have zero student loan debt because they learn through paid apprenticeships where employers sponsor tuition.

Q8: What is the difference between a journeyman and master plumber?

A journeyman is licensed to work independently; a master plumber can design systems, pull municipal permits, and own a plumbing contracting business.
