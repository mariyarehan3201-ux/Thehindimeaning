Does New Hampshire Have a Lemon Law?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Yes, New Hampshire has a comprehensive and consumer-friendly Lemon Law codified under New Hampshire Revised Statutes Annotated (RSA) Chapter 357-D. Known officially as the Motor Vehicle Warranty Law, it provides that if a new motor vehicle cannot be repaired after a reasonable number of attempts, the manufacturer must provide a full purchase refund or a comparable new replacement vehicle.

Statutory Framework: New Hampshire RSA Chapter 357-D

Purchasing or leasing a brand-new automobile is one of the largest financial investments a consumer makes. When a newly purchased vehicle exhibits persistent, severe mechanical defects?"such as transmission failures, electronic engine stalling, malfunctioning braking systems, or recurrent electrical blackouts?"consumers are protected under New Hampshire state law. Under New Hampshire RSA Chapter 357-D, consumers are safeguarded against defective motor vehicles that fail to conform to manufacturer express warranties.

The New Hampshire Lemon Law applies to any new passenger motor vehicle, motorcycle, or truck with a gross vehicle weight rating under 9,000 pounds sold or leased in the State of New Hampshire. The law covers nonconformities?"defined as any defect, malfunction, or condition that substantially impairs the vehicle use, market value, or physical safety. The statutory coverage window extends throughout the manufacturer express warranty term, or within one year from the original vehicle delivery date, whichever occurs first.

A distinctive feature of New Hampshire Lemon Law is its state-administered dispute resolution mechanism: the New Hampshire Motor Vehicle Arbitration Board. Operated directly under the New Hampshire Department of Justice (Office of the Attorney General), this specialized administrative board provides consumers with a fast, binding, and low-cost alternative to expensive, drawn-out civil court litigation. The Legal Presumption: 3 Attempts or 30 Business Days

To establish that a vehicle is legally a "lemon," the consumer must prove that the manufacturer or its authorized franchised dealership has been afforded a "reasonable number of repair attempts" to correct the defect. Under RSA 357-D:3, New Hampshire law creates an unassailable legal presumption that a reasonable number of attempts have occurred if the vehicle meets either of two statutory benchmarks.

The first benchmark is the Three-Repair-Attempt Rule. If the same substantial nonconformity has been subjected to repair three or more times by an authorized dealer within the warranty period, and the defect continues to persist or recurs, the vehicle qualifies as a lemon. The second independent benchmark is the Thirty-Business-Day Rule. If the vehicle is out of service by reason of repair for a cumulative total of 30 or more business days (excluding Sundays and legal holidays) within the warranty period, the consumer is entitled to statutory relief, regardless of whether the downtime was for a single major defect or multiple unrelated mechanical problems. The New Hampshire Motor Vehicle Arbitration Board Process

Unlike many states that force consumers into manufacturer-sponsored private arbitration programs (such as the Better Business Bureau Auto Line) with inherent corporate biases, New Hampshire provides an impartial state board: the New Hampshire Motor Vehicle Arbitration Board. Consisting of citizen consumers and independent automotive technical experts appointed by the Governor, the board meets monthly in Concord to conduct administrative hearings.

Filing for state arbitration requires paying a nominal $50 administrative fee. Once a complete application is submitted with copies of dealership work orders and repair invoices, the Board schedules a formal hearing within 40 days. At the hearing, the consumer presents repair orders, and the Board technical experts physically inspect and test-drive the vehicle. The Board renders a final, legally binding written decision within 14 days of the hearing. If the Board rules in the consumer favor, the manufacturer must issue a full refund?"including the purchase price, finance charges, registration fees, and state sales/excise taxes, minus a modest statutory mileage deduction for pre-defect driving?"within 30 days.

How to File a Lemon Law Claim in New Hampshire

A step-by-step procedural roadmap for New Hampshire vehicle owners to file and win a state lemon law arbitration claim.

  1. Compile Complete Dealership Repair Invoices and Work Orders

    Collect every written repair order and invoice from authorized dealerships. Verify that each invoice clearly documents the date in, date out, mileage, and the exact consumer complaint.

  2. Send Mandatory Written Notice to the Manufacturer

    Send a formal certified letter (return receipt requested) directly to the manufacturer corporate headquarters, notifying them of the recurring defect and providing a final 7-day opportunity to repair.

  3. Download and Complete the NH Arbitration Application

    Download the official Motor Vehicle Arbitration Application from the New Hampshire Department of Justice (Office of the Attorney General) website, attaching all repair orders.

  4. Submit Application with $50 Administrative Fee to the State

    Mail your completed application package and the $50 state filing fee to the Motor Vehicle Arbitration Board in Concord, New Hampshire, triggering a formal hearing within 40 days.

  5. Attend the State Arbitration Hearing in Concord

    Present your repair documentation to the five-member board. Board automotive experts will inspect and test-drive your vehicle on-site before issuing a final binding decision within 14 days.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does New Hampshire have a lemon law?

Yes. New Hampshire has a comprehensive Lemon Law under RSA Chapter 357-D that requires manufacturers to refund or replace new vehicles that cannot be repaired after a reasonable number of attempts.

Q2: What qualifies a car as a lemon in New Hampshire?

A car qualifies if it has been subjected to repair 3 or more times for the same substantial defect, or if it has been out of service for repair for 30 or more cumulative business days within the warranty period.

Q3: Does the NH lemon law apply to leased vehicles?

Yes. New Hampshire's Lemon Law explicitly covers both purchased and leased new passenger motor vehicles, light trucks under 9,000 lbs, and motorcycles.

Q4: Does New Hampshire's lemon law cover used cars?

Generally no. RSA 357-D applies to new vehicles sold or leased with manufacturer express warranties. However, used cars may be covered under federal Magnuson-Moss Warranty laws or state implied warranty protections.

Q5: What is the New Hampshire Motor Vehicle Arbitration Board?

It is an independent state administrative board operated under the NH Attorney General's Office that hears consumer lemon law disputes and issues legally binding refund or replacement rulings within 14 days.

Q6: How much does it cost to file for lemon law arbitration in NH?

Filing an application with the New Hampshire Motor Vehicle Arbitration Board requires a modest, one-time administrative fee of only $50.

Q7: What refund do you get under New Hampshire lemon law?

A full refund includes the full purchase or lease price, down payment, trade-in allowance, sales taxes, registration fees, and finance charges, minus a reasonable mileage offset for driving prior to the defect.

Q8: How long do I have to file a lemon law claim in New Hampshire?

You must notify the manufacturer and file during the express manufacturer warranty period or within one year from the original vehicle delivery date, whichever occurs first.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

New Hampshire possesses one of the strongest, most accessible Lemon Laws in the United States, giving consumers powerful legal mechanisms to force manufacturers to repurchase defective vehicles. If your new vehicle has been in the repair shop three times for the same problem or sat out of service for 30 business days, you are legally entitled to a full refund or brand-new replacement vehicle. Maintaining meticulous copies of all dealership repair orders and filing with the New Hampshire Motor Vehicle Arbitration Board ensures your consumer rights are fully vindicated.
