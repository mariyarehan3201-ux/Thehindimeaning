What Happens If You Hit a Cop Car?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

If you hit a cop car, you face an immediate, high-scrutiny emergency investigation involving supervisory police lieutenants, state highway patrol reconstructionists, and aggressive municipal legal departments. In addition to severe traffic citations under state 'Move Over' laws, you face immediate criminal charges if alcohol was involved, alongside municipal subrogation lawsuits to pay for cruiser repairs.

The Immediate Tactical and Operational Response to a Cruiser Crash

Colliding with any motor vehicle is nerve-wracking, but crashing into a marked law enforcement patrol vehicle—whether an active municipal police cruiser, a county sheriff's SUV, or a state highway patrol interceptor—triggers a massive, instantaneous police response.

When an officer's cruiser is struck, the radio dispatch goes out across county police frequencies as 'Unit 10-50 Officer Involved Collision.' Within minutes, multiple patrol units, supervisory field sergeants, traffic homicide reconstruction investigators, and emergency fire rescue units swarm the scene with flashing emergency lights. The psychological atmosphere is intensely charged; responding officers do not view the incident as a routine fender-bender—they view it as a potential threat to their fellow officer's physical life.

To eliminate allegations of departmental bias or conflict of interest, standard municipal police operating procedures mandate that the local police agency cannot investigate their own vehicle crash. Instead, jurisdiction is immediately transferred to an outside independent law enforcement agency—typically the State Highway Patrol or County Sheriff's Office—to conduct an impartial, scientific crash investigation. Criminal Penalties: Move Over Laws vs Felony Charges

The criminal and administrative penalties resulting from striking a police vehicle vary dramatically depending on the driver's sobriety, intent, and whether the cruiser was parked or in active motion.

In all fifty states, traffic safety codes strictly enforce mandatory 'Move Over Laws.' Under these statutes, when approaching any stationary emergency vehicle displaying flashing emergency lights on the shoulder of a highway, drivers are legally required to vacate the adjacent lane closest to the emergency vehicle or slow down to a speed 20 miles per hour below the posted speed limit. Violating the Move Over law and striking a parked police cruiser carries massive traffic fines (typically $500 to $1,500), mandatory court appearances, and automatic driver license suspensions.

However, if the collision involved impaired driving or reckless conduct, the criminal charges escalate exponentially. If an intoxicated driver strikes an occupied police vehicle and the officer sustains physical injuries, state prosecutors immediately file Aggravated Assault on a Peace Officer with a Deadly Weapon (a motor vehicle) or Felony DUI with Serious Bodily Injury. These offenses are classified as Second-Degree or First-Degree Felonies carrying mandatory minimum prison terms of 5 to 20 years in state prison. Civil Liability and Municipal City Subrogation Claims

Beyond the criminal courtroom, hitting a police vehicle triggers an immediate, aggressive civil financial collection action orchestrated by municipal city attorneys or county risk management departments.

Modern law enforcement vehicles are extraordinarily expensive pieces of specialized municipal property. A fully outfitted Ford Police Interceptor Utility or Dodge Charger cruiser costs between $65,000 and $95,000. Beyond the base vehicle, the cruiser is equipped with $30,000 to $50,000 in sensitive tactical electronics: high-definition dual-camera mobile video systems (Axon Fleet), digital mobile data terminals (MDT laptop computers), emergency light bars, dual-band Motorola radios, and digital radar speed measurement antennas.

The city's legal department will file a massive Property Damage Subrogation Claim against your personal auto insurance policy for the total replacement value of the cruiser and all tactical hardware, alongside towing and decontamination fees. If the police officer sustained physical injuries, the city's Workers' Compensation insurer will assert a secondary statutory lien to recover every dollar paid for the officer's emergency medical care, physical therapy, and disability wage replacement. If your auto insurance property damage liability limit is only $10,000 or $25,000, you will be held personally liable for the remaining tens of thousands of dollars.

How to Safely Handle a Collision with a Police Cruiser

Critical emergency protocol if your vehicle collides with a law enforcement vehicle.

  1. Stop Immediately and Keep Your Hands Visible on the Wheel

    Pull over safely to the right shoulder, turn off the engine, roll down your window, turn on interior dome lights, and keep both hands flat on the steering wheel.

  2. Avoid Sudden Physical Movements and Remain Inside Car

    Never jump out of your vehicle running toward the cruiser; wait for responding officers to approach you to prevent mistaken weapon draw reactions.

  3. Confirm an Independent Investigating Agency Is Summoned

    Politely verify that the State Highway Patrol or an outside agency is handling the formal crash report to ensure an unbiased traffic investigation.

  4. Notify Your Auto Insurer and Retain Legal Counsel

    Report the collision to your auto insurance carrier immediately and retain an attorney to handle municipal subrogation claims and traffic defense.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is hitting a cop car an automatic felony?

No. Accidental fender-benders are traffic infractions, but hitting a cruiser while impaired, fleeing, or causing injury elevates to a felony.

Q2: What is the penalty for violating the Move Over law?

Fines typically range from $500 to $1,500, with 3 to 4 moving violation points and potential driver license suspension.

Q3: Who pays for the damage to the police car?

Your auto insurance company's Property Damage Liability coverage pays for the cruiser repairs, equipment replacement, and city towing costs.

Q4: What if the police car hit me while running a red light?

Emergency vehicles must exercise 'due regard' for safety; if the officer ran a red light recklessly without sirens, the city can be held liable.

Q5: Can I get bodycam and dashcam video of the crash?

Yes. Your defense attorney can obtain all police cruiser dashcam (Axon Fleet) and officer body-worn camera recordings through public records and discovery.

Q6: What if the police officer was injured in the crash?

The city's workers' comp carrier will file a subrogation lien against your bodily injury insurance to recover medical costs and officer disability wages.

Q7: Can a city take my personal assets if my insurance is too low?

Yes. If the totaled police vehicle and equipment exceed your property damage limits, the city attorney can sue you personally for the deficiency.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Hitting a police car triggers an intense multi-agency investigation, severe traffic citations under Move Over laws, and aggressive financial collection from municipal attorneys. If alcohol, reckless driving, or leaving the scene occurred, prosecutors aggressively pursue multi-year felony state prison sentences. If you collide with a police vehicle, pull over immediately, turn on your hazard lights, keep your hands visible on the steering wheel, cooperate with independent investigating troopers, and retain an experienced criminal defense and traffic attorney immediately.
