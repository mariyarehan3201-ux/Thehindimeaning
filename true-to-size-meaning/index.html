Industrial Sizing Standards and the Definition of True to Size

In the footwear and fashion retail industries, true to size (TTS) indicates that a shoe or piece of clothing conforms accurately to standardized international sizing charts and industry benchmarks without requiring the shopper to order a size larger or smaller than their normal baseline size.

For footwear, true to size means a shoe is constructed around an industry-standard geometric foot mold known as a shoe last, following standardized scale standards established by organizations like the American Society for Testing and Materials (ASTM) or the International Organization for Standardization (ISO). If a customer measures a US Men Size 10 on a standard metal Brannock Device, a true-to-size sneaker will fit their foot comfortably with roughly a half-inch of space in the toe box.

Vanity Sizing, Shoe Lasts, and Brand-Specific Sizing Discrepancies

Despite standardized measurement scales, true-to-size experiences often vary between competing brands due to proprietary shoe lasts, fabric elasticity, and brand demographics. In women denim and dresses, vanity sizing has systematically enlarged garment dimensions over decades, meaning a modern Size 4 often possesses the exact measurements of a 1970s Size 8.

In athletic footwear, purpose-built functionality dictates fit. For example, Nike basketball shoes and soccer cleats frequently run a half-size small because athletes require a snug, locked-in lockdown fit to prevent foot slippage during lateral cuts. Conversely, heritage boot makers like Red Wing or Timberland run a half to full size large because their lasts historically accommodate thick wool work socks.

How to Determine Your Exact True to Size Measurements

A four-step home measurement protocol to establish your precise baseline shoe and apparel sizing.

  1. Measure Your Feet on a Brannock Device

    Visit a local shoe store to measure both your heel-to-toe length, arch length, and foot width on an authentic metal Brannock Device.

  2. Trace Your Bare Foot on Paper at Home

    Stand on a piece of paper in the evening (when feet are slightly swollen), trace your foot perimeter, and measure length from heel to longest toe in centimeters.

  3. Consult Metric Millimeter / Centimeter Size Charts

    When shopping international brands, ignore US/UK numbers and match your exact foot length to the Japanese (CM/JP) size chart, which uses absolute millimeters.

  4. Read Verified Buyer Review Aggregates

    Check online retail fit sliders showing crowd-sourced feedback (e.g., '82% of 1,400 reviewers say this item fits true to size') before checking out.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does true to size mean when buying clothes or shoes?

True to size means the item fits exactly according to standard sizing charts, meaning you should buy your normal everyday size.

Q2: What does TTS stand for in sneaker reviews?

In sneaker communities and retail reviews, TTS stands for True To Size.

Q3: If shoes are true to size, should I size up or down?

Neither. If an item is true to size, you should order your standard, regular size without adjusting up or down.

Q4: Why do some shoes run small even if labeled the same size?

Different brands use different anatomical molds (shoe lasts), interior padding thicknesses, and toe box tapers that alter the interior volume.

Q5: What is vanity sizing in clothing?

Vanity sizing is the practice where clothing brands assign smaller size numbers to larger physical measurements to make customers feel flatteringly smaller.

Q6: Does Converse Chuck Taylor fit true to size?

No. Classic Converse Chuck Taylor sneakers run notoriously large; most people must order a half size to a full size smaller than normal.

Q7: Are Nike sneakers generally true to size?

Most Nike lifestyle models are true to size, but narrow performance models and running shoes often run a half size small.

Q8: How much space should you have in the toe of a true-to-size shoe?

You should have approximately a thumb width of space (about 3/8 to 1/2 inch) between your longest toe and the tip of the shoe.
