How Do You Get Into Canada with a DUI?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how do you get into canada with a dui is an essential legal consideration for American citizens, cross-border business professionals, and vacationers traveling north of the border. Under Canadian immigration law and the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (IRPA), driving under the influence (DUI)—as well as driving while intoxicated (DWI) and wet reckless charges—is classified as 'Serious Criminality.' Because the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) shares real-time biometric database access with the FBI, travelers with an impaired driving conviction will be turned away at border crossings unless they obtain proper legal authorization.

Bill C-46 and the Legal Threshold of Serious Criminality

In December 2018, the Parliament of Canada passed Bill C-46, fundamentally transforming how Canadian immigration officers treat foreign impaired driving offenses. Prior to Bill C-46, a first-offense DUI was treated as ordinary criminality, allowing travelers to qualify for automatic 'Deemed Rehabilitation' once ten years had passed following the completion of their sentence.

Under Bill C-46, the maximum sentence under the Canadian Criminal Code for impaired driving was increased from five years to ten years. This statutory increase automatically reclassified DUI offenses as serious criminality under IRPA Section 36(1). Consequently, foreign nationals convicted of a DUI after December 2018 can never become deemed rehabilitated by the passage of time; they remain permanently inadmissible to Canada until they obtain formal governmental clearance.

Temporary Resident Permits (TRP) for Urgent Travel

If you must enter Canada before five years have elapsed since completing your DUI sentence, applying for a Temporary Resident Permit (TRP) is your primary option. Under IRPA Section 24, a TRP is a discretionary waiver issued to a criminally inadmissible traveler when their reason for entering Canada outweighs any health, safety, or security risks to Canadian society.

To succeed, you must demonstrate a compelling, justifiable reason for travel—such as executive corporate negotiations, performing essential contracted labor, or attending a close family funeral or wedding. Standard vacation travel is rarely considered sufficiently compelling. TRP applications can be submitted months in advance to a Canadian consulate, or presented in person at a land port of entry directly to a senior CBSA border officer in urgent circumstances.

Individual Criminal Rehabilitation: The Permanent Solution

For individuals seeking permanent, unhindered access to Canada, applying for Individual Criminal Rehabilitation through Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) is the definitive legal remedy. Once exactly five calendar years have elapsed since completing every element of your DUI sentence—including payment of all fines, completion of community service, termination of supervised probation, and restoration of driver privileges—you become eligible to petition.

An approved Criminal Rehabilitation application permanently erases your criminal inadmissibility regarding the specific disclosed offenses. Once granted, you can cross into Canada freely for the rest of your life without applying for permits or waivers, provided you commit no further offenses. Because processing times average 6 to 18 months, applicants should file well ahead of planned travel.

How to Legally Travel to Canada with a DUI Conviction

Follow these five strategic steps to prepare documentation and resolve Canadian criminal inadmissibility.

  1. Calculate Your Exact Sentence Completion Date

    Verify the precise calendar date your probation terminated and all court fees were paid in full.

  2. Order an Official FBI Fingerprint Background Check

    Submit biometric fingerprints to the FBI CJIS division to obtain your official criminal history summary.

  3. Gather Certified Court Records and Police Clearances

    Collect certified copies of your court docket, conviction judgment, and probation completion letter.

  4. Determine TRP vs. Criminal Rehabilitation Pathway

    Apply for a TRP if travel is urgent or under 5 years; apply for Criminal Rehabilitation if past 5 years.

  5. Submit Application with Required Government Fees

    File your completed dossier through the Canadian visa office or present it at a land port of entry.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can you go to Canada if you have a misdemeanor DUI?

No, under Canadian law, even a misdemeanor US DUI is treated as serious criminality, rendering you inadmissible without a TRP or Criminal Rehabilitation.

Q2: Does Canada check FBI records at the border?

Yes, the CBSA has real-time biometric access to the FBI NCIC database whenever your passport is scanned at border booths.

Q3: How long after a DUI can you enter Canada without a waiver?

Since Bill C-46 was passed in 2018, there is no automatic entry after 10 years; you must apply for Individual Criminal Rehabilitation.

Q4: What is the fee for a Canadian Criminal Rehabilitation application?

Because DUI is classified as serious criminality, the IRCC application fee is $1,000 CAD (approximately $750 USD).

Q5: Can I get a TRP at the border for a family vacation?

Rarely; CBSA border officers typically deny port-of-entry TRP applications for pure leisure tourism, requiring compelling business or family reasons.

Q6: Does an expunged DUI count at the Canadian border?

Yes, US state expungements generally do not erase convictions under Canadian immigration law unless equivalent to a full executive pardon.

Q7: Can you fly over Canada with a DUI if you don't leave the airport?

If your flight has a layover in Canada, you must clear Canadian customs and are subject to criminal inadmissibility screening.

Q8: Does a wet reckless charge prevent entry to Canada?

Yes, reckless driving involving alcohol is evaluated under equivalent Canadian Criminal Code impaired driving provisions.
