How Much Does a Probate Lawyer Cost in Florida?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a probate lawyer cost in Florida requires examining whether the estate qualifies for summary administration or formal probate administration, as well as Florida's statutory attorney fee schedule. On average, probate in Florida costs between $2,000 and $7,500 for summary administration, and $5,000 to $25,000+ for formal administration. Under Florida Statute Section 733.6171, attorneys can bill a statutory percentage (typically 3% of the gross estate) or charge hourly rates between $300 and $550.

Florida Statutory Fee Schedule: Florida Statute Section 733.6171

Florida is one of the few states with a statutory fee schedule establishing what is legally presumed to be 'reasonable compensation' for probate attorneys. Under Florida Statute Section 733.6171, an attorney handling a formal administration is entitled to compensation calculated from the inventory value of the gross probate estate plus any income earned during administration. The statutory rate begins at $1,500 for estates under $40,000, $2,250 for estates between $40,000 and $70,000, and $3,000 for estates between $70,000 and $100,000.

For estates valued over $100,000, the statutory percentage sliding scale applies: 3% on the first $1 million of the estate, 2.5% on the value between $1 million and $3 million, 2% on the value between $3 million and $5 million, and 1.5% on amounts between $5 million and $10 million. Under this schedule, probating an estate valued at $500,000 carries a statutory attorney fee of $15,000. However, clients have the legal right to negotiate an alternative fee agreement, such as an hourly rate or a flat fee.

Summary Administration vs. Formal Administration in Florida

Florida probate law provides two primary paths for administering an estate: Summary Administration and Formal Administration. Summary Administration is an expedited, simplified probate procedure available only if the non-exempt probate estate is valued under $75,000, or if the decedent has been deceased for more than two years. Summary administration does not require appointing a personal representative, and attorneys typically handle it for a flat fee of $1,500 to $3,500 plus court filing fees ($345 to $400).

Formal Administration is required for estates exceeding $75,000 in non-exempt probate assets, or when the will requires a personal representative to administer complex debts or sell real property. Under Florida Probate Rule 5.030, a personal representative must be represented by an attorney admitted to The Florida Bar (unless the personal representative is the sole interested beneficiary). Formal probate involves issuing Letters of Administration, creditor notification periods, and inventory filings, averaging $4,500 to $15,000+ in legal fees.

Ancillary Court Expenses and Extraordinary Legal Service Fees

Beyond base attorney fees, the estate must pay ancillary administrative expenses. Circuit Court filing fees in Florida range from $345 to $450 depending on the county. Publishing the mandatory Notice to Creditors in a local legal newspaper costs $150 to $300. Certified copies of Letters of Administration, vital statistics filings, and certified mailings add another $100 to $250.

Additionally, the statutory fee schedule only covers ordinary administrative services. Florida Statute 733.6171(7) authorizes attorneys to bill additional hourly fees for 'extraordinary services.' Extraordinary services include handling will contests or adversary probate litigation, resolving disputed IRS estate tax audits, managing decedent business operations, and selling probate real estate. These complex tasks are billed at standard hourly rates of $350 to $550.

How to Manage and Minimize Florida Probate Costs

Follow these five tactical steps to reduce legal expenses during Florida probate proceedings.

  1. Determine Summary Administration Eligibility

    Calculate non-exempt assets to see if the estate is under $75,000 or if the decedent has been deceased over two years to qualify for summary probate.

  2. Negotiate an Hourly or Flat Fee Instead of Statutory 3%

    Ask the attorney to agree in writing to bill hourly ($300 to $450) or quote a fixed flat fee rather than the statutory 3% gross estate fee.

  3. Organize Financial Records and Real Estate Deeds Promptly

    Compile bank account numbers, death certificates, mortgage statements, and original wills to reduce billable attorney paralegal hours.

  4. Petition for Determination of Homestead Early

    Have counsel file a petition to declare the primary residence exempt homestead property, removing its equity from probate creditor claims.

  5. Audit Extraordinary Billing Invoices Line-by-Line

    Carefully review monthly billing statements to ensure separate charges for real estate sales or tax filings were pre-authorized.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the average cost of a probate lawyer in Florida?

A probate lawyer in Florida typically costs $1,500 to $3,500 for summary administration, and 3% of the gross estate or $5,000 to $15,000+ for formal administration.

Q2: Do you need a lawyer for probate in Florida?

Yes, under Florida Probate Rule 5.030, a personal representative must be represented by an attorney in formal administration unless they are the sole beneficiary.

Q3: What is Florida's statutory probate fee?

Under Florida Statute 733.6171, attorneys can claim 3% of the first $1 million of the estate, 2.5% of the next $2 million, and 2% up to $5 million.

Q4: How long does probate take in Florida?

Summary administration takes 4 to 8 weeks, while formal probate administration typically takes 6 to 12 months.

Q5: Does Florida homestead property go through probate?

Yes, homestead passes through probate to establish an Order Determining Homestead, but its value is exempt from claims of general creditors.

Q6: Who pays the probate lawyer fees in Florida?

The attorney fees are paid directly from the estate's liquid assets before final inheritance funds are distributed to beneficiaries.

Q7: Can you negotiate probate attorney fees in Florida?

Yes, Florida law explicitly allows personal representatives to negotiate hourly rates or flat fees rather than paying the statutory 3% percentage.

Q8: What is the filing fee for probate in Florida?

Circuit Court probate filing fees in Florida range between $345 and $450 depending on the county and whether it is summary or formal administration.
