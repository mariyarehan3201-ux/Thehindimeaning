Is There a Shot for Dogs for Fleas and Ticks?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Yes, there is now an officially approved veterinary injectable shot that protects dogs against fleas and ticks for up to an entire year. Developed by Merck Animal Health and approved by international veterinary regulatory authorities under the brand name Bravecto Quantum (injectable fluralaner suspension), this breakthrough veterinary medicine delivers continuous flea and tick protection for a full twelve months from a single subcutaneous injection. While historically dog owners were limited to monthly oral chewable tablets or messy topical spot-on liquids, injectable parasite control is transforming modern veterinary preventative care.

The Evolution of Flea and Tick Preventatives: From Topicals to Injections

For decades, veterinary parasite prevention was plagued by a widespread human problem: owner compliance. Traditional flea and tick control required pet parents to remember to apply greasy topical spot-on chemical liquids (like fipronil or permethrin) to their dog's shoulder blades every thirty days, or convince their pets to ingest monthly flavored chewable tablets (such as NexGard, Simparica, or Credelio).

Veterinary epidemiology studies reveal that over 70% of pet owners accidentally miss monthly doses or administer preventatives weeks late, creating dangerous gaps in parasite defense that leave dogs vulnerable to Lyme disease, Rocky Mountain spotted fever, Ehrlichiosis, and Flea Allergy Dermatitis (FAD). The development of long-acting injectable formulations—building upon the multi-decade success of ProHeart heartworm injections—eliminates human forgetfulness, ensuring year-round, unbroken chemical protection administered directly by a licensed veterinarian during annual wellness visits.

How the Injectable Flea and Tick Shot Works: Pharmacokinetics

The science powering Bravecto Quantum is based on micro-particulate suspension technology. During the routine annual exam, the veterinarian injects a specialized liquid suspension of micronized fluralaner subcutaneously beneath the loose skin at the base of the dog's neck. Because fluralaner molecules have a high binding affinity to bodily plasma proteins and adipose tissue, the drug forms a benign subcutaneous micro-depot.

Over the subsequent 365 days, microscopic quantities of fluralaner are slowly and consistently released into the dog's bloodstream at a controlled, therapeutic rate. When a feeding flea or tick punctures the dog's skin and ingests blood, the fluralaner molecule acts as a potent antagonist against insect GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) and glutamate-gated chloride channels. This hyper-stimulates the parasite's nervous system, killing fleas within 8 to 12 hours (before they can lay eggs) and paralyzing ticks within 24 to 48 hours, preventing the transmission of dangerous vector-borne pathogens.

Safety Considerations and Veterinary Screening Protocols

While long-acting injectable parasite prevention offers incredible convenience, pet owners must evaluate safety profiles with their veterinarians. Fluralaner belongs to the Isoxazoline chemical class. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has noted that isoxazoline drugs can occasionally trigger neurological adverse reactions in sensitive animals, including muscle tremors, ataxia (loss of coordination), and seizures.

Dogs with a documented history of epilepsy, seizure disorders, or neuro-muscular sensitivities should not receive isoxazoline injections; once injected, a 12-month medication cannot be un-injected or pumped from the stomach. For healthy dogs over six to twelve months of age, extensive worldwide clinical safety trials have demonstrated that injectable fluralaner is well tolerated, with minor temporary injection-site swelling or transient lethargy representing the most common mild reactions.

How to Protect Your Dog with a Flea and Tick Shot

Step-by-step guidance on veterinary evaluation, administration, and monitoring for canine parasite injections.

  1. Schedule an Annual Veterinary Wellness Examination

    Book an appointment with your veterinarian for an annual health checkup. Inquire whether your clinic stocks Bravecto Quantum or participates in long-acting injectable parasite programs.

  2. Review Your Dog's Complete Neurological History

    Discuss your dog's medical history thoroughly. Disclose any past occurrences of seizures, head tremors, or adverse reactions to oral isoxazoline chews (NexGard, Bravecto, Simparica).

  3. Perform Baseline Heartworm and Tick Disease Bloodwork

    Run an annual 4Dx blood screen to confirm your dog is currently negative for heartworms, Lyme disease, and Ehrlichia before beginning new parasite therapies.

  4. Have the Veterinarian Administer the Subcutaneous Shot

    The veterinarian calculates the exact dosage based on your dog's current weight and injects the suspension under the skin between the shoulder blades.

  5. Monitor the Injection Site for 48 Hours

    Check the injection area for temporary swelling or tenderness. Note the date on your veterinary records; your dog is now protected against fleas and ticks for the next 365 days.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is there a shot for dogs for fleas and ticks?

Yes. Bravecto Quantum (injectable fluralaner) is an approved veterinary prescription injection that provides continuous protection against fleas and ticks for up to 12 months.

Q2: How long does a flea and tick shot last in dogs?

A single veterinary subcutaneous injection of fluralaner provides unbroken protection against fleas and common tick species for up to a full year (12 months).

Q3: Can any dog get the flea and tick injection?

The shot is generally approved for healthy dogs over six to twelve months of age who have reached adult weight. Dogs with a history of seizures or epilepsy should avoid it.

Q4: What are the side effects of the flea and tick shot for dogs?

Most dogs experience zero side effects. Mild temporary reactions include slight swelling at the injection site, transient lethargy, or minor decreased appetite for 24 hours.

Q5: Can you undo or reverse the flea and tick shot if a dog reacts?

No. Because it is a long-acting depot injection designed to release medication slowly over a year, it cannot be flushed from the body. Treatment for reactions is supportive care.

Q6: How much does the 12-month flea and tick shot cost?

The injection typically costs between $150 and $250 depending on the dog's weight, which is comparable to or cheaper than buying twelve individual monthly oral chewable tablets.

Q7: Does the flea and tick shot protect against heartworms?

No. Fluralaner kills external parasites (fleas and ticks) only. Heartworm prevention requires a separate medication, such as the ProHeart 12 injection or monthly heartworm chews.

Q8: Is the flea and tick injection better than chewable tablets?

It is better for pet owners who frequently forget monthly pills, ensuring 100% year-round protection without compliance lapses. However, oral chews remain preferable for dogs with sensitive neurological systems.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

In conclusion, the availability of a 12-month veterinary flea and tick shot represents one of the greatest innovations in companion animal healthcare. By replacing monthly messy topicals and chewable pills with a single annual subcutaneous injection administered during your dog's regular veterinary checkup, it guarantees 365 days of unbroken defense against fleas, ticks, and debilitating vector-borne diseases. Pet owners should consult their veterinarian to determine if their dog's health profile and lifestyle make them an ideal candidate for long-acting injectable protection.
