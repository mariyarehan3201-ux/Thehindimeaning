Asbestos Disposal Sacramento

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

Asbestos disposal in Sacramento is governed by strict local, state, and federal hazardous waste regulations designed to eliminate environmental contamination across the Central Valley. Under the jurisdiction of the Sacramento Metropolitan Air Quality Management District and the California Department of Toxic Substances Control, all asbestos-containing debris must be properly contained, labeled, manifested, and transported to designated regional landfills.

SMAQMD Rule 902 Framework and Cal/EPA Waste Classifications

Asbestos disposal throughout Sacramento County is strictly regulated under Rule 902 of the Sacramento Metropolitan Air Quality Management District. Designed to protect public health and maintain regional ambient air quality standards across the Sacramento Valley basin, Rule 902 establishes comprehensive mandates governing project notification, handling, containment, and final disposal of asbestos-containing waste materials. The district's rules align with federal EPA NESHAP standards under 40 CFR Part 61 while enforcing California's rigorous environmental protection statutes.

In California, waste classification for asbestos differs significantly from federal definitions. Under the California Department of Toxic Substances Control and Title 22 of the California Code of Regulations, friable asbestos waste containing greater than one percent asbestos is officially classified as a California hazardous waste. Non-friable asbestos waste (such as intact vinyl composition tile or asphalt roofing shingles) is categorized as non-hazardous industrial waste, provided it remains intact. However, if non-friable materials become crushed, pulverized, or crumbled during removal, they automatically reclassify as regulated friable hazardous waste requiring specialized handling and manifesting.

Packaging Standards, Kiefer Landfill Protocols, and Hauling Rules

Sacramento regulations require rigorous packaging controls before any asbestos waste can leave an abatement site. Materials must be thoroughly saturated with surfactant-amended water and placed into two separate, leak-tight 6-mil polyethylene disposal bags, or inside sealed, plastic-lined fiber drums. Every container must be securely sealed with heavy-duty duct tape and labeled with mandatory bilingual OSHA warning text: DANGER - CONTAINS ASBESTOS FIBERS - AVOID CREATING DUST - CANCER AND LUNG DISEASE HAZARD. Furthermore, each container must feature a generator label displaying the property address, owner name, and abatement contractor details.

The primary authorized municipal facility for asbestos waste in Sacramento County is Kiefer Landfill, operated by the Sacramento County Department of Waste Management and Recycling in Sloughhouse. Kiefer Landfill accepts both friable and non-friable asbestos by advance appointment only, requiring a minimum of twenty-four to forty-eight hours notice. Waste shipments must be accompanied by an approved Uniform Hazardous Waste Manifest or Non-Hazardous Waste Manifest, depending on material classification. At the landfill, waste is directed to a specialized disposal cell and covered immediately with six inches of compacted soil.

Transportation of hazardous asbestos waste across Sacramento public roadways requires strict adherence to Department of Transportation standards. Commercial shipments must be transported by a registered hazardous waste hauler licensed by the California Department of Toxic Substances Control and inspected by the California Highway Patrol. Commercial haulers must maintain active pollution liability insurance and carry a copy of the active hazardous waste manifest in the vehicle cab, detailing the waste generator, transporter license, and receiving disposal facility.

How to Lawfully Dispose of Asbestos Waste in Sacramento

Step-by-step procedures for packaging, manifesting, and transporting asbestos waste under SMAQMD and Kiefer Landfill requirements.

  1. Segregate and Saturate Materials with Amended Water

    Thoroughly mist all removed asbestos materials with surfactant-amended water to eliminate dust generation and maintain continuous dampness during handling.

  2. Package in Double 6-Mil Polyethylene Bags

    Place damp waste inside an initial 6-mil leak-tight plastic bag, seal it securely with duct tape, and encase it within a second labeled 6-mil bag.

  3. Affix Required Warning Labels and Generator Details

    Ensure both bags display OSHA asbestos warning notices, DTSC hazardous waste placards, and generator tracking stickers noting site origin and contact information.

  4. Schedule Advance Appointment with Kiefer Landfill

    Contact the Kiefer Landfill special waste division at least 24 to 48 hours in advance to schedule a delivery appointment and submit waste characterization profiles.

  5. Transport via Certified Hauler with Waste Manifest

    Retain a DTSC-registered hazardous waste transporter to haul the manifested waste directly to the permitted disposal facility under an active chain of custody.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I throw asbestos floor tiles in my curbside Sacramento trash bin?

No, putting asbestos-containing materials into curbside garbage bins or standard construction dumpsters is strictly illegal under Sacramento County and California state law.

Q2: Where can residents legally dispose of asbestos in Sacramento County?

Asbestos waste can be legally disposed of at Kiefer Landfill in Sloughhouse by advance appointment, following specific bagging and manifesting guidelines.

Q3: What is the penalty for illegal asbestos disposal in Sacramento?

Violations of SMAQMD Rule 902 and California hazardous waste statutes carry administrative civil penalties up to 25,000 dollars per day and potential criminal prosecution.

Q4: How must asbestos waste be packaged for disposal in Sacramento?

Waste must be kept wet and double-bagged inside two leak-tight 6-mil polyethylene bags sealed with duct tape and marked with OSHA warning and generator identification labels.

Q5: Does Kiefer Landfill accept friable asbestos waste?

Yes, Kiefer Landfill accepts both friable and non-friable asbestos waste by prior appointment, directing materials to a designated trench for immediate earth burial.

Q6: Can a homeowner haul their own asbestos waste in California?

California regulations allow homeowners to transport very small quantities of non-hazardous waste under strict limits, but commercial or friable quantities require a licensed hazardous hauler.

Q7: What is SMAQMD Rule 902?

Rule 902 is the Sacramento Metropolitan Air Quality Management District regulation that governs asbestos removal notifications, emission controls, handling, and disposal standards.

Q8: How much does it cost to dispose of asbestos at Kiefer Landfill?

Kiefer Landfill charges a specialized waste disposal fee, typically ranging between 120 and 160 dollars per ton, in addition to standard landfill processing fees.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Lawful asbestos disposal in Sacramento requires disciplined adherence to packaging, paperwork, and transport rules. Disposing of asbestos debris in regular curbside trash bins, commercial dumpsters, or open land is strictly illegal under California law and triggers severe administrative penalties reaching up to twenty-five thousand dollars per day from SMAQMD and Cal/EPA. By partnering with certified abatement contractors, utilizing proper double-bagging techniques, and coordinating directly with Kiefer Landfill, property owners protect local air quality and ensure full environmental compliance.
