How to Beat a Simple Assault Charge in TN?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Facing a simple assault charge in Tennessee can dramatically disrupt your life, threatening you with jail time, hefty criminal fines, and a permanent violent misdemeanor record. Under Tennessee Code Annotated (T.C.A.) Section 39-13-101, an individual can be charged with simple assault for intentionally causing bodily injury, reasonably causing someone to fear imminent bodily injury, or causing offensive physical contact. Overcoming simple assault charges in Tennessee General Sessions and Criminal Courts requires establishing legitimate self-defense under state stand-your-ground laws, proving mutual combat or consent, exposing complainant credibility flaws, and pursuing pre-trial diversion programs.

Tennessee Simple Assault Classifications and Elements

To successfully defeat an assault charge in Tennessee, you must understand the specific statutory subsection filed against you. Under T.C.A. Section 39-13-101, simple assault is categorized into three distinct offenses. Subsection (a)(1) involves intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly causing bodily injury to another person, which is classified as a serious Class A misdemeanor carrying up to eleven months and twenty-nine days in jail and fines up to twenty-five hundred dollars. Subsection (a)(2) involves intentionally or knowingly causing another to reasonably fear imminent bodily injury, also a Class A misdemeanor.

Conversely, subsection (a)(3) addresses intentionally or knowingly causing physical contact with another that a reasonable person would regard as extremely offensive or provocative, which is graded as a Class B misdemeanor punishable by up to six months in jail and fines up to five hundred dollars. Crucially, the prosecution bears the burden of proving each statutory element beyond a reasonable doubt. Challenging subjective claims of fear, showing lack of intent, or demonstrating that alleged injuries were accidental or pre-existing provides immediate leverage for dismissal.

Asserting Tennessee Stand Your Ground and Self-Defense

One of the most potent legal defenses against simple assault charges in Tennessee is the statutory right to self-defense under T.C.A. Section 39-11-611. Tennessee is a robust Stand Your Ground state; you have no legal duty to retreat from any place where you have a lawful right to be before utilizing reasonable force to defend yourself or another person against unlawful physical force. If you reasonably believed force was immediately necessary to protect yourself against another person's unlawful aggression, your physical actions are entirely justified under Tennessee law.

To substantiate a self-defense claim, your defense attorney must compile corroborating evidence demonstrating that the alleged victim was the initial physical aggressor. Surveillance video footage from commercial bars, bystander cell phone recordings, 911 dispatch audio tapes, and medical documentation of your own defensive wounds serve as vital evidence. If defense counsel establishes valid self-defense, Tennessee law grants complete criminal immunity, requiring prosecutors to drop the charges or facing an acquittal at trial.

Furthermore, when two parties willingly engage in an altercation, the doctrine of mutual combat or mutual consent can refute charges under subsection (a)(3). Showing that the complainant provoked the dispute, challenged you to fight, or consented to physical engagement undermines their claim of being an innocent, aggrieved victim.

General Sessions Resolution, Diversion, and Expungement

In Tennessee, misdemeanor assault cases originate in General Sessions Court. This procedural venue provides an invaluable opportunity for skilled defense counsel to resolve matters before they are bound over to a Grand Jury or transferred to Criminal Court. During preliminary hearings in General Sessions, an attorney can cross-examine the complaining witness under oath. If the complainant contradicts their written police statement, refuses to cooperate, or admits to initiating the conflict, judges frequently dismiss the warrant outright.

Even when facts are disputed, qualifying defendants with clean criminal backgrounds can take advantage of Tennessee's judicial diversion statutes under T.C.A. Section 40-35-313. Under judicial diversion, the court defers proceedings without entering a judgment of guilt. Upon successful completion of anger management classes, community service, and unsupervised probation, the case is completely dismissed. Under Tennessee law, a dismissed case resolved through judicial diversion is eligible for complete statutory expungement, erasing all public arrest records.

How to Beat a Simple Assault Charge in Tennessee Courts

Follow these tactical legal steps to construct a formidable defense against simple assault charges in Tennessee.

  1. Do Not Give Statements to Responding Law Enforcement

    Politely assert your right to counsel and decline to provide roadside or stationhouse statements explaining your side of the altercation.

  2. Photograph and Document All Defensive Injuries

    Take high-resolution, dated photographs of any bruises, cuts, bite marks, or torn clothing sustained during the altercation to prove self-defense.

  3. Retain a Skilled Tennessee Criminal Defense Attorney

    Hire an attorney experienced in your local Tennessee General Sessions and Criminal Courts who understands T.C.A. assault statutes and self-defense law.

  4. Subpoena 911 Calls and Nearby Video Surveillance

    Immediately request preservation of 911 dispatch tapes, police body-camera footage, and bar or commercial surveillance video of the encounter.

  5. Pursue Case Dismissal in General Sessions or Seek Diversion

    Challenge complainant credibility during preliminary hearings to win an outright dismissal or petition for statutory judicial diversion under T.C.A. 40-35-313.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is simple assault in Tennessee?

Under T.C.A. Section 39-13-101, simple assault involves causing bodily injury, causing reasonable fear of imminent injury, or engaging in provocative offensive physical contact.

Q2: Can simple assault charges be dropped if the victim wants to drop them?

In Tennessee, only the state prosecutor holds the legal authority to drop charges; a victim request helps, but prosecutors can proceed if evidence exists.

Q3: Does Tennessee have a Stand Your Ground law for assault?

Yes, under T.C.A. Section 39-11-611, Tennessee permits individuals with a lawful right to be present to use reasonable force in self-defense with no duty to retreat.

Q4: What is the penalty for simple assault in Tennessee?

Class A misdemeanor assault carries up to eleven months and twenty-nine days in jail and fines up to $2,500; Class B carries up to six months and a $500 fine.

Q5: Can a simple assault conviction in Tennessee take away gun rights?

A standard simple assault conviction does not automatically revoke federal gun rights, but domestic assault under T.C.A. 39-13-111 imposes a lifetime federal firearm ban.

Q6: What is judicial diversion under T.C.A. 40-35-313?

Judicial diversion allows eligible first-time offenders to complete probation without an adjudication of guilt, culminating in total dismissal and record expungement.

Q7: What is the difference between simple assault and aggravated assault in TN?

Simple assault is a misdemeanor involving minor injury or fear, while aggravated assault is a felony involving serious bodily injury, strangulation, or deadly weapons.

Q8: Can an assault arrest in Tennessee be expunged from your record?

Yes, if your assault charge is dismissed, retired, acquitted, or completed via judicial diversion, you are entitled to full record expungement.
