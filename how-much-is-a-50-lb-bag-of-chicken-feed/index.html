How Much Is a 50 Lb Bag of Chicken Feed?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Understanding how much a 50 lb bag of chicken feed costs is the foundation of budgeting for any backyard flock, homestead, or commercial egg operation. Feeding represents approximately seventy percent of ongoing poultry keeping expenses. Depending on the life stage formulation—such as chick starter crumbles, grower mash, or 16 percent calcium-enriched layer pellets—and whether you choose conventional, non-GMO, or USDA-certified organic grain, a 50-pound bag of chicken feed ranges from $14 to $38 at agricultural supply retailers.

Feed Formulations and Life-Stage Pricing Tiers

Chicken feed is formulated to match specific developmental stages of poultry, and protein content directly dictates production costs. Chick starter and grower feeds contain twenty to twenty-four percent crude protein to support rapid skeletal growth and feathering, frequently supplemented with amprolium (medicated feed) to prevent coccidiosis. These starter bags typically retail between $18 and $26 for a 50-pound sack.

Once hens reach sixteen to twenty weeks of age and begin laying eggs, keepers transition them to layer feed. Standard layer rations contain sixteen percent protein and three to four percent supplemental calcium (derived from ground limestone or oyster shells) to support strong eggshell calcification. Standard 50-pound bags of commercial layer crumbles or pellets represent the most economical category, selling for $14 to $20 at farm supply chains like Tractor Supply Company and local agricultural cooperatives.

Conventional, Non-GMO, and Certified Organic Ingredients

The agricultural sourcing of grains creates the widest pricing gap in poultry feed. Conventional feeds rely heavily on commodity yellow corn, soybean meal, wheat middlings, and synthetic amino acids like methionine. These mass-produced rations offer consistent, balanced nutrition at the lowest consumer price point ($14 to $18 per bag).

In contrast, homesteaders seeking soy-free, non-GMO, or USDA-certified organic feeds pay a fifty to one hundred percent price premium ($28 to $38 per 50-pound bag). Organic feeds utilize certified organic field peas, flaxseed, barley, and non-GMO corn grown without synthetic pesticides or chemical fertilizers. Brands like Scratch and Peck, New Country Organics, and Purina Organic appeal to consumers prioritizing holistic egg production, though the higher feed cost raises the cost per dozen eggs produced.

Consumption Metrics and Flock Budget Management

To budget annual poultry expenses accurately, flock managers calculate feed intake per bird. An average standard-breed laying hen (such as a Rhode Island Red, Plymouth Rock, or Australorp) consumes approximately one-quarter pound (0.25 lbs) of balanced feed per day. This means a single hen consumes about 7.5 pounds of feed per month, or roughly 90 pounds of feed per year.

For a typical backyard flock of six laying hens, total consumption equals 1.5 pounds of feed daily, meaning one 50-pound bag lasts approximately thirty-three days—essentially one bag per month. Keepers can optimize feed budgets by using waste-proof gravity PVC feeders, supplementing diets with garden greens and clean kitchen scraps, and buying whole grain bags in bulk pallets from local agricultural grain mills to secure ten to twenty percent volume discounts.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How long does a 50 lb bag of chicken feed last?

A 50-pound bag lasts one chicken about 200 days, a flock of four hens about 50 days, and a flock of six hens approximately 33 days.

Q2: Are chicken feed pellets or crumbles better?

Pellets reduce feed waste because chickens cannot easily flick them onto the dirt, while crumbles are preferred for smaller bantams and younger birds.

Q3: Why is organic chicken feed twice as expensive as conventional?

Organic grains require certified chemical-free farmland, organic pest management, and expensive non-GMO protein sources like field peas and flaxseed.

Q4: Can chickens survive on scratch grains alone?

No, scratch grains are only 8 to 10 percent protein and lack calcium; feeding only scratch leads to egg-laying cessation and nutritional deficiency.

Q5: How much does a bag of chick starter cost?

A 50-pound bag of chick starter typically costs between $18 and $25, while smaller 10-pound bags cost $8 to $12.

Q6: Does chicken feed expire or go bad in the bag?

Yes, chicken feed should be used within six to eight weeks of the mill date to prevent vitamin degradation, mold growth, and insect weevil infestation.

Q7: Do laying hens need extra calcium if eating layer feed?

Commercial layer feed contains 3 to 4 percent calcium, but offering free-choice oyster shell in a separate cup ensures high producers get sufficient calcium.

Q8: How can I reduce my chicken feed bill?

Reduce waste using no-spill port feeders, allow hens to free-range for insects, feed clean vegetable scraps, and ferment whole grains to boost digestibility.
