How Much Do You Get for Being a Foster Parent?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much do you get for being a foster parent requires clarifying the legal nature of foster care compensation: foster parents do not receive a commercial salary or hourly wage. Instead, state child welfare departments provide a monthly, tax-free foster care maintenance reimbursement stipend intended strictly to offset the child's daily living expenses. Across the United States, baseline monthly foster care stipends typically range from $450 to $1,000 per child for standard care, increasing to $1,200 to $2,500+ per month for children requiring specialized medical or therapeutic foster care.

State Stipend Ranges: Basic Care Rates by Child Age

In the United States, foster care is administered at the county level and funded through a partnership of state revenues and federal Title IV-E funding under the Social Security Act. Because each state sets its own reimbursement schedules based on local cost-of-living indices, baseline stipend rates vary widely.

Virtually all states utilize an escalating age-tiered payment structure: infants and toddlers (ages 0 to 5) receive the lowest baseline rate (averaging $450 to $700 per month); elementary children (ages 6 to 12) receive moderate support (averaging $550 to $850 per month); and teenagers (ages 13 to 18) receive the highest allowance (averaging $700 to $1,100 per month) to account for adult clothing sizes, extracurricular school fees, hygiene products, and higher food consumption.

Specialized, Therapeutic, and Medical Foster Care Tiers

When a foster child exhibits profound emotional trauma, psychiatric disorders, developmental disabilities, or complex medical conditions (such as feeding tubes, tracheostomies, or severe autism), they are placed in Specialized, Treatment, or Therapeutic Foster Care (TFC). Caring for these high-needs children requires intensive training, administering multiple prescription medications, transporting them to weekly occupational and psychotherapy appointments, and maintaining strict behavioral logs.

To enable one foster parent to stay home full-time as a dedicated caregiver, state child welfare agencies provide significantly elevated 'Difficulty of Care' (DOC) supplemental stipends. Therapeutic foster care rates typically range between $1,500 and $3,500 per month. In severe cases involving medically fragile infants requiring continuous nursing care, monthly payments can exceed $4,000 to $5,000.

Tax Treatment: Why Foster Care Payments Are 100% Tax-Free

From a tax perspective, foster care maintenance stipends enjoy extraordinary federal protection. Under Section 131 of the Internal Revenue Code (IRC § 131), qualified foster care payments received from a state government, political subdivision, or certified foster placement agency are completely excluded from gross income.

This means foster parents pay zero federal income tax, zero state income tax, and zero FICA payroll taxes on the monthly reimbursement money. The funds do not need to be reported as earned income on IRS Form 1040, and the payments do not jeopardize eligibility for certain income-tested credits. Furthermore, if a foster child resides in your home for more than half the tax year and you provide over half of their support from personal funds, you may qualify to claim them for the Child Tax Credit.

How to Apply and Qualify for Foster Care Stipends

Follow these five steps to navigate licensing and receive foster care maintenance payments.

  1. Verify Personal Financial Stability and Income

    States require proof that your household income can comfortably cover your own family bills before taking in a child.

  2. Complete State-Mandated Pre-Service Training (PRIDE / MAPP)

    Attend 24 to 36 hours of trauma-informed parenting classes to understand child welfare systems.

  3. Pass Comprehensive Criminal and FBI Background Checks

    Submit fingerprinting and clear child abuse registry checks for all household adults.

  4. Complete the Home Study and Physical Safety Inspection

    Ensure your home has adequate dedicated bedroom space, working smoke alarms, fire extinguishers, and locked medication boxes.

  5. Set Up Direct Deposit with the Child Welfare Agency

    Provide checking account routing numbers for automated monthly electronic maintenance stipend deposits.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Do foster parents get paid a salary?

No, foster parents receive a monthly reimbursement stipend intended strictly to offset the child's living expenses, not a salary.

Q2: How much does a foster parent make a month?

Baseline monthly stipends average between $500 and $1,000 per child, rising to $1,500 to $2,500+ for therapeutic care.

Q3: Are foster care payments taxable by the IRS?

No, under IRC Section 131, qualified foster care payments are 100% tax-free and excluded from gross income.

Q4: Does a foster child get free healthcare?

Yes, 100% of the child's medical, dental, psychiatric, and prescription healthcare is covered by state Medicaid.

Q5: Do foster parents pay for daycare?

Most states provide child care subsidies or vouchers to cover licensed daycare costs for working foster parents.

Q6: Can you be a foster parent if you work full-time?

Yes, as long as you have stable income, flexible hours, and arrange licensed daycare or after-school care.

Q7: Do you get a clothing allowance for foster children?

Yes, states typically provide an initial and annual clothing voucher ranging between $200 and $600 per child.

Q8: What is therapeutic foster care pay?

Therapeutic foster care pays higher rates ($1,500 to $3,000/mo) for caring for youth with severe trauma, psychiatric, or medical needs.
