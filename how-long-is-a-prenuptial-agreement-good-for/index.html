How Long Is a Prenuptial Agreement Good For?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how long is a prenuptial agreement good for reveals that, by default, a properly drafted and executed prenuptial agreement remains legally valid and binding indefinitely throughout the entire duration of the marriage, unless the contract explicitly includes an expiration date known as a 'sunset clause' or the spouses mutually sign a formal written revocation. However, while prenuptial agreements do not automatically expire over time, changing life circumstances, procedural defects, or unconscionable clauses can leave them vulnerable to legal challenge in court decades later.

Indefinite Validity vs. The Sunset Clause Mechanism

Under the Uniform Premarital Agreement Act (UPAA) and state marital family codes across the United States, a prenuptial agreement takes effect immediately upon the legal solemnization of the marriage and continues in full legal force until death or divorce. There is no statutory expiration period; an agreement signed in 1995 remains just as enforceable in 2026 as it was on the wedding day, provided both parties adhered to statutory execution standards.

However, couples frequently negotiate a 'sunset clause' into their prenuptial contract. A sunset clause establishes a specific calendar date or marriage anniversary upon which the prenuptial agreement either automatically terminates completely or gradually phases out. For example, a couple might stipulate that the prenuptial agreement becomes void after 10, 15, or 20 years of marriage, or that spousal support waivers incrementally increase in the non-monied spouse's favor for every 5 years the marriage endures.

How Prenuptial Agreements Get Challenged Decades Later

While a prenuptial agreement does not expire from old age, the passage of decades can create vulnerabilities if the original contract was sloppily constructed. When a divorce occurs 15 or 25 years after the wedding, family court judges closely scrutinize the historical circumstances surrounding the document's signing to verify that constitutional due process and state fairness standards were satisfied.

The most common legal grounds for invalidating a long-standing prenuptial agreement include lack of independent legal counsel (both spouses must be represented by separate, independent attorneys), coercion or duress (such as presenting the agreement hours before the wedding ceremony under threat of cancellation), and failure to provide full, fair, and honest financial asset disclosure. Hiding bank accounts, real estate holdings, or business valuations on the initial financial exhibit will completely void the agreement.

Child Custody, Lifestyle Clauses, and Unconscionability at Enforcement

Another critical legal reality is that certain contract terms are never valid, regardless of how long the agreement has existed. Under universal family law jurisprudence, parents cannot legally contract away child support or dictate future child custody arrangements in a prenuptial agreement. The family court maintains exclusive statutory jurisdiction to determine child custody and child support based solely on the 'best interests of the child' at the time of separation.

Furthermore, in states that evaluate 'unconscionability at enforcement' (such as New York or California), an agreement that was fair when signed 20 years earlier may be deemed unenforceable if severe unforeseen circumstances occurred—such as one spouse suffering a catastrophic disabling injury while the other accumulated tens of millions of dollars. If enforcing the spousal support waiver would force the disabled spouse onto public welfare assistance, the court will strike the waiver.

How to Ensure a Prenuptial Agreement Remains Enforceable Over Time

Follow these five legal best practices to ensure your marital agreement withstands scrutiny for decades.

  1. Retain Two Completely Separate, Qualified Family Lawyers

    Never use the same attorney or firm; each spouse must have independent legal representation advocating for their interests.

  2. Execute the Document Well in Advance of the Wedding

    Finalize and notarize the prenuptial agreement at least 30 to 60 days before the wedding ceremony to eliminate duress claims.

  3. Provide Full, Transparent, Certified Financial Disclosures

    Attach comprehensive net worth exhibits listing all bank accounts, real estate, debts, retirement funds, and business interests.

  4. Avoid Prohibited Terms Like Child Support or Custody

    Restrict the contract strictly to separate property division, marital asset allocation, and spousal maintenance provisions.

  5. Consider Periodic Review or Postnuptial Updates

    If having children or experiencing massive wealth shifts, consult legal counsel to draft an updated postnuptial agreement.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does a prenuptial agreement expire automatically after 10 years?

No, prenuptial agreements do not expire automatically unless both parties deliberately included an explicit 10-year sunset clause.

Q2: What is a sunset clause in a prenuptial agreement?

A sunset clause is a provision that specifies an exact date or anniversary upon which the prenuptial agreement becomes void.

Q3: Can a prenuptial agreement be changed after marriage?

Yes, spouses can modify, amend, or replace a prenuptial agreement at any time during the marriage by signing a postnuptial agreement.

Q4: Can a prenup protect future earnings and retirement?

Yes, prenuptial agreements routinely define whether future salary, bonuses, 401(k) retirement contributions, and pensions remain separate property.

Q5: Can a prenup be thrown out if one spouse cheated?

Infidelity alone does not invalidate a valid prenup unless the contract contained a specific enforceable infidelity fault clause.

Q6: What happens if a prenuptial agreement is ruled invalid?

If a judge invalidates the prenup, divorce asset division and alimony default entirely to standard state equitable distribution or community property laws.

Q7: Can a prenuptial agreement decide who gets the children?

No, provisions regarding child custody, visitation, or child support are strictly unenforceable and void as against public policy.

Q8: How long before the wedding should a prenup be signed?

Best practice is to execute and notarize the agreement at least 30 to 60 days before the wedding ceremony to prevent duress claims.
