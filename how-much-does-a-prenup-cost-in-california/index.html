How Much Does a Prenup Cost in California?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a prenup cost in California requires evaluating community property laws, independent legal counsel mandates, and asset complexity. On average, a prenuptial agreement in California costs between $2,500 and $8,000 in total combined legal fees. Straightforward agreements with basic separate property waivers range from $2,000 to $4,500, while complex agreements involving tech startup equity (RSUs/options), family businesses, or real estate portfolios average $5,000 to $12,000+.

Dual Independent Counsel Requirement and Attorney Billing Rates

In California, prenuptial agreements are governed by the Uniform Premarital Agreement Act (UPAA) codified under California Family Code Sections 1600-1617. California law establishes stringent enforceability standards: one attorney cannot legally represent both parties. Attempting to use a single lawyer or a cheap DIY online template creates a severe conflict of interest that will lead a family court judge to invalidate the agreement during a contested divorce.

Furthermore, under Family Code Section 1612(c), if the prenuptial agreement modifies or waives spousal support (alimony), the party waiving support must be represented by independent legal counsel at the time of signing. Family law attorneys in California charge hourly rates ranging from $350 to $650 per hour. The drafting attorney typically bills $2,000 to $5,000 to negotiate and draft the agreement, while the reviewing attorney for the second spouse charges $1,200 to $3,000.

The Mandatory California '7-Day Rule' and Financial Disclosures

California enforces one of the strictest statutory timing rules in the nation: the mandatory 7-calendar-day waiting period under California Family Code Section 1615(c)(2). The law dictates that at least seven full calendar days must elapse between the moment the final, fully drafted prenuptial agreement is presented to the unrepresented party (or their counsel) and the date the agreement is executed. Signing a prenup the night before the wedding makes it legally voidable under duress.

Equally critical is the requirement for full, fair, and transparent financial disclosures. Both parties must exchange exhaustive schedules detailing all assets, real estate equity, bank accounts, investment portfolios, closely held business valuations, credit card debts, student loans, and tax liabilities. Hiding an asset or materially understating income provides automatic legal grounds for a court to invalidate the entire contract.

Protecting Community Property, Tech Equity, and Business Assets

California is a strict community property state under Family Code Section 760, presuming that all property, income, and assets acquired by either spouse during marriage belong equally (50/50) to both spouses. Without a prenuptial agreement, stock options, RSUs granted by Silicon Valley tech employers, business appreciation, and 401(k) contributions made during the marriage are divided in half upon divorce.

A customized California prenuptial agreement allows couples to opt out of community property statutes. It can stipulate that each spouse's earnings, business appreciation, and tech equity remain their sole and separate property. Given that high-asset divorces in California routinely cost $50,000 to $100,000+ in contested legal fees, spending $4,000 to $7,000 on a prenuptial agreement provides invaluable financial certainty and marital peace of mind.

How to Create a Legally Binding California Prenup

Follow these five statutory steps to ensure your California prenuptial agreement is completely enforceable.

  1. Start the Process 3 to 6 Months Before the Wedding

    Initiate discussions early to allow ample time for document drafting, negotiation, and full financial disclosure exchange.

  2. Retain Two Separate Independent Family Law Attorneys

    Ensure both you and your fiancé hire independent lawyers licensed in California to avoid conflicts of interest.

  3. Compile Complete and Honest Financial Disclosure Schedules

    Attach comprehensive asset ledgers, bank statements, tax returns, and debt schedules to the agreement.

  4. Observe the Mandatory 7-Calendar-Day Cooling-Off Period

    Ensure seven full calendar days pass between receiving the final agreed draft and signing the document.

  5. Execute the Agreement Before a California Notary Public

    Sign the agreement in the presence of a notary public with formal certificates of independent legal counsel attached.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the average cost of a prenup in California?

A California prenup typically costs between $2,500 and $8,000 in total legal fees for both spouses' independent attorneys.

Q2: Can one lawyer represent both of us for a California prenup?

No, dual representation is a direct conflict of interest and will cause a California court to invalidate the agreement.

Q3: What is the 7-day rule for prenups in California?

California law requires at least 7 full calendar days between presenting the final agreement and signing it to prevent coercion.

Q4: Can a California prenup waive spousal support (alimony)?

Yes, but only if the waiving spouse was represented by independent counsel and the terms are not unconscionable at enforcement.

Q5: Can a prenuptial agreement include child custody or child support?

No, California law strictly prohibits prenups from determining child support or custody; those decisions remain under judicial authority.

Q6: Is California a community property state?

Yes, all earnings, assets, and debts acquired during marriage are presumed 50/50 community property unless altered by a prenup.

Q7: Can you get a prenup without an attorney in California?

While theoretically possible for property-only agreements, any spousal support waiver strictly requires legal representation by law.

Q8: Can a prenup protect tech stock options and RSUs in California?

Yes, a well-drafted prenup can specify that all RSUs, stock options, and vesting equity remain separate property.
