Asbestos Siding Disposal NJ: Guide

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 23, 2026

Disposing of asbestos siding in New Jersey is strictly regulated by the New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection (NJDEP) under state solid waste management rules. Widely installed across New Jersey's suburban and coastal communities prior to the 1980s, asbestos-cement transite siding cannot be commingled with ordinary municipal trash or standard construction and demolition debris. Property owners and contractors must follow specific packaging, manifesting, and disposal protocols at state-authorized facilities.

NJDEP Regulatory Standards for Asbestos Waste Packaging

Under New Jersey Administrative Code (N.J.A.C. 7:26-2.12), all asbestos-containing waste materials generated in the state must be packaged and managed to prevent any visible particulate emissions into the open air. While intact transite siding is categorized as non-friable asbestos waste (ID Type 27), the physical stress of removal, handling, and transport frequently creates broken edges and chips that can release respirable fibers.

NJDEP regulations require all asbestos siding waste to be thoroughly wetted during removal and packaged in two layers of 6-mil polyethylene plastic sheeting or sealed inside heavy-duty, puncture-resistant 6-mil poly bags. Every outer package must be securely sealed with duct tape and bear standardized OSHA and DOT warning labels displaying the generator's name, address, and the specific site location where the siding was removed.

Permitted Disposal Facilities and Waste Transport in New Jersey

In New Jersey, asbestos siding waste can only be accepted at authorized solid waste facilities specifically permitted by the NJDEP to receive non-friable or friable asbestos. Most municipal county transfer stations do not accept asbestos siding from private vehicles; instead, waste must be delivered to specialized regional disposal facilities, such as the Middlesex County Landfill or the Cumberland County Solid Waste Complex, under prior authorization.

Transporting asbestos waste requires compliance with state solid waste hauler licensing. While homeowners removing siding from their own primary residence may transport limited quantities of non-friable siding in their personal vehicles under specific county exemptions, commercial contractors must utilize NJDEP-licensed A-901 solid waste haulers. Shipments must be accompanied by a completed Non-Hazardous Solid Waste Manifest or federal hazardous waste manifest depending on friability.

How to Package and Legally Dispose of Asbestos Siding in New Jersey

Structured procedure for packaging, transporting, and disposing of transite siding under NJDEP regulations.

  1. Wet Down Siding Before Packing

    Mist all removed siding shingles thoroughly with amended water to prevent fine cement dust from aerosolizing during packaging.

  2. Double-Wrap in 6-Mil Polyethylene

    Stack shingles in manageable bundles (under 40 pounds) and wrap them securely in two layers of heavy-duty 6-mil polyethylene sheeting.

  3. Seal with Gooseneck Duct Tape

    Twist the ends of the plastic wrap into a gooseneck fold and seal them tightly with heavy-duty construction duct tape.

  4. Affix Required OSHA/DOT Warning Labels

    Attach permanent hazard labels to every bundle displaying the standard asbestos warning along with your name and job site address.

  5. Deliver to NJDEP Permitted Landfill

    Transport bundles to an authorized New Jersey asbestos landfill using an approved hauler and retain the signed waste manifest for your records.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I put asbestos siding in a regular dumpster in NJ?

No, placing asbestos siding in a regular dumpster is illegal in New Jersey and will result in rejected loads and severe NJDEP fines.

Q2: Can a homeowner transport their own asbestos siding in NJ?

Certain counties allow homeowners to transport small quantities of double-wrapped non-friable siding in personal vehicles with advance approval.

Q3: What thickness plastic is required for asbestos disposal in NJ?

NJDEP regulations require double wrapping in 6-mil polyethylene plastic sheeting (two separate 6-mil layers or two 6-mil bags).

Q4: How much does it cost to dispose of asbestos siding in New Jersey?

Permitted landfills typically charge between $120 and $250 per ton, plus administrative manifest processing fees.

Q5: What is an A-901 licensed hauler in New Jersey?

An A-901 hauler is a commercial waste transport company licensed and vetted by the NJDEP to haul regulated solid and hazardous waste.

Q6: Do all county landfills in New Jersey accept asbestos siding?

No, many county transfer stations do not accept asbestos; you must verify in advance whether your county facility accepts ID Type 27 waste.

Q7: What information must be on the asbestos warning label in NJ?

Labels must state: 'DANGER CONTAINS ASBESTOS FIBERS AVOID CREATING DUST' along with the generator's name and extraction site address.

Q8: Why does asbestos siding need to be wetted during disposal?

Wetting suppresses microscopic fibers, ensuring that if the outer plastic barrier is accidentally punctured, fibers cannot escape into the air.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Properly disposing of asbestos siding in New Jersey protects the state's natural environment and shields property owners from severe NJDEP environmental enforcement penalties. Commingling asbestos shingles with standard dumpster debris is a serious environmental violation that can result in hefty state fines and mandatory site remediation. Double-bagging all materials in labeled 6-mil plastic, coordinating with permitted county facilities, and securing signed manifests ensures complete legal compliance.
