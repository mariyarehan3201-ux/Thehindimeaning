How Long Does It Take to Get a Skydiving License?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how long does it take to get a skydiving license requires examining the progression established by the United States Parachute Association (USPA). To earn your first recognized skydiving credential—the USPA 'A' License—you must complete ground school, graduate through the Accelerated Freefall (AFF) training progression, log a minimum of 25 total jumps, learn parachute packing, and pass an oral and written exam. While a full-time, dedicated student can complete this in as little as 1 to 2 weeks during an intensive skydiving camp, most recreational weekend jumpers take between 2 to 6 months.

Ground School and the Accelerated Freefall (AFF) Progression (Jumps 1–8)

Your skydiving licensing journey begins with First Jump Course (FJC) ground school. This intensive 6-to-8-hour classroom course covers parachute deployment mechanics, stable belly-to-earth body flight aerodynamics, altitude awareness via altimeters, emergency canopy cutaway protocols, and landing flare techniques.

Immediately following ground school, students begin the 7-to-8-stage Accelerated Freefall (AFF) progression. In stages 1 through 3, you jump from 14,000 feet accompanied by two certified AFF instructors holding your harness grips. You learn altitude checks, practice 'dummy' rip-cord pulls, and deploy your own parachute at 5,000 feet. In stages 4 through 7, a single instructor flies alongside as you execute solo turns, forward motion, backflips, and barrel rolls, culminating in a clear-and-pull jump at 3,500 feet. Clearing AFF takes 2 to 4 days of consistent jumping weather.

Solo Consolidation, Canopy Coaching, and Packing Requirements (Jumps 9–25)

After graduating from AFF, students hold 'solo student status' and begin the consolidation phase (Jumps 9 through 25). During this phase, you are no longer held by instructors, but remain under the supervision of the dropzone's safety officer. You refine high-speed horizontal tracking drills—a vital safety maneuver required to separate from group formations before pulling your parachute.

To satisfy USPA A-license criteria, you must also complete at least 5 coached jumps with a USPA Coach, learning basic 2-way belly formation relative work (approaching other jumpers in freefall, taking grips, and executing safe break-offs at 4,000 feet). You must also demonstrate canopy piloting accuracy by landing within 20 meters of a designated target on 5 unassisted jumps, and successfully pack a main parachute unassisted under instructor observation.

The Biggest Bottlenecks: Weather Delays and Jump Currency

While the physical requirement of 25 jumps can theoretically be completed in 4 to 5 busy jumping days, weather is the primary obstacle for student skydivers. Student skydivers are legally restricted by FAA and USPA rules to maximum wind speeds of 14 knots (approx. 16 mph) and must maintain strict cloud clearance limits. Wind gusts, overcast cloud ceilings below 10,000 feet, or summer rainstorms can easily ground student operations for entire weekends.

Furthermore, USPA rules enforce strict currency requirements: an unverified student skydiver who does not make a jump within 30 days loses currency and must pay for a recurrent ground refresher and repeat an instructor jump before progressing. To finish your license within 2 to 3 months, you must commit to jumping at least 2 to 4 times every weekend that the weather allows.

How to Get Your USPA 'A' Skydiving License

Follow these five chronological milestones to progress from complete beginner to certified solo skydiver.

  1. Complete First Jump Course (FJC) Ground School

    Attend an 8-hour classroom clinic on body flight, altimeter checks, emergency procedures, and landing flare mechanics.

  2. Clear All Accelerated Freefall (AFF) Levels

    Execute jumps 1 through 8 with certified AFF instructors, proving stability, 360-degree turns, backflips, and tracking.

  3. Complete Coached 2-Way Relative Work Jumps

    Complete jumps 9 through 24 practicing formation flying, docking maneuvers, and break-off altitude tracking with a coach.

  4. Master Main Parachute Packing and Spotting

    Learn to pack your own Ram-air canopy unassisted and demonstrate understanding of wind drift and aircraft jump-run spotting.

  5. Pass the A-License Check Dive and Written Exam

    Score 80%+ on the 40-question USPA exam and execute a flawless 25th check skydive with an examiner.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can you get a skydiving license in one week?

Yes, by attending an intensive 7-to-10-day summer skydiving immersion camp with good weather, you can complete all 25 jumps.

Q2: How many jumps do you need for a skydiving license?

You need a minimum of 25 logged freefall jumps to qualify for the USPA 'A' License.

Q3: Do you have to do a tandem jump first?

Most dropzones require 1 or 2 tandem training jumps before entering ground school, though some allow direct entry into AFF.

Q4: How much does it cost to get a skydiving license?

Total costs range between $2,500 and $3,500, which includes ground school, instructor jumps, gear rentals, pack fees, and licensing.

Q5: What privileges does an 'A' license give you?

An A-license allows you to jump at any dropzone worldwide without supervision, pack your own parachute, and jump in group formations.

Q6: What happens if you don't jump for a month while a student?

If more than 30 days pass between jumps, you lose currency and must complete a refresher ground review and an instructor jump.

Q7: Is solo skydiving scary compared to tandem?

Solo freefall requires immense focus on altimeters and tasks, which often replaces panic with high situational awareness.

Q8: What is the next license after the 'A' license?

The USPA offers B (50 jumps), C (200 jumps), and D (500 jumps) licenses, unlocking night jumps, demo jumps, and instructor ratings.
