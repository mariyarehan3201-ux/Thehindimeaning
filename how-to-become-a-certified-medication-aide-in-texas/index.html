How to Become a Certified Medication Aide in Texas?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Becoming a Certified Medication Aide (CMA) in Texas allows you to take on expanded clinical responsibilities within licensed nursing facilities, assisted living communities, and correctional institutions. Regulated under Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 242 and overseen by the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC), CMAs administer physician-prescribed non-parenteral medications under the direct supervision of a licensed vocational nurse (LVN) or registered nurse (RN). Navigating the certification pathway requires satisfying prerequisite Certified Nurse Aide (CNA) experience, completing an HHSC-approved training curriculum, passing state competency examinations, and maintaining active registry listing.

HHSC Eligibility Criteria and CNA Prerequisites

The Texas Health and Human Services Commission establishes rigorous baseline prerequisites before a healthcare worker can enroll in a state-approved Medication Aide Training Program. Under 26 Texas Administrative Code Chapter 557, candidates must possess a high school diploma or accredited GED certificate, be at least eighteen years of age, and be free of disqualifying criminal convictions listed in Texas Health and Safety Code Section 250.006.

Most importantly, applicants must demonstrate active professional standing as a Certified Nurse Aide (CNA) on the Texas Nurse Aide Registry or hold comparable institutional healthcare experience. To enroll, candidates must document at least ninety days or six continuous months (depending on facility setting) of full-time employment as a direct-care nursing assistant in an HHSC-licensed facility within the previous twelve months. This prerequisite guarantees that candidates already understand vital signs, infection control, patient positioning, and clinical documentation before handling prescription drugs.

HHSC Curriculum: Pharmacology, Dosage, and Clinicals

The state-mandated Medication Aide Training Program comprises exactly one hundred and forty clock hours of comprehensive instruction delivered by approved vocational schools, community colleges, or healthcare institutions. The program is divided into one hundred hours of classroom theory and forty hours of supervised clinical practicum within an HHSC-licensed long-term care facility. The didactic curriculum covers basic pharmacology, drug interactions, therapeutic actions, side effects, medical abbreviations, and dosage calculations.

During the forty-hour clinical practicum, students administer medications to real residents under the continuous, one-on-one watchful eye of a designated Registered Nurse instructor. Students master the six rights of medication administration: right resident, right medication, right dose, right route, right time, and right documentation. Students are strictly trained on legal scope boundaries; Texas CMAs are prohibited from administering intravenous (IV) fluids, injecting intramuscular medications, calculating initial insulin dosages, or administering medications via tube feedings.

Following coursework completion, students must pass the Texas state medication aide examination administered through HHSC testing portals. The exam consists of one hundred multiple-choice questions covering clinical safety, pharmacology, resident rights, and state administrative rules, requiring a passing score of at least seventy percent.

Registry Placement, Annual Renewal, and Career Mobility

Upon passing the state examination, your name is entered onto the Texas Medication Aide Registry, and HHSC issues your official Medication Aide Permit. Holding a dual CNA and CMA credential significantly increases your marketability and hourly compensation in long-term care environments. While standard CNAs handle bathing, transferring, and feeding, CMAs manage complex medication carts, supervise med-pass schedules, and monitor residents for adverse drug reactions.

Texas CMA permits remain valid for exactly one year and must be renewed annually through the HHSC online portal. Renewal mandates completing seven clock hours of state-approved continuing education covering pharmacology and medication administration updates, alongside paying the annual renewal fee. For many healthcare workers, working as a CMA serves as an invaluable stepping stone toward earning an associate degree in nursing (ADN) to become a Licensed Vocational Nurse or Registered Nurse.

How to Become a Certified Medication Aide in Texas in 5 Steps

Follow this systematic roadmap to satisfy state prerequisites, complete clinical hours, and earn your Texas CMA permit.

  1. Gain Required Experience as an Active Texas CNA

    Work at least ninety days to six months as a full-time certified nurse aide in an HHSC-licensed facility to secure administrator endorsement.

  2. Enroll in an HHSC-Approved Medication Aide Program

    Register for an accredited 140-hour state training course comprising 100 classroom hours and 40 supervised clinical practicum hours.

  3. Complete Supervised Clinical Med-Pass Training

    Administer prescribed oral and topical medications under the direct physical supervision of an RN instructor within a licensed nursing facility.

  4. Pass the Texas HHSC State Medication Aide Exam

    Achieve a passing score of at least 70% on the 100-question state board exam evaluating pharmacology, dosage math, and state rules.

  5. Obtain Your Official HHSC Medication Aide Permit

    Receive confirmation of your placement on the Texas Medication Aide Registry and begin practicing under licensed nurse supervision.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the difference between a CNA and a CMA in Texas?

A CNA provides direct activities of daily living care; a CMA is an advanced aide certified to administer routine prescription medications under nurse supervision.

Q2: How long is the medication aide course in Texas?

The state curriculum requires exactly 140 clock hours, consisting of 100 hours of classroom theory and 40 hours of supervised clinical practicum.

Q3: Can a CMA give insulin injections in Texas?

No, Texas regulations strictly prohibit CMAs from administering injectable medications, including insulin, intramuscular shots, and intravenous lines.

Q4: How many hours of continuing education do Texas CMAs need to renew?

Texas CMAs must complete seven clock hours of approved continuing education every year prior to permit renewal.

Q5: What agency regulates medication aides in Texas?

The Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC) oversees training provider approvals, testing, and the Medication Aide Registry.

Q6: Can a certified medication aide work in a hospital in Texas?

In Texas, CMA permits apply primarily to licensed nursing facilities, assisted living facilities, intermediate care facilities, and correctional facilities.

Q7: How much does a medication aide make in Texas?

CMAs in Texas typically earn between $17 and $23 per hour, which is noticeably higher than entry-level nurse aide wages.

Q8: Can you take the Texas CMA course online?

Certain approved training programs offer hybrid theory lectures online, but the mandatory 40-hour clinical practicum must be completed in-person.
