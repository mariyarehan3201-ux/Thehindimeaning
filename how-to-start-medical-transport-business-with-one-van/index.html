How to Start Medical Transport Business with One Van?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Starting a Non-Emergency Medical Transportation (NEMT) business with just one van is one of the most accessible and recession-resilient entry points into commercial specialized logistics. Every day, thousands of elderly patients, dialysis recipients, and individuals with physical disabilities require safe, reliable transportation to scheduled clinical consultations, chemotherapy infusions, and rehabilitation therapies. By starting lean with a single wheelchair-accessible vehicle, ambitious owner-operators can master state licensing, establish relationships with local Medicaid brokerage networks, optimize daily routing, and build the cash reserves necessary to scale into a multi-vehicle fleet.

Vehicle Procurement, ADA Specifications, and Equipment Compliance

Launching a single-van NEMT business begins with procuring a vehicle that satisfies all relevant Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) accessibility guidelines and state transportation board regulations. Not every passenger van can be converted for commercial medical transport. The vehicle must feature a raised roof or dropped floor providing sufficient interior head clearance, a certified hydraulic wheelchair lift or commercial-grade manual ramp, and compliant four-point wheelchair tie-down systems with lap and shoulder occupant restraints.

Common base vehicles include full-size platforms like the Ford Transit, Ram ProMaster, or Mercedes-Benz Sprinter, alongside converted minivans like the Toyota Sienna or Chrysler Pacifica. Selecting the right vehicle configuration dictates the type of passenger trips your business can accept, directly influencing daily gross revenue potential.

Regulatory Licensing, Commercial Insurance, and Medicaid Enrollment

Because NEMT operators transport vulnerable medical patients, state transportation authorities and health departments enforce strict operating standards. Founders must register their business entity, obtain a commercial Department of Transportation (USDOT) number if operating interstate or within commercial state mandates, and secure specialized Commercial Auto and Public Livery Insurance.

Standard personal auto policies or general commercial delivery insurance policies strictly exclude passenger transportation for hire. Commercial NEMT insurance must include bodily injury, property damage, and specialized passenger-loading liability (protecting against falls while assisting a client into the vehicle). Most state regulators and brokerage agencies require a minimum of $1,000,000 in combined single-limit auto liability coverage.

Optimizing the Single-Van Schedule for Maximum Profitability

The biggest challenge for a single-van operator is deadhead mileage—driving empty between passenger drops and pickups. To generate substantial profit, an operator must cluster appointments geographically and create tight recurring schedules around recurring treatment cycles, such as Monday-Wednesday-Friday and Tuesday-Thursday-Saturday kidney dialysis runs.

By coordinating morning clinic drop-offs with midday return runs and filling afternoon gaps with hospital discharges or private-pay doctor appointments, a dedicated single-van operator can complete 8 to 14 billable passenger trips per day, generating predictable daily revenues while conserving fuel and vehicle wear.

How to Launch an NEMT Business with One Van

Step-by-step roadmap to acquire an ADA vehicle, obtain operating licenses, enroll with Medicaid brokers, and dispatch your first passenger trip.

  1. Form Legal Entity and Acquire Commercial Insurance

    Register an LLC, secure an Employer Identification Number, and bind a $1,000,000 commercial auto public livery insurance policy that includes passenger assistance liability.

  2. Acquire an ADA-Compliant Wheelchair Van

    Purchase or lease a certified wheelchair-accessible van equipped with an ADA-compliant hydraulic lift or ramp and certified Q'Straint tie-down floor tracks.

  3. Obtain Driver Certifications and Background Checks

    Complete CPR/First Aid certification, PASS (Passenger Assistance Safety and Sensitivity) training, and clear 10-panel drug screens and motor vehicle record checks.

  4. Enroll with State Medicaid Transportation Brokers

    Submit provider applications to national and state NEMT brokers such as Modivcare and MTM to gain access to daily dispatch trip boards.

  5. Market Directly to Dialysis and Rehabilitation Clinics

    Visit local dialysis centers, physical therapy practices, and assisted living facilities to establish direct facility relationships for private-pay and scheduled recurring trips.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much money can a single NEMT van generate per month?

A dedicated single-van NEMT business operating 5 to 6 days a week typically generates between $8,000 and $15,000 in gross monthly revenue, yielding net owner profits of $3,500 to $7,000 after fuel, insurance, and maintenance.

Q2: What is the difference between ambulatory and wheelchair NEMT trips?

Ambulatory trips involve passengers who can walk independently but lack reliable personal transportation. Wheelchair trips require specialized vans with ramps or hydraulic lifts and command significantly higher base dispatch fees.

Q3: Can I use a regular minivan for medical transportation?

No, a standard factory minivan cannot be used for wheelchair transport unless it has undergone certified ADA conversion modifications, including lowered floor pans, reinforced ramp structures, and crash-tested tie-down anchor points.

Q4: What insurance is required to operate an NEMT van?

Operators must carry Commercial Auto Liability insurance with public livery endorsements (typically $1,000,000 combined single limit), General Liability coverage, and passenger loading/unloading injury endorsements.

Q5: Who are the major Medicaid transportation brokers?

Major national brokers that manage state Medicaid transportation contracts include Modivcare (formerly LogistiCare), MTM Inc., Access2Care, and Southeastrans.

Q6: Do NEMT drivers need a Commercial Driver's License (CDL)?

Generally no. Standard vans transporting fewer than 15 passengers do not require a CDL under federal guidelines, though drivers must hold a clean standard driver's license and pass PASS passenger assistance training.

Q7: How much does a converted wheelchair van cost?

A reliable used ADA-compliant wheelchair van typically costs between $30,000 and $55,000, while a brand-new converted high-roof commercial van ranges from $65,000 to $95,000.

Q8: What is PASS certification for NEMT drivers?

PASS stands for Passenger Assistance Safety and Sensitivity. It is an industry-standard credential that trains drivers in wheelchair securement, emergency procedures, and respectful interaction with disabled passengers.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Starting a medical transportation business with one van is a practical, highly scalable route to entrepreneurial independence. By acquiring an ADA-compliant wheelchair-accessible vehicle, securing adequate commercial liability insurance, enrolling with statewide Medicaid transportation brokers, and maintaining strict on-time punctuality, you can generate strong positive cash flow from your first vehicle and lay the groundwork to purchase additional vans as demand surges.
