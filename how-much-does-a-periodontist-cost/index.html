How Much Does a Periodontist Cost?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a periodontist cost requires evaluating the severity of periodontal disease, the diagnostic procedures required, and whether treatment involves non-surgical deep cleanings or advanced regenerative gum surgery. On average, a visit to a periodontist costs between $150 and $400 for a comprehensive periodontal examination and full-mouth probing. Treatment costs range from $800 to $1,800 for full-mouth scaling and root planing, to $1,500 to $5,000+ for surgical gum grafting, bone regeneration, or laser periodontal surgery.

Non-Surgical Treatments: Scaling, Root Planing, and Antimicrobial Therapy

Periodontists specialize in the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of periodontal disease (gum disease) and the placement of dental implants. The first-line intervention for mild-to-moderate periodontitis is scaling and root planing (SRP), commonly known as a dental deep cleaning. Unlike a standard preventive dental cleaning that polishes crowns above the gumline, SRP involves ultrasonic scaling and hand curettage beneath the gingival margin to remove calcified subgingival calculus (tartar) and bacterial endotoxins from infected root surfaces.

Periodontists bill scaling and root planing by dental quadrant (four quadrants in a full mouth). The national average cost ranges from $200 to $450 per quadrant ($800 to $1,800 for the entire mouth). Periodontists frequently place localized subgingival antibiotic microspheres (such as Arestin / minocycline hydrochloride) into deep periodontal pockets exceeding 5 millimeters, adding $45 to $95 per treated tooth site.

Surgical Periodontal Procedures: Gum Grafts, Bone Grafts, and LANAP

When periodontal pocket depths exceed 6 to 8 millimeters or severe gingival recession exposes sensitive tooth roots, surgical intervention becomes mandatory to prevent tooth loss. Soft tissue gum grafting—harvesting donor connective tissue from the patient's hard palate or using acellular dermal matrix (Alloderm)—costs $800 to $1,600 per tooth site. Osseous flap surgery, which reflects the gum tissue back to reshape damaged jawbone and place mineralized bone graft matrix, costs $1,200 to $3,000 per quadrant.

A modern, minimally invasive alternative to traditional scalpel osseous surgery is the LANAP protocol (Laser-Assisted New Attachment Procedure). Utilizing a specialized pulsed Nd:YAG dental laser (PerioLase MVP-7), the periodontist selectively destroys black-pigmented periodontopathogenic bacteria without cutting or suturing healthy gum tissue. LANAP promotes true bone regeneration around natural teeth, costing $3,500 to $7,500 for a full-mouth treatment.

Dental Insurance Coverage and Periodontal Maintenance Schedules

Most comprehensive dental insurance plans categorize periodontal treatments as basic or major restorative services. Scaling and root planing is generally reimbursed at 50% to 80% of allowable fees, while periodontal surgeries (gum grafts and osseous surgery) are typically reimbursed at 50%, subject to the patient's annual policy maximum benefit ($1,000 to $2,000). Because advanced periodontal treatment can quickly exhaust annual maximums, treatments are often phased across calendar benefit years.

Following active periodontal therapy, patients must transition into a lifetime periodontal maintenance program rather than returning to routine semi-annual cleanings. Periodontal maintenance visits occur every three to four months ($130 to $250 per visit). This strict quarterly schedule disrupts subgingival bacterial biofilm before pathogenic microflora can repopulate and destroy alveolar bone support.

How to Prepare for a Periodontist Appointment

Follow these five tactical steps to evaluate periodontal costs and maximize your insurance benefits.

  1. Obtain Referral Notes and Recent X-Rays from Your Dentist

    Have your general dentist send your recent full-mouth X-rays (FMX) and periodontal probing records to avoid duplicative imaging fees.

  2. Submit Pre-Treatment Authorizations to Dental Insurance

    Request the periodontist's billing department submit pre-determinations for scaling or grafting to confirm out-of-pocket co-pays.

  3. Discuss Local Anesthesia and Sedation Options

    Confirm whether local numbing is included and evaluate whether nitrous oxide ($75-$150) or IV sedation ($400-$800) is necessary.

  4. Phase Treatment Across Insurance Calendar Years

    If requiring multiple surgical quadrants, schedule two quadrants in December and two in January to utilize two annual maximum benefit caps.

  5. Commit Strictly to 3-Month Periodontal Maintenance Cleanings

    Maintain quarterly periodontal maintenance cleanings to prevent costly disease relapse and preserve your surgical investment.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a periodontist cost without insurance?

An initial exam and probing costs $150 to $350; deep cleanings range from $800 to $1,800 full-mouth; gum surgery ranges from $1,200 to $4,000+.

Q2: Is a periodontist more expensive than a regular dentist?

Yes, periodontists undergo three additional years of surgical residency and specialize in complex gum disease and implants, commanding 20% to 40% higher fees.

Q3: Does dental insurance cover periodontist visits?

Yes, dental insurance typically covers 50% to 80% of scaling and root planing and up to 50% of gum surgeries, subject to annual maximums.

Q4: How much does gum graft surgery cost per tooth?

Gum grafting typically costs between $800 and $1,600 per tooth site without insurance, depending on donor tissue source.

Q5: What is the difference between a dental cleaning and periodontal maintenance?

A standard cleaning is preventive above the gumline; periodontal maintenance is therapeutic, scaling deep subgingival pockets every 3 to 4 months.

Q6: Can a periodontist save loose teeth?

Yes, through deep debridement, bone grafting, splinting, and laser LANAP therapy, periodontists frequently stabilize and save loose teeth.

Q7: How much does LANAP laser gum surgery cost?

LANAP laser surgery typically costs between $3,500 and $7,500 for a full-mouth procedure.

Q8: Does scaling and root planing hurt?

No, the periodontist administers local anesthesia to numb the gums completely; mild soreness lasts 24 to 48 hours afterward.
