Does the X5 Have a 3rd Row?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 12, 2026

Yes, the BMW X5 has historically offered an optional third-row seat, but it is not a standard feature and is not available on every model trim or model year. On certain fourth-generation BMW X5 models (G05 chassis, 2019 to present), buyers could select an optional two-passenger third row on standard gasoline models like the sDrive40i and xDrive40i. However, this third-row option is strictly excluded from plug-in hybrid models like the xDrive45e and xDrive50e, as well as high-performance M variants, due to chassis packaging and high-voltage battery placements.

Chassis Packaging and Model Availability Across Generations

The BMW X5 has positioned itself primarily as an athletic, driver-focused midsize luxury Sports Activity Vehicle rather than a dedicated family hauler. Across the second generation (E70, 2007-2013), third generation (F15, 2014-2018), and fourth generation (G05, 2019-present), BMW offered an optional fold-flat third row to provide occasional seating for larger families while preserving classic rear cargo utility.

However, selecting the third row requires careful attention to specific model trims and equipment packages. On G05 generation models, the third-row package also mandated the inclusion of third-row climate controls and rear-axle two-axle air suspension to maintain optimal ride leveling under heavy passenger payloads. Notably, the third row cannot be equipped on plug-in hybrid variants because the lithium-ion battery pack occupies the exact underfloor storage compartment where folded seats would reside.

Real-World Spatial Dimensions and Passenger Usability

While having an extra pair of seats in a luxury German SUV sounds appealing, prospective buyers should maintain realistic expectations regarding interior comfort. The BMW X5 third row provides modest legroom of approximately twenty-seven inches, coupled with a low seat cushion height that forces adult passengers into a knees-up sitting posture. Consequently, automotive journalists and owners universally classify the X5 third row as an occasional jump seat intended primarily for young children or short emergency trips.

Furthermore, deploying the third row impacts cargo volume significantly. In standard two-row configuration, the BMW X5 offers an expansive 33.9 cubic feet of cargo room behind the second-row split-folding tailgate. When the auxiliary third row is raised, cargo room drops to less than 11 cubic feet, leaving just enough room for a row of backpacks or slim grocery bags. Folding the seats flat restores the primary cargo deck without sacrificing luggage utility.

Choosing Between a 3-Row X5 and the Larger BMW X7

For luxury buyers who frequently transport six or seven passengers, cross-shopping the three-row BMW X5 with the larger BMW X7 is an important exercise. The X7 was developed on an elongated version of the same CLAR platform specifically to solve the third-row spatial limitations of the X5. In the X7, the third row is standard equipment, featuring power-adjustable heated seating, dedicated dual USB-C ports, an independent panoramic glass moonroof panel, and genuine adult-friendly legroom.

Conversely, if you only need extra seats a few times a year for impromptu carpools or visiting relatives, a three-row equipped X5 remains an outstanding option. It delivers sharper handling dynamics, easier parallel parking in urban environments, and superior fuel economy compared to full-size three-row luxury SUVs, all while retaining BMW signature driving precision.

How to Inspect a Pre-Owned BMW X5 for 3rd Row Seating in 5 Steps

Follow these five verification steps to determine whether a pre-owned BMW X5 is equipped with the genuine factory third-row seating package.

  1. Decode the Vehicle Identification Number (VIN)

    Enter the 17-digit VIN into a reputable BMW option decoder to verify factory option code 4UB (3rd Row Seating).

  2. Inspect the Rear Cargo Floor Area

    Open the two-piece rear split tailgate and check if the floor houses fold-flat seatbacks instead of the deep underfloor storage bin.

  3. Verify Second-Row Power Slide Controls

    Look for electric tilt-and-slide adjustment buttons mounted on the shoulders of the second-row outboard seats.

  4. Check for Rear Air Suspension Equipment

    Confirm that the center console features the toggle height switch indicating the required two-axle air suspension system.

  5. Test Third-Row Latching and Seatbelts

    Raise both third-row seats, fasten the three-point seatbelts, and verify that the headrests lock firmly in their upright positions.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is the third row standard equipment on the BMW X5?

No, the third row has never been standard on the X5; it was offered strictly as an optional add-on package.

Q2: Can adults fit in the BMW X5 third row comfortably?

No, the third row has limited legroom and low cushions, making it suitable almost exclusively for young children.

Q3: Can you get a third row on the BMW X5 hybrid?

No, plug-in hybrid models like the xDrive45e and xDrive50e cannot accommodate a third row due to underfloor battery packs.

Q4: Does the BMW X5 M have a third row?

No, high-performance X5 M and M Competition variants are produced exclusively in two-row, five-passenger configurations.

Q5: What is the BMW option code for third row seating on the X5?

The factory BMW build option code for third-row seating on the G05 generation X5 is option code 4UB.

Q6: Does adding the third row eliminate the spare tire?

Yes, the folding third-row seats occupy the underfloor storage compartment, eliminating space for an optional spare tire kit.

Q7: Which BMW SUV has a full-size third row for adults?

The full-size BMW X7 features standard three-row seating designed specifically to accommodate adult passengers comfortably.

Q8: How much cargo space is left when the X5 third row is up?

There is approximately 11.1 cubic feet of cargo volume remaining behind the deployed third-row seats.
