Is Nurtec a Specialty Drug?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 30, 2026

Navigating prescription drug coverage for advanced migraine therapies often leaves patients confused about classification, insurance tiers, and pharmacy fulfillment channels. Nurtec ODT (rimegepant) is an innovative, dual-action calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) receptor antagonist approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for both the acute treatment of active migraine attacks and the preventive treatment of episodic migraine. While Nurtec shares characteristics with complex biologics, whether health insurance plans categorize Nurtec as a specialty drug or a high-tier brand-name medication depends entirely on your specific pharmacy benefit manager (PBM) formulary design.

Classification Criteria: Specialty Drug vs Non-Preferred Brand Medication

To determine whether Nurtec ODT qualifies as a specialty drug, one must understand how health insurance plans and pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs) define the specialty pharmaceutical category. Historically, specialty medications are high-cost prescription drugs used to treat complex, chronic, or rare medical conditions such as multiple sclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis, oncology, or hepatitis C. Specialty drugs typically cost more than $670 to $1,000 per month, require specialized handling such as cold-chain refrigeration, involve complex clinical administration such as subcutaneous self-injection or intravenous infusion, and necessitate ongoing clinical monitoring by specialized pharmacists.

Nurtec ODT exhibits a hybrid profile within this framework. Pharmacologically, rimegepant is a small-molecule CGRP receptor antagonist delivered as an orally disintegrating tablet (ODT) that dissolves instantly on or under the tongue without water. It does not require refrigeration, reconstituted mixing, or sterile needle injection, and it is stored at controlled room temperature. Because of these convenient handling characteristics, many commercial health insurance plans classify Nurtec as a Tier 3 Non-Preferred Brand medication rather than a Tier 4 or Tier 5 Specialty medication. Similar considerations emerge when analyzing other modern oral migraine therapies, such as evaluating whether Qulipta specialty drug status applies across commercial and Medicare formularies.

However, the definition of a specialty medication is not purely clinical—it is heavily financial. With wholesale acquisition costs (WAC) for Nurtec ODT generally ranging between $900 and $1,100 for a package of eight orally disintegrating tablets, some health insurance plans and employer formularies assign Nurtec directly to their specialty drug tier solely based on cost thresholds. When placed on a specialty tier, patients face coinsurance percentages (such as 20% to 33% of the drug's retail cost) rather than predictable flat copayments, unless manufacturer copay cards are applied. Overcoming Prior Authorization, Step Therapy, and Copay Assistance

Regardless of whether your insurer designates Nurtec ODT as a Tier 3 brand or a Tier 4 specialty drug, nearly all health insurance plans enforce rigorous utilization management protocols before approving coverage. The primary administrative hurdle is Prior Authorization (PA). Insurers require your prescribing neurologist, headache specialist, or primary care provider to submit detailed clinical documentation proving that you have a confirmed diagnosis of episodic or chronic migraine, detailing the average number of monthly migraine headache days, and verifying that the medication is being used appropriately for acute relief, prevention, or both.

Step therapy—often termed fail first protocol—is almost universally required before an insurance plan will authorize coverage for CGRP inhibitors. Under step therapy guidelines, patients must demonstrate documented failure, clinical intolerance, or contraindication to older, less expensive generic medications. For acute migraine indications, insurers typically require trials of at least two different generic triptans (such as sumatriptan, rizatriptan, or eletriptan). For preventive indications, plans routinely require prior trials of at least two classes of traditional preventive drugs, such as beta-blockers (propranolol), antiepileptic medications (topiramate), or tricyclic antidepressants (amitriptyline). If a patient has cardiovascular contraindications that make triptans dangerous, the prescriber must clearly state this in the prior authorization appeal.

For patients with commercial health insurance, manufacturer financial assistance programs drastically reduce the burden of high copayments or specialty coinsurance. The manufacturer offers the Nurtec ODT Copay Card, which allows commercially insured patients with participating coverage to pay as little as $0 per month, subject to annual maximum benefit caps. However, federal healthcare regulations strictly prohibit patients enrolled in government-funded healthcare programs—including Medicare Part D, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, and TRICARE—from utilizing manufacturer copay cards under the federal Anti-Kickback Statute. Patients with Medicare must navigate standard Part D coverage phases, including the deductible and initial coverage stages. Specialty Pharmacy Dispensing vs Traditional Retail Pharmacies

Another practical aspect of Nurtec's classification involves where the medication can physically be filled. True specialty drugs are typically locked behind closed-distribution networks, meaning patients cannot walk into a neighborhood pharmacy to pick up their prescription; instead, an accredited specialty pharmacy—such as Accredo, CVS Caremark Specialty, AllianceRx Walgreens Prime, or Optum Specialty—must coordinate cold-chain delivery via courier to the patient's home.

Because Nurtec ODT is a stable oral tablet, it is broadly available at standard retail pharmacies, including independent community pharmacies, grocery store pharmacies, and national chains like CVS, Walgreens, and Walmart. In the vast majority of commercial insurance plans, patients can fill their Nurtec prescription directly at their local retail counter. Only in cases where an employer or health plan operates a strict mandatory specialty carve-out policy will patients be forced to transition their Nurtec prescription from a local retail pharmacy to a mail-order specialty pharmacy provider.

How to Get Insurance Coverage and Approval for Nurtec ODT

Follow this practical step-by-step process to secure prior authorization and minimize out-of-pocket costs for Nurtec ODT.

  1. Verify Formulary Tier and Pharmacy Requirements with Your Insurance PBM

    Call the pharmacy member services number on your insurance card or log into your pharmacy portal to verify whether Nurtec is classified as a brand or specialty tier drug.

  2. Document Prior Migraine Treatments and Step Therapy Failures

    Provide your doctor with a comprehensive record of previously tried migraine medications, including generic triptans, detailing lack of efficacy or intolerable side effects.

  3. Have Your Prescriber Submit a Comprehensive Prior Authorization Request

    Ensure your healthcare provider submits an electronic prior authorization including your official migraine diagnosis, monthly headache frequency, and treatment history.

  4. Enroll in the Manufacturer Copay Savings Card Program

    Visit the official Nurtec website and download the copay card to your smartphone, which can reduce your out-of-pocket cost to as low as $0 if you have commercial insurance.

  5. Appeal Denials Promptly with Supporting Clinical Documentation

    If your insurer denies initial coverage, work with your prescriber to file an appeal with a detailed letter of medical necessity citing clinical trial data.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is Nurtec ODT officially considered a specialty drug by insurance companies?

Not universally. Many commercial insurance plans categorize Nurtec ODT as a Tier 3 non-preferred brand-name drug rather than a specialty drug because it is an oral tablet that does not require refrigeration or injection. However, some plans place it on a specialty tier due to its high cost.

Q2: Can I pick up Nurtec at my local retail pharmacy like Walgreens or CVS?

Yes. Unlike injectable biologic medications that must come from mail-order specialty pharmacies, Nurtec ODT is routinely stocked and dispensed by traditional retail pharmacies, grocery pharmacies, and independent drugstores.

Q3: Why does my insurance require prior authorization for Nurtec ODT?

Insurers require prior authorization because Nurtec is a high-cost brand-name medication. Prior authorization ensures that the patient meets FDA-approved criteria for migraine treatment and has attempted lower-cost generic alternatives first.

Q4: What generic drugs do insurers require you to try before approving Nurtec?

Insurers typically mandate step therapy trials of generic triptans (such as sumatriptan, rizatriptan, or eletriptan) for acute migraine, or traditional preventive drugs (such as topiramate, propranolol, or amitriptyline) for migraine prevention.

Q5: How can I get Nurtec for $0 per month?

Patients with commercial health insurance can apply for the manufacturer's Nurtec ODT Copay Savings Card online, which can reduce out-of-pocket prescription expenses to $0 per month up to an annual maximum benefit cap.

Q6: Can Medicare Part D patients use the Nurtec copay savings card?

No. Federal law, specifically the Anti-Kickback Statute, prohibits patients enrolled in Medicare, Medicaid, or any federal healthcare program from using manufacturer copay savings cards. Medicare patients must pay standard formulary copays or coinsurance.

Q7: Is Nurtec ODT used for treating active migraines or preventing them?

Nurtec ODT is unique because it is FDA-approved for both indications: taken as needed (up to 75 mg once daily) for acute migraine relief, or taken every other day for the preventive treatment of episodic migraine.

Q8: What is the difference between Nurtec ODT and injectable CGRP medications like Aimovig?

Injectable CGRP drugs like Aimovig, Emgality, and Ajovy are large-molecule monoclonal antibodies administered via monthly subcutaneous injections for prevention only. Nurtec ODT is a small-molecule oral disintegrating tablet used for both acute treatment and prevention.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Whether Nurtec ODT is classified as a specialty drug or a non-preferred brand-name medication depends on your individual health insurance policy and pharmacy benefit manager. While its small-molecule formulation, room-temperature oral dosing, and broad retail pharmacy availability align with traditional brand medications, its high retail price causes some restrictive insurers to place it on a specialty tier. By working closely with your prescribing physician to satisfy prior authorization and step therapy requirements, and by taking advantage of manufacturer copay savings cards, patients can successfully access this innovative migraine therapy at minimal out-of-pocket cost.
