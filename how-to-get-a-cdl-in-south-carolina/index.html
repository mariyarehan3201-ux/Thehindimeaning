How to Get a CDL in South Carolina?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Obtaining a Commercial Driver License (CDL) in South Carolina opens up lucrative, high-demand career pathways in regional freight hauling, interstate logistics, municipal transit, and heavy construction. Regulated by the South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles (SCDMV) and the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA), earning a commercial credential requires passing stringent medical physicals, written knowledge examinations, mandatory Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT), and a rigorous three-part practical skills road examination. Whether your ambition is operating an eighteen-wheel Class A tractor-trailer across the country, driving a Class B heavy dump truck, or operating a municipal passenger bus, understanding SCDMV administrative procedures and training requirements ensures you achieve your commercial driving license efficiently.

SCDMV Commercial License Classes, Qualifications, and Endorsements

Before beginning your commercial training in South Carolina, you must select the appropriate commercial license classification based on the gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) of the equipment you intend to operate. The SCDMV issues three primary commercial license classes: Class A, Class B, and Class C. A Class A CDL authorizes drivers to operate any combination of commercial vehicles with a gross combination weight rating (GCWR) of 26,001 pounds or more, provided the towed vehicle or trailer exceeds 10,000 pounds GVWR.

A Class B CDL permits the operation of any single heavy straight vehicle with a GVWR of 26,001 pounds or more, or towing a trailer weighing under 10,000 pounds (such as cement mixers, dump trucks, and large box trucks). A Class C CDL covers commercial vehicles designed to transport sixteen or more passengers (including the driver) or placarded hazardous materials that do not meet Class A or B weight specifications. Additionally, drivers can earn specialized endorsements—such as Tanker (N), Doubles/Triples (T), Passenger (P), School Bus (S), and Hazardous Materials (H)—which require supplementary written testing and federal TSA background fingerprint checks.

Federal ELDT Requirements, Medical Physicals, and the CLP

Under federal regulations established by the FMCSA, all first-time commercial driver applicants in South Carolina must complete certified Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT) before taking the state skills road exam. Your training provider must be officially listed on the federal Training Provider Registry (TPR). ELDT comprises both classroom theory instruction (covering federal safety rules, trip planning, and vehicle systems) and behind-the-wheel (BTW) range and public road practice. The SCDMV will electronically verify your ELDT graduation record on the federal TPR database before allowing you to book an official road exam appointment.

To begin training, you must obtain a South Carolina Commercial Learner Permit (CLP). First, visit a certified medical examiner listed on the National Registry of Certified Medical Examiners (NRCME) to complete a Department of Transportation (DOT) physical. The examiner issues a Medical Examiner Certificate (Form MCSA-5876), which must be self-certified with the SCDMV. Once cleared, visit an SCDMV branch with your valid South Carolina driver license, proof of citizenship (birth certificate or U.S. passport), Social Security card, and two documents proving South Carolina residency. You must pass a standard vision screening and computerized knowledge exams to receive your CLP, which you must hold for at least fourteen full days before taking the road test.

Mastering the Three-Part SCDMV Practical Skills Examination

After completing your fourteen-day CLP holding period and finishing ELDT training, you will schedule the practical skills road exam at an SCDMV commercial testing site. The exam must be taken in a commercial vehicle representative of the specific CDL class and braking systems you wish to operate. The practical exam is split into three rigorous phases: the Pre-Trip Vehicle Inspection, Basic Vehicle Control Skills, and the On-Road Driving Test.

Phase one is the Pre-Trip Inspection, where you must conduct an exhaustive verbal and physical inspection of the entire vehicle, demonstrating to the examiner that components are properly mounted, secure, not cracked, and free of leaks. This includes the mandatory air brake test (applied pressure leak check, low-air warning alarm, and emergency spring brake pop-out); failing any step of the air brake sequence results in an immediate automatic test failure. Phase two tests basic backing maneuvers on a closed course, including straight-line backing, offset backing, and alley docking. Phase three tests safe on-road driving across South Carolina public roads, evaluating highway merging, lane tracking, bridge clearance awareness, railroad crossing procedures, and defensive driving. Passing all three segments grants you your South Carolina CDL.

How to Obtain a Commercial Drivers License in South Carolina

A step-by-step administrative and practical guide to fulfilling all SCDMV requirements to secure a South Carolina Commercial Driver License.

  1. Complete a DOT Medical Exam with a Certified Examiner

    Schedule a physical examination with an NRCME-certified doctor to receive your DOT Medical Examiner Certificate (MCSA-5876), and submit your medical self-certification to the SCDMV.

  2. Study the SCDMV Commercial Driver License Manual

    Download the official South Carolina CDL Manual from SCDMVonline.com. Master the chapters covering General Knowledge, Air Brakes, Combination Vehicles, and any specific endorsements you require.

  3. Pass Written Tests and Obtain Your Commercial Learner Permit

    Visit an SCDMV branch with proof of identity, residency, and your DOT medical card. Pass the computerized written knowledge exams and vision test to receive your Commercial Learner Permit (CLP).

  4. Complete Certified FMCSA Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT)

    Enroll in an FMCSA Training Provider Registry certified truck driving program or technical college to complete mandatory theory and behind-the-wheel training on range maneuvers and highway driving.

  5. Pass the Three-Part SCDMV CDL Skills Road Examination

    After holding your CLP for at least fourteen days, take your road exam at an SCDMV CDL site, successfully executing the pre-trip inspection, backing skills maneuvers, and road test to receive your CDL.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How old do you have to be to get a CDL in South Carolina?

You must be at least 18 years old to obtain an intrastate CDL (driving only within South Carolina borders) and at least 21 years old to obtain an interstate CDL (driving commercial trucks across state lines).

Q2: What is ELDT and is it mandatory in South Carolina?

Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT) is a federally mandated training curriculum required by the FMCSA for all new CDL applicants, requiring certified classroom and behind-the-wheel instruction before taking the road test.

Q3: How long must you hold a South Carolina CLP before taking the road test?

You must hold your Commercial Learner Permit (CLP) for a mandatory minimum of fourteen consecutive days before you are legally permitted to take the SCDMV commercial skills road examination.

Q4: What happens if you fail the air brake test during the pre-trip exam?

The air brake check is a critical safety system evaluation. Missing any individual step, misstating pressure thresholds, or failing to identify leaks results in an immediate automatic failure of the skills test.

Q5: Can you get a South Carolina CDL with a manual transmission restriction removed?

If you complete your SCDMV road skills examination in a commercial vehicle equipped with an automatic transmission, your CDL will carry an "E" restriction prohibiting you from operating manual transmission trucks.

Q6: How much does CDL school cost in South Carolina?

Private commercial truck driving schools in South Carolina generally charge between $3,500 and $6,500, while state technical colleges often offer workforce grant scholarships that reduce out-of-pocket tuition costs.

Q7: What documents are required to apply for a South Carolina CLP?

You must present your current South Carolina driver license, certified birth certificate or valid U.S. passport, Social Security card, DOT Medical Examiner Certificate, and two proofs of physical SC residency.

Q8: How long is a South Carolina CDL valid?

A standard South Carolina Commercial Driver License is valid for eight years, although the underlying DOT medical card must be renewed every twenty-four months (or sooner if specified by the medical examiner).

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Earning a Commercial Driver License in South Carolina is a rigorous but rewarding professional achievement that provides high-earning stability in the transportation industry. By preparing thoroughly for written knowledge tests, completing certified FMCSA ELDT behind-the-wheel instruction, and mastering the three-part practical skills evaluation, you position yourself for commercial driving success. Commit to learning pre-trip inspection routines thoroughly, maintain a clean driving record, and prioritize highway safety as you embark on your commercial driving career in the Palmetto State.
