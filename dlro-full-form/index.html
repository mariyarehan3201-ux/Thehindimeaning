DLRO Full Form: Land & Land Reforms Officer

Full Form
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 17, 2026

In land administration, revenue governance, agrarian reforms, and civil property registry in West Bengal, India, the full form of DLRO is District Land and Land Reforms Officer. Operating under the Land and Land Reforms and Refugee Relief and Rehabilitation Department of the Government of West Bengal, the DLRO is the senior district-level administrative authority responsible for implementing land ceiling laws, executing agrarian tenancy reforms, maintaining cadastral land records (Record of Rights / Khatian), processing land character conversion petitions, and resolving property ownership disputes. Serving as the administrative anchor of the district revenue machinery, the DLRO safeguards public land, oversees sand and mineral quarries, and coordinates land acquisition for state infrastructure.

The Historical Genesis of Agrarian Land Reforms in West Bengal

Land governance in West Bengal holds a distinctive place in India’s socioeconomic history. In the decades following independence, rural society was dominated by feudal intermediary landholders (zamindars and jotedars) while millions of impoverished sharecroppers (bargadars) toiled without legal security of tenure, vulnerable to arbitrary evictions. The landmark passage of the West Bengal Land Reforms Act, 1955, followed by the historic 'Operation Barga' in the late 1970s, transformed agrarian economics by legally recording sharecroppers and redistributing millions of acres of ceiling-surplus agricultural land to landless farmers.

To implement this massive agrarian restructuring and administer millions of cadastral land parcels, the state established a specialized, decentralized revenue cadre led at the district level by the District Land and Land Reforms Officer (DLRO). Unlike general district collectors who manage broad public administration, the DLRO functions as a dedicated statutory authority focused on land rights, cadastral surveying, land tenure protection, and sustainable natural resource management.

Administrative Hierarchy of the Land and Land Reforms Department

The administrative machinery overseen by the DLRO operates across a rigorous, multi-tiered bureaucratic pyramid reaching from the state secretariat down to village gram panchayats. This structured hierarchy ensures that land record maintenance, mutation petitions, and boundary disputes are resolved close to citizen doorsteps. The administrative structure table below outlines the governing tiers of the West Bengal land revenue department.

Administrative Hierarchy Tier Designated Officer in Charge Jurisdiction & Operational Scope Key Statutory Powers & Responsibilities
District Level Authority District Land & Land Reforms Officer (DLRO) Entire Revenue District (Acts as Additional District Magistrate) Overall policy execution, appellate authority, land conversion & sand mining
Sub-Divisional Level Sub-Divisional Land & Land Reforms Officer (SDL&LRO) Sub-Division comprising multiple blocks Supervisory inspections, departmental inquiries & intermediate appeals
Block Level Field Office Block Land & Land Reforms Officer (BL&LRO) Individual administrative Community Block Primary mutation of land, spot field inquiries & original record maintenance
Gram Panchayat / Mouza Tier Revenue Inspector (RI) / Amin Surveyor Group of village Mouzas (Revenue Inspector Office) Physical site boundary demarcation, rent collection (khajna) & ground audits

Core Statutory Powers and Daily Administrative Functions

The DLRO exercises extensive quasi-judicial and executive powers under various state and central statutes. A primary function is overseeing Land Mutation—the formal updating of the Record of Rights (RoR / Khatian) following property transactions, inheritances, or court partition decrees. By ensuring that every legal transfer is accurately reflected in the digital Banglarbhumi database, the DLRO protects property owners against fraudulent duplicate sales and boundary encroachments.

Another critical statutory duty is Land Character Conversion under Section 4C of the West Bengal Land Reforms Act. In a developing economy, agricultural land parcels (classified as Sali or Danga) must frequently be repurposed for residential housing (Bastoo), commercial establishments, or industrial manufacturing units. The DLRO reviews environmental clearances, verified site inspections, and master plan zoning rules before granting formal legal conversion certificates, ensuring balanced agrarian conservation alongside economic growth.

Common Land Classifications Evaluated by the DLRO

In West Bengal land administration, every parcel of land is designated with a specific character classification in the government Record of Rights. The land character table below details common land classifications evaluated during conversion and mutation proceedings before the DLRO.

Bengali Land Character Term English Classification Meaning Statutory Restrictions & Rules Conversion Feasibility
Sali (শালি) Low-lying, highly fertile single/double-crop paddy land Strict conservation to safeguard state food security Requires rigorous scrutiny and higher conversion fees
Danga (ডাঙা) High agricultural land, dry upland or orchard parcel Fewer agricultural restrictions than low-lying Sali plots Readily convertible for residential and industrial development
Bastoo (বাস্তু) Homestead land, residential building plot Designated for human habitation and domestic construction No conversion needed for residential house construction
Karkhana (কারখানা) Industrial commercial manufacturing factory land Requires statutory pollution control board consent to operate Permitted in designated industrial zones and growth corridors
Pukur / Jalashay (পুকুর) Pond, natural water body or wetland parcel Absolute statutory prohibition on filling under Fisheries Act Strictly non-convertible; filling attracts criminal prosecution

Digital Modernization and the Banglarbhumi Platform

In recent years, the office of the DLRO has undergone comprehensive digital modernization. The Government of West Bengal pioneered the Banglarbhumi portal, completely digitizing hundreds of millions of cadastral land records, village mouza maps (cadastral sheets), and tenancy khatians. Citizens no longer need to navigate manual paper record rooms to inspect property ownership or pay annual land revenue taxes (khajna).

Through the integration of digital signatures, online GRIPS fee payments, and automated SMS alerts, mutation and conversion petitions submitted to the DLRO are tracked with transparent statutory turnaround times. Geographic Information System (GIS) mapping and drone-assisted land surveys are further reducing boundary disputes, establishing an open, transparent, and citizen-friendly land administration framework.

How Citizens Apply for Land Character Conversion Through the DLRO

  1. Access the Official Banglarbhumi Land Records Portal

    Log into the official West Bengal Banglarbhumi web portal using citizen credentials to initiate an online land character conversion application.

  2. Enter Land Identification Details and Existing Classification

    Input the specific district, block, mouza, JL number, khatian number, and plot (dag) numbers, selecting the current classification (e.g., Sali/Danga) and proposed use (Bastoo/Commercial).

  3. Upload Mandatory Ownership Deeds and Cadastral Site Maps

    Attach certified copies of the registered purchase deed, updated mutation certificate, land tax receipts (khajna), approved building site plan, and environmental clearance (if applicable).

  4. Pay Government Processing and Conversion Fees

    Calculate and deposit statutory land conversion fees online via the GRIPS payment gateway based on the land's market valuation.

  5. Participate in Field Verification and Receive Conversion Order

    Facilitate physical site inspection by the Revenue Inspector (RI) and Block Land and Land Reforms Officer (BL&LRO) before receiving the final conversion certificate signed by the DLRO.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the primary full form of DLRO in administrative governance?

DLRO stands for District Land and Land Reforms Officer, the head of land revenue and agrarian administration in a district of West Bengal.

Q2: Which government department does the DLRO function under?

The DLRO operates under the Land and Land Reforms and Refugee Relief and Rehabilitation Department, Government of West Bengal.

Q3: What is the administrative hierarchy between DLRO, SDL&LRO, and BL&LRO?

The DLRO oversees the entire district; the Sub-Divisional Officer (SDL&LRO) manages sub-divisions; and the Block Land Officer (BL&LRO) administers blocks.

Q4: What role does the DLRO play in land character conversion?

Under Section 4C of the West Bengal Land Reforms Act, the DLRO holds statutory authority to approve or reject petitions converting agricultural land to homestead or commercial use.

Q5: How does the DLRO enforce the West Bengal Land Reforms Act ceiling limits?

The DLRO audits landholdings to ensure individuals and corporate entities do not exceed statutory ceiling limits, vesting surplus land in the State for redistribution.

Q6: Can appeals against a BL&LRO order be filed before the DLRO?

Yes, under Section 54 of the West Bengal Land Reforms Act, the DLRO functions as an appellate authority hearing appeals against orders passed by lower revenue officers.

Q7: What is the DLRO's role regarding minor minerals and river sand mining?

The DLRO oversees the licensing, royalty collection, and anti-illegal mining enforcement for minor minerals, sand beds, and brickfield earth within the district.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The District Land and Land Reforms Officer (DLRO) is an indispensable administrative institution safeguarding land rights, agricultural heritage, and revenue governance in West Bengal. By balancing the preservation of fertile agrarian soils with the dynamic land requirements of modern infrastructure and urbanization, the DLRO ensures equitable socioeconomic development. As digital platforms like Banglarbhumi bring greater transparency to public records, the DLRO continues to uphold the rule of law and protect property rights across the state.
