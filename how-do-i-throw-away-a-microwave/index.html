How Do I Throw Away a Microwave?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how do i throw away a microwave is an important household safety and environmental responsibility when modernizing kitchen appliances or handling a dead microwave oven. Microwaves cannot simply be tossed into standard curbside garbage cans in many municipalities because they are classified as electronic waste (e-waste) and contain high-voltage internal components. Knowing local waste regulations, electronic recycling depots, scrap metal opportunities, and high-voltage capacitor safety protocols guarantees responsible disposal.

Municipal Regulations: E-Waste Bans and Curbside Bulk Pickup Rules

Across dozens of states and hundreds of municipalities, disposing of consumer electronics in standard municipal solid waste landfills is illegal under state Electronic Waste Recycling acts. Microwaves occupy a unique middle ground: while classified mechanically as small household appliances (white goods), their complex electronic control boards, digital display circuitry, and heavy electromagnetic transformers qualify them as electronic waste.

Before wheeling an old microwave to your street curb, check your local sanitation department's guidelines. Some municipal haulers offer scheduled 'Bulky Appliance Pickup' days where residents can place metal appliances curbside for dedicated scrap collection. Other cities require residents to transport e-waste directly to municipal civic drop-off convenience centers or designated household hazardous waste (HHW) recycling events.

Internal Hazards: High-Voltage Capacitors and Beryllium Oxide

While an unplugged microwave poses zero external hazard to homeowners, attempting to take the outer metal casing apart for hobby tinkering or scrap extraction carries serious risks. Inside every microwave oven is a high-voltage transformer, a magnetron tube, and a massive high-voltage oil-filled capacitor. This capacitor can retain a lethal electrical charge (2,000 to 4,000 volts DC) for days or weeks even after being completely unplugged from electrical wall outlets.

Furthermore, inside the magnetron tube that produces microwave radiation, ceramic insulators often contain Beryllium Oxide (BeO). While completely safe and inert in its solid ceramic state, if someone attempts to smash or grind the magnetron for scrap, inhaling toxic beryllium ceramic dust can cause severe, irreversible chronic lung disease (berylliosis). Therefore, consumer microwaves should always be recycled intact without opening the internal electrical chassis.

Retailer Take-Backs, Charity Donations, and Scrap Metal Recycling

If your microwave is still fully functional, clean, and in good cosmetic condition, disposing of it in the trash is wasteful. Charitable organizations such as Goodwill, the Salvation Army, Habitat for Humanity ReStores, and local homeless shelters eagerly accept working microwave ovens to distribute to low-income families or transitional housing programs.

If the microwave is completely dead, major national electronics retailers such as Best Buy offer convenient consumer electronics recycling programs. Best Buy allows customers to bring up to three small household appliances or electronics into stores daily for free certified recycling. Alternatively, professional scrap metal recyclers accept whole microwaves, weighing them on industrial vehicle scales and paying cash based on current mixed-metal shred commodity rates.

How to Safely Throw Away or Recycle an Old Microwave

Follow these five simple steps to responsibly dispose of a working or broken microwave oven.

  1. Test Operational Status

    Heat a mug of water for 30 seconds; if it heats properly, donate the microwave to a local charity shelter.

  2. Unplug and Secure the Power Cable

    Disconnect the power cord from the wall and wrap it securely around the unit using electrical tape.

  3. Remove the Glass Turntable and Roller Ring

    Take out the interior glass carousel and roller ring to prevent shattering during transportation.

  4. Keep the Metal Housing Sealed Intact

    Never unscrew or pry open the back electrical cabinet to avoid lethal high-voltage capacitor shocks.

  5. Deliver to an E-Waste Depot or Retailer

    Drop the microwave off at Best Buy, your city municipal convenience station, or scheduled bulky curbside day.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I put a microwave in the regular trash can?

In most areas no; microwaves are classified as e-waste or bulky white goods and must be recycled or set out for special bulk collection.

Q2: Can a microwave shock you if it is unplugged?

Yes, the internal high-voltage capacitor can store a lethal charge (2,000+ volts) for days or weeks after being unplugged if opened.

Q3: Does Best Buy take old microwaves for recycling?

Yes, Best Buy accepts small countertop microwaves under their store recycling program, though fees may apply for commercial units.

Q4: Is there gold or precious metals inside a microwave?

Very tiny trace amounts exist in the printed circuit board, but not enough to justify dismantling; the primary scrap metal value is steel and copper.

Q5: Can the glass plate inside a microwave be recycled in curbside bins?

No, microwave turntables are made of heat-resistant borosilicate glass which cannot be recycled with standard food container glass jars.

Q6: What should I do with a built-in over-the-range microwave?

Carefully unscrew the mounting bracket bolts, support the weight with a helper, and take it to an appliance recycling depot.

Q7: Do scrap metal yards pay for old microwaves?

Yes, scrap yards pay for microwaves based on light iron or shred steel pricing, typically paying $2 to $5 per unit.

Q8: Does a broken microwave emit dangerous radiation?

No, microwaves produce non-ionizing electromagnetic waves only when actively operating; an unplugged microwave emits zero radiation.
