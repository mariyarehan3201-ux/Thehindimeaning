Asbestos Removal Denver

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Asbestos removal Denver represents a critical environmental remediation requirement for property owners, general contractors, and facility managers across the Mile High City. Due to Denver rich architectural history—spanning Victorian brick homes in Highlands, mid-century bungalows in Wash Park, and expansive commercial developments across the Front Range—legacy building materials containing asbestos remain widespread. Executing safe and lawful abatement requires strict compliance with Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE) Regulation No. 8, City and County of Denver building codes, and federal EPA guidelines.

Colorado Regulatory Framework: CDPHE Regulation 8 and Permitting

In Colorado, asbestos abatement activities are governed by the Colorado Air Quality Control Commission Regulation No. 8, Part B, which is enforced directly by the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE). Regulation 8 establishes strict operational requirements for inspections, contractor licensing, project notifications, and containment protocols. Unlike federal NESHAP standards that exempt single-family residences, Colorado state law mandates that any residential renovation or demolition disturbing more than trigger levels (50 linear feet on pipes, 32 square feet on other surfaces, or the volume equivalent of a 55-gallon drum) must undergo professional certified abatement.

Before initiating abatement work in Denver, the licensed General Abatement Contractor (GAC) must submit a formal project notification and permit application to CDPHE at least ten working days prior to disturbing asbestos-containing materials. Furthermore, the City and County of Denver Community Planning and Development department requires an approved CDPHE asbestos permit or certified inspection sign-off before issuing structural alteration or demolition permits, preventing unpermitted releases into neighborhood airsheds.

Common Asbestos Materials in Denver and Abatement Methodologies

Denver architectural stock features numerous building components formulated with chrysotile, amosite, and crocidolite asbestos between 1880 and the late 1980s. In older residential properties, asbestos is frequently discovered in hot water gravity furnace duct wrap, steam pipe magnesia insulation, vermiculite attic insulation (frequently sourced from the Libby, Montana mine), exterior transite siding, and multi-layered linoleum flooring. In commercial properties, spray-applied structural fireproofing, acoustic ceiling plaster, and vinyl composition tiles represent the most common remediation targets.

Professional asbestos removal requires rigorous engineering controls designed to prevent cross-contamination. Licensed contractors erect critical barriers using two layers of 6-mil polyethylene sheeting, sealing all HVAC vents, windows, and doors. The containment area is placed under negative air pressure utilizing HEPA-filtered negative air machines exhausting outdoors. Remediation technicians wearing full-face Powered Air-Purifying Respirators (PAPRs) and disposable Tyvek suits apply amended water solutions to thoroughly wet materials before carefully stripping and packaging them into labeled 6-mil waste disposal bags.

Clearance Testing, Landfill Disposal, and Selecting Denver Contractors

Following the physical removal of asbestos materials and thorough HEPA vacuuming and wet wiping of all interior containment surfaces, an independent third-party Air Monitoring Specialist (AMS) certified by CDPHE conducts mandatory visual and air clearance inspections. Colorado law strictly prohibits the abatement contractor from performing their own clearance testing, ensuring total analytical objectivity. The AMS conducts aggressive air sampling using Phase Contrast Microscopy (PCM) for residential projects or Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM) for schools and public facilities to verify fiber counts fall below 0.01 fibers per cubic centimeter.

Once air clearance standards are achieved, containment barriers are dismantled, and the building is certified safe for re-occupancy. All packaged asbestos waste must be transported under an official CDPHE waste manifest to a licensed Colorado landfill permitted to accept regulated asbestos waste, such as the Denver Arapahoe Disposal Site (DADS). Property owners receive a comprehensive project closeout binder containing the state permit, contractor daily logs, waste manifest receipts, and certified laboratory clearance certificates.

How to Commission Asbestos Removal in Denver

Follow these five professional steps to safely and legally commission asbestos removal in Denver under Colorado state regulations.

  1. Hire a Certified Asbestos Building Inspector

    Engage an independent CDPHE-certified asbestos inspector to survey the property and extract bulk samples for NVLAP laboratory analysis.

  2. Obtain Bids from Licensed General Abatement Contractors

    Solicit itemized proposals from state-licensed General Abatement Contractors (GAC) possessing current CDPHE credentials.

  3. Submit Mandatory 10-Day CDPHE State Notification

    Ensure the selected contractor files the formal project notification with the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment.

  4. Conduct Negative-Pressure Containment Abatement

    Verify the contractor establishes full containment with HEPA filtration, wet removal methods, and approved personal protective gear.

  5. Obtain Independent Third-Party Air Clearance

    Hire an independent Air Monitoring Specialist to perform aggressive air clearance testing and secure your official re-occupancy certificate.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is asbestos testing mandatory before renovating a home in Denver?

Yes, under CDPHE Regulation 8, property owners must have building materials inspected by a certified asbestos building inspector prior to any renovation disturbing trigger levels.

Q2: Can a homeowner remove asbestos themselves in Denver?

While Colorado law permits homeowners to remove asbestos from their own primary single-family residence, self-removal is strongly discouraged due to severe health hazards, and disposal still requires strict CDPHE waste manifesting.

Q3: What are the trigger levels for asbestos permits in Colorado?

Trigger levels requiring a state permit are 50 linear feet of pipe insulation, 32 square feet of surface materials, or the volume equivalent of a 55-gallon drum.

Q4: How much does asbestos removal cost in Denver?

Costs vary widely based on scope: removing an asbestos-wrapped heating duct typically costs $1,500 to $3,500, while full popcorn ceiling or floor tile removal can range from $2,500 to $8,000 or more.

Q5: Can the abatement company perform its own air clearance testing in Colorado?

No, Colorado state law strictly mandates that air clearance testing must be performed by an independent, certified third-party Air Monitoring Specialist to prevent conflicts of interest.

Q6: Where is asbestos waste disposed of in the Denver area?

Asbestos waste must be transported under manifest to a state-permitted hazardous waste landfill, such as the Denver Arapahoe Disposal Site (DADS) in Arapahoe County.

Q7: How long is the waiting period for an asbestos permit in Colorado?

CDPHE requires a mandatory ten-working-day notification and review period before any regulated asbestos removal project can begin.

Q8: Does homeowners insurance pay for asbestos removal in Denver?

Standard homeowners insurance policies exclude asbestos removal unless the material was directly damaged by a covered peril, such as a burst water pipe or fire.
