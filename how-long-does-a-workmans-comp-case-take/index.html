How Long Does a Workmans Comp Case Take?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how long does a workmans comp case take is a vital practical question for employees recovering from job-related injuries, repetitive strain trauma, or occupational illnesses. From the moment an injury occurs on the job site to the final administrative closure, a workman's comp case moves through several structured statutory stages. On average, a standard undisputed claim takes between 6 and 12 months, while contested cases involving denied liability, surgical interventions, or administrative hearings take 12 to 24+ months.

Initial Reporting and Temporary Disability Benefits (Day 1 to Month 3)

The initial phase of a workman's comp claim moves with statutory urgency. State workers' compensation acts mandate that an injured employee must notify their employer in writing within a strict statutory window—typically 30 days from the date of injury. The employer then provides an official claim form (such as Form DWC-1 in California) and reports the incident to their workers' compensation insurance carrier.

The insurance company standardly has 14 to 90 days (varying by state) to accept or deny the claim. During this investigative window, if the worker is unable to perform their job duties, the insurer begins issuing Temporary Total Disability (TTD) wage replacement benefits, typically paying two-thirds (66.6%) of the employee's average gross weekly wage. Concurrently, the worker begins authorized medical treatment through the employer's designated occupational clinic.

The Active Treatment Phase: Surgery and Physical Rehabilitation

The bulk of a workman's comp case timeline is consumed by medical healing. In serious claims involving torn ligaments, crushed vertebrae, or nerve damage, medical care involves diagnostic MRI imaging, physical therapy regimens, cortisone injections, and orthopedic surgeries.

Workers' compensation insurance companies closely monitor medical utilization. Every requested procedure, prescription, and therapy visit must be authorized through a statutory 'Utilization Review' (UR) process. If the insurer's utilization review doctor denies a recommended surgery, your attorney must file an appeal through an Independent Medical Review (IMR), adding 30 to 60 days to the medical treatment schedule. Healing cannot be rushed, as premature case settlement forfeits medical benefits.

Contested Claims and Administrative Law Hearings

When an insurance carrier denies a claim—arguing that the injury was pre-existing, caused by horseplay, or occurred outside the scope of employment—the case enters the formal administrative court system. Your attorney must file an Application for Adjudication of Claim with the state workers' compensation appeals board.

The court process involves mandatory settlement conferences, witness depositions, and eventual trial before a state Workers' Compensation Administrative Law Judge (ALJ). Because administrative courts manage heavy dockets, scheduling a contested trial date standardly takes 6 to 12 months from the initial filing. Following the trial, the judge takes the matter under submission, issuing a written 'Findings and Award' within 30 to 90 days.

How to Manage Your Workman's Comp Case Timeline

Follow these five practical steps to keep your workman's comp claim moving forward smoothly.

  1. Report the Work Injury in Writing Immediately

    Notify your employer on the day of the incident to prevent insurance adjusters from questioning the injury's validity.

  2. Attend Every Medical and Physical Therapy Appointment

    Consistent medical attendance proves injury severity and ensures your temporary disability checks continue uninterrupted.

  3. Obtain Work Restriction Slips from Your Doctor

    Deliver updated doctor notes to your employer and claims adjuster immediately after every medical evaluation.

  4. Keep Detailed Personal Logs of All Lost Wages

    Maintain records of missing overtime, partial paychecks, and mileage expenses to and from medical appointments.

  5. Consult a Specialized Workman's Comp Attorney Early

    Retain counsel if the insurance company delays treatment approvals, reduces wage checks, or issues a denial.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How long can a workman's comp case remain open?

A case can remain open for years if medical care is ongoing; in some states, cases stay open for life under open medical awards.

Q2: How long does it take for workman's comp to start paying you?

Temporary disability checks typically begin within 14 to 21 days after your doctor certifies you cannot work due to the injury.

Q3: Can you be fired while on workman's comp?

Employers cannot legally fire you in retaliation for filing a claim, though they can fill your position if you have permanent restrictions and cannot do the job.

Q4: What happens if workman's comp denies my claim?

Your attorney files an appeal with the state workers' compensation board, scheduling an administrative hearing before a judge.

Q5: How much does workman's comp pay for lost wages?

Temporary Total Disability (TTD) typically pays two-thirds (66.6%) of your pre-injury gross average weekly wage, subject to state maximums.

Q6: What is an Independent Medical Review (IMR)?

A state-administered process where independent doctors review and overturn insurance treatment denials.

Q7: Can I choose my own doctor for workman's comp?

In most states, you must treat within the employer's authorized Medical Provider Network (MPN) for the first 30 to 90 days.

Q8: How long do I have to file a workman's comp claim?

Statutes of limitations typically require filing formal claims within 1 to 2 years from the date of injury or manifestation of illness.
