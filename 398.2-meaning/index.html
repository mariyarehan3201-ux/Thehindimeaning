When exploring library stacks or researching world literature, understanding 398.2 meaning reveals the definitive classification code for humanity's shared folklore, fairy tales, and fables. In the Dewey Decimal Classification (DDC) system, call number 398.2 catalogs folk literature across global cultures.

Many library patrons are surprised to discover that beloved tales like Cinderella, Jack and the Beanstalk, and Robin Hood are not shelved in the general fiction department. Instead, library classification founder Melvil Dewey categorized folklore under the broader umbrella of Social Sciences, recognizing that traditional folk narratives represent ethnographic records of human customs, moral values, and communal anthropology.

The Hierarchical Classification of 398.2

The Dewey Decimal system organizes human knowledge into ten primary classes, which progressively divide into hundreds, tens, and decimal fractions. Call number 398.2 resides inside the 300s, which encompasses sociology, politics, education, and cultural customs. By tracing the hierarchical tree, researchers can see how specific fairy tales connect to broader social anthropology.

Within 398.2, catalogers apply further decimal extensions to differentiate specific narrative themes. For example, animal tales like Aesop's Fables are assigned 398.24, heroic legends receive 398.22, and supernatural folk ghost tales are indexed under 398.25, ensuring orderly shelf organization.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does call number 398.2 represent in a library?

In the Dewey Decimal Classification system, 398.2 specifically designates Folk Literature, including traditional fairy tales, folk narratives, legends, myths, and animal fables.

Q2: Why are fairy tales shelved in 398.2 rather than general fiction?

Melvil Dewey classified traditional folklore under Social Sciences (the 300s) as cultural customs and anthropology rather than modern copyright fiction, reflecting their oral storytelling roots.

Q3: What is the parent class for 398.2 in the Dewey system?

The parent category is Class 300 (Social Sciences), Subclass 390 (Customs, Etiquette, Folklore), and Division 398 (Folklore).

Q4: Are modern fictional fantasy novels kept in 398.2?

No. Modern authored fantasy fiction (such as Harry Potter or Lord of the Rings) is shelved in general fiction under author surnames, while 398.2 is reserved for traditional cultural folk tales.

Q5: How do libraries categorize different fairy tales within 398.2?

Libraries append cutter numbers and geographic subdivisions to 398.2 (e.g., 398.2094 for European tales, 398.24 for animal fables like Aesop).

Q6: What famous stories are cataloged under 398.2?

Cinderella, Little Red Riding Hood, Robin Hood legends, King Arthur narratives, Anansi trickster tales, and Aesop's Fables are all housed under 398.2.

Q7: How does 398.2 differ from 290 religious mythology?

Class 290 catalogs sacred religious scriptures and established theological mythologies, whereas 398.2 catalogs secular cultural folk narratives and moral tales.
