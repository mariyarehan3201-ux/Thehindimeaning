How to Obtain a Guard Card in Arizona?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Securing an official unarmed or armed security guard card in Arizona is your legal gateway into the state's booming corporate, residential, healthcare, and industrial protection industries. In the Grand Canyon State, all private security personnel are rigorously regulated and licensed by the Arizona Department of Public Safety (AZDPS) Licensing Unit under Arizona Revised Statutes (A.R.S. Title 32, Chapter 26). Operating as a security officer without a valid DPS-issued credential is a Class 1 misdemeanor that carries severe criminal penalties and bars individuals from future security careers.

The AZDPS Regulatory Framework and Eligibility Criteria

The Arizona Department of Public Safety (AZDPS) oversees the licensing, background vetting, and certification of all security guards and private investigators operating statewide. Arizona maintains two primary tiers of security licensure: the Unarmed Security Guard Card and the Armed Security Guard Card. Both credentials require passing an FBI criminal history background check, submitting biometric fingerprints, and demonstrating moral character free of disqualifying felony convictions or crimes of dishonesty.

To qualify for an Arizona unarmed guard card, applicants must be at least eighteen years of age, hold legal United States citizenship or permanent resident alien work authorization, and possess zero felony convictions within the past five years. Certain specific offenses—such as identity theft, domestic violence, weapon offenses, and controlled substance trafficking—result in permanent mandatory denial by the DPS Licensing Board. Aspiring officers must complete an approved 8-hour pre-licensing curriculum taught by a DPS-certified security instructor before submitting their documentation.

The 8-Hour Pre-Licensing Curriculum and Fingerprinting

The mandatory 8-hour unarmed training course must be administered by a certified security training instructor or an authorized security agency licensed by AZDPS. The state-approved curriculum provides foundational instruction in Arizona security statutes (A.R.S. Title 32), the legal limits of a private citizen's arrest, the legal use of force, trespassing laws, emergency incident response, crime scene preservation, conflict de-escalation, and written report documentation.

Following classroom instruction, candidates must complete fingerprinting for state and federal background vetting. AZDPS utilizes the Public Services Portal (PSP) for electronic application processing. Applicants can visit an authorized electronic Live Scan fingerprinting location (utilizing Fieldprint or local law enforcement agencies) to transmit their biometric prints directly to DPS, or complete a traditional blue-and-white FBI FD-258 paper fingerprint card. Submitting electronic Live Scan prints dramatically accelerates approval times from six to eight weeks down to as little as ten to fourteen business days.

Securing Employment and Operating Compliantly in Arizona

Once AZDPS approves your application, your official hard-plastic Arizona Security Guard Card is mailed directly to your address. Under Arizona law, you must carry your physical guard card on your person at all times while performing security duties and present it immediately upon request to any peace officer or DPS investigator. Your guard card is valid for two years from the date of issuance, requiring an eight-hour refresher training course and renewal filing prior to expiration.

The Arizona security market is robust, with continuous demand across metropolitan Phoenix, Tucson, Mesa, and Scottsdale. Major commercial employers include contract security firms (such as Allied Universal and Securitas), semiconductor fabrication plants, distribution logistics centers, luxury resort properties, and acute healthcare hospitals. Holding both an unarmed card and completing the sixteen-hour armed qualification opens lucrative executive protection, cash-in-transit, and critical infrastructure patrol opportunities with significantly higher hourly wages.

How to Apply for and Receive an Arizona Guard Card

A complete step-by-step procedure to complete training, pass background checks, and obtain an AZDPS unarmed security license.

  1. Verify Personal Eligibility Requirements

    Confirm you are at least 18 years old, possess legal US work authorization, and have zero disqualifying felony convictions on your record.

  2. Complete the 8-Hour State-Approved Training Course

    Enroll in and complete the mandatory 8-hour unarmed pre-assignment training curriculum taught by a certified AZDPS security instructor.

  3. Obtain Fingerprints via Live Scan or FD-258 Card

    Schedule an electronic Live Scan appointment through Fieldprint or visit your local police department to have an official FBI FD-258 fingerprint card rolled.

  4. Submit Application Online via AZDPS Portal

    Create an account on the AZDPS Public Services Portal (PSP), upload your training certificate and photo ID, and pay the $72 licensing and fingerprint fee.

  5. Receive Your Guard Card and Start Working

    Monitor your portal status during the 10-to-14-day background review; once approved, carry your physical hard-plastic card on shift at all times.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How long does it take to get an Arizona guard card?

Electronic applications submitted through the AZDPS Public Services Portal with Live Scan fingerprints typically process in 10 to 14 business days.

Q2: Can I work as a security guard while waiting for my Arizona guard card?

No. Arizona law strictly prohibits working as a security guard until your physical card has been approved and issued by the AZDPS Licensing Unit.

Q3: How long is an Arizona security guard card valid for?

An Arizona unarmed guard card is valid for two years from the date of approval, requiring an 8-hour refresher class and fee payment for renewal.

Q4: What will disqualify you from getting an Arizona guard card?

Any felony conviction within five years, pending criminal charges, crimes of moral turpitude, domestic violence, or serious drug offenses cause mandatory denial.

Q5: How much does it cost to get an unarmed guard card in Arizona?

The AZDPS state application and fingerprint processing fee is $72.00, while the mandatory 8-hour training course typically costs between $40.00 and $75.00.

Q6: What is required to upgrade to an armed security guard card in AZ?

Applicants must be at least 21 years old, complete an additional 16-hour armed firearms training curriculum, and qualify on a live-fire range with at least a 70% score.

Q7: Can a non-US citizen get an Arizona guard card?

Yes, permanent resident aliens (green card holders) and individuals with valid federal employment authorization documents (EAD) are eligible to apply.

Q8: Do you have to be sponsored by a security company to get an Arizona guard card?

No. Individuals can apply independently without agency sponsorship, allowing you to obtain your card first and apply to multiple security firms.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Obtaining an Arizona security guard card is a straightforward, highly accessible professional investment that opens doors to stable, rewarding careers in public safety and asset protection. By completing your certified 8-hour coursework, submitting pristine electronic fingerprints through the AZDPS portal, and maintaining an unblemished personal record, you establish yourself as a trusted, licensed security professional in the state of Arizona.
