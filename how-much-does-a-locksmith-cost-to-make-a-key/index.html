How Much Does a Locksmith Cost to Make a Key?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a locksmith cost to make a key requires distinguishing between basic residential house keys, high-security commercial keys, and sophisticated automotive smart key fobs. On average, a locksmith charges between $5 and $25 to copy a standard residential brass key at their shop, and $75 to $150 if mobile service to your home is required. Making an automotive key ranges from $80 to $180 for standard transponder chip keys, and $150 to $350+ for laser-cut smart key fobs.

Residential and Commercial Key Duplication vs. Originating Keys

The cost of making a residential key depends heavily on whether you possess an existing key to duplicate or if the locksmith must 'originate' a brand-new key from scratch. Walking into a locksmith shop to duplicate a standard Kwikset, Schlage, or Yale brass key costs between $3 and $8 per copy. Duplicating restricted, high-security patented keys (such as Medeco, Mul-T-Lock, or ASSA Abloy) costs $15 to $40 per key due to patented keyway blanks and card authorization requirements.

If you have lost all keys and need a locksmith to make a key to fit an existing door lock cylinder, costs increase significantly. Mobile locksmiths charge a service call fee of $50 to $100 just to dispatch to your location, plus $35 to $75 in labor to pick the lock cylinder, decode the internal pin tumbler depths with a Lishi tool or caliper, and cut a factory-spec working key.

Automotive Keys: Transponders, Laser-Cut Blades, and Smart Fobs

Modern automotive key replacement represents the most technologically demanding segment of locksmithing. Nearly all passenger vehicles built after 1998 incorporate an RFID transponder chip embedded within the plastic key head. When inserted into the ignition cylinder, the vehicle's immobilizer antenna interrogates the chip; if the encrypted digital handshake fails, the engine will crank but immediately shut off. Cutting and programming a basic transponder key costs $90 to $180.

Luxury and late-model push-button start vehicles utilize proximity smart keys and keyless entry fobs. These require internal high-security laser cutting (milling a wavy serpentine channel down the center of the emergency metal key blade) and connecting specialized computer diagnostic hardware to the vehicle's OBD2 diagnostic port to program the immobilizer ECU. Mobile locksmiths typically charge $175 to $350 for proximity fobs, saving vehicle owners 30% to 50% compared to dealership service departments.

Service Call Fees, After-Hours Emergency Surcharges, and Scams

When evaluating locksmith costs, understanding mobile dispatch fee structures is essential. Professional mobile locksmiths charge a standard trip service call fee of $50 to $90 during normal business hours to cover mobile vehicle fuel, commercial insurance, and travel time. If you experience an emergency lockout after hours, late at night, or on major holidays, emergency trip surcharges add $50 to $120 to the baseline dispatch fee.

Consumers must also be vigilant against locksmith dispatch call-center scams. Unscrupulous operators advertise unrealistically low rates online, such as '$15 to $29 service call.' Once the technician arrives on site, they falsely claim the lock cannot be picked or decoded, drill out the cylinder unnecessarily, and present an extortionate bill for $400 to $600. Always demand an all-inclusive binding quote over the phone before authorizing dispatch.

How to Get a Replacement Key from a Locksmith

Follow these five straightforward steps to obtain an accurate, fairly priced replacement key from a locksmith.

  1. Determine Your Exact Key and Lock Type

    Identify whether you need a residential brass key, a commercial security key, or an automotive push-to-start smart fob.

  2. Locate Your Vehicle Identification Number (VIN)

    If replacing a lost car key, write down your 17-digit VIN and provide proof of ownership (vehicle registration or title).

  3. Call Established Brick-and-Mortar Locksmiths

    Contact a local locksmith shop with a physical storefront or verifiable ALOA (Associated Locksmiths of America) certification.

  4. Demand a Firm All-Inclusive Quote Over the Phone

    Request the total price including service call fee, key blank hardware, mechanical cutting, and computer programming.

  5. Test the New Key in All Cylinders Before Paying

    Verify that the new key smoothly turns both door lock cylinders, ignition cylinders, and that the remote buttons operate properly.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a locksmith cost to make a car key without the original?

Making a new car key when all keys are lost typically costs between $150 and $350 depending on whether it requires transponder programming.

Q2: How much does it cost to duplicate a house key at a locksmith?

Duplicating a standard residential brass key at a locksmith shop costs between $3 and $8 per key.

Q3: Why are car keys with chips so expensive to replace?

They contain encrypted microchips that must be mechanically cut on precision laser machines and paired to the car's computer via specialized OBD2 scanners.

Q4: Can a locksmith make a key from a lock cylinder?

Yes, locksmiths can decode the internal pin tumbler wafer depths of a door or ignition lock cylinder using specialized decoders or impressioning.

Q5: Is a locksmith cheaper than the dealership for car keys?

Yes, automotive locksmiths are typically 30% to 50% cheaper than dealerships and come directly to your vehicle's location, eliminating towing costs.

Q6: What is a locksmith service call fee?

A service call fee is a flat charge ($50 to $90) to cover the locksmith's travel, fuel, and vehicle overhead to dispatch to your physical location.

Q7: How long does it take for a locksmith to make a car key?

Cutting and programming a modern transponder or smart key typically takes 20 to 45 minutes on-site.

Q8: Can a locksmith make a 'Do Not Duplicate' key?

Legally, yes, unless it is a patented, restricted keyway (like Medeco); standard keys stamped 'Do Not Duplicate' are not legally restricted.
