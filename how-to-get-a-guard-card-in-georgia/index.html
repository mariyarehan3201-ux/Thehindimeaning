How to Get a Guard Card in Georgia?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Working as a professional security guard in Georgia provides consistent employment across corporate commercial hubs, healthcare networks, industrial facilities, and private residential developments. In Georgia, private security personnel are regulated by the Georgia Board of Private Detective and Security Agencies, operating under the Georgia Secretary of State Professional Licensing Division. Whether you seek an entry-level unarmed security officer registration or an advanced armed security guard blue card, candidates must complete state-mandated training, pass rigorous criminal background vetting, and secure formal employer sponsorship to obtain legal credentialing.

Georgia Security Guard Regulations and Licensing Categories

The private security sector in Georgia is strictly governed under the Georgia Private Detective and Security Agencies Act, codified in the Official Code of Georgia Annotated (O.C.G.A.) Title 43, Chapter 38. The state divides security officers into two primary regulatory categories: unarmed security guards and armed security guards. Unlike states that issue standalone individual guard cards directly to freelance applicants, Georgia operates under an employer-sponsored regulatory model. An individual completes certified security instruction, but the formal application for registration must be submitted in conjunction with a licensed Georgia security agency that employs the guard.

To qualify for basic unarmed security guard registration in Georgia, an applicant must be at least eighteen years of age, a United States citizen or legally authorized resident alien, of good moral character, and possess a valid high school diploma or GED equivalent. Candidates cannot have any felony convictions, crimes involving moral turpitude, or active domestic violence restraining orders on their criminal record. For armed security guards, the minimum age requirement increases to twenty-one years of age, reflecting the advanced legal responsibilities associated with carrying a firearm during duty shifts.

Mandatory Training Curriculum and Firearms Range Qualification

Before an unarmed security guard can perform active duties, Georgia administrative rule 509-3-.02 mandates the completion of at least twenty-four classroom hours of basic security instruction taught by a Board-licensed instructor. The comprehensive curriculum covers vital operational topics, including the role of private security in crime prevention, Georgia criminal law, search and seizure limitations, citizen's arrest procedures, emergency medical response, fire safety protocols, note-taking and incident report writing, and conflict de-escalation tactics. Training must conclude with a formal written examination assessing comprehension of state security statutes.

For armed security guards, applicants must complete the initial twenty-four-hour basic course plus an additional sixteen hours of specialized classroom and range firearms training under Board rule 509-3-.03. The firearms course covers handgun safety, mechanical operation, holster draw techniques, weapons retention, and legal principles governing justifiable deadly force under Georgia law (O.C.G.A. § 16-3-21). Furthermore, armed candidates must complete a live-fire range qualification, firing a minimum of fifty rounds and achieving a passing score of at least eighty percent under the supervision of a state-certified firearms instructor.

Fingerprinting via GAPS, Application Submission, and Credential Renewal

Once training is successfully completed, the applicant must undergo a comprehensive criminal history background check through the Georgia Applicant Processing Service (GAPS), managed by Fieldprint. The applicant registers online with GAPS, selects the Georgia Board of Private Detective and Security Agencies as the receiving agency, and visits an authorized biometric fingerprinting site. Fingerprint data is electronically cross-referenced against both Georgia Crime Information Center (GCIC) and Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) criminal databases.

Following fingerprint clearance, the employing security contractor submits the formal employee registration application through the Georgia Secretary of State online licensing portal, accompanied by the employee's training certificate, passport-style photograph, and state processing fees (typically forty-five dollars for unarmed registration or seventy dollars for armed blue card registration). Once approved by the Board, the security officer is issued their official registration credentials. Georgia security registrations remain valid for two years, requiring biannual renewal alongside continuing education and, for armed guards, annual firearms requalification.

How to Obtain Security Guard Registration in Georgia

A step-by-step roadmap to completing state training, submitting fingerprints, and securing a Georgia security guard license.

  1. Verify Age and Educational Eligibility

    Confirm you are at least 18 years old for unarmed security (or 21 for armed security), hold a high school diploma or GED, and have a clean criminal record without felony convictions.

  2. Secure Employment with a Licensed Georgia Security Agency

    Apply for and secure a position with a security company licensed by the Georgia Board of Private Detective and Security Agencies to sponsor your registration.

  3. Complete the Mandatory 24-Hour Basic Training

    Attend an approved 24-hour classroom instruction program taught by a Board-licensed instructor, covering Georgia law, patrol techniques, and conflict de-escalation.

  4. Complete 16-Hour Firearms Training if Seeking Armed Status

    If applying for an armed blue card, complete an additional 16 hours of firearms training and achieve at least 80% on the live-fire range qualification.

  5. Schedule and Complete Biometric Fingerprinting via GAPS

    Register online with the Georgia Applicant Processing Service (Fieldprint) and visit an authorized site for electronic fingerprinting submitted to the GCIC and FBI.

  6. Submit Registration Application to the Georgia Secretary of State

    Have your sponsoring security employer submit your completed application, training certificate, and state fees through the Secretary of State online portal.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is a security guard card called in Georgia?

In Georgia, it is officially called an Employee Registration Certificate for unarmed guards, or an Armed Security Guard Blue Card for armed security officers, issued by the Georgia Board.

Q2: Can I get a Georgia guard card on my own without an employer?

No, Georgia operates under an employer-sponsored system. While you can complete the certified training independently, your final registration application must be submitted through a licensed Georgia security company.

Q3: How long is security guard training in Georgia?

Unarmed security officers must complete 24 classroom hours of basic instruction. Armed security guards must complete the 24-hour course plus an additional 16 hours of firearms training and range qualification.

Q4: How much does a Georgia guard card cost?

State board registration fees range from 45 dollars for unarmed guards to 70 dollars for armed officers, plus approximately 50 dollars for GAPS electronic fingerprinting and private training academy tuition fees.

Q5: Can someone with a felony become a security guard in Georgia?

Under Georgia law (O.C.G.A. § 43-38-7), individuals convicted of a felony or a crime involving moral turpitude are disqualified from receiving a security guard registration, unless granted a full pardon.

Q6: How long is a Georgia security registration valid?

Georgia security guard employee registrations and armed blue cards are valid for two years and expire on even-numbered years, requiring biennial renewal and ongoing training.

Q7: Do armed security guards in Georgia need to requalify with firearms?

Yes, armed security officers holding a Georgia blue card must complete an annual firearms requalification course and score at least 80% on the live-fire range to maintain their armed status.

Q8: What agency regulates security guards in Georgia?

The Georgia Board of Private Detective and Security Agencies, operating under the Professional Licensing Division of the Georgia Secretary of State, oversees all security licensing and enforcement.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Launching a private security career in Georgia requires meeting age and character prerequisites, undergoing structured classroom training through Board-approved academies, and passing background checks. Because Georgia utilizes an employer-sponsored registration framework, partnering with a reputable licensed security contractor simplifies the application pipeline. Whether maintaining an unarmed watch at a commercial facility or providing armed protection, licensed security guards play an indispensable role in maintaining community and corporate safety across Georgia.
