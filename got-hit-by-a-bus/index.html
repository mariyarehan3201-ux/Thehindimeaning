Got Hit by a Bus

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

If you got hit by a bus, you are facing a complex legal battle involving severe crush trauma, governmental immunity laws, and strict statutory filing deadlines. Whether struck by a municipal city transit bus, a school bus, or a private charter coach, recovering compensation requires immediate evidence collection and filing formal legal notices.

The Unique Legal Complexity of Bus Accident Claims

Being struck by a bus?"whether as a pedestrian, bicyclist, or occupant of a passenger vehicle?"is a devastating physical event. Modern city transit buses, regional commuter shuttles, and commercial motor coaches weigh between 25,000 and 40,000 pounds. When these massive transit vehicles collide with a pedestrian or automobile, the resulting blunt-force trauma frequently causes traumatic brain injuries (TBIs), severe spinal cord damage, crushed pelvises, and catastrophic internal hemorrhaging.

However, what sets bus accident claims apart from standard automotive litigation is the legal identity of the vehicle owner. The vast majority of transit buses and public school buses are owned and operated by municipal government entities, regional transportation authorities, or local public school districts. When pursuing a claim against a public government body, injured victims must navigate the complex doctrine of Sovereign Immunity under state Tort Claims Acts.

Historically, sovereign immunity protected government bodies from being sued by private citizens. While modern statutes have waived immunity for negligent motor vehicle operation, they have replaced it with strict procedural barriers. Foremost among these is the mandatory Notice of Claim requirement. Unlike standard personal injury lawsuits that provide a generous two-to-three-year statute of limitations, claims against city transit authorities typically require filing a formal administrative Notice of Claim within just 30, 60, or 90 days of the collision. Missing this short deadline forever forfeits your legal right to compensation. The table below compares municipal transit claims with private charter bus claims.

Entity Type Government transit authority or school district Private transportation corporation or tour line
Notice of Claim Deadline Extremely Short (Typically 30 to 90 days) Standard state statute of limitations (2 to 3 years)

Common Causes of Bus Collisions and Common Carrier Duty of Care

Under civil tort law, bus operators are held to the legal standard of a common carrier. A common carrier is a business or agency that transports members of the general public for a fee. Because common carriers carry human passengers, the law imposes a heightened duty of care?"requiring bus operators to exercise the highest degree of vigilance, caution, and foresight to ensure the safety of passengers, pedestrians, and surrounding motorists.

Despite this elevated legal duty, bus crashes occur with alarming frequency. Driver fatigue is a primary factor: transit bus operators often work split shifts, navigating congested urban streets with poor ergonomic seating and high stress levels. Another prevalent hazard is massive blind spots. Modern 40-foot transit buses possess extensive blind zones along the right-hand curb and directly in front of the high windshield cab. Drivers turning right or pulling away from curbside bus stops frequently fail to see pedestrians in crosswalks or cyclists in adjacent bike lanes. Critical Evidence: Onboard Multi-Camera Surveillance and Telematics

Modern transit and commercial buses are among the most technologically monitored vehicles on the roadway. A typical city transit bus is equipped with four to eight high-definition digital surveillance cameras?"capturing forward windshield traffic views, driver cab behavior, interior passenger movements, and side-mirror blind spot angles. These buses also record automated vehicle location (AVL) GPS data, speed telemetry, and mechanical braking data.

However, transit agencies operate under internal data retention policies where video footage is automatically recorded over or purged within 7 to 30 days unless a formal legal hold is served. If you fail to retain legal counsel immediately following the collision, the very video footage that proves the bus driver was looking at their phone or ran a red light will be permanently erased. An attorney must immediately file a formal Emergency Motion for Preservation of Evidence and serve public records requests under the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) or state open records laws.

How to File a Legal Claim After Being Hit by a Municipal Bus

A step-by-step procedural roadmap for victims of municipal bus crashes to comply with statutory notice laws and preserve evidence.

  1. Undergo Immediate Emergency Medical Evaluation

    Allow paramedics to transport you to an accredited emergency trauma center. Undergo complete diagnostic imaging to document internal bleeding, spinal fractures, and head injuries.

  2. Identify the Transit Authority and Bus Route Details

    Obtain the bus number, route number, driver identification badge, and the exact governmental agency operating the bus (e.g., city transit authority, county school board, or private contractor).

  3. Retain a Transit Accident Litigator Immediately

    Engage an attorney experienced in municipal transit claims within days of the accident. Confirm they are familiar with state Tort Claims Acts and short notice deadlines.

  4. File a Formal Notice of Claim Within Statutory Deadlines

    Have your attorney draft and serve a formal administrative Notice of Claim upon the city clerk or transit authority within the mandatory 30-to-90-day statutory window.

  5. Subpoena and Secure Onboard Video Footage

    Serve an immediate legal spoliation demand and FOIA request for all interior and exterior bus surveillance video footage, driver telematics, and maintenance records before automatic overwrite cycles.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can you sue a city bus if it hits your car?

Yes, you can sue the municipal transit authority or city government operating the bus, but you must first file a formal administrative Notice of Claim within a short statutory deadline (often 30 to 90 days).

Q2: What is a Notice of Claim in a bus accident case?

A Notice of Claim is a mandatory written legal document notifying a government agency of your intent to seek damages for injuries caused by their employee. Missing this deadline bars any lawsuit.

Q3: Do buses have cameras that record accidents?

Yes. Most modern transit buses have multiple interior and exterior cameras recording the driver, the road ahead, and side blind spots. However, footage is typically overwritten within 30 days unless legally preserved.

Q4: Are government damage caps applied to bus accident claims?

Yes. Many states have Tort Claims Acts that limit total damages recoverable against government entities (such as $200,000 to $500,000 per person), though private charter buses have no such caps.

Q5: What is the common carrier standard of care for buses?

A common carrier is legally held to the highest standard of care and diligence for the safety of its passengers and the public, exceeding the standard duty of care expected of private passenger drivers.

Q6: What if I was hit by a private charter or tour bus?

Private charter and tour buses are commercial entities governed by federal FMCSA rules rather than sovereign immunity, meaning standard 2-to-3-year personal injury statutes apply without government damage caps.

Q7: Who pays my medical bills after being hit by a bus?

In no-fault states, your own auto PIP insurance pays first. In fault states, the at-fault bus transit authority or commercial insurer pays via settlement or judgment, often alongside your health insurance.

Q8: Can bus passengers sue if injured during a crash?

Yes. Passengers injured while riding inside a bus can file claims against the bus operator or any other at-fault motorist involved in the collision to recover medical costs and pain and suffering.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Getting hit by a bus is an extraordinary trauma that demands immediate, highly strategic legal intervention. Navigating short municipal notice of claim deadlines, overcoming sovereign immunity damage caps, and securing onboard video footage requires counsel deeply experienced in public transit litigation. By acting within the first 30 days and building a rigorous evidentiary record, injured victims can overcome government bureaucracy and secure the compensation needed for full recovery.
