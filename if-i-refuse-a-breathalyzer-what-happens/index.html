If I Refuse a Breathalyzer What Happens?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Facing a law enforcement officer during a late-night traffic stop and being asked to blow into a breathalyzer tube is one of the most high-stakes legal situations a driver can encounter. Many motorists believe that refusing a breathalyzer is an easy way to prevent prosecutors from obtaining blood alcohol concentration (BAC) evidence to prove a Driving Under the Influence (DUI) charge. However, refusing a breath test triggers immediate, severe civil administrative penalties under state 'Implied Consent' statutes, including automatic driver's license suspensions, and may prompt police to obtain an electronic search warrant for a forced blood draw.

The Two Types of Breath Tests: Roadside PBT vs. Evidential Chemical Testing

To understand the legal consequences of refusing a breathalyzer, one must distinguish between the two completely different breath tests used by law enforcement: the Preliminary Alcohol Screening (PAS or PBT) and the Post-Arrest Evidential Chemical Breath Test (such as an Intoxilyzer 9000 or DataMaster DMT).

The Preliminary Breath Test is a handheld, battery-powered portable device administered on the roadside prior to an arrest. In most states, refusing the roadside PBT is either completely voluntary or carries a minor non-criminal civil traffic infraction, because PBT machines are notoriously prone to calibration drift and are used merely to establish probable cause for an arrest. Conversely, the evidential chemical breath test is administered at the police station or jail booking facility AFTER you have been formally placed under arrest. Refusing this post-arrest chemical test triggers the full weight of state Implied Consent penalties.

State Implied Consent Laws and Administrative License Revocation

Every state in the nation enforces an 'Implied Consent' statute. Under implied consent laws, by exercising the privilege of driving a motor vehicle on public highways, you have legally agreed in advance to submit to a chemical test of your breath, blood, or urine if an officer has lawful probable cause to arrest you for impaired driving.

When you refuse a post-arrest evidential chemical test, the officer immediately confiscates your physical driver's license and issues a temporary driving permit alongside a Notice of Administrative License Revocation (ALR). The state DMV automatically suspends your driving privileges—typically for six months to one year for a first-time refusal, and two to three years for a repeat refusal—regardless of whether you are ultimately convicted or acquitted of the criminal DUI in court.

Electronic Warrants and Forced Blood Draws

Many drivers assume that refusing a breathalyzer stops the police from collecting chemical evidence. In modern criminal justice, this assumption is false. Under the Supreme Court's ruling in Missouri v. McNeely (2013), police officers who encounter a breath test refusal can quickly apply for an electronic search warrant from an on-call magistrate judge using a tablet or smartphone.

Magistrates review sworn officer affidavits digitally and issue electronic search warrants within fifteen to thirty minutes. Once a judicial search warrant is signed, the suspect is transported to a hospital or certified phlebotomy suite where a medical technician draws blood—using physical restraint if necessary. The suspect now faces the worst of both worlds: an automatic 1-year driver's license suspension for refusing the breath test, plus blood test evidence showing their exact chemical BAC in criminal court.

How to Navigate a Breathalyzer Traffic Stop

Follow this step-by-step roadmap to understand your rights, differentiate breath tests, and request an administrative license hearing.

  1. Differentiate Roadside PBT from Station Chemical Test

    Recognize that a handheld breath test on the side of the road is voluntary in most states, while the post-arrest machine at the station is mandatory.

  2. Decline Voluntary Roadside Field Sobriety Tests

    Politely decline voluntary roadside balance acrobatics and preliminary handheld breath tests without being rude or aggressive.

  3. Evaluate Implied Consent Warnings Post-Arrest

    Listen carefully as the officer reads statutory Implied Consent warnings at the station regarding mandatory license suspension penalties.

  4. Request an Immediate Confidential Attorney Call

    Exercise your constitutional right to speak with a licensed criminal defense attorney before making a final decision on breath testing.

  5. File an Administrative License Suspension Appeal in Time

    Have your lawyer file for an administrative license hearing within your state's strict deadline (typically 10 to 15 days) to fight suspension.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can you refuse a breathalyzer without getting arrested?

Refusing a roadside handheld breath test does not prevent an arrest if the officer already observed slurred speech, alcohol odor, or erratic driving. The officer will simply arrest you based on probable cause.

Q2: What is an Implied Consent law?

Implied Consent laws state that by driving on public roads, you have automatically agreed to submit to chemical testing (breath, blood, or urine) if lawfully arrested for suspected impaired driving.

Q3: How long is your license suspended for refusing a breathalyzer?

For a first offense, refusing an evidential chemical breath test typically results in an automatic administrative suspension of 6 to 12 months, and up to 2 to 3 years for repeat refusals.

Q4: Can the police force you to give blood if you refuse a breath test?

Yes. Police can obtain an electronic search warrant signed by a judge within minutes, authorizing medical staff to draw your blood to test for alcohol and drugs, even over your objection.

Q5: Can a breathalyzer refusal be used against you in court?

Yes. In most states, prosecutors are legally permitted to inform the jury that you refused the chemical breath test, arguing that your refusal demonstrates 'consciousness of guilt.'

Q6: Is refusing a breathalyzer a crime in itself?

In most states, a first-time refusal is a civil administrative violation handled by the DMV. However, in states like Florida (on second refusal) or under federal jurisdiction, refusing is a separate criminal misdemeanor.

Q7: How long do you have to appeal an automatic license suspension?

Most states give drivers only 10 to 15 calendar days from the date of arrest to request a formal administrative hearing with the DMV to challenge the suspension.

Q8: Can you get a restricted occupational license after a refusal?

In many states, drivers who refuse can obtain a restricted occupational or hardship license permitting driving to work and medical care, provided they install an Ignition Interlock Device (IID) on their vehicle.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Refusing a breathalyzer test is a complex legal decision that rarely produces the simple escape drivers anticipate. While refusing a roadside handheld PBT is generally advisable because those devices are unreliable, refusing the official post-arrest chemical machine at the police station triggers automatic mandatory driver's license suspensions under Implied Consent laws, permits prosecutors to argue consciousness of guilt at trial, and often leads to an electronic search warrant for a forced blood draw. If you are arrested for DUI, contact an experienced criminal defense lawyer immediately to request an administrative license hearing within your state's strict appeal window.
