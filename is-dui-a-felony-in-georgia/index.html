Is DUI a Felony in Georgia?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

In the state of Georgia, a first, second, or third Driving Under the Influence (DUI) offense within a ten-year 'lookback' period is classified as a misdemeanor under Official Code of Georgia Annotated (O.C.G.A.) § 40-6-391. However, a DUI offense in Georgia immediately elevates into a severe felony under specific statutory conditions: if a driver commits a fourth DUI within ten years, if the impaired driving causes serious physical injury to another person, if it causes the termination of a pregnancy, or if an impaired collision results in a fatality.

The Statutory Framework: Georgia DUI Offense Classifications

Under Georgia motor vehicle criminal code (O.C.G.A. § 40-6-391), driving under the influence of alcohol, drugs, or toxic inhalants to the point where it is less safe to drive, or operating a vehicle with a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.08% or higher (0.04% for commercial drivers, 0.02% for drivers under 21), is vigorously prosecuted. For decades, repeat DUIs in Georgia were prosecuted strictly as high and aggravated misdemeanors, allowing repeat offenders to avoid state prison sentences.

To curb chronic recidivism, the Georgia General Assembly enacted legislation establishing that any fourth DUI conviction occurring within a rolling ten-year period is a mandatory felony offense. When calculating the ten-year lookback window, Georgia courts measure from the arrest date of the prior offenses to the arrest date of the current charge. A felony conviction brings devastating statutory penalties: mandatory state prison time, hefty felony fines, five-year driver's license revocation, permanent loss of civil voting rights, and lifelong disqualification from firearms ownership.

Aggravating Factors That Turn a First or Second DUI into a Felony

A driver does not need three prior convictions to face felony charges in Georgia. State criminal statutes create several distinct felony offenses arising directly out of impaired driving incidents involving physical harm, high-risk endangerment, or death.

The most common felony charge is Serious Injury by Vehicle (O.C.G.A. § 40-6-394). If an impaired motorist causes an accident resulting in bodily harm that renders a body part useless, causes severe disfigurement, or inflicts organic brain damage, the offense is charged as a felony carrying 1 to 15 years in state prison. Similarly, Homicide by Vehicle in the First Degree (O.C.G.A. § 40-6-393) applies whenever a DUI causes the death of any person (including an unborn child under Feticide by Vehicle, O.C.G.A. § 40-6-393.1), carrying a mandatory prison term of 3 to 15 years per fatality.

Collateral Consequences of a Felony DUI Conviction in Georgia

The consequences of a felony DUI conviction in Georgia extend far beyond serving time in a state correctional facility. Unlike standard misdemeanors that may be mitigated through conditional discharge in certain non-DUI contexts, Georgia law explicitly forbids the expungement or record restriction of any DUI conviction—misdemeanor or felony. A DUI conviction remains on a Georgia criminal history permanently.

Felony status strips an individual of their right to vote until all probation and parole terms are concluded. It triggers immediate revocation of state professional licenses for nurses, doctors, real estate brokers, and commercial pilots. Furthermore, federal law permanently bans convicted felons from purchasing or possessing firearms or ammunition, and auto insurance rates escalate into high-risk SR-22 assigned risk pools with crushing premiums.

How to Protect Your Rights After a Georgia DUI Arrest

Step-by-step guidance on navigating legal deadlines and court appearances following a DUI arrest in Georgia.

  1. File the 30-Day DDS Appeal Letter or Request Interlock Permit

    Georgia gives you exactly 30 calendar days from the arrest date to either file an administrative license suspension (ALS) appeal with the Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS) or opt for a 12-month ignition interlock permit.

  2. Retain a Georgia Criminal Defense Attorney Specializing in DUI

    Hire an attorney licensed in Georgia with certification in National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) field sobriety protocols and Intoxilyzer 9000 breath testing.

  3. Review the Uniform Traffic Citation and Arrest Video Footage

    Instruct your attorney to file formal discovery motions requesting the arresting officer's body-worn and dashboard camera video, CAD dispatch logs, and Intoxilyzer 9000 diagnostic logs.

  4. Enroll in the State-Approved DUI Risk Reduction Program

    Proactively complete the certified 20-hour Georgia DUI Risk Reduction course (commonly called DUI School) and undergo a clinical alcohol evaluation before your court arraignment.

  5. Attend All Arraignment and Motions Hearings in State or Superior Court

    Appear at all scheduled court dates with your attorney. If facing felony charges, your case will be prosecuted by the County District Attorney in the County Superior Court.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is a first DUI in Georgia a felony or misdemeanor?

A first DUI in Georgia is a misdemeanor, provided no aggravating circumstances occurred, such as serious physical injury or fatality.

Q2: When does a DUI become a felony in Georgia?

A DUI becomes a felony in Georgia upon a fourth conviction within a 10-year period, or if the crash causes serious bodily injury, feticide, or death.

Q3: What is the lookback period for repeat DUIs in Georgia?

Georgia uses a 10-year lookback period, measured from arrest date to arrest date, to determine repeat offense sentencing enhancements.

Q4: Can a DUI conviction be expunged from your record in Georgia?

No. Under Georgia law (O.C.G.A. § 35-3-37), DUI convictions are strictly exempt from record restriction (expungement) and remain on your record for life.

Q5: What is Serious Injury by Vehicle in Georgia?

Serious Injury by Vehicle is a felony charge filed when an impaired driver causes severe bodily harm, broken bones, or permanent disfigurement to another person, carrying 1 to 15 years in prison.

Q6: Can having a child in the car make a DUI a felony in Georgia?

Having a child under 14 in the vehicle results in an additional child endangerment charge. While the first two are misdemeanors, a third child endangerment charge is a felony.

Q7: What happens to your driver's license after a 4th DUI in Georgia?

A fourth DUI conviction results in a mandatory 5-year driver's license revocation, with the possibility of applying for a probationary license after two years under strict ignition interlock rules.

Q8: Do you have to serve mandatory jail time for a Georgia DUI?

Yes. Georgia law mandates at least 24 hours in jail for a first offense with BAC above 0.08%, 72 hours for a second offense, 15 days for a third offense, and 90 days for a felony fourth offense.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

To conclude, while a first, second, or third DUI in Georgia is classified as a misdemeanor, repeat offenses and aggravating factors quickly escalate charges to the felony level. A fourth DUI within ten years is a statutory felony, as are any impaired driving incidents causing serious bodily injury or fatality. Given the mandatory prison terms, permanent criminal records, and forfeiture of fundamental civil rights associated with felony DUI convictions in Georgia, obtaining skilled criminal defense representation immediately following an arrest is critical.
