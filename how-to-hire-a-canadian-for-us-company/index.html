How to Hire a Canadian for US Company

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Hiring a Canadian for a US company provides access to world-class technical, engineering, and managerial talent across aligned time zones. However, US employers must navigate complex cross-border labor laws, decide between remote employment via an Employer of Record (EOR) or US work authorization visas (like the TN visa), and ensure strict Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) tax compliance.

The Two Strategic Pathways: Remote in Canada vs. US Relocation

When a US enterprise identifies top-tier talent in Canada, the first strategic decision dictates the entire legal, tax, and immigration framework: Will the Canadian worker remain physically resident in Canada working remotely, or will they relocate to the United States to work on-site? Each pathway operates under completely distinct legal regimes.

If the Canadian employee will relocate physically to the United States, the US company must sponsor them for appropriate US work authorization. Thanks to the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA, formerly NAFTA), Canadian citizens enjoy a streamlined, fast-track immigration option known as the TN Nonimmigrant Visa (TN-1). The TN visa allows qualified Canadian professionals in over 60 designated occupations (such as software engineers, management consultants, accountants, and scientists) to obtain immediate US work authorization directly at a US port of entry or pre-clearance airport without the costly, slow lottery delays of the H-1B visa program.

Conversely, if the Canadian worker will remain physically in Canada working remotely from Toronto, Vancouver, or Montreal, US immigration laws do not apply. Instead, the engagement is governed by Canadian federal tax laws and provincial employment standards legislation. A US company cannot simply issue a standard US W-2 form to a Canadian resident. To hire remotely without incorporating a costly Canadian subsidiary, US companies typically partner with a certified Employer of Record (EOR). The Employer of Record (EOR) Solution for Remote Canadian Staff

For US businesses seeking to employ Canadian remote workers without establishing a physical corporate branch in Canada, an Employer of Record (EOR) is the industry standard compliance mechanism. Global EOR providers (such as Deel, Remote, Oyster, and Velocity Global) possess legally registered, active corporate entities in Canada. Under this co-employment structure, the EOR serves as the legal employer on paper?"administering local payroll, remitting statutory taxes to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), and managing provincial workers compensation.

Meanwhile, the US company maintains complete day-to-day operational control over the employee: directing projects, managing performance, setting work hours, and integrating the worker into daily corporate workflows. The EOR ensures that employment contracts adhere strictly to the Employment Standards Act of the worker specific province (such as Ontario, British Columbia, or Quebec). Provincial laws strictly govern mandatory minimum notice periods for termination, statutory severance pay, mandatory paid vacation (minimum two to three weeks annually), and provincial statutory holidays. Contractor Misclassification and Permanent Establishment Risks

Many US businesses attempt to bypass the complexity of cross-border employment by simply hiring Canadian workers as "independent contractors" and paying them via wire transfer or PayPal. While engaging true freelance specialists for defined, short-term project deliverables is lawful, classifying a full-time, core team member as a contractor is an extraordinarily dangerous practice under Canadian law.

The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) and Canadian courts apply strict legal tests to determine employment status: the degree of employer control, ownership of tools and computer hardware, chance of profit versus risk of loss, and integration into the business. If the CRA audits the worker and determines that an employer-employee relationship exists, the US company faces devastating penalties: retroactive liability for all unremitted employer Canada Pension Plan (CPP) contributions, unremitted Employment Insurance (EI) premiums, unpaid provincial health taxes, interest, and substantial misclassification fines. Furthermore, having remote Canadian employees negotiating commercial contracts can trigger Permanent Establishment (PE), subjecting the US company's global profits to Canadian corporate taxation.

How to Compliantly Hire and Onboard a Canadian Remote Worker

A step-by-step practical guide for US companies to legally hire, onboard, and pay a Canadian remote employee.

  1. Select Your Hiring Vehicle (EOR vs. US TN Visa)

    Determine whether the candidate will relocate to the US under a TN-1 nonimmigrant visa or work remotely from Canada through a certified Employer of Record (EOR).

  2. Partner with a Canadian Employer of Record (EOR)

    Engage an established EOR provider (such as Deel, Remote, or Oyster) to serve as the legal employing entity in the worker specific Canadian province.

  3. Draft a Province-Specific Employment Contract

    Collaborate with the EOR to draft a compliant employment contract adhering to provincial labor standards regarding vacation pay, statutory holidays, and termination notice.

  4. Collect Required Canadian Tax and Identity Forms

    Collect the employee 9-digit Social Insurance Number (SIN), completed Federal TD1 tax credits return, provincial TD1 form, and CAD direct deposit banking details.

  5. Execute Cross-Border Intellectual Property Assignment

    Ensure the candidate signs comprehensive proprietary information, intellectual property assignment, and non-disclosure agreements enforceable under Canadian provincial law.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can a US company hire a Canadian citizen as a remote employee?

Yes. A US company can hire a Canadian remote worker either by partnering with an Employer of Record (EOR), establishing a Canadian corporate subsidiary, or hiring a true independent contractor.

Q2: What is a TN visa for Canadian professionals?

The TN-1 visa is a fast-track US work authorization established under the USMCA treaty that allows Canadian citizens in over 60 professional categories to work on-site in the US without an H-1B lottery.

Q3: Can a US company pay a Canadian worker on a US W-2?

No. A US W-2 is strictly for workers subject to US payroll taxes. A remote employee residing in Canada must be paid in Canadian dollars with statutory Canadian T4 tax deductions.

Q4: What is an Employer of Record (EOR) in Canada?

An EOR is a third-party company that legally employs Canadian workers on behalf of foreign businesses, managing local payroll, CRA tax remittances, benefits, and provincial labor law compliance.

Q5: What are the risks of hiring a Canadian as an independent contractor?

If the CRA determines the contractor is actually a misclassified employee, the US company is retroactively liable for unpaid employer CPP/EI contributions, back taxes, interest, and severe penalties.

Q6: What is Permanent Establishment risk in Canada?

Permanent Establishment (PE) occurs when a Canadian-based employee has the authority to conclude binding sales contracts for a US company, potentially exposing the US company to Canadian corporate taxes.

Q7: Do Canadian employees get healthcare benefits from a US employer?

While basic medical care is covered under Canadian provincial healthcare, competitive employers provide supplemental private health insurance covering dental, vision, and prescription medications.

Q8: How much vacation time are Canadian employees legally entitled to?

Under Canadian provincial employment standards, full-time employees are legally entitled to a minimum of two weeks paid vacation annually (accrued at 4% of gross pay), increasing with tenure.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Hiring Canadian professionals allows US companies to scale rapidly with exceptional, culturally aligned talent in identical time zones. Whether bringing workers to the US on streamlined TN visas or employing them remotely through an established Employer of Record, compliance with local labor and tax laws is paramount. By avoiding contractor misclassification and partnering with experienced cross-border employment specialists, US businesses can build a thriving, compliant international workforce.
