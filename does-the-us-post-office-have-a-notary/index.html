Does the US Post Office Have a Notary?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 12, 2026

No, United States Postal Service post offices do not have a notary public and do not offer notary services to the general public. While USPS branches are federal facilities that verify identity documents for passport applications, postal employees are not commissioned as state notaries public. Because notary public commissions are granted and regulated strictly at the state government level rather than through federal postal channels, you must look to retail shipping franchises, banking institutions, or mobile notary providers to notarize legal documents.

Jurisdictional Differences Between Federal Post Offices and State Notaries

The absence of notary public services at the post office stems from constitutional and statutory jurisdictional divisions. A notary public is an official commissioned directly by an individual state government—typically through the Secretary of State office. Their legal role is to witness document signings, administer solemn oaths, verify personal identity, and certify document authenticity in accordance with strict state-specific statutory codes.

In contrast, the United States Postal Service is an independent establishment of the executive branch of the federal government. Postal retail employees operate exclusively under federal postal regulations and collective bargaining agreements. Mandating that postal clerks maintain individual state notary commissions, manage personal notary bond insurance, keep official state logbooks, and navigate varying state liability laws would create severe administrative friction and legal conflicts within the federal postal system.

Top Commercial and Community Locations to Notarize Documents

If you need to execute a power of attorney, bill of sale, deed, affidavit, or financial contract, several accessible institutions provide convenient notary public services. Your local bank or credit union is frequently the most economical starting point. Major retail banking institutions such as Chase, Bank of America, and Wells Fargo maintain licensed notaries on staff across most branches, and they commonly provide notary services completely free of charge to active account holders.

When banking branches are closed or unavailable, packaging and shipping storefronts like The UPS Store serve as the primary walk-in solution. Most UPS Store locations have multiple commissioned notaries on duty during peak daytime and weekend hours. Furthermore, local town halls, municipal clerk offices, real estate title agencies, and public libraries frequently have staff members commissioned to notarize papers for community residents at nominal statutory rates.

Remote Online Notarization (RON) and Digital Solutions

In recent years, the rapid enactment of state Remote Online Notarization statutes has revolutionized document verification. Over forty-five states have passed permanent legislation authorizing licensed notaries to verify identities and witness signatures via secure, two-way audio-video internet connections. This technology enables individuals to notarize important papers from the comfort of their home computer or smartphone in minutes.

Platforms such as Notarize, PandaDoc Notary, and OneNotary utilize credential analysis software and knowledge-based authentication questions to verify government IDs with remarkable security. Once verified, you sign your digital document electronically while on a recorded video call with the remote notary, who attaches a tamper-evident digital certificate and cryptographic seal that is legally accepted across all fifty states.

How to Get Documents Notarized Successfully in 5 Steps

Follow these five essential steps to prepare and execute your notarized paperwork without delays or administrative rejections.

  1. Verify Notary Acceptance Requirements

    Confirm with the recipient agency whether they require traditional wet-ink in-person notarization or accept digital Remote Online Notarization.

  2. Bring Valid Government-Issued Photo ID

    Ensure your driver license, state ID card, or unexpired passport is valid, undamaged, and matches your legal name exactly.

  3. Do Not Pre-Sign Your Documents

    Leave all signature and date lines blank because you must sign the paperwork in the direct presence of the commissioned notary.

  4. Complete All Other Document Sections

    Fill out every other required field and exhibit prior to your appointment, as notaries cannot execute incomplete or blank documents.

  5. Sign, Pay Fee, and Receive Official Seal

    Sign your name under oath in view of the notary, pay the statutory fee, and ensure the official ink stamp and commission expiration are legible.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Why doesn't the post office provide notary services?

The post office is a federal entity, whereas notaries public are commissioned and regulated exclusively by state governments.

Q2: Does The UPS Store offer notary services?

Yes, most UPS Store locations have commissioned notaries available six to seven days a week for walk-in customers.

Q3: Can I get something notarized at my bank for free?

Yes, major retail banks and credit unions frequently provide free notary services to customers with active accounts.

Q4: Can a notary give legal advice on what forms to sign?

No, notaries are strictly prohibited from providing legal advice or drafting documents unless they are also licensed attorneys.

Q5: What is Remote Online Notarization?

Remote Online Notarization allows signers to verify their identity and sign documents over a secure two-way video call with a licensed notary.

Q6: Will a notary certify a copy of a birth certificate?

No, notaries cannot certify vital records like birth, death, or marriage certificates; those must be issued by the vital records department.

Q7: Do public libraries have notaries available?

Many municipal and county public libraries have staff members who provide free or low-cost notary services by appointment.

Q8: What should I do if my ID is expired?

You must renew your government identification before visiting a notary, as expired identification cannot be legally accepted.
