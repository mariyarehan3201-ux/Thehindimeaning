How Much Does a Summer Camp Cost?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a summer camp cost requires analyzing whether the program is a local traditional day camp or a residential overnight sleepaway camp, alongside specialty curriculum and camp duration. On average, a traditional day camp costs between $200 and $600 per week across the United States. In contrast, overnight sleepaway camps typically cost between $1,000 and $2,500 per week, with premier 4-to-7-week Northeast sleepaway camps ranging from $6,500 to $15,000+ per summer.

Day Camp vs. Overnight Sleepaway Camp Pricing

Summer camp programs represent a cornerstone of childhood enrichment and essential working-parent childcare. Traditional day camps operate from 9:00 AM to 3:00 or 4:00 PM on weekdays. Community-based non-profit day camps (such as the YMCA, Boys & Girls Clubs, or municipal parks and recreation departments) offer the most affordable rates, averaging $175 to $350 per week ($700 to $1,400 per month), with sliding-scale financial aid for low-income families.

Private commercial day camps—featuring swimming pools, sports coaching, archery, arts and crafts, and bus transportation—average $400 to $800 per week. Overnight sleepaway camps encompass 24/7 room, board, dining, cabin counselor supervision, and extensive waterfront activities. Weekly sleepaway sessions average $1,200 to $2,400 per camper. Historic private sleepaway camps in Maine, New Hampshire, New York's Adirondacks, and the Berkshires charge $10,000 to $16,000 for full 7-week summer seasons.

Specialty Camps: STEM, Coding, Sports, and Arts

Specialty camps focusing on targeted academic or technical skill development carry higher tuition rates due to low student-to-instructor ratios and specialized technology. Summer coding, robotics, and artificial intelligence day camps (such as iD Tech, Lavner Education, or Code Ninjas) cost $600 to $1,100 per week, requiring high-end laptops, microcontrollers, and expert instructors.

Competitive sports training camps (elite soccer academies, Nike tennis camps, gymnastics, and ice hockey camps) range from $500 to $900 weekly for day sessions, and $1,400 to $2,600 weekly for residential university campus sleepaway programs. Performing arts camps specializing in musical theater, vocal training, and film production average $600 to $1,200 per week.

Tax Credits, Dependent Care FSA, and Financial Aid

Working parents can leverage federal tax incentives to substantially offset day camp expenses. Day camp tuition qualifies for the federal Child and Dependent Care Credit (IRS Form 2441) for children under age 13, allowing parents to claim up to $3,000 in expenses for one child or $6,000 for two or more children, yielding a 20% to 35% tax credit. Overnight sleepaway camp tuition does NOT qualify for the tax credit.

Furthermore, parents can pay for eligible day camps using pre-tax dollars through an employer-sponsored Dependent Care Flexible Spending Account (DCFSA), setting aside up to $5,000 annually tax-free. Additionally, the American Camp Association (ACA) estimates that over 80% of accredited overnight camps offer need-based camperships (scholarships) and sibling discounts of 5% to 15%.

How to Budget and Save on Summer Camp

Follow these five tactical steps to secure affordable, high-quality summer camp enrollment.

  1. Take Advantage of Early-Bird Registration Discounts

    Register in December, January, or February to capture 10% to 20% early-bird tuition discounts.

  2. Utilize a Dependent Care FSA for Pre-Tax Day Camp

    Elect Dependent Care FSA payroll deductions through your employer to pay day camp costs with pre-tax income.

  3. Apply Early for Campership Financial Aid

    Submit financial aid applications and W-2 documentation to non-profit camps well before annual scholarship funds deplete.

  4. Coordinate Neighborhood Parent Carpools

    Partner with nearby parents to share morning and afternoon drop-offs and avoid expensive camp bus fees.

  5. Claim the Child and Dependent Care Tax Credit

    Retain all day camp receipts and camp EIN tax identification numbers to claim the tax credit on your federal return.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the average cost of a week of summer camp?

A traditional day camp averages $200 to $600 per week, while an overnight sleepaway camp averages $1,000 to $2,500 per week.

Q2: Are summer camps tax deductible?

Day camps are tax-deductible under the Child and Dependent Care Credit and eligible for Dependent Care FSA funds; overnight sleepaway camps are not.

Q3: How much does YMCA summer camp cost?

YMCA day camps typically cost between $175 and $325 per week, with significant sliding-scale financial assistance available.

Q4: Why are overnight sleepaway camps so expensive?

Tuition covers 24/7 lodging, three daily meals, certified waterfront lifeguards, activity equipment, medical infirmary staff, and round-the-clock counselor supervision.

Q5: What age do kids usually start sleepaway camp?

Most children begin attending overnight sleepaway camps between the ages of 8 and 11, often starting with 1-week or 2-week trial sessions.

Q6: Do summer camps offer financial aid or scholarships?

Yes, non-profit camps and ACA-accredited private camps offer need-based 'camperships' that reduce tuition by 25% to 90%.

Q7: How much does a coding or STEM camp cost?

Specialized STEM, robotics, and coding day camps (such as iD Tech) typically cost between $600 and $1,100 per week.

Q8: What is the cheapest summer camp option?

Municipal town parks and recreation day camps and community centers offer the lowest prices, often $120 to $220 per week.
