Cost to Remove Asbestos Siding

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Cost to remove asbestos siding is a major financial and logistical question for homeowners restoring mid-century homes built between 1930 and 1975. Asbestos-cement siding shingles—frequently marketed under brand names like Transite, Careystone, and Johns-Manville—were celebrated for being fireproof, rot-proof, and impervious to termites. However, when siding panels crack, weather, or require replacement during whole-house modernizations, property owners must weigh the costs of professional abatement, hazardous disposal, and replacement cladding.

Average Cost Benchmarks and Square Footage Rates

In the United States, the average cost to professionally remove asbestos-cement siding ranges from $7.00 to $15.00 per square foot for abatement alone. For a typical single-story 1,500-square-foot home with approximately 1,200 to 1,500 square feet of exterior wall siding, complete abatement generally totals between $8,500 and $18,000. Larger two-story homes requiring multi-level scaffolding, extensive plastic drop sheeting, and complex staging can easily reach $16,000 to $28,000.

Crucially, abatement quotes cover only the removal, packaging, transport, and hazardous landfill disposal of the old siding; they do not include installing new exterior cladding. When homeowners factor in subsequent installation of modern vinyl, fiber-cement (such as James Hardie), or engineered wood siding, the total exterior renovation expenditure typically ranges from $18,000 to $38,000 depending on material quality and architectural details.

Cost Drivers: Site Staging, Hazardous Disposal, and Permits

Multiple site-specific variables drive the final cost of an exterior asbestos siding abatement project. Labor and site containment represent approximately fifty-five percent of the total cost. Certified crews must lay heavy six-mil plastic drop cloths around the entire foundation perimeter extending ten feet outward to catch any stray chips. Technicians must work methodically from top to bottom, manually unfastening each shingle using specialized nail-cutters or flat pry bars while continually misting panels with surfactant water.

Hazardous waste disposal and administrative permitting comprise another twenty-five percent of overall costs. Asbestos-cement siding is heavy and dense, weighing roughly three to four pounds per square foot. Because landfill tip fees for asbestos waste are billed by the ton, disposing of several tons of brittle siding can cost $800 to $2,500 in tipping and transport fees alone. State NESHAP notifications, municipal building permits, and mandatory air clearance monitoring comprise the remaining balance.

Encapsulation: The Cost-Saving Over-Siding Alternative

Homeowners seeking to modernize their home exterior without the substantial expense of full abatement often choose encapsulation through over-siding. Because intact asbestos siding shingles are non-friable Category II materials that release zero fibers when undisturbed, building codes across most jurisdictions allow contractors to install new siding directly over existing asbestos shingles.

During over-siding, carpenters nail vertical wood or composite furring strips directly through the asbestos shingles into underlying wall studs, or install rigid foam insulation boards over the shingles. New vinyl or fiber-cement siding is then fastened to the furring strips, permanently sealing the asbestos behind a modern, weatherproof barrier. Over-siding typically saves homeowners $8,000 to $15,000 by eliminating hazardous abatement labor, landfill tipping fees, and permit delays.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to remove asbestos siding?

Professional asbestos siding removal typically costs between $7.00 and $15.00 per square foot, totaling $8,500 to $18,000 for an average-sized home.

Q2: Can I put new vinyl siding over old asbestos siding?

Yes, installing furring strips or foam insulation and hanging new vinyl siding directly over intact asbestos shingles is an approved, cost-effective encapsulation method.

Q3: Why is removing asbestos siding so expensive?

High costs result from licensed hazardous materials labor, negative-pressure containment requirements, heavy scaffolding, and expensive landfill tipping fees.

Q4: Can a homeowner remove asbestos siding legally?

Some states allow single-family homeowners to remove siding, but DIY removal is hazardous, generates brittle broken shards, and complicates legal disposal.

Q5: How much does it cost to dispose of asbestos siding at a dump?

Certified hazardous waste landfills charge between sixty and one hundred fifty dollars per ton for asbestos-cement siding disposal, plus transport fees.

Q6: Is asbestos siding dangerous if left in place?

No, undisturbed, unpainted asbestos-cement siding in good condition is non-friable and poses zero immediate health risk to household occupants.

Q7: How long does professional asbestos siding removal take?

A standard residential siding abatement project typically takes three to six business days depending on house height, layout, and weather conditions.

Q8: Does homeowners insurance cover asbestos siding removal?

Standard policies do not cover voluntary removal, though abatement may be covered if the siding was damaged by a covered storm, fire, or vehicle impact.
