What Does Withhold Adjudication Mean?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

In state criminal sentencing—most notably in Florida jurisprudence under Florida Statute § 948.01—'adjudication withheld' is a specialized judicial plea mechanism where a judge accepts a defendant's guilty or no-contest plea and places them on probation while formally withholding a conviction. Under this legal framework, although the defendant acknowledges guilt, the court declines to enter a formal adjudication of guilt. Consequently, if the defendant successfully completes probation without violations, they legally avoid the label of a convicted felon or convicted misdemeanant.

How to Successfully Complete Probation and Seal an Adjudicated Withheld Case

Strategic legal roadmap for clearing a court record after receiving an adjudication withheld.

  1. Complete All Court-Ordered Probation Conditions

    Pay all court costs, fines, and restitution immediately, and complete required community service hours and counseling programs ahead of deadline.

  2. Request Early Termination of Probation

    Once you have completed fifty percent of your probation term without violations and paid all financial obligations, petition for early termination.

  3. Obtain Written Certificate of Discharge

    Ensure your probation officer files a formal Notice of Termination of Probation with the clerk of court confirming case closure.

  4. Apply for a Certificate of Eligibility for Sealing

    Submit fingerprints and an application to the State Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE) to verify eligibility to seal your court record.

  5. File a Petition to Seal in Circuit Court

    Once certified, have your defense attorney file a formal Petition and Affidavit to Seal Criminal History Records before the presiding circuit judge.

Yes. It remains visible as an arrest and court record showing 'Adjudication Withheld' until you file a court petition to have the record legally sealed.

You cannot purchase a firearm while on active probation. Once probation ends, state gun rights depend on whether the charge was sealed and specific state gun laws.

Q5: Can an adjudication withheld be expunged?

In Florida, an adjudication withheld can be legally sealed. Once sealed for 10 years without new offenses, it can then be fully expunged from the record.

The judge can revoke the withhold, formally adjudicate you guilty as a convicted felon, and sentence you to the maximum statutory prison term for the offense.
