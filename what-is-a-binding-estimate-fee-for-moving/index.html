What Is a Binding Estimate Fee for Moving?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: October 1, 2026

A binding estimate fee for moving is a fee that an interstate moving company may legally charge to prepare a legally binding moving contract that guarantees the total price of your relocation based on a predetermined inventory of household goods. Under federal regulations established by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) in 49 CFR Part 375, movers are permitted to charge a reasonable fee for providing a binding estimate, provided this fee is clearly disclosed to the shipper in advance. A binding estimate legally obligates the mover to transport the inventoried shipment for the exact contracted price, shielding consumers from unexpected price hikes on moving day.

Federal Regulatory Framework: FMCSA Rules and 49 CFR Part 375

Interstate transportation of household goods in the United States is strictly regulated by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA), an operating administration of the United States Department of Transportation. Under Title 49 of the Code of Federal Regulations, Section 375.401 and Section 375.403, federal law recognizes three distinct categories of moving estimates: non-binding estimates, binding estimates, and binding not-to-exceed estimates. A binding estimate is a formal contract between the shipper (the customer) and the motor carrier establishing that the carrier will perform the specified transportation services for the exact total dollar amount specified in the agreement.

Federal regulations specifically address whether a carrier can charge for providing this estimate. Under 49 CFR Section 375.401(c), a household goods motor carrier may charge a fee for providing a binding estimate, provided that the mover discloses the fee amount in its published tariff and informs the shipper in writing before conducting the physical survey. While many moving companies offer complimentary binding estimates to remain competitive, premier moving van lines charge a modest administrative fee ($50 to $150) to offset the cost of dispatching an experienced in-home estimator to survey bulky furniture and specialty items.

The Mandatory Visual Survey and Accessorial Service Fees

A binding estimate is legally invalid unless the moving company performs a thorough visual survey of your household goods. Under 49 CFR Section 375.401, if a mover's physical terminal is located within fifty miles of your home, the carrier is legally required to conduct an in-person, physical walkthrough of your residence to visually inspect every room, closet, attic, and garage. The only exception occurs if the shipper voluntarily signs a written waiver opting for a live, real-time video survey. Moving companies that issue binding estimates over the telephone based purely on self-reported verbal item lists are violating federal law.

It is equally important to understand what a binding estimate does not cover. A binding estimate guarantees the cost to transport the specific items cataloged on the written inventory cube sheet. If you add additional furniture boxes on moving day that were not on the survey, the mover has the legal right to void the binding contract, negotiate a revised binding estimate, or convert the entire shipment to a non-binding rate. Furthermore, accessorial services—such as carrying furniture up multiple flights of stairs (flight charges), walking goods over seventy-five feet from the truck (long carry fees), or hiring a smaller shuttle van for narrow streets—can be billed separately if not included in the original agreement.

How to Spot and Avoid Rogue Mover Hostage Scams

Understanding binding estimate rules is the single most effective defense against rogue mover scams. Rogue movers routinely lure unsuspecting consumers with suspiciously cheap online or telephone quotes. Once your household goods are loaded into their moving van on moving day, these fraudulent operators claim your shipment weighed double the initial estimate and demand cash ransoms of three to four thousand dollars, refusing to deliver your possessions until the inflated balance is paid—a federal crime known as holding household goods hostage.

Legitimate, federally licensed interstate movers hold active USDOT and Motor Carrier (MC) numbers verifiable on the FMCSA Protect Your Move registry (protectyourmove.gov). When securing a binding estimate, insist on a Binding Not-to-Exceed estimate. This superior contract ensures that if your household shipment weighs less than estimated, you pay only for the actual lighter weight, but if the shipment weighs more, your cost is capped at the agreed binding estimate ceiling.

How to Obtain and Verify a Binding Moving Estimate

Follow this practical regulatory guide to schedule visual surveys, verify federal licensing, and secure a binding price contract for your move.

  1. Verify Mover USDOT and MC Numbers on FMCSA Database

    Check the mover's licensing, safety record, and commercial surety protection coverage on the federal FMCSA Protect Your Move website (protectyourmove.gov) before requesting an estimate.

  2. Schedule a Mandatory In-Home or Virtual Visual Survey

    Require the mover to conduct an in-person walkthrough or live video survey of every closet, basement, and storage shed to catalog an accurate cube sheet inventory.

  3. Inquire About Any Binding Estimate Administrative Fees

    Ask upfront whether the mover charges an estimate preparation fee, ensuring that any charge is disclosed in writing prior to the estimator's visual survey.

  4. Request a 'Binding Not-to-Exceed' Contract Form

    Request a Binding Not-to-Exceed estimate rather than a standard binding quote, guaranteeing that your price drops if actual shipment weight is less than estimated.

  5. Review Inventory Cube Sheets and Accessorial Service Terms

    Review the finalized inventory sheet to confirm all furniture items are listed and ensure potential stair, long carry, and shuttle charges are clearly specified.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can a moving company legally charge for a binding estimate?

Yes. Under FMCSA regulation 49 CFR Section 375.401(c), interstate moving companies may charge a fee for providing a binding estimate, provided the fee is disclosed in writing in advance.

Q2: What is the difference between a binding and non-binding estimate?

A binding estimate guarantees the exact total price for moving inventoried goods, while a non-binding estimate is an approximate quote where the final cost is calculated based on the actual weight of the truck.

Q3: What is a binding not-to-exceed estimate?

A binding not-to-exceed estimate caps the maximum price you will pay, but if your shipment weighs less than estimated, you are billed the lower price based on actual weight.

Q4: Can a mover raise the price on a binding estimate on moving day?

A mover cannot raise the price on items listed on the binding estimate. However, if you add additional items not on the original inventory, the mover can revise the estimate or charge extra.

Q5: What is the 110% rule in moving estimates?

Under FMCSA rules for non-binding estimates, a mover cannot demand more than 110% of the estimated price at delivery; any remaining balance must be billed thirty days later.

Q6: Is an in-home visual survey legally required for a binding estimate?

Yes. Federal law mandates that interstate movers located within fifty miles must perform an in-person visual survey unless the consumer waives this right in writing for a virtual video survey.

Q7: What are accessorial charges on a moving invoice?

Accessorial charges are extra fees for specialized labor or physical obstacles, such as carrying goods up multiple flights of stairs, carrying items over 75 feet, or shuttle truck transfers.

Q8: How do I know if a moving company is legitimate?

Verify that the mover has an active USDOT number, physical company address, valid motor carrier authority, and positive reviews on the FMCSA Protect Your Move government registry.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

A binding estimate fee for moving is an allowable administrative charge that secures a federally protected, guaranteed price contract for your household relocation. Under FMCSA regulations, binding quotes eliminate moving day pricing surprises by holding the carrier to the exact contracted rate for all inventoried items. By insisting on a mandatory in-home or virtual visual survey, verifying that accessorial services like stairs and long carries are accounted for, and choosing a Binding Not-to-Exceed estimate, you ensure a transparent, stress-free moving experience.
