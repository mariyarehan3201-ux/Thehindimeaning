If You Refuse a Breathalyzer During a Traffic Stop?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Finding yourself pulled over by law enforcement during a late-night traffic stop and being asked to blow into a handheld roadside breathalyzer is one of the most critical legal confrontations a motorist can face. Many drivers wonder whether refusing the device at the vehicle window protects them from an arrest or guarantees an immediate trip to county jail. Navigating a roadside encounter requires understanding the vital distinction between non-mandatory roadside preliminary screening tests and mandatory post-arrest chemical tests, how bodycam footage records your demeanor, and the practical consequences of vehicle towing.

The Roadside Window Confrontation: PBT Devices vs. Fourth Amendment Rights

When an officer suspects driver impairment following a traffic stop, they will typically retrieve a small, battery-operated handheld gadget known as a Preliminary Alcohol Screening (PAS) device or Preliminary Breath Tester (PBT). The officer will hold the plastic tube near your window and request that you blow into it to 'see if you are safe to drive home.'

In the vast majority of jurisdictions, this roadside screening test is completely voluntary for non-commercial drivers aged twenty-one and older. Portable breath testers rely on electrochemical fuel cell sensors that are highly susceptible to false positives from mouth alcohol (such as recent mouthwash, breath sprays, or acid reflux), ambient temperature fluctuations, and electromagnetic radio interference from police cruisers. Because the Supreme Court has recognized that physical breath sampling constitutes a search under the Fourth Amendment, you generally have the constitutional right to decline the roadside PBT without triggering statutory implied consent license suspensions, unless you are on probation or under legal drinking age.

Body-Worn Camera Dynamics and Officer Articulation

If you choose to refuse a roadside breathalyzer, your physical behavior, tone of voice, and body language during the refusal become the state's primary evidence against you. Modern patrol officers wear high-definition body cameras with continuous audio recording that capture every second of the roadside interaction.

If a driver acts combative, slurs words while shouting, uses profanity, or stumbles when stepping out of the vehicle, the prosecutor will play the body camera footage in court to convince a jury that the driver was visibly intoxicated. Conversely, a driver who remains calm, speaks with crisp diction, politely states: 'Officer, I respectfully decline to take any voluntary roadside preliminary tests on the advice of legal counsel,' and complies with lawful orders presents an impressive, composed image on video that severely undermines the officer's claim of visible impairment.

Vehicle Towing, Impoundment, and Inventory Searches

An immediate practical consequence of refusing a breath test and being arrested at the roadside is the loss of your vehicle. Under the 'Community Caretaking' doctrine, police officers cannot leave an unattended vehicle parked on the shoulder of a dark highway or active traffic lane. The officer will summon a commercial contract tow truck to impound the vehicle.

Before the tow truck arrives, officers execute an 'administrative inventory search' of your vehicle's cabin and trunk, ostensibly to catalogue valuables and protect against theft claims. Any open containers of alcohol, controlled prescription drugs, or illegal contraband discovered during this inventory search will be seized and added as separate criminal charges. If a sober passenger is present in the car, you can formally request that the officer release the vehicle to that passenger, saving hundreds of dollars in towing and storage fees.

How to Handle a Roadside Breathalyzer Request

Follow this step-by-step roadmap to protect your constitutional rights, handle roadside questioning, and manage a DUI arrest safely.

  1. Provide Mandatory Identification and Documents

    Hand over your valid driver's license, vehicle registration, and proof of insurance promptly without fumbling or arguing with the officer.

  2. Politely Decline Voluntary Roadside Balance Tests

    When asked to perform one-leg stands or follow a pen light, politely state that you respectfully decline all voluntary roadside balance tests.

  3. Decline the Handheld Roadside PBT Breath Device

    Refuse the preliminary handheld breath test at the window, recognizing it is an uncalibrated field screening tool designed to justify arrest.

  4. Maintain Absolute Civility on Police Bodycam Video

    Keep your voice calm, courteous, and composed, ensuring body camera audio reflects clear, unslurred, professional communication.

  5. Invoke Right to Counsel Upon Formal Station Arrival

    Once transported to the booking facility, immediately invoke your Sixth Amendment right to consult with a criminal defense attorney.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is the handheld breathalyzer at the car window mandatory?

No. In almost all states, the roadside portable breath test (PBT) is completely voluntary for adult, non-commercial drivers. Refusing it carries no driver's license suspension penalty.

Q2: Will the officer let me go if I refuse the roadside breathalyzer?

Generally no. If the officer smells alcohol or observed erratic driving, refusing the roadside test will simply cause the officer to arrest you based on their sensory observations and transport you to the station.

Q3: Can police search my car if I refuse a breathalyzer?

If you are arrested, police are legally permitted to conduct an administrative inventory search of your vehicle before it is towed to an impound lot, searching the glove box, cabin, and trunk.

Q4: What is the difference between a PBT and an Intoxilyzer 9000?

A PBT is an uncalibrated handheld fuel-cell device used on the roadside for screening. An Intoxilyzer 9000 is an infrared spectroscopy bench-top machine at the police station used as official court evidence.

Q5: Can I call my lawyer before deciding on a breathalyzer?

In many states, drivers have a statutory right to a reasonable opportunity (typically 15 to 30 minutes) to consult with an attorney by phone before submitting to the official station chemical test.

Q6: What happens to my car when I am arrested for DUI?

Your car will be hooked up by a commercial tow truck and taken to a private impound yard, accruing daily storage fees until you or an authorized representative can pay the fees and retrieve it.

Q7: Does mouthwash cause a false positive on a breathalyzer?

Yes. Many mouthwashes contain up to 20% ethanol. Swishing mouthwash within 15 minutes of a breath test coats mouth tissue in concentrated alcohol vapors, producing falsely elevated BAC readings.

Q8: Should I ever blow into a breathalyzer if I had drinks?

This is a complex legal choice. Submitting provides scientific evidence for prosecutors if your BAC is over 0.08%, while refusing triggers an automatic administrative license suspension of 6 to 12 months under Implied Consent.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Refusing a breathalyzer during a roadside traffic stop is your constitutional right in most states regarding the preliminary handheld PBT. However, understanding that declining a roadside test does not prevent an arrest if the officer claims they smell alcohol or observed erratic driving is vital. Always remain calm and polite, never consent to voluntary physical balance tests, exercise your right to remain silent, and immediately ask to speak with an attorney once taken to the police station for official chemical testing.
