How Much Is a Casket at a Funeral Home?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

When arranging funeral services for a loved one, the casket is often the single most expensive merchandise item on the final bill. If you are asking how much is a casket at a funeral home, typical retail showroom prices range between $2,000 and $5,500 for standard steel or wood models, with luxury bronze, copper, or mahogany options exceeding $10,000 to $25,000. Understanding how funeral homes price their merchandise, the markup involved, and your legal rights under federal consumer protection laws can save your family thousands of dollars during a vulnerable time.

Funeral Home Casket Markups and Material Pricing Tiers

The price you see inside a funeral home showroom reflects substantial retail markup. While wholesale casket costs to funeral directors typically range from $400 to $1,500 for standard models, funeral homes routinely mark up caskets by 300 to 500 percent to cover overhead, facility maintenance, hearse fleets, and staff salaries. The entry-level price point in most funeral homes is an 18-gauge or 20-gauge carbon steel casket, which retails between $1,800 and $3,500 depending on corner styling and interior crepe or velvet lining.

Wood caskets represent a middle-to-high pricing tier, with softer woods like poplar and pine starting around $2,200 to $4,000, while fine dense hardwoods such as oak, cherry, maple, and black walnut retail between $3,800 and $8,500. At the luxury apex are semi-precious metal caskets made from non-corrosive solid copper or bronze (measured by 32-ounce or 48-ounce per square foot thickness). These elite funeral home models feature hand-polished finishes and luxury velvet interiors, routinely commanding $8,000 to $18,000+.

The FTC Funeral Rule: Your Legal Rights When Buying Caskets

Many consumers are unaware that they are under no legal obligation to purchase a casket directly from the funeral home conducting the service. Under the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) Funeral Rule, funeral homes are strictly required by federal law to provide an itemized General Price List (GPL) and a standalone Casket Price List (CPL) before showing you any floor models. This transparency allows families to review the lowest-priced options without being steered exclusively toward high-margin premium models.

Crucially, the FTC Funeral Rule prohibits funeral directors from charging a handling fee, receiving fee, or delivery surcharge if you supply a casket purchased from a third-party retailer such as Costco, Walmart, Amazon, or a direct online casket distributor (like Titan Casket or Overnight Caskets). Online caskets built to identical specifications usually sell for $800 to $1,800 with guaranteed overnight freight delivery, offering identical dignity while saving 50 to 70 percent compared to funeral home showroom pricing.

Cremation Caskets, Rental Options, and Eco-Burials

If your family has chosen cremation with a prior traditional public viewing or church visitation, purchasing an expensive burial casket is completely unnecessary. Most funeral homes offer ceremonial rental caskets for a fee of $600 to $1,400. A rental casket features an exterior shell of fine solid oak or cherry that houses a removable, temporary interior cremation container. Following the funeral service, the inner wooden or cardboard container holding the deceased is slid out for dignified cremation, saving the family several thousand dollars.

For green burials, eco-conscious consumers frequently select biodegradable caskets made from woven willow, sea grass, bamboo, or plain unvarnished pine that contain zero metal hinges or toxic chemical glues. These green caskets cost between $500 and $1,500 and are engineered to naturally decompose in conservation cemeteries. Always verify cemetery and crematory guidelines before purchasing to ensure compliance with specific dimensional and composition standards.

How to Shop for a Casket Without Overpaying

Follow these five strategic steps to navigate funeral home purchases, compare third-party vendors, and protect your budget.

  1. Request the Written Casket Price List (CPL)

    Ask the funeral director for their printed CPL before stepping into the casket selection showroom to see all pricing tiers.

  2. Compare Online Third-Party Casket Prices

    Check reputable direct online casket retailers (Titan Casket, Costco) for identical 18-gauge or wood models.

  3. Confirm Free Casket Receipt Under FTC Rules

    Remind the funeral director that federal law mandates they accept third-party casket deliveries without charging handling fees.

  4. Inquire About Ceremonial Rental Caskets

    If choosing cremation after a viewing, ask about a rental casket rather than purchasing a permanent burial unit.

  5. Verify Cemetery Outer Burial Container Rules

    Check whether your cemetery mandates a concrete vault, which protects the grave soil regardless of whether the casket is sealed.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Why are caskets so expensive at funeral homes?

Funeral homes apply a 300% to 500% markup on wholesale merchandise to offset facility overhead, transportation, and staffing costs.

Q2: Can a funeral home refuse a casket bought from Amazon or Costco?

No, under the federal FTC Funeral Rule, funeral homes are legally required to accept third-party caskets without penalty.

Q3: What is the cheapest casket available at a funeral home?

The most affordable options are 20-gauge non-gasketed steel or simple fiberboard cloth-covered caskets, usually priced around $900 to $1,600.

Q4: What does a gasketed or protective casket mean?

A gasketed casket has a rubber seal between the lid and base designed to delay the entrance of outside elements, but it does not prevent decomposition.

Q5: How much is a rental casket for a viewing before cremation?

A ceremonial rental casket typically costs between $600 and $1,400, including the removable inner cardboard cremation container.

Q6: Is a casket required for cremation?

No, state laws only require an alternative combustible container (such as a rigid cardboard or plywood box) for the cremation process.

Q7: What is the difference between 18-gauge and 20-gauge steel?

In metal measurement, lower numbers mean thicker metal; 18-gauge steel is roughly 30 percent thicker and more durable than 20-gauge steel.

Q8: Can I make my own casket?

Yes, family members can build their own wooden casket, and funeral homes must accept it without imposing any additional handling charges.
