How Much Is It to Fix a Cracked Tooth?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Discovering a crack in your tooth can trigger immediate concern, ranging from mild sensitivity when sipping hot tea to excruciating pain when biting down. The cost to repair a cracked tooth typically ranges from $150 to $3,500 depending on the depth, location, and structural severity of the fracture. Minor superficial enamel craze lines can be smoothed or bonded for $150 to $400, whereas deep fractures reaching the inner dental pulp necessitate root canal therapy and a porcelain crown costing $1,500 to $2,800, or surgical extraction and an implant reaching $3,000 to $4,500.

Fracture Severity Classifications and Restorative Procedures

Dentists evaluate cracked teeth across a spectrum of structural damage, dictating both the clinical approach and restorative price point. At the mildest stage are craze lines: superficial micro-cracks limited to the outer translucent enamel layer. These cause no pain, pose no biological danger, and are primarily treated for cosmetic appearance with micro-abrasion or composite resin bonding. In contrast, a fractured cusp occurs when a piece of a molar chewing surface chips or breaks off, frequently around an old amalgam metal filling. Fixing a fractured cusp generally requires a direct composite resin restoration ($200 to $450) or a custom laboratory porcelain onlay ($650 to $1,200).

When a crack extends vertically from the biting surface toward the gumline, it represents a true cracked tooth. If the crack has not yet breached the dental pulp chamber, placing a full-coverage crown ($900 to $1,800) binds the split tooth segments together like an architectural barrel hoop, preventing further propagation. However, if masticatory pressure has driven bacteria into the pulp, endodontic root canal therapy ($700 to $1,500) must precede the crown. Should the fissure travel below the alveolar bone crest into the root, the tooth becomes non-restorable and must be extracted.

Diagnostic Technologies and Endodontic Evaluation

Diagnosing the precise trajectory of a tooth fracture is among the most challenging tasks in general dentistry because standard two-dimensional bitewing X-rays frequently fail to reveal cracks that run parallel to the radiation beam. To pinpoint fractures, clinicians utilize specialized diagnostic tools such as fiber-optic transillumination (which shows light blocking across fracture lines), tooth slooth bite-pressure sticks to reproduce rebound pain, and methylene blue diagnostic staining dyes.

In modern dental and endodontic practices, three-dimensional Cone Beam Computed Tomography (CBCT) provides microscopic volumetric imaging of root canals and alveolar bone architecture. A CBCT diagnostic scan costs roughly $150 to $350 but is invaluable for verifying whether a vertical root fracture exists before a patient invests thousands of dollars into a root canal on a doomed root. Investing in precise diagnostics prevents costly misdirected treatment.

Insurance Reimbursement Realities, Dental Savings Plans, and Timing

Navigating dental insurance for a cracked tooth requires understanding standard policy annual maximums, which typically cap out between $1,000 and $2,000 per benefit year. Because a combined root canal and porcelain crown easily totals $2,200 or more, a single cracked tooth can exhaust your entire annual insurance allowance in one visit. Dental insurance generally classifies root canals under basic services (covered at 80 percent after deductible) and crowns under major services (covered at 50 percent), subject to waiting periods on newly established policies.

If you lack dental insurance, consider dental discount plans (such as Careington or Aetna Dental Access) which offer pre-negotiated fee schedules delivering 20 to 50 percent discounts on restorative work for an annual membership of $100 to $150. Above all, do not postpone treatment when you suspect a crack. Chewing on an untreated cracked tooth inevitably drives the fracture deeper toward the jawbone, turning an affordable $1,200 crown repair into a complex $4,000 tooth extraction and titanium implant procedure.

How to Manage and Treat a Cracked Tooth in 5 Steps

Follow these five critical steps from the moment you suspect a tooth fracture to completed clinical restoration.

  1. Cease Chewing on the Affected Side

    Stop eating hard, crunchy, or chewy foods immediately to prevent chewing forces from splitting the fracture deeper into the root.

  2. Rinse with Warm Salt Water and Manage Swelling

    Gargle with lukewarm saline solution to cleanse food debris and apply a cold compress to your cheek if facial inflammation develops.

  3. Schedule an Immediate Emergency Dental Evaluation

    Contact your dentist or an endodontic specialist promptly, describing whether pain occurs spontaneously or exclusively when releasing bite pressure.

  4. Undergo Diagnostic Testing and Transillumination

    Allow the dentist to perform pulp vitality cold tests, bite-stick evaluations, and periapical or 3D CBCT imaging to map the crack.

  5. Execute the Recommended Restorative Treatment Plan

    Proceed with composite bonding, a protective lab crown, or root canal therapy as indicated to permanently reinforce remaining tooth structure.

Frequently Asked Questions (9 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to fix a cracked tooth without insurance?

Without insurance, repair costs range from $200 for minor bonding to $1,200 to $1,800 for a crown, or $2,000 to $3,200 for a combined root canal and crown.

Q2: Can a cracked tooth heal on its own?

No, human enamel and dentin cannot regenerate or heal across a fracture line; mechanical dental intervention is required to stabilize the tooth.

Q3: What happens if you leave a cracked tooth untreated?

An untreated crack will gradually spread deeper into the root, exposing the nerve to infection, causing severe abscesses, and ultimately requiring extraction.

Q4: Does fixing a cracked tooth hurt?

No, dental procedures are performed under local anesthesia, ensuring you feel no pain during bonding, root canal therapy, or crown preparation.

Q5: What is the cheapest way to fix a cracked tooth?

Direct composite resin bonding is the most affordable solution at $150 to $400, but it is only suitable for minor, superficial enamel chips and craze lines.

Q6: Why does a cracked tooth hurt when you stop biting?

Rebound pain occurs when bite pressure temporarily closes the crack and releasing pressure allows the crack to flex open, stimulating fluid motion in the nerve.

Q7: Is a cracked tooth considered a dental emergency?

Yes, sudden fractures accompanied by sharp pain, bleeding, or jagged edges should be evaluated by an emergency dentist within 24 to 48 hours.

Q8: How long does a crown on a cracked tooth last?

A high-quality porcelain or zirconia crown placed over a stabilized cracked tooth typically lasts 10 to 15 years or longer with proper oral hygiene.

Q9: Can a cracked tooth always be saved?

No, if the crack splits the tooth in half or travels vertically down the root below the gumline, the tooth cannot be saved and must be extracted.
