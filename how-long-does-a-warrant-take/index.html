How Long Does a Warrant Take?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how long does a warrant take is a critical legal inquiry across both criminal investigations and court procedural defaults. The timeframe encompasses two distinct inquiries: how long it takes for a judge to issue a warrant (ranging from 15 minutes for an emergency electronic search warrant to several months for complex financial arrest warrants) and how long an active warrant remains valid before expiration (which, for criminal arrest and bench warrants, is permanently for life until served or recalled).

Issuance Timelines: Search Warrants vs. Arrest Warrants

The duration required to obtain a warrant depends on whether law enforcement seeks a Search Warrant or an Arrest Warrant. Under Fourth Amendment jurisprudence, both require establishing Probable Cause through a sworn Affidavit presented to a neutral magistrate or judge.

For Search Warrants, speed is often paramount. In urgent cases—such as blood draws for suspected DUI drivers who refuse testing, active hostage scenarios, or ongoing drug raids—detectives utilize modern Electronic Warrants (E-Warrants). Police upload digital affidavits via secure tablet portals to an on-call judge, who reviews the document and applies a digital signature within 15 to 45 minutes. Routine property search warrants typically take 24 to 72 hours for prosecutors to draft and judges to sign.

Arrest Warrants: Grand Juries and Multi-Month Prosecutorial Reviews

Unlike emergency search warrants, obtaining an Arrest Warrant for a complex crime can take months. In felony investigations involving financial fraud, white-collar embezzlement, sexual assault, or homicide, police detectives compile evidence over weeks before presenting a comprehensive case dossier to the District Attorney's office.

The prosecutor conducts a charging review to ensure the evidence can prove guilt beyond a reasonable doubt at trial. In many jurisdictions, felony charges require a formal Grand Jury Indictment. Coordinating grand jury testimony from witnesses, waiting for forensic DNA laboratory results, and presenting evidence can take 2 to 6 months before the grand jury votes a 'True Bill' indictment, prompting the judge to issue the arrest warrant.

Do Warrants Ever Expire? The Reality of Outstanding Warrants

A dangerous and persistent myth among citizens is that an arrest or bench warrant will expire if you successfully evade the police for a few years. While search warrants carry strict statutory execution windows (typically requiring police to search the premises within 10 to 14 days of issuance or the warrant becomes void), arrest warrants and bench warrants NEVER expire.

An active bench warrant issued for a missed traffic court date twenty years ago remains actively logged in state police computers. The moment an officer runs your driver's license during a routine traffic stop for a broken taillight or when entering through customs at an international airport, the active warrant alerts dispatch, leading to immediate arrest and jail detention.

How to Check for and Resolve an Active Warrant Quickly

Follow these five legal steps to discover, verify, and resolve an outstanding warrant.

  1. Conduct an Anonymous Warrant Check

    Have an attorney or bail bondsman check county court databases so you do not alert law enforcement directly.

  2. Verify the Specific Type of Warrant and Bond Amount

    Confirm whether it is a Failure to Appear (FTA) bench warrant or a formal criminal arrest warrant.

  3. File a Motion to Quash (For Bench Warrants)

    Have your defense lawyer file a motion to recall or quash the warrant, scheduling a voluntary walk-in court date.

  4. Pre-Arrange a Surety Bail Bond Before Surrendering

    If self-surrender at county jail is required, coordinate with a bail bondsman to post release paperwork immediately.

  5. Surrender on a Mid-Week Morning (Tuesday or Wednesday)

    Turn yourself in early in the morning so judges and clerks are on duty to handle your arraignment on the same day.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How fast can police get a search warrant?

Using modern digital E-Warrant systems, police can obtain a judge's signature on an emergency search warrant in 15 to 45 minutes.

Q2: Do arrest warrants ever expire if you don't get caught?

No, criminal arrest warrants and court bench warrants never expire; they remain permanently active in law enforcement registries until served.

Q3: How long does it take for a bench warrant to be issued?

A bench warrant is issued instantly in open court the moment a defendant fails to appear (FTA) for a scheduled hearing.

Q4: How long does a search warrant stay valid before expiring?

Under state and federal rules, search warrants must be executed within 10 to 14 days of issuance; otherwise, they expire and become void.

Q5: Can a lawyer get a warrant removed without going to jail?

Yes, for non-violent misdemeanor bench warrants, defense lawyers can often file a motion to quash and appear with you in court without jail booking.

Q6: How long does a felony arrest warrant take to process?

Felony warrants requiring detective investigations and grand jury indictments typically take 1 to 6 months to be approved and issued.

Q7: Will a warrant from another state show up on a traffic stop?

Yes, all active warrants are entered into the FBI's National Crime Information Center (NCIC) system, accessible to all US police officers.

Q8: What is an electronic warrant (E-Warrant)?

A system where officers submit sworn digital affidavits via mobile tablets to on-call judges who sign warrants electronically within minutes.
