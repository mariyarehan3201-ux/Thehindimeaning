How Long to Get CDL Class A?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how long to get cdl class a reveals that obtaining your commercial driver's license typically takes between 3 to 7 weeks of full-time trade school training, or 8 to 12 weeks through a part-time community college program. Under federal Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT) mandates established by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA), all applicants must complete an approved theoretical and behind-the-wheel curriculum before taking the state skills test. From your first permit study session to holding an unrestricted Class A CDL in hand, expect a total timeline of 1 to 3 months.

The Federal ELDT Framework and Classroom Hours (Weeks 1–2)

Since federal ELDT regulations took effect in February 2022, prospective commercial truck drivers can no longer simply study on their own and borrow a friend's semi-truck to take the state road test. Every candidate must enroll with an entity listed on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry (TPR). Full-time programs typically deliver 40 to 60 hours of classroom theory during the first 1 to 2 weeks.

The classroom curriculum covers federal safety regulations, Department of Transportation (DOT) hours-of-service (HOS) logbooks, electronic logging devices (ELDs), cargo securement standards, weight distribution across tandem axles, skid avoidance, and extreme weather driving techniques. Concurrently, students take their state written knowledge tests to obtain their Commercial Learner's Permit (CLP).

Yard Skills and Behind-the-Wheel Road Training (Weeks 3–4)

Once students earn their CLP and clear the mandatory 14-day federal waiting period, training shifts outdoors to the practice yard and public roadways. Students spend 100 to 120 hours developing practical vehicle control skills behind the wheel of an 80,000-pound combination vehicle.

The yard phase is notoriously demanding: students spend dozens of hours mastering straight-line backing, offset backing, 90-degree alley docking, and coupling tractor-trailers. Concurrently, instructors train students to memorize the comprehensive 100-point pre-trip inspection walkaround. On public roads, students log hours navigating highway merges, executing wide right-hand turns, managing blind spots, and downshifting manual transmissions (or training on modern automated manuals).

State Testing, Endorsements, and Carrier Finishing

Upon completing ELDT certification, your school uploads your graduation record to the FMCSA registry, allowing you to schedule your state skills test. Passing the 3-part exam earns you an official Class A CDL. Adding lucrative commercial endorsements—such as Tanker (N), Doubles/Triples (T), or Hazardous Materials (H)—takes an additional 1 to 3 weeks of written test prep and TSA background fingerprinting.

Finally, new CDL holders entering the commercial freight industry undergo carrier finishing training (driver onboarding) lasting 3 to 6 weeks. New drivers are paired with an experienced driver-trainer, logging 10,000 to 20,000 live interstate miles before being dispatched as independent solo over-the-road (OTR), regional, or local drivers.

How to Earn Your Class A CDL

Follow these five chronological milestones to transition into a professional Class A truck driving career.

  1. Pass the DOT Physical and Obtain Medical Examiner's Certificate

    Visit an FMCSA-certified medical examiner to verify blood pressure, vision, and physical fitness benchmarks.

  2. Pass the Written CLP Knowledge Exams at the DMV

    Study the commercial driver manual and pass tests for General Knowledge, Air Brakes, and Combination Vehicles.

  3. Complete an FMCSA-Registered ELDT Truck School Program

    Attend an accredited 160-hour commercial driving school covering theory, pre-trip inspections, and yard maneuvers.

  4. Pass the Three-Part State CDL Skills Examination

    Successfully demonstrate the vehicle inspection walkaround, alley-dock backing maneuvers, and real-world road driving.

  5. Complete Carrier Onboarding and Finishing Training

    Join a motor carrier, spend 3 to 6 weeks on the road with an experienced trainer, and graduate to solo driving.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can you get a Class A CDL in 3 weeks?

Yes, full-time private driving academies offer intensive 160-hour programs that can be completed in 3 to 4 weeks.

Q2: What is the 14-day rule for a CDL permit?

Federal law mandates that you must hold your Commercial Learner's Permit (CLP) for at least 14 days before taking the road skills test.

Q3: How much does Class A CDL school cost?

Private CDL schools typically cost between $3,500 and $7,000, while carrier-paid programs offer free tuition in exchange for employment contracts.

Q4: What is the hardest part of the CDL test?

Most students find memorizing the extensive vehicle pre-trip inspection and executing the 90-degree alley dock backing maneuver to be the hardest.

Q5: Can you get a CDL with an automatic transmission restriction?

Yes, testing in an automatic truck places an 'E' restriction on your license barring you from operating commercial manual transmissions.

Q6: How old do you have to be to get a Class A CDL?

You must be 18 to drive intrastate (within state lines) and 21 to haul freight across state lines (interstate).

Q7: How long does it take to get a HazMat endorsement?

Obtaining a HazMat (H) endorsement takes 4 to 8 weeks due to the required TSA background check and fingerprint vetting.

Q8: What is the FMCSA ELDT mandate?

Enacted in 2022, ELDT mandates that all new CDL drivers must complete certified training through an approved provider before testing.
