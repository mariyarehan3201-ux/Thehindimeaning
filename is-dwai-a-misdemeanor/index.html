Is DWAI a Misdemeanor?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Whether Driving While Ability Impaired (DWAI) is a misdemeanor depends entirely on the specific state jurisdiction in which the charge is filed. In the State of New York, a standard first-offense DWAI (alcohol) under VTL § 1192.1 is not a misdemeanor; it is legally classified as a non-criminal traffic infraction. However, in the State of Colorado, a first-offense DWAI under C.R.S. § 42-4-1301 is legally classified as an unclassified criminal misdemeanor that creates a permanent criminal record, carries potential jail time, and adds eight points to your driver's license.

The Legal Distinction: DWAI vs. DWI Across State Jurisdictions

In American motor vehicle law, Driving While Intoxicated (DWI) and Driving While Ability Impaired (DWAI) represent different tiers of physical and mental impairment. While standard DWI charges typically require proving that a motorist had a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.08% or higher, DWAI laws are designed to prosecute drivers who exhibit measurable impairment at lower chemical concentrations, commonly between 0.05% and 0.07% BAC.

Only a select group of states utilize the specific acronym 'DWAI,' most notably New York and Colorado. Despite sharing identical nomenclature, the statutory gravity of the charge could not be more divergent between these two states. Understanding whether your DWAI charge will result in a permanent criminal record requires examining the legislative framework of the prosecuting state.

The comparative jurisdictional table below contrasts the legal classification, chemical thresholds, and record permanence of a first-offense DWAI between New York and Colorado.

New York (VTL § 1192.4 - DWAI Drugs) Unclassified Misdemeanor Any illicit / impairing drug Yes (Permanent criminal record) Up to 1 Year in county jail

New York State: When DWAI is a Violation vs. a Criminal Misdemeanor

In New York, the distinction between a traffic violation and a criminal conviction is crucial. Under New York Vehicle and Traffic Law (VTL) § 1192.1, Driving While Ability Impaired by Alcohol is explicitly designated as a traffic infraction. This means that a person convicted of a first-offense DWAI alcohol does not have a criminal conviction, does not have to report a criminal history on standard employment job applications, and does not have fingerprints retained on state FBI criminal databases.

However, New York enforces critical exceptions. If a motorist is charged with DWAI-Drugs (VTL § 1192.4) or DWAI-Combined Influence of Drugs and Alcohol (VTL § 1192.4-a), the offense is immediately classified as a full unclassified criminal misdemeanor. Furthermore, if a driver is convicted of two DWAI offenses within five years, the second offense elevates to an unclassified misdemeanor with mandatory minimum fines and increased license revocations.

Colorado State: DWAI as an Unclassified Criminal Misdemeanor

Unlike New York's lenient traffic infraction classification, Colorado takes a much stricter approach. Under Colorado Revised Statutes (C.R.S.) § 42-4-1301, a driver is guilty of DWAI if alcohol or drugs affected them 'to the slightest degree' such that they were less able than ordinarily to exercise clear judgment.

In Colorado, even a first-offense DWAI is legally an unclassified criminal misdemeanor. A conviction results in a public criminal record that appears on employer background checks, mandates between 2 and 180 days in county jail (which may sometimes be suspended upon completion of community service), imposes 24 to 48 hours of useful public service, adds 8 points to the driver's license, and mandates completion of Level II alcohol education courses.

How to Address and Resolve a DWAI Charge

Step-by-step guidance on understanding legal implications and mitigating penalties following a DWAI citation.

  1. Identify the Exact State Statute on the Citation

    Examine the traffic ticket or criminal complaint. Check whether the charge is filed under New York VTL § 1192.1 (traffic infraction) or Colorado C.R.S. § 42-4-1301 (criminal misdemeanor).

  2. Retain a Dedicated Impaired Driving Defense Attorney

    Hire an attorney experienced in your specific court jurisdiction. In New York, defense counsel frequently negotiates misdemeanor DWI charges down to non-criminal DWAI infractions.

  3. Challenge Blood Alcohol Concentration and Field Tests

    Request chemical test calibration logs and police dashcam footage to challenge whether the officer possessed reasonable suspicion for the stop and whether BAC was accurately measured between 0.05% and 0.07%.

  4. Enroll in Alcohol Screening and Assessment Programs

    Proactively complete an evaluation by an OASAS-certified provider (in New York) or state-approved behavioral health facility (in Colorado) to show positive rehabilitation steps to the prosecutor.

  5. Comply with Driver License Restoration Protocols

    Complete all state DMV suspension periods, pay administrative license restoration fees, and apply for a conditional or restricted driver's permit if eligible for work transportation.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is DWAI a criminal record in New York?

No. A first-offense DWAI alcohol in New York is a traffic infraction, not a crime. It will appear on your driving record, but not on a criminal background check.

Q2: Is DWAI a criminal record in Colorado?

Yes. In Colorado, DWAI is an unclassified criminal misdemeanor that creates a permanent criminal record on state and FBI databases.

Q3: What BAC level triggers a DWAI?

A DWAI is typically charged when a driver's Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) is between 0.05% and 0.07%, indicating noticeable impairment below the 0.08% standard DWI threshold.

Q4: Is DWAI-Drugs a misdemeanor in New York?

Yes. While alcohol DWAI is a traffic violation, DWAI involving drugs (VTL § 1192.4) is classified as an unclassified criminal misdemeanor.

Q5: Can you go to jail for a DWAI?

Yes. While rare for first offenders, New York allows up to 15 days in jail, and Colorado statutes mandate 2 to 180 days in county jail.

Q6: Does DWAI suspend your driver's license?

Yes. In New York, a first DWAI carries a mandatory 90-day license suspension. In Colorado, it adds 8 points to your DMV driving record.

Q7: Can a DWI be reduced to a DWAI in court?

Yes. In New York, prosecutors frequently offer a DWAI plea bargain to first-time DWI offenders with borderline BAC levels, allowing them to avoid a criminal record.

Q8: Will a DWAI show up on an employment background check?

In Colorado, yes, because it is a misdemeanor crime. In New York, it will not appear on criminal history checks, but will appear if the employer pulls your DMV driving record.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

In summary, whether a DWAI is a misdemeanor depends entirely on the state in which the offense occurred and the impairing substance involved. In New York, a first-offense alcohol DWAI is a non-criminal traffic violation, offering an advantageous plea-bargain resolution that keeps a defendant's record free of criminal convictions. In Colorado, however, any DWAI is an unclassified criminal misdemeanor that creates a permanent criminal record. Understanding your state's specific statutory penal codes is essential when mounting a legal defense.
