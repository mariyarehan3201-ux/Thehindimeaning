What Does Mitsubishi Mean? Japanese Logo History

Meaning
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 17, 2026

The name Mitsubishi translates directly from Japanese into English as Three Diamonds (or Three Rhombuses / Water Chestnuts). The name is a linguistic compound formed by combining the Japanese word mitsu (meaning three) and hishi (a term denoting a diamond or rhombus shape, originally named after the rhombic seed pod of the aquatic water chestnut plant). Under the rules of Japanese phonology known as rendaku (sequential voicing), when the unvoiced 'h' consonant in hishi follows an earlier syllable, it softens into a voiced 'b' sound, transforming mitsu-hishi into the world-renowned brand name Mitsubishi.

Understanding the name Mitsubishi unlocks over one hundred fifty years of Japanese industrial, samurai, and corporate history. Founded in 1870 by Yataro Iwasaki during the Meiji Restoration, the company iconic three-diamond corporate emblem was created by merging two historic samurai clan crests: the three-tiered water chestnuts of the Iwasaki family crest and the three-leaf oak crest of the Tosa samurai clan. Today, the three red diamonds stand as an internationally recognized symbol of engineering excellence, trust, and industrial innovation.

The birth of Mitsubishi coincided with one of the most transformative epochs in Asian history: the Meiji Restoration of 1868. As Japan dismantled its feudal shogunate system and opened its borders to global commerce, traditional samurai warrior classes found themselves needing to adapt to modern industrial enterprise. Yataro Iwasaki, a fiercely ambitious administrative samurai from the Tosa domain (modern-day Kochi Prefecture on Shikoku Island), seized this moment to launch a private maritime shipping venture named Tsukumo Shokai in 1870.

To create a lasting corporate emblem for his fleet of steamships, Iwasaki looked to ancestral heraldry. Japanese aristocratic families had used pictorial crests called mon for centuries to identify samurai banners on battlefields. When Iwasaki established the three-diamond trademark, he also laid down a business philosophy known as the Sankoryo (The Three Principles). Codified by the fourth Mitsubishi president, Koyata Iwasaki, these principles continue to guide hundreds of autonomous businesses bearing the Mitsubishi name worldwide. The principles mandate corporate responsibility to society, absolute integrity and fairness in business conduct, and international trade expansion that fosters mutual global prosperity.

Today, the Mitsubishi Group does not operate as a single monolithic corporation with a central holding company. Following the dissolution of the historic zaibatsu conglomerates after World War II, Mitsubishi re-emerged as an alliance of independent corporations sharing a common heritage, trademark, and core values. More than a century and a half after its founding, the three red diamonds remain an enduring tribute to samurai heritage, craftsmanship, and forward-thinking innovation.

How to Trace the Etymology and Visual Heritage of Mitsubishi

  1. Break Down the Japanese Linguistic Roots

    Study the words 'mitsu' (three) and 'hishi' (water chestnut/rhombus), noting the rendaku consonant shift that produces the pronunciation 'bishi'.

  2. Examine Founder Yataro Iwasaki Samurai Lineage

    Research the Iwasaki family mon (heraldic crest), which featured three stacked rhombuses arranged vertically in a triangular hierarchy.

  3. Analyze the Yamauchi Samurai Clan Mon

    Explore the crest of the Yamauchi family, feudal lords of the Tosa domain, which featured three oak leaves radiating symmetrically from a central hub.

  4. Observe the Harmonized Modern Logo Geometry

    See how Iwasaki fused the three rhombuses into a radial, three-pointed arrangement that embodies both historical family crests.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the direct English translation of Mitsubishi?

Mitsubishi translates directly to 'Three Diamonds' or 'Three Rhombuses' from the Japanese words mitsu (three) and hishi (diamond/water chestnut).

Q2: Why is it pronounced Mitsubishi instead of Mitsuhishi?

In Japanese linguistics, the phonetic process called rendaku softens the 'h' sound in hishi into a 'b' sound when combined with mitsu.

Q3: Who founded Mitsubishi?

Mitsubishi was founded in October 1870 by Yataro Iwasaki, a visionary Japanese entrepreneur from the samurai-class Tosa clan.

Q4: What does the red color of the Mitsubishi logo symbolize?

The vibrant red color symbolizes passion, integrity, dynamism, and industrial strength, chosen to stand out boldly on ships and machinery.

Q5: What two samurai crests inspired the Mitsubishi logo?

The logo combines the Iwasaki family crest (three stacked rhombuses) with the Yamauchi clan crest (three radiating oak leaves).

Q6: Is Mitsubishi one single company today?

No, Mitsubishi is a keiretsu—a loose alliance of independent public corporations including Mitsubishi Motors, Heavy Industries, and MUFG Bank.

Q7: What did Mitsubishi originally produce when it was founded?

Mitsubishi began as a maritime steamship shipping enterprise, known originally as Tsukumo Shokai, transporting cargo across East Asia.

Q8: What are the core Three Principles of Mitsubishi management?

The principles are Shoki Hoko (Corporate Social Responsibility), Shoji Komei (Integrity & Fairness), and Ritsugyo Boeki (Global Understanding).

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Mitsubishi is a Japanese name meaning Three Diamonds, born from the linguistic fusion of mitsu (three) and hishi (rhombus/water chestnut). Created in 1870 by founder Yataro Iwasaki through the merger of his family samurai crest and the Tosa clan oak leaves, the iconic three red diamonds represent over a century and a half of engineering prowess, integrity, and global industrial innovation. Understanding its roots reveals the fascinating intersection of feudal samurai heritage and modern technology.
