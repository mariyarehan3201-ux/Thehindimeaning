Onegai Shimasu Means

Meaning
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

In the Japanese language, social etiquette, traditional martial arts (Budo), and corporate business culture, Onegai Shimasu (お願いします) is one of the most vital, nuanced, and culturally indispensable expressions. While frequently translated in English dictionaries simply as 'please', its authentic sociolinguistic meaning extends far deeper into concepts of mutual trust, humble petition, cooperative respect, and psychological alignment. Translating literally to 'I make a humble request of you' or 'I place myself in your care', Onegai Shimasu initiates collaborative endeavors, establishes respectful partnerships, and acknowledges mutual interdependence.

Linguistic Anatomy and Politeness Registers

To understand Onegai Shimasu, one must examine the architecture of Japanese honorific language (Keigo). The word is composed of the honorific prefix 'O' (お), the noun 'negai' (願い, meaning wish, prayer, or heartfelt request), and the polite humble verb 'shimasu' (します, meaning to do or make). In uttering the phrase, the speaker intentionally lowers their own social posture while elevating the listener, creating an atmosphere of gentle, courteous humility.

The expression changes formality depending on the social hierarchy. In casual peer settings, friends might say 'onegai' (お願い). In standard polite social discourse—such as ordering at a restaurant, asking a coworker for assistance, or boarding a taxi—'onegai shimasu' is standard. In high-stakes corporate diplomacy and client relations, the hyper-polite honorific form 'Yoroshiku onegai itashimasu' (よろしくお願いいたします) is universally employed to establish lifelong professional rapport.

The table below details variations of Onegai Shimasu and their social registers.

Onegai (お願い) Casual / Informal Please! / Do me a favor Close friends, siblings, informal peer requests
Onegai shimasu (お願いします) Polite (Teineigo) Please / I make this request of you Restaurants, train stations, coworkers, martial arts dojos
Yoroshiku onegai shimasu (よろしくお願いします) Polite / Cooperative Please treat me favorably / Let's work well together Introducing yourself, starting a shared team project
Yoroshiku onegai itashimasu (よろしくお願いいたします) Hyper-Polite / Business Keigo I humbly place myself in your honorable care Formal business emails, client proposals, job interviews

The Sacred Ritual of the Martial Arts Dojo

Nowhere is the profound spiritual weight of Onegai Shimasu more visible than inside traditional Japanese martial arts dojos—including Karate, Judo, Aikido, and Kendo. At the beginning of every training session, practitioners kneel in formal seiza posture and bow deeply to the sensei and to each other, proclaiming 'Onegai shimasu!' loudly in unison.

In martial arts, the phrase is not a casual polite pleasantry; it is a solemn contract of mutual protection and trust. Combat training involves dangerous joint locks, throws, and strikes. By bowing and saying Onegai Shimasu before sparring, martial artists declare: 'I trust you with my physical body. Please teach me, push my limits, and protect me from injury as we train together.' The practice ends with 'Domo arigato gozaimashita' (thank you very much) to close the circle of gratitude.

Mastering Onegai Shimasu is the master key to cultural acceptance in Japan, signaling to native speakers that you respect their timeless social values of humility, cooperation, and reciprocal care.

How to Use 'Onegai Shimasu' Correctly in Daily Life

Follow these cultural steps to pronounce and apply this essential phrase with natural confidence.

  1. Step 1

    Pronounce the vowels crisply: oh-neh-gah-ee shee-mahs (the final 'u' in shimasu is whispered softly and unvoiced).

  2. Step 2

    Pair the phrase with a courteous physical bow: bow your head and upper torso 15 to 30 degrees while speaking.

  3. Step 3

    Use it when handing documents or payment to a clerk: say 'Onegai shimasu' as you place items on the money tray.

  4. Step 4

    When introducing yourself to new colleagues, say: 'Hajimemashite. [Your Name] desu. Yoroshiku onegai shimasu.'

  5. Step 5

    Bow and state 'Onegai shimasu' firmly when entering the training mat in any Japanese martial arts dojo.

  6. Step 6

    Use 'Kore o onegai shimasu' (This one, please) when ordering food from illustrated Japanese restaurant menus.

  7. Step 7

    Observe native responses: notice how this simple humble phrase instantly elicits warm smiles and generous service.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does Onegai Shimasu mean in Japanese?

It means 'please', 'I make a humble request of you', or 'I place myself in your care', used to request assistance or establish a cooperative relationship.

Q2: What is the difference between Kudasai and Onegai Shimasu?

Kudasai is used when asking for a physical object (e.g., water please); Onegai Shimasu is more polite and used for actions, services, and relationships.

Q3: Why do martial artists say Onegai Shimasu before sparring?

It is a mutual pledge of respect and safety, asking the partner to train earnestly while protecting each other from harm.

Q4: What does 'Yoroshiku onegai shimasu' mean?

It translates to 'Please treat me favorably' or 'I look forward to working with you', the universal greeting when meeting people or starting projects.

Q5: How do you pronounce Onegai Shimasu?

It is pronounced 'oh-neh-gah-ee shee-mahs', with the final 'u' almost silent.

Q6: What do you say in response to Onegai Shimasu?

In mutual situations (like martial arts or new partnerships), you reply with the exact same phrase: 'Kochira koso, onegai shimasu' (Likewise, please).

Q7: Can you just say 'Onegai'?

Yes, but strictly with close friends, family members, or younger peers; using just 'onegai' with teachers or bosses is considered impolite.
