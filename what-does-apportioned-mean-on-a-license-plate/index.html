What Does Apportioned Mean on a License Plate?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Spotting an eighteen-wheeler on the interstate often reveals a license plate stamped with the word APPORTIONED across the bottom. Understanding what does apportioned mean on a license plate demystifies the International Registration Plan (IRP), commercial freight tax distribution, and interstate carrier regulations.

The International Registration Plan (IRP) Explained

When a license plate bears the word Apportioned, it indicates that the commercial motor vehicle is registered under the International Registration Plan (IRP). The IRP is a reciprocal reciprocity agreement between the lower 48 contiguous United States and ten Canadian provinces.

Before the IRP was established in 1973, an interstate trucking company had to purchase separate commercial license plates and registration permits for every individual state or province its trucks traveled through. This created a paperwork nightmare, with semi-truck front bumpers weighed down by dozens of metal plates. The IRP streamlined this into a single apportioned plate issued by the carrier base jurisdiction, with registration fees divided proportionately among all states based on the percentage of total miles traveled in each territory.

How Apportioned Fees Are Calculated and The Cab Card

The core principle of an apportioned plate is mathematical fairness. A trucking company based in Texas whose trucks run 40 percent of their annual miles in Texas, 30 percent in Oklahoma, 20 percent in Missouri, and 10 percent in Illinois pays a combined registration fee apportioned across those four state revenue departments.

Along with the physical apportioned metal plate, the driver carries an essential official paper document known as the Apportioned Cab Card. The cab card lists the vehicle VIN, carrier US DOT number, gross registered vehicle weight, and an itemized breakdown of every state and Canadian province the vehicle is authorized to operate in, along with specific registered weight allowances.

Operating under apportioned registration ensures motor carriers contribute fairly to interstate highway infrastructure while moving commerce across the nation.

How Motor Carriers Obtain and Maintain Apportioned Plates

Commercial fleet owners follow this regulatory roadmap to secure and renew IRP credentials.

  1. Establish a Valid Base Jurisdiction

    Prove an established physical place of business in your home state, complete with a physical street address and telephone service.

  2. Acquire Federal Operating Authority and USDOT Number

    Ensure your Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) authority (MC number) is active with required commercial liability insurance.

  3. Compile Accurate Annual Mileage Records

    Extract detailed mileage reports by state using your fleet Electronic Logging Device (ELD) or GPS fleet management software.

  4. Submit IRP Schedule A and Schedule B Forms

    File vehicle equipment lists and state mileage percentages with your state commercial vehicle licensing agency.

  5. Display Plate and Store Cab Card in Tractor Cab

    Mount the metal plate on the front bumper (for truck tractors) or rear (for straight trucks), keeping the laminated cab card inside the vehicle at all times.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does an apportioned license plate mean?

It means the commercial vehicle is registered under the International Registration Plan (IRP) to legally haul freight across state lines with fees shared among states.

Q2: Who is required to have an apportioned plate?

Commercial motor vehicles traveling across state lines that have a gross weight over 26,000 lbs or three or more axles must have apportioned plates.

Q3: What is an apportioned cab card?

A cab card is the legal registration document carried inside the truck listing all states and provinces where the vehicle is authorized to operate.

Q4: Why do semi-trucks have their license plate on the front bumper?

Tractors pull trailers that obscure the rear of the truck; mounting the apportioned plate on the front bumper ensures it is visible to oncoming police and weigh stations.

Q5: Can an apportioned truck haul freight in Canada?

Yes. The IRP agreement includes all ten Canadian provinces, allowing apportioned US trucks to operate internationally.

Q6: What happens if a commercial truck crosses state lines without apportioned plates?

The carrier can be cited by DOT, forced to purchase an expensive temporary trip permit on the spot, or have the truck placed out of service.

Q7: How are apportioned registration fees calculated?

Fees are calculated mathematically based on the percentage of total fleet miles traveled in each state multiplied by that state registration tax rate.

Q8: Does the trailer need an apportioned plate?

No. Only the power unit (the truck tractor) carries the apportioned plate; cargo trailers carry standard or permanent commercial trailer plates.
