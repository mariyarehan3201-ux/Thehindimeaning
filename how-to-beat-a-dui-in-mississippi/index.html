How to Beat a DUI in Mississippi?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Knowing how to beat a DUI in Mississippi requires an intricate understanding of the Mississippi Implied Consent Law under Mississippi Code § 63-11-30. Under state law, a driver can be charged under two distinct theories: 'Per Se' DUI for operating a vehicle with a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.08% or higher, or 'Common Law' DUI for driving while impaired by alcohol or drugs to a degree that diminishes your driving ability. Successfully defeating a Mississippi DUI involves attacking the legality of the initial traffic stop, challenging field sobriety testing protocols, exposing breathalyzer maintenance flaws, or pursuing statutory non-adjudication.

Mississippi DUI Offense Levels and Statutory Penalties

Under Mississippi Code § 63-11-30, a first-offense DUI is classified as a misdemeanor carrying up to 48 hours in jail, mandatory fines between $250 and $1,000, suspension of driving privileges for 120 days, and required attendance at the Mississippi Alcohol Safety Education Program (MASEP). However, if you refuse the chemical breath test, the Mississippi Department of Public Safety automatically suspends your driver's license for 90 days under administrative implied consent rules.

Penalties escalate dramatically for subsequent offenses within a five-year lookback window. A second conviction carries mandatory jail time of 5 days to 6 months, fines up to $1,500, a one-year license suspension, and mandatory ignition interlock installation. A third or subsequent DUI offense within five years transforms into a serious felony punishable by one to five years in the Mississippi state penitentiary, substantial fines, and permanent vehicle forfeiture.

Challenging the Initial Stop and Field Sobriety Testing

The primary constitutional battleground in any Mississippi DUI case is the Fourth Amendment legality of the initial vehicle stop. Law enforcement officers must possess specific, articulable 'reasonable suspicion' that a traffic infraction or crime occurred before activating their emergency lights. If an officer pulled you over for a trivial reason that does not constitute a traffic violation under Mississippi statutes (such as touching a fog line momentarily without crossing it), all evidence obtained after the unlawful stop must be suppressed under the Exclusionary Rule.

Furthermore, roadside Standardized Field Sobriety Tests (SFSTs)—including the Horizontal Gaze Nystagmus (HGN), Walk-and-Turn, and One-Leg Stand—are highly vulnerable to technical attack. Officers must administer these tests in strict compliance with National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) protocols. Factors such as performing tests on uneven highway gravel, heavy rain, flashing police strobe lights, poor footwear, natural eye conditions, or physical fatigue compromise test validity and can be excluded from trial.

The Intoxilyzer 8000, Breath Tests, and Non-Adjudication

In Mississippi, chemical breath testing is conducted on the Intoxilyzer 8000. Under Mississippi Department of Health regulations, the administering officer must conduct a continuous, uninterrupted 20-minute deprivation observation period prior to testing. During this 20-minute window, the suspect must not burp, regurgitate, smoke, or put anything in their mouth. If the officer leaves the room, completes paperwork with their back turned, or fails to notice acid reflux, mouth alcohol can contaminate the chamber, artificially inflating the BAC reading above 0.08%.

For eligible first-time offenders who cannot achieve a complete dismissal on constitutional grounds, Mississippi Code § 63-11-30(14) provides an exceptional statutory alternative: Non-Adjudication. Under non-adjudication, you plead guilty, but the court withholds entry of a formal judgment of conviction. Upon completing MASEP courses, installing an ignition interlock device for 120 days, and paying court costs, the court dismisses the charges entirely, keeping your criminal record free of any DUI conviction.

How to Fight and Beat a DUI in Mississippi

Follow these five tactical steps to defend against a Mississippi DUI prosecution.

  1. Request an Administrative License Hearing Within 10 Days

    Submit a timely petition to contest the administrative driver's license suspension triggered by your arrest or chemical test refusal.

  2. Obtain Dashcam, Bodycam, and Intoxilyzer Maintenance Logs

    Subpoena video footage of your driving and arrest, along with the calibration and diagnostic records for the specific breathalyzer machine used.

  3. File a Motion to Suppress the Unlawful Traffic Stop

    Challenge the officer's initial legal basis for pulling you over if no clear traffic violation occurred prior to the stop.

  4. Scrutinize Field Sobriety Tests for NHTSA Violations

    Demonstrate that roadside physical agility tests were administered on uneven ground, in inclement weather, or without proper standardized instructions.

  5. Apply for Statutory Non-Adjudication if Pretrial Motions Fail

    If constitutional suppression is denied, utilize Mississippi's non-adjudication statute to complete required programs and have your record cleared.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What happens if you refuse a breathalyzer in Mississippi?

Refusing a breath test triggers an automatic 90-day administrative license suspension under implied consent, but denies prosecutors chemical BAC evidence.

Q2: Can a DUI be expunged in Mississippi?

Yes, under Mississippi Code § 63-11-30(2)(a), a first-offense DUI can be petitioned for expungement five years after successful completion of all sentencing terms.

Q3: What is the legal BAC limit in Mississippi?

The legal limit is 0.08% for non-commercial adult drivers, 0.04% for commercial CDL operators, and 0.02% for drivers under 21 years old.

Q4: What is non-adjudication for a DUI in Mississippi?

Non-adjudication allows first-time offenders to fulfill probation terms, complete MASEP, and use an ignition interlock without a formal conviction on their record.

Q5: What is MASEP in Mississippi?

MASEP stands for the Mississippi Alcohol Safety Education Program, a state-mandated 12-hour driver intervention course required for all DUI offenders.

Q6: Can you beat a DUI if you were sleeping in a parked car?

Yes, if the vehicle was safely parked, the engine turned off, and the keys away from the ignition, you can argue lack of 'actual physical control'.

Q7: How long does a DUI stay on your driving record in Mississippi?

A DUI conviction remains on your Mississippi driving history permanently unless successfully dismissed, non-adjudicated, or formally expunged.

Q8: Can medical conditions cause a false high breathalyzer reading?

Yes, conditions such as Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD), acid reflux, or ketosis from diabetes can introduce stomach alcohol into breath samples.
