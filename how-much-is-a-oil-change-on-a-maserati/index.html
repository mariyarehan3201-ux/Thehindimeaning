How Much Is a Oil Change on a Maserati?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Understanding how much is a oil change on a maserati requires examining authorized dealership labor rates, Ferrari-derived powertrain architectures, specialized synthetic oil specifications, and independent European auto repair options. On average, having an oil and filter change performed on a modern Maserati—such as the Ghibli, Levante, Quattroporte, or Grecale—costs between $400 and $800 at an authorized franchised Maserati dealership. High-performance Trofeo models featuring twin-turbo V8 engines can climb to $850 to $1,100. Conversely, having the service performed at a reputable independent Italian automotive specialist typically reduces the invoice to between $250 and $450.

Engine Architectures, Ferrari Heritage, and Lubricant Specifications

The elevated cost of servicing a Maserati engine stems directly from its sophisticated Italian engineering heritage. For over two decades, modern Maserati models have been powered by engines cast and hand-assembled by Ferrari in Maranello, Italy. These high-output, twin-turbocharged 3.0-liter V6 and 3.8-liter V8 powerplants operate at extreme internal combustion pressures and high operating temperatures. Maintaining engine longevity and protecting the twin turbochargers from thermal coking demands premium, OEM-certified full-synthetic motor oils, primarily Pennzoil Platinum Euro or Shell Helix Ultra 5W-40 or 0W-40.

A typical Maserati twin-turbo V6 engine requires between 7.5 and 8.5 quarts of synthetic oil, while the muscular V8 variants hold approximately 9.5 to 10.5 quarts. Factory OEM Maserati oil filters, manufactured with high-density synthetic micro-glass filtering media, cost between $50 and $90 at dealership parts counters. The combination of premium high-capacity synthetic lubricants, specialty filters, and crush washers accounts for $160 to $260 in raw parts alone before a single minute of technician labor is added.

Labor Procedures: Skid Plate Removal and Electronic Service Resets

Performing an oil change on a modern Maserati requires substantial physical labor that far exceeds a quick 15-minute quick-lube drive-through service. To access the engine oil pan and drain plug, technicians must raise the vehicle and remove extensive underbody composite acoustic splash shields and aerodynamic belly pans secured by ten to twenty specialized fasteners. Furthermore, on certain V6 models, the oil filter housing is tightly nestled beneath engine intake ducting, requiring careful component disassembly to avoid cracking plastic clips or damaging wiring harnesses.

Another critical procedural requirement is resetting the vehicle's electronic maintenance reminder system. Maserati instrument clusters feature an onboard computerized service indicator that tracks mileage and elapsed days. Resetting this indicator cannot be achieved through simple steering wheel button sequences; it requires connecting a high-end diagnostic computer (such as the factory Maserati Leonardo or an advanced Autel MaxiSys scanner) to communicate with the central body control module. Dealerships typically charge 1.5 to 2.0 billed labor hours ($300 to $500 in labor) to complete the physical service and electronic reset.

Dealership Upsells, Warranty Protection, and Independent Alternatives

When visiting a Maserati dealership for an oil change, vehicle owners should be prepared for the service advisor to recommend an all-encompassing 'Annual Maintenance Package.' Rather than billing a simple oil service, dealerships bundle the oil change with cabin air filters, engine air filters, wiper blades, and fluid flushes, presenting an invoice ranging from $1,200 to $2,200. Owners on a budget should clearly instruct the advisor to execute only the necessary engine oil and filter service along with the multi-point inspection, postponing ancillary filter replacements until factory-specified mileage intervals.

Furthermore, Maserati owners are not obligated to patronize franchised dealerships to preserve their factory bumper-to-bumper or powertrain warranties. Under the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act, an independent European specialist can legally perform routine scheduled maintenance without voiding warranty coverage, provided they use manufacturer-approved lubricants and filters and provide detailed receipts. Finding a reputable independent Italian automotive specialist will save an owner $200 to $400 on every single oil service throughout vehicle ownership.

How to Save Money on Your Maserati Oil Change Service

Follow these five tactical steps to schedule professional service while avoiding inflated dealership surcharges.

  1. Locate an Independent Italian Auto Specialist

    Find local independent shops experienced with Ferrari, Alfa Romeo, and Maserati vehicles with proper diagnostic scanners.

  2. Request an Explicit Itemized Price Quote

    Confirm the quote covers OEM filter parts, approved synthetic oil, labor, and the electronic dash counter reset.

  3. Verify Approved Synthetic Oil Viscosity

    Ensure the shop utilizes Pennzoil Platinum Euro or Shell Helix Ultra meeting Maserati factory specifications.

  4. Decline Generic Flushes and Early Air Filters

    Decline dealer-suggested fuel additives and premature engine air filter swaps unless visual wear is demonstrated.

  5. Maintain a Dedicated Maintenance Binder

    Save every itemized service invoice to prove continuous factory-grade maintenance when you eventually sell or trade the car.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does an oil change cost on a Maserati Ghibli?

At an authorized dealership, it typically costs $420 to $650; an independent specialist charges between $250 and $380.

Q2: How often does a Maserati need an oil change?

Maserati recommends an oil and filter change every 10,000 to 12,500 miles or once every 12 months, whichever comes first.

Q3: Can you get a Maserati oil change at Jiffy Lube?

It is strongly discouraged; quick-lube shops lack factory oil specifications, belly-pan tools, and scanners to reset the service dash light.

Q4: Are Maserati engines made by Ferrari?

Most modern Maserati V6 and V8 engines (from 2002 to 2023) were engineered and assembled by Ferrari in Maranello, Italy.

Q5: What kind of oil does a Maserati take?

Maserati utilizes high-grade full-synthetic motor oils such as Pennzoil Platinum Euro or Shell Helix Ultra in 5W-40 or 0W-40 viscosities.

Q6: Why is a Maserati oil change so expensive?

High labor rates, composite aerodynamic belly pan removal, expensive OEM filters, and specialty diagnostic scan tools drive up costs.

Q7: Can an independent shop reset the Maserati service light?

Yes, provided the independent European repair shop owns an advanced scan tool (such as an Autel or Leonardo) compatible with Maserati ECUs.

Q8: Can I change the oil on my Maserati myself?

Yes, DIY mechanics can complete the service with proper ramps and Torx tools, but resetting the dashboard service alert requires a specialty OBD scanner.
