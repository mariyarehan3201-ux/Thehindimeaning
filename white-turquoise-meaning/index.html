Gemological Truth: White Turquoise vs White Buffalo

In the gemological community, the term white turquoise often generates spirited debate. Mineralogically, true turquoise is a hydrated phosphate of copper and aluminum. Copper is the specific chromophore that gives turquoise its signature blue color, while iron turns it green. In the absence of copper and iron, true white turquoise is exceedingly rare.

The vast majority of genuine fine Southwestern jewelry sold as white turquoise is actually White Buffalo stone, mined from a single deposit near Tonopah, Nevada. Discovered in 1993 by the Otteson mining family, White Buffalo is a rare blend of calcite, chert, and aluminum phosphate. Because it forms alongside turquoise veins and shares an identical hardness (5.5 to 6.0) and lapidary matrix, miners and jewelers reverently refer to it as white turquoise.

How to Identify Real White Buffalo from Fake Howlite

Avoid porous dyed or synthetic imitations with this expert field authentication guide.

  1. Examine the Matrix Depth Under Magnification

    Authentic White Buffalo shows deep, irregular, jagged manganese veins; fake howlite displays flat, painted-looking grey spiderwebs.

  2. Check Surface Hardness and Scratch Resistance

    Real White Buffalo rates 5.5 to 6.0 on the Mohs scale and cannot be easily scratched with a copper penny; cheap howlite or plastic scratches easily.

  3. Perform a Temperature Touch Test

    Natural stone feels distinctly cool against the skin and warms slowly; lightweight plastic or resin imitations feel warm immediately.

  4. Inspect the Backing of the Cabochon

    Artisan Southwestern lapidaries often back natural turquoise cabochons with devcon or epoxy for stability; look for authentic handcrafted beveling.

  5. Buy from Reputable Native American or Nevada Dealers

    Always verify provenance and certificates of authenticity from established Southwestern trading posts and certified silversmiths.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is white turquoise?

White turquoise is the popular trade name for rare white Southwestern gemstones, most notably Nevada White Buffalo stone and pale Dry Creek turquoise.

Q2: Is white turquoise real turquoise?

True turquoise requires copper to be blue; white stone from Nevada is White Buffalo (calcite/aluminum phosphate) which forms in the same vein and shares turquoise properties.

Q3: What does white turquoise symbolize?

White turquoise symbolizes sacred purity, spiritual peace, grounding, emotional stability, and the harmonious balance of life opposites.

Q4: What is the difference between white turquoise and howlite?

White Buffalo is a hard, valuable Nevada gemstone with dark black manganese webbing; howlite is an inexpensive, porous borosilicate mineral.

Q5: Where is white turquoise found?

The most famous authentic deposits are mined in the Tonopah and Battle Mountain regions of Nevada, USA.

Q6: Can white turquoise get wet?

Quick splashing is fine, but prolonged soaking or harsh soaps can degrade natural matrix veins; always wipe clean with a soft damp cloth.

Q7: How do you care for white turquoise jewelry?

Store it away from direct sunlight, avoid perfume or lotion contact, and clean gently with a soft dry polishing cloth.

Q8: What chakra does white turquoise heal?

It bridges the crown chakra (divine clarity and purity) with the root chakra (physical stability and protection).
