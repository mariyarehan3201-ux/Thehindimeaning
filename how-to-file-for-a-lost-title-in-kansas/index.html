How to File for a Lost Title in Kansas?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Losing a motor vehicle title certificate creates significant obstacles when attempting to sell, trade in, or register an automobile in Kansas. The Kansas Department of Revenue (KDOR) Division of Vehicles governs all motor vehicle titling and maintains strict procedural protocols to prevent automotive fraud and unlawful title transfers. Fortunately, whether your certificate of title was misplaced, stolen, destroyed, or physically damaged, Kansas law provides a clear statutory procedure to obtain a duplicate replacement title.

Kansas Department of Revenue Titling Framework and Electronic Titles

In Kansas, vehicle titling operations are administered by the Kansas Department of Revenue (KDOR) Division of Vehicles in coordination with County Treasurer motor vehicle offices across the state 105 counties. Before initiating a title replacement application, it is essential to determine whether your vehicle currently possesses an electronic title (e-title) or an existing physical paper certificate.

Kansas operates an Electronic Titling Program (ELT) where vehicle titles with active recorded financial liens are held electronically by the state. If you are currently financing your automobile through a bank, credit union, or auto dealership, KDOR will not print a paper certificate of title until the underlying debt is satisfied and the lienholder issues an electronic lien release. If your loan is paid off but the bank never released the lien in the KDOR system, you must obtain a formal notarized lien release before a duplicate title can be issued.

Completing Form TR-720B and Handling Lien Releases

The official instrument used to request a duplicate certificate of title is the Kansas Application for Duplicate / Secured / Reissued Title, officially designated as Form TR-720B. This document requires accurate reporting of your vehicle identification number (VIN), year, make, model, current Kansas license plate number, and registered owner personal information matching KDOR records exactly.

A crucial section of Form TR-720B addresses outstanding financial liens. If a lienholder is named on the vehicle record, Kansas statutes prohibit issuing a clear duplicate paper title directly to the vehicle owner without written proof that the debt has been fully satisfied. You must obtain an original, signed, and notarized Lien Release (Form TR-150) from the financial institution on corporate letterhead, or ensure the lender has submitted an electronic release through the KDOR ELT network.

Submission at County Treasurer Offices and Delivery Logistics

While completed paperwork can be mailed directly to Title Services in Topeka, visiting your local County Treasurer Motor Vehicle office is significantly faster and prevents processing rejections. County clerks immediately cross-reference your vehicle VIN and owner identity against the statewide motor vehicle database, identifying any unpaid vehicle property taxes or registration holds that could stall printing.

Once your application is approved and the ten-dollar statutory fee is paid, KDOR prints the replacement title at the state central facility in Topeka. Under Kansas law, the paper title is mailed directly to the registered owner address on file within ten to fourteen business days. Note that once a duplicate title is issued, any previously lost original certificate becomes completely void. If you subsequently locate the old paper title, it must be destroyed immediately to avoid legal complications.

How to Obtain a Duplicate Vehicle Title in Kansas

A step-by-step roadmap for replacing a lost or destroyed car title in Kansas.

  1. Download and Complete Kansas Form TR-720B

    Obtain Form TR-720B from the KDOR website or local County Treasurer office, filling in the exact VIN, make, model, and owner details.

  2. Secure a Formal Lien Release if Applicable

    If your vehicle previously had a loan, obtain a notarized Form TR-150 lien release from your financial lender before filing.

  3. Gather Valid Government-Issued Photo Identification

    Prepare your active Kansas driver license, military ID, or state identification card to prove registered vehicle ownership.

  4. Submit Application and $10 Fee to County Treasurer

    Visit your local County Treasurer Motor Vehicle office to submit your application in person, paying the ten-dollar statutory state fee.

  5. Receive Your Replacement Title via Postal Mail

    KDOR will verify your records and mail your official duplicate paper certificate of title to your residential address within two weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a replacement vehicle title cost in Kansas?

The statutory fee for a duplicate or replacement certificate of title in Kansas is ten dollars when submitted through standard processing channels.

Q2: How long does it take to receive a duplicate title in Kansas?

Standard duplicate titles are processed and mailed by the KDOR central printing facility in Topeka within ten to fourteen business days.

Q3: Can I get a same-day replacement title in Kansas?

Yes, KDOR offers walk-in expedited title service at its central Title Services office in Topeka for an additional twenty-dollar rush fee.

Q4: Can I apply for a duplicate Kansas title online?

Kansas currently requires paper submission of Form TR-720B either in person at a County Treasurer office or via postal mail to Topeka.

Q5: What form is needed to replace a lost title in Kansas?

You must complete Kansas Department of Revenue Form TR-720B, titled 'Application for Duplicate / Secured / Reissued Title'.

Q6: What happens if I find the original title after receiving a duplicate?

Once a duplicate title is issued by KDOR, the original certificate becomes legally void and should be destroyed to prevent fraudulent title use.

Q7: Can I sell my car immediately after applying for a duplicate title?

In Kansas, you cannot legally execute a vehicle sale or transfer ownership without possessing the physical paper title to assign to the buyer.

Q8: What if there is a deceased owner on the Kansas title?

If a co-owner is deceased, you must provide a certified copy of the death certificate along with a surviving spouse affidavit or probate letters.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Replacing a lost vehicle title in Kansas is a systematic administrative process governed by Form TR-720B and County Treasurer oversight. By confirming lien releases, gathering proper identification, and submitting verified records at your local county office, you can secure your replacement certificate of title smoothly and maintain clean, marketable vehicle ownership.
