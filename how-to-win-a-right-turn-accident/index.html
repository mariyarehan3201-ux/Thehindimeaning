How to Win a Right Turn Accident

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Winning a right turn accident claim requires proving that the turning motorist violated statutory traffic safety rules, failed to yield the right-of-way, or executed an improper turn across active traffic lanes. Whether you were struck as another motorist, a pedestrian in a crosswalk, or a bicyclist in a designated bike lane, securing traffic camera video, skid mark measurements, and witness testimony is vital to establishing driver negligence.

The Statutory Rules Governing Right Turns and Driver Negligence

Under uniform traffic safety codes enforced across all fifty states, executing a right turn in a motor vehicle is not an absolute right; it is a conditional maneuver strictly governed by statutory duties of care. Motorists making right-hand turns are legally mandated to execute the maneuver as close as practicable to the right-hand curb or edge of the roadway (the "Curbside Rule"), maintain an active turn signal for a minimum distance (typically 100 feet prior to the turn), and yield the right-of-way to all oncoming vehicles, pedestrians, and pedalcyclists.

The most frequent scenario in right-turn litigation involves the Right Turn on Red (RTOR) rule. Under state traffic statutes, while motorists are permitted to turn right at a red traffic signal unless prohibited by signage, they must first bring their vehicle to a complete, full stop at the marked stop line or crosswalk before turning. Furthermore, vehicles turning right on red yield the right-of-way to all traffic proceeding straight through the intersection on a green light, as well as oncoming drivers making protected U-turns. Drivers who roll through red lights without stopping are guilty of negligence per se.

Another dangerous scenario is the commercial "Wide Right Turn" executed by heavy semi-trucks and delivery vans. Because long commercial trailers experience off-tracking?"where the rear trailer wheels track significantly inside the path of the front steering cab?"truck drivers frequently swing wide into adjacent left lanes before turning right. When truck drivers fail to monitor their right-side blind spots, they crush smaller passenger vehicles trapped in the opening gap, creating a catastrophic "squeeze play" collision. Assembling Unassailable Evidence: Video, Telematics, and Witnesses

To win a right-turn accident claim, your legal team must overcome standard insurance adjuster defense narratives. Turning motorists almost universally claim that the oncoming vehicle was speeding, had its headlights off, or accelerated aggressively to prevent them from completing the turn. Dismantling these false defenses requires objective, timestamped physical and digital evidence.

The most decisive piece of evidence in right-turn litigation is digital video footage. Modern urban intersections are heavily surveilled by municipal traffic monitoring cameras, red-light enforcement cameras, and exterior commercial security cameras installed on gas stations, banks, and convenience stores. Video footage provides indisputable visual proof of whether the turning driver executed a complete stop at the red light, whether their turn signal was activated, and the exact lane position of the impact. Because commercial surveillance cameras automatically overwrite footage within 7 to 14 days, your attorney must immediately issue formal Emergency Evidence Spoliation Notices to property owners. Defeating Comparative Negligence Arguments

In right-turn collisions, defense insurance adjusters aggressively attempt to reduce financial payouts by arguing Comparative Negligence. If you were the oncoming driver proceeding straight on green, the defense will allege that you were traveling 5 MPH over the posted speed limit, were distracted by your mobile phone, or failed to take evasive braking action to avoid the collision. In comparative fault states, successfully assigning even 20 percent of fault to you reduces your settlement check by 20 percent.

Your attorney defeats these comparative fault allegations by deploying certified accident reconstruction engineers. Using physical skid mark measurements, vehicle crush profiles, and Event Data Recorder (EDR) downloads, reconstructionists prove that the turning driver created an "unavoidable sudden emergency." By calculating perception-reaction times, your experts demonstrate that a reasonable driver traveling at the legal speed limit would have had insufficient distance and time to brake once the turning car illegally encroached into the travel lane. Proving unavoidable hazard exposure completely eliminates comparative fault defenses.

How to Build and Win a Right Turn Collision Lawsuit

A step-by-step practical guide for accident victims to document evidence, prove motorist fault, and maximize settlement recovery.

  1. Insist on an Immediate Law Enforcement Crash Investigation

    Ensure police respond to the scene to interview drivers, inspect point-of-impact damage, and issue citations to the turning driver for failure to yield.

  2. Photograph Vehicle Point-of-Impact and Lane Markings

    Photograph both vehicles before they are moved, focusing on crush damage angles, tire skid marks, intersection lane arrows, and traffic signal heads.

  3. Canvass the Intersection for Surveillance Video Cameras

    Identify all exterior security cameras on adjacent gas stations, retail buildings, and city traffic poles. Request that managers preserve video recordings.

  4. Undergo Immediate Medical Screening at an Emergency Facility

    Seek medical evaluation at an emergency room or urgent care center within 24 hours to document whiplash, fractures, and internal trauma on day one.

  5. Retain an Experienced Personal Injury Trial Litigator

    Hire an attorney to issue formal spoliation letters for traffic video and vehicle black box data, calculating damages and filing formal legal claims.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Who is at fault in a right turn on red accident?

The driver turning right on red is almost always at fault. Under traffic laws, vehicles turning right on red must yield the right-of-way to all oncoming traffic, pedestrians, and cyclists.

Q2: What is a 'Right Hook' collision involving a bicycle?

A right hook occurs when a motorist overtakes a bicyclist on the left and turns right across the bike lane directly into the cyclist's path. The turning driver is 100% legally liable.

Q3: Can I be found at fault if I hit a car turning right in front of me?

Generally no. The driver turning into active traffic has the primary duty to yield. You are only partially at fault if the defense proves you were speeding excessively or intentionally failed to brake.

Q4: What is a commercial truck squeeze play collision?

It occurs when a long semi-truck swings wide into the left lane before turning right, and an adjacent car moves into the open right space, getting crushed by the trailer off-tracking.

Q5: How do you prove a driver did not stop before turning right on red?

Proof requires intersection red-light camera video, nearby business surveillance footage, vehicle black box EDR braking data, or independent eyewitness testimony.

Q6: Who has the right of way: right turn or protected U-turn?

In most states, a driver executing a protected U-turn on an active green arrow has the right of way over a vehicle turning right on red facing a red traffic signal.

Q7: What damages can you recover in a right-turn accident claim?

You can recover past and future medical expenses, surgeries, physical therapy, lost income, reduced earning capacity, pain and suffering, and vehicle replacement costs.

Q8: How long do I have to file a right-turn accident lawsuit?

The statute of limitations for personal injury claims typically ranges from two to three years from the date of the collision, depending on the state where the crash occurred.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Winning a right-turn accident claim requires prompt investigation, rigorous statutory analysis, and aggressive evidence preservation. Turning drivers bear a strict legal duty to ensure maneuvers can be completed with reasonable safety without impeding oncoming traffic or endangering vulnerable crosswalk users. By securing intersection video footage immediately, obtaining vehicle black box telemetry, and partnering with an experienced personal injury trial litigator, you can overcome insurance tactics and secure full financial recovery.
