How to Become a Barber in Texas?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Launching a career as a professional barber in Texas allows you to combine artistic craftsmanship, personal entrepreneurship, and community connection in a thriving grooming industry. Barber licensing in Texas is overseen by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). Following recent legislative modernization under Texas House Bill 1560, the state consolidated barber and cosmetology boards into a streamlined regulatory structure, establishing a standardized 1,000-hour education curriculum. Successfully entering this profession requires completing an accredited barbering program, passing dual state board examinations, and obtaining official licensure from TDLR.

TDLR Licensing Modernization and 1,000-Hour Training

The Texas barbering landscape experienced transformative modernization when the 87th Texas Legislature enacted House Bill 1560, formally merging the Barbering and Cosmetology Boards under TDLR oversight. Previously, prospective Class A barbers were required to complete 1,500 clock hours of instruction. Under modern state administrative codes, the mandatory training requirement has been streamlined to exactly 1,000 hours of curriculum in an accredited Texas barber or cosmetology school.

The standardized 1,000-hour curriculum provides intensive practical and theoretical instruction across essential styling disciplines. Students spend significant time mastering straight razor shaving techniques, facial hair grooming, precision scissor work, clipper fading, hair coloring, chemical relaxing, scalp massage therapies, and bacteriology sanitation standards. Students must also learn Texas sanitation laws to prevent transmission of bloodborne pathogens in commercial salon environments.

Passing the PSI Written and Practical State Board Exams

Upon completing the 1,000 clock hours of coursework, schools certify student completion to TDLR, enabling candidates to schedule their state examinations administered by PSI Services. The examination process comprises two independent assessments: a computer-based written theory exam and a hands-on practical exam. The written test consists of eighty-five scored questions covering infection control practices, client consultation protocols, hair and scalp histology, and TDLR regulatory statutes.

The PSI practical exam requires candidates to demonstrate hands-on mastery in a strictly timed, simulated clinical environment. Candidates must bring their own professional kit, sanitized implements, and an approved mannequin head with facial hair. Key scored evaluation sections include professional setup and disinfection, hair cutting and blending, straight razor shaving on the face, chemical wave service, hair coloring application, and emergency blood-spill sanitation protocols. Achieving at least a seventy percent on both exams is mandatory.

Barbershop Career Paths: Booth Rental Versus Commission

With a valid Class A Barber License issued by TDLR, graduates can enter the workforce under several business models. The most common arrangement in modern Texas barbershops is booth rental, where the barber operates as an independent contractor, leasing a chair from a shop owner for a fixed weekly fee (typically one hundred and fifty to three hundred and fifty dollars). This model grants ultimate schedule autonomy, service pricing control, and retail profit retention, though it requires disciplined self-employment tax management.

Alternatively, newly licensed barbers often begin in commission-based shops or high-volume franchised salons. Under a commission structure, service revenues are split (usually 50/50 or 60/40 in favor of the barber), and the shop owner provides foot traffic, booking software, backbar supplies, and marketing. Texas also offers pathways for licensed barbers to establish independent brick-and-mortar barbershops or mobile grooming rigs by securing a TDLR Barbershop Establishment Permit.

How to Become a Licensed Barber in Texas in 5 Steps

Follow this five-step career roadmap to complete training, pass board examinations, and obtain your Texas barber license.

  1. Enroll in an Accredited Texas Barber College

    Select a TDLR-approved barbering school offering the modernized 1,000-hour comprehensive curriculum and obtain your state student permit.

  2. Complete 1,000 Hours of Practical and Theory Coursework

    Master haircutting, clipper blending, straight razor shaving, bacteriology, and Texas sanitation regulations across 1,000 recorded clock hours.

  3. Register and Pass the PSI Written Theory Examination

    Schedule your computer-based exam via PSI and achieve a passing score of at least 70% covering anatomy, hygiene, and barbering laws.

  4. Pass the PSI Hands-On Practical Examination

    Demonstrate live straight razor shaving, haircutting, and blood-spill sanitation on a designated mannequin head at an official PSI testing site.

  5. Apply for Licensure and Secure Shop Placement

    Submit your official application and $50 license fee to TDLR to receive your Class A Barber license and begin working in a shop.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How many hours do you need for a barber license in Texas?

Under Texas House Bill 1560, the required instruction for a Class A barber license is 1,000 clock hours in an accredited school.

Q2: How much does barber school cost in Texas?

Tuition typically ranges between $6,000 and $18,000 depending on school prestige, location in major cities, and included equipment kits.

Q3: Can you transfer a cosmetology license to a barber license in Texas?

Yes, licensed cosmetologists in Texas can obtain a Class A barber license by completing a condensed 300-hour crossover curriculum.

Q4: Can a felon get a barber license in Texas?

Yes, TDLR evaluates criminal histories on an individual basis via a Criminal History Evaluation Letter prior to school enrollment.

Q5: How often do you have to renew a Texas barber license?

Texas barber licenses must be renewed every two years through TDLR, requiring four hours of continuing education including sanitation.

Q6: Can barbers do straight razor shaves in Texas?

Yes, Class A licensed barbers are fully authorized under Texas law to perform full facial straight razor shaves and beard sculpting.

Q7: What is the passing score for the Texas barber exam?

Candidates must achieve a minimum score of 70 percent on both the PSI written theory exam and the PSI hands-on practical exam.

Q8: How much do barbers make in Texas?

Earnings vary widely; established barbers working full-time in Texas metropolitan areas typically earn between $45,000 and $90,000+ annually.
