Where Can You Get Rid of Asbestos?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

Where can you get rid of asbestos safely, legally, and responsibly? Because asbestos minerals are chemically indestructible and represent a chronic human inhalation hazard, disposing of asbestos waste is subject to strict federal, state, and local environmental statutes. Asbestos cannot be placed in standard household trash, curbside bins, or general construction dumpsters; it must be transported to permitted hazardous or industrial waste landfills.

Authorized Facilities for Lawful Asbestos Waste Disposal

Disposal of asbestos waste is governed by the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) under Title 40 of the Code of Federal Regulations, Part 61, Subpart M (NESHAP). Under these federal regulations, asbestos-containing waste materials (ACWM) must be transported exclusively to landfill facilities permitted by state environmental agencies to accept industrial or hazardous solid waste. These include Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA) Subtitle D non-hazardous industrial landfills with dedicated asbestos disposal cells, as well as RCRA Subtitle C hazardous waste facilities.

Permitted asbestos landfills utilize specialized monofills or isolated trenches engineered to prevent fiber migration. Once an asbestos shipment arrives, operators bury the sealed waste under a minimum of six inches of non-asbestos material or compacted soil before the end of the operating day, or apply synthetic chemical dust suppressants. This daily cover prevents surface erosion, equipment puncture, and airborne fiber dispersal, permanently entombing the hazardous mineral underground.

Packaging Regulations, Manifest Documentation, and Transportation Rules

Before any disposal facility will accept asbestos waste, strict packaging standards established by EPA NESHAP and OSHA must be satisfied. All friable materials must be saturated with surfactant-amended water to eliminate dust emissions and sealed inside two leak-tight, 6-mil polyethylene bags. Non-friable materials with sharp edges, such as cement Transite siding or broken floor tiles, are typically sealed in rigid fiber drums, heavy-duty lined cardboard boxes, or sealed roll-off dumpster containers lined with double 6-mil poly.

Every package must bear prominent OSHA danger labels warning of cancer and lung disease hazards, alongside a Department of Transportation (DOT) shipping label. Furthermore, an EPA Waste Shipment Record (WSR) or state hazardous waste manifest must accompany the shipment from the point of origin to the disposal facility. This legal chain of custody documents the waste generator's identity, the licensed transport driver, the volume of waste, and the final receiving facility's certification of burial.

Tipping fees for asbestos disposal at permitted landfills typically range from $75 to $250 per ton, with many facilities imposing a minimum load surcharge of $100 to $200 regardless of quantity. While a handful of regional counties host periodic residential drop-off days for small homeowner quantities, most municipalities prohibit private individuals from delivering asbestos, mandating that waste be hauled by registered hazardous waste transport firms.

How to Legally Prepare and Transport Asbestos for Disposal

Step-by-step procedure for packaging, manifesting, and delivering asbestos waste to permitted facilities.

  1. Locate a Permitted Asbestos Landfill

    Contact your state department of environmental protection or regional solid waste authority to find a licensed disposal facility.

  2. Saturate and Double-Bag All Waste

    Wet all materials with amended water and seal inside two 6-mil polyethylene bags using goose-neck duct tape closures.

  3. Affix Mandatory Warning and Tracking Labels

    Attach OSHA danger warnings and generator identification stickers displaying the jobsite address, contractor, and date.

  4. Execute a Waste Shipment Record Manifest

    Complete a multi-copy EPA Waste Shipment Record detailing the number of containers and volume of asbestos transported.

  5. Deliver via a Licensed Environmental Hauler

    Transport the waste in an enclosed vehicle driven by a registered hazardous hauler and obtain a signed delivery receipt from the landfill.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I throw asbestos in my regular household garbage can?

No, putting asbestos in curbside trash is illegal nationwide, violates EPA regulations, and poses severe hazards to sanitation workers.

Q2: Can standard construction dumpsters be used for asbestos waste?

No, general dumpsters cannot accept asbestos unless they are specifically permitted, lined with double 6-mil poly, and manifested.

Q3: Where is asbestos buried once delivered to a landfill?

Asbestos is deposited in dedicated, engineered trenches and buried under at least six inches of clean soil daily to prevent airborne release.

Q4: How much does it cost to dispose of asbestos at a landfill?

Landfills generally charge between 75 and 250 dollars per ton, often with a minimum disposal fee ranging from 100 to 200 dollars per load.

Q5: Can a homeowner haul their own asbestos waste to the dump?

Some local municipal landfills permit homeowner drop-offs with advance registration, but many jurisdictions require licensed hazardous haulers.

Q6: What is an asbestos waste shipment record (manifest)?

A legal chain-of-custody tracking document identifying the waste generator, transportation hauler, total volume, and receiving landfill.

Q7: What thickness must asbestos waste bags be?

Federal regulations require heavy-duty polyethylene bags with a minimum thickness of 6 mils, doubled for redundant puncture protection.

Q8: Can asbestos ever decompose naturally in a landfill?

No, asbestos minerals are chemically inert and practically indestructible; deep landfill burial isolates the fibers permanently underground.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Disposing of asbestos waste through authorized, permitted environmental channels is both a legal imperative and an essential civic responsibility. Never attempt to bury asbestos on private land, burn it, or abandon it in rural areas or commercial dumpsters. Engaging licensed abatement professionals and permitted landfill operators guarantees complete compliance with federal NESHAP standards and protects our soil, air, and communities for future generations.
