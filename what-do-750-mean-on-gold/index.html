What Do 750 Mean on Gold?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

When you see the number 750 stamped inside a ring, bracelet, necklace clasp, or watch casing, it indicates that the item is crafted from 18-karat gold with a certified purity of 75 percent. In international metallurgy and precious metal hallmarking, purity is expressed through the millesimal fineness system, which measures gold content in parts per thousand rather than traditional karats. Therefore, a stamp of 750 signifies that the alloy contains exactly 750 parts of pure elemental gold out of 1,000 parts, with the remaining 250 parts consisting of stabilizing alloy metals such as copper, silver, zinc, or palladium.

The Millesimal Fineness System and 18-Karat Metallurgy

Pure elemental gold, designated as 24 karats or 999 fineness, is exceptionally soft, malleable, and prone to bending, scratching, and rapid surface wear under daily physical contact. To manufacture durable jewelry that withstands decades of active wear, master jewelers melt pure gold alongside carefully calibrated proportions of complementary transition metals. This fusion creates an alloy that possesses the rich, warm luster of precious gold while dramatically enhancing mechanical hardness, structural tensile strength, and scratch resistance.

The stamp 750 represents the premier European and global hallmark for 18-karat gold jewelry. While American manufacturers frequently stamp jewelry with traditional karat marks like "18K," "18Kt," or "18ct," European, Swiss, and Asian assay offices mandate the millesimal fineness number 750. By establishing a direct mathematical ratio—750 divided by 1,000 equals exactly 18 divided by 24—consumers and appraisers can instantly determine the intrinsic precious metal content regardless of the country of origin.

How Alloy Composition Shapes the Color of 750 Gold

While all 750 gold items contain precisely 75 percent pure gold, they can exhibit vastly different aesthetic colors based on the specific recipe of the remaining 25 percent alloy base. Pure gold is naturally yellow, but metallurgists can manipulate the optical appearance of the finished metal by introducing white or reddish elements. For example, 18K yellow gold typically combines 75 percent gold with 12.5 percent fine silver and 12.5 percent copper, preserving a classic buttery warmth.

To produce 18K white gold marked 750, jewelers alloy 75 percent pure gold with white bleaching metals such as palladium, silver, or nickel, followed by an electroplated surface layer of ultra-bright rhodium. For 18K rose gold, the alloy mixture incorporates approximately 20 to 22 percent copper alongside a minor touch of silver, generating a rich pinkish or reddish blush. Regardless of whether an 18K piece appears yellow, bright silvery white, or rose pink, the 750 hallmark guarantees that the intrinsic gold content remains identical at 75 percent.

Authenticating and Testing 750 Gold Jewelry

Because gold is a high-value commodity, counterfeiters frequently stamp fraudulent 750 marks onto gold-plated brass, copper, or base alloy jewelry. Discerning collectors and buyers should never rely solely on a stamped number without secondary physical verification. A preliminary non-destructive check involves testing with a strong neodymium rare-earth magnet. Pure gold and non-ferrous jewelry alloys are completely non-magnetic; if a piece marked 750 jumps to the magnet, it contains heavy iron, nickel, or steel cores indicating a counterfeit.

Professional jewelers and pawn appraisers verify 750 gold using nitric acid testing or digital X-ray fluorescence (XRF) spectrometry. In an acid scratch test, a minute streak of metal is deposited onto an abrasive touchstone and treated with 18K nitric acid solution. If the streak dissolves or discolors, the item possesses less than 75 percent gold. XRF spectrometry provides a 100 percent non-destructive analysis, instantly reading the atomic composition through the surface to confirm exact karat purity.

How to Verify and Inspect a 750 Gold Hallmark

Follow these step-by-step methods to inspect, test, and authenticate a 750 gold stamp on fine jewelry.

  1. Inspect the Item with a 10x Jewelers Loupe

    Use a 10x magnification loupe under bright white light to locate the 750 stamp inside ring shanks, clasp tongues, or pendant bails.

  2. Perform a Neodymium Rare-Earth Magnet Test

    Bring a strong neodymium magnet close to the jewelry; genuine 18K gold will display zero magnetic attraction (excluding steel clasp springs).

  3. Inspect Wear Points for Discoloration

    Check high-friction edges, prongs, and clasps for base metal show-through, green oxidation, or flaking that indicates electroplating.

  4. Execute an Electronic Gold Tester Evaluation

    Place an electronic conductivity probe onto a clean surface of the jewelry to read electrical resistance corresponding to 18K gold.

  5. Request an XRF Spectrometer Assay from a Jeweler

    Visit a certified gemologist or jeweler equipped with an X-ray fluorescence scanner to measure exact elemental percentages non-destructively.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does 750 mean on a piece of jewelry?

The 750 hallmark means the piece is 18-karat gold, containing 75% pure elemental gold and 25% other strengthening alloy metals.

Q2: Is 750 gold real gold?

Yes, 750 gold is genuine solid 18K gold and is considered a high-grade luxury standard in fine jewelry and Swiss watchmaking.

Q3: Is 750 gold better than 585 gold?

750 gold (18K) is higher in gold purity, richer in color, and more valuable than 585 gold (14K), although 14K is slightly harder and more scratch-resistant.

Q4: Can 750 gold tarnish or turn your finger green?

Genuine 750 gold will not tarnish or turn skin green under normal conditions due to its high 75% noble metal content, unless the wearer has severe copper allergies.

Q5: What does 750 mean on white gold?

It means the white gold alloy contains 75% pure gold alloyed with 25% white metals like palladium or silver, typically plated with rhodium.

Q6: Does 750 mean gold-plated?

No. A standalone 750 stamp designates solid 18K gold. Plated items are typically marked 750 GP, 750 HGE, or 750 RGP.

Q7: How much is 750 gold worth per gram?

The scrap melt value equals exactly 75% of the live spot price of pure 24K gold per gram, plus any collectible or designer brand premium.

Q8: Can fake jewelry have a 750 stamp?

Yes. Counterfeiters often stamp base metals with 750, making magnet testing, acid scratch testing, or XRF analysis important for verification.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

A 750 stamp on a piece of jewelry is the globally recognized hallmark for 18-karat gold, signifying that three-quarters of the alloy by weight is pure, precious gold. Offering an optimal harmony between luxurious aesthetic depth, tangible weight, and everyday physical durability, 750 gold remains the gold standard for high-end engagement rings, luxury timepieces, and timeless heirloom jewelry.
