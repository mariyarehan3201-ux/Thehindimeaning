How to File a Harassment Complaint in California?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

California maintains some of the strongest anti-harassment protections in the United States under the Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA) and California Civil Code. Whether experiencing hostile workplace harassment based on protected characteristics like race, sex, or disability, or facing personal stalking and threats outside of work, California law provides formal administrative and judicial pathways to stop the misconduct and seek legal accountability.

Distinguishing Workplace Harassment from Civil Non-Workplace Harassment

Before initiating legal action in California, you must clearly distinguish between workplace harassment and non-workplace civil harassment. Workplace harassment is governed primarily by the Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA), enforced by the California Civil Rights Department (CRD, formerly known as the DFEH) and the federal Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC). FEHA prohibits unwelcome verbal, visual, or physical conduct based on protected characteristics including race, gender, sexual orientation, disability, age, and religious creed.

Conversely, if the harassment involves an abusive neighbor, former acquaintance, stalker, or stranger outside of an employment or domestic relationship, the remedy lies in California Superior Court under Code of Civil Procedure Section 527.6. This judicial mechanism allows victims to petition for a Civil Harassment Restraining Order (CHRO) to prohibit unlawful violence, credible threats of violence, or knowing, willful courses of conduct that seriously alarm, annoy, or harass without legitimate purpose.

Filing Workplace Complaints with the Civil Rights Department (CRD)

For workplace harassment, California requires employees to exhaust their administrative remedies before filing a private lawsuit in court. The process begins by submitting an online Intake Form through the California Civil Rights Department (CRD) Cal Civil Rights System (CCRS) portal. Once submitted, the CRD evaluates the allegations to determine jurisdiction. If the agency accepts the case for investigation, an investigator interviews witnesses, gathers company records, and attempts voluntary mediation between the parties.

If you intend to bypass an administrative state investigation to retain a private employment attorney and immediately file a lawsuit in California Superior Court, you can request an immediate Right-to-Sue Notice from the CRD. Once issued, you have exactly one year from the date of the Right-to-Sue letter to file your formal civil complaint in state court. However, requesting an immediate right-to-sue closes the administrative case without CRD investigation.

Petitioning for a Civil Harassment Restraining Order

When seeking immediate judicial protection from non-workplace harassment, you must complete Judicial Council Form CH-100 (Request for Civil Harassment Restraining Orders). In this petition, describe specific, recent incidents of harassment, physical threats, stalking, or unwanted surveillance in chronological order. Attach supporting evidence such as printouts of threatening text messages, police incident reports, and photographs.

Submit your completed forms to the clerk of the Superior Court in your county. A judge reviews your petition the same day or following morning to decide whether to issue a Temporary Restraining Order (TRO) that takes effect immediately. The court schedules a full evidentiary hearing within twenty-one days, where both parties appear to present testimony. If the judge finds clear and convincing evidence of unlawful harassment, they will issue a permanent restraining order lasting up to five years.

How to File a Formal Harassment Complaint in California

A step-by-step roadmap for filing a workplace harassment claim with the CRD.

  1. Compile Comprehensive Contemporaneous Evidence

    Gather all written communications, HR complaint emails, performance reviews, witness contacts, and detailed journal entries.

  2. Create an Account in the CRD Cal Civil Rights System

    Register an account on the California Civil Rights Department portal (calcivilrights.ca.gov) to begin your administrative intake.

  3. Submit the Detailed Pre-Complaint Intake Form

    Complete the online intake form outlining the dates of harassment, protected categories involved, names of perpetrators, and employer details.

  4. Participate in the CRD Intake Interview

    Speak with a assigned CRD investigator to discuss the facts of your claim and determine whether the department will investigate or mediate.

  5. Obtain an Administrative Closure or Right-to-Sue Notice

    Await CRD formal investigation findings or request an immediate Right-to-Sue Notice to pursue civil litigation with an employment attorney.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How long do I have to file a workplace harassment complaint in California?

Under California FEHA, you have three years from the date of the most recent unlawful harassment incident to file an administrative complaint with the CRD.

Q2: Can an employer fire me for filing a harassment complaint in California?

No, California law strictly prohibits employer retaliation; firing, demoting, or punishing an employee for reporting harassment is illegal under FEHA Section 12940(h).

Q3: What is the difference between CRD and the EEOC?

The CRD enforces California state law (FEHA), which covers employers with five or more employees and has a 3-year deadline, while the EEOC enforces federal law with a 300-day deadline.

Q4: What is a Right-to-Sue letter from the CRD?

A Right-to-Sue notice certifies that you have exhausted state administrative remedies, giving you one year from its issuance date to file a civil lawsuit in court.

Q5: How much does it cost to file a harassment complaint with the CRD?

Filing an administrative harassment complaint with the California Civil Rights Department is completely free for all workers and tenants.

Q6: What legal standard must be met for a civil harassment restraining order?

Under CCP Section 527.6, the petitioner must prove by clear and convincing evidence that knowing and willful conduct caused substantial emotional distress without legitimate purpose.

Q7: Can independent contractors file harassment claims in California?

Yes, unlike federal Title VII, California FEHA explicitly extends protection against workplace harassment to independent contractors and unpaid interns.

Q8: What should I do if HR ignores my workplace harassment report?

Document HR refusal to act in writing, save all copies to your personal email, and immediately submit an administrative complaint to the California Civil Rights Department.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Standing up against harassment in California is protected by robust state statutes designed to shield victims from intimidation and retaliation. By documenting every encounter meticulously, understanding the distinction between CRD workplace filings and Superior Court civil orders, and acting within statutory filing deadlines, you can effectively invoke California legal protections to reclaim your peace, dignity, and security.
