How to Select and Maintain a Natural Sapphire Engagement Ring

Ensure your sapphire remains radiant for generations by following these expert jeweler recommendations.

  1. Prioritize Color Saturation and Uniformity

    Look for a lively medium-to-deep blue that does not look overly dark or inky in low indoor lighting.

  2. Inspect Faceting Symmetry and Depth

    Ensure the sapphire cut is well proportioned; an overly shallow cut causes an empty window in the center where light leaks away.

  3. Request GIA or Certified Lab Documentation

    Obtain an independent gemological report verifying whether the sapphire is natural, unheated, or traditionally heat-treated.

  4. Select High-Purity Precious Metals

    Pair blue sapphire with platinum or 14k/18k white gold to accentuate cool brilliance, or warm yellow gold for rich vintage contrast.

  5. Clean at Home with Dish Soap and Warm Water

    Soak the ring in warm water with mild dish soap for ten minutes, brush gently behind the pavilion facets, and rinse thoroughly.

Q2: Why did Princess Diana have a sapphire engagement ring?

Q3: Is sapphire good for everyday wear?

Yes. Sapphire rates 9.0 on the Mohs hardness scale, making it one of the most durable gemstones suitable for daily wear.

Q4: Are sapphire engagement rings cheaper than diamonds?

Generally yes. Fine quality sapphires usually cost 30% to 50% less per carat than comparable natural white diamonds.

Q5: Do sapphires chip or scratch easily?

No. Sapphires are extremely tough and scratch-resistant, though hard direct blows against hard granite could chip thin girdles.

Q6: Can you wear a sapphire ring in the shower?

While water won't harm natural sapphire, soap film and hard water minerals can build up behind the stone, dulling its sparkle.
