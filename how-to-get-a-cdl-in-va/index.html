How to Get a CDL in VA?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Securing a Commercial Driver License (CDL) in the Commonwealth of Virginia is a significant milestone that unlocks dependable, well-compensated career opportunities in interstate freight transport, heavy construction, passenger transit, and logistics operations. Administered by the Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) in close coordination with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA), obtaining a commercial license requires fulfilling strict physical health standards, mastering computerized commercial knowledge exams, completing accredited Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT), and demonstrating hands-on mastery during a rigorous three-part skills road examination. Understanding Virginia DMV regulatory requirements, holding periods, and testing standards ensures a seamless transition into professional commercial driving.

Virginia CDL Classifications, Endorsements, and Driver Qualifications

The Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles licenses commercial drivers under three standard classifications based on gross vehicle weight ratings (GVWR) and vehicle configuration. A Class A CDL authorizes the operation of combination vehicles with a gross combination weight rating (GCWR) of 26,001 or more pounds, provided the vehicle in tow exceeds 10,000 pounds GVWR. This is the primary license required for regional and long-haul tractor-trailers, livestock haulers, and heavy flatbed combinations.

A Class B CDL authorizes you to drive any single vehicle with a GVWR of 26,001 or more pounds, or any such vehicle towing a trailer not exceeding 10,000 pounds (such as commercial dump trucks, concrete mixers, municipal sanitation trucks, and large straight delivery vehicles). A Class C CDL covers commercial vehicles designed to transport sixteen or more occupants (including the operator) or haul placarded quantities of hazardous materials that do not meet Class A or B weight thresholds. Drivers can also expand their operational credentials by testing for specialized endorsements, such as Tanker (N), Passenger (P), School Bus (S), Doubles/Triples (T), and Hazardous Materials (H).

FMCSA ELDT Compliance, DOT Medical Exams, and the Virginia CLP

Under federal regulations enforced by the Virginia DMV, all new CDL candidates must complete certified Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT) from a school registered on the FMCSA Training Provider Registry (TPR). The ELDT curriculum combines in-depth classroom theory instruction with hands-on range maneuvers and supervised public road driving. The Virginia DMV system directly checks the federal TPR database before allowing any applicant to schedule a state skills examination.

To begin practical training, you must obtain a Virginia Commercial Learner Permit (CLP). First, visit an authorized medical examiner on the National Registry of Certified Medical Examiners (NRCME) to pass a DOT physical and receive a Medical Examiner Certificate (Form MCSA-5876). Bring your medical card, unexpired Virginia driver license, proof of legal U.S. presence (birth certificate or passport), Social Security card, and two documents proving Virginia residency to a full-service Virginia DMV customer service center. After passing a vision exam and the computerized written knowledge exams, you will receive your Virginia CLP, which you must hold for a mandatory minimum of fourteen days before taking your road skills exam.

Mastering the Virginia DMV Three-Stage Practical Skills Examination

The final hurdle to earning your Virginia CDL is passing the practical skills road exam administered at a certified Virginia DMV CDL testing center or through an approved third-party tester. You must provide a commercially registered vehicle that represents the exact license class and braking configurations you intend to operate. The practical exam consists of three distinct testing phases: the Pre-Trip Vehicle Inspection, Basic Vehicle Control Skills, and On-Road Driving.

Phase one begins with the comprehensive Pre-Trip Inspection, where you verbally guide the DMV examiner through a thorough mechanical safety check of the engine compartment, steering linkage, suspension, brakes, tires, coupling system (fifth wheel and kingpin), and emergency safety equipment. You must flawlessly execute the three-step air brake test; misstating pressure thresholds or failing to monitor gauges results in an immediate automatic exam failure. Phase two tests your low-speed backing precision through straight-line backing, offset backing, and alley dock maneuvers. Phase three takes you onto Virginia highways and urban roads, evaluating smooth shifting, lane control, intersection safety, defensive following distances, and railroad crossing compliance. Successfully passing all three phases grants you your official Virginia CDL.

How to Obtain a Commercial Drivers License in Virginia

A step-by-step procedural guide to completing all Virginia DMV and FMCSA requirements to earn a Virginia Commercial Driver License.

  1. Pass a DOT Physical with an NRCME Certified Examiner

    Visit a federally certified medical doctor on the National Registry of Certified Medical Examiners to receive your DOT Medical Examiner Certificate (MCSA-5876) and self-certify with Virginia DMV.

  2. Study the Virginia Commercial Driver License Manual

    Review the official Virginia DMV Commercial Driver License Manual, focusing on general trucking knowledge, combination vehicles, air brake mechanics, and endorsement regulations.

  3. Pass Written Knowledge Exams at Virginia DMV for CLP

    Visit a Virginia DMV customer service center with proof of identity, legal presence, residency, and your DOT medical card. Pass the computerized exams to receive your Commercial Learner Permit (CLP).

  4. Complete Certified FMCSA Entry-Level Driver Training

    Enroll in an FMCSA-registered commercial driving academy in Virginia to complete certified theory education and behind-the-wheel range and public road driving training.

  5. Pass the Three-Part Virginia DMV CDL Skills Examination

    After holding your CLP for at least fourteen days, complete the pre-trip vehicle inspection, basic vehicle backing maneuvers, and public road driving test at a DMV CDL testing facility.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How old do you need to be to get a CDL in Virginia?

You must be at least 18 years old to hold an intrastate Virginia CDL (driving solely within Virginia) and at least 21 years old to operate commercial vehicles in interstate commerce across state borders.

Q2: How long must you hold a Virginia CLP before taking the road test?

Under Virginia DMV law, you must hold your Commercial Learner Permit (CLP) for a mandatory minimum of fourteen consecutive days before you are eligible to take the CDL skills road examination.

Q3: Is ELDT training mandatory in Virginia?

Yes, completing an FMCSA-approved Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT) program is a mandatory federal requirement for all first-time Class A and Class B CDL applicants before taking the road test.

Q4: What happens if you fail the air brake test on the Virginia CDL exam?

The air brake inspection is a mandatory zero-tolerance safety test. Failing to correctly conduct any step of the static leak, warning buzzer, or emergency spring brake test results in an immediate exam failure.

Q5: Can you drive a manual transmission truck if you test in an automatic?

No, if you perform your Virginia DMV skills test in a commercial vehicle equipped with an automatic transmission, your CDL will carry an "E" restriction prohibiting you from operating manual transmission trucks.

Q6: What documents are required to apply for a Virginia CLP?

You must present your valid Virginia driver license, proof of legal U.S. presence (birth certificate or passport), Social Security card, two proofs of Virginia residency, and your DOT Medical Examiner Certificate.

Q7: How much does it cost to get a CDL in Virginia?

Virginia DMV testing and license fees total approximately $100 to $120, while accredited CDL driving school tuition typically ranges from $3,000 to $6,000 depending on the training provider.

Q8: How long is a Virginia CDL valid?

A standard Virginia Commercial Driver License is valid for eight years, although drivers must renew their DOT medical physical certification every twenty-four months to maintain valid operating privileges.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Securing a Commercial Driver License in Virginia demands discipline, mechanical comprehension, and strict adherence to state and federal safety regulations. By preparing rigorously for written knowledge tests, investing in accredited FMCSA ELDT behind-the-wheel instruction, and practicing pre-trip vehicle inspections diligently, you position yourself for a safe and lucrative career in transportation. Maintain an exemplary driving record, prioritize defensive driving on the highway, and take pride in operating as a certified commercial driver in the Commonwealth of Virginia.
