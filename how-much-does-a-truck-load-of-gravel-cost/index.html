How Much Does a Truck Load of Gravel Cost?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a truck load of gravel cost requires examining aggregate material type, dump truck payload capacity (in tons or cubic yards), and delivery distance from the quarry. On average, a standard tandem dump truck load of gravel carrying 12 to 15 tons (roughly 10 to 12 cubic yards) costs between $350 and $850 fully delivered. Basic crushed run / crusher run gravel costs $300 to $600 per truckload, while decorative river rock, marble chips, or pea gravel ranges from $600 to $1,200 delivered.

Gravel Types: Crusher Run, Crushed Stone #57, Pea Gravel, and River Rock

The specific aggregate grade selected dictates both material pricing and compaction performance. For driveway foundations, parking pads, and sub-bases, Crusher Run (also known as road bond, ABC stone, or 21A) is the industry standard. Composed of crushed limestone or granite blended with stone dust, crusher run locks together when rolled to create an impervious, hard-packed surface ($20 to $35 per ton delivered, or $280 to $500 per tandem load).

Clean crushed stone—such as AASHTO #57 crushed limestone or washed trap rock (3/4-inch to 1-inch stones washed clean of stone dust)—costs $30 to $50 per ton ($400 to $700 per truckload), serving as the premier material for drainage French drains, driveway top layers, and concrete mixing. Smooth decorative stones like rounded pea gravel, decomposed granite, and rainbow river rock command $45 to $85 per ton ($600 to $1,200 per truckload).

Dump Truck Capacities: Single-Axle, Tandem, and Tri-Axle Loads

Quarries and aggregate haulers bill gravel deliveries based on dump truck capacity and certified scale weight tickets. A small single-axle dump truck carries 5 to 7 tons (4 to 5 cubic yards) and is ideal for small residential landscape jobs or tight driveways. A standard tandem-axle dump truck hauls 12 to 16 tons (9 to 12 cubic yards), representing the most economical delivery option for residential gravel driveway installations.

Large commercial projects utilize heavy tri-axle dump trucks (carrying 18 to 22 tons) or semi-end dump trailers (hauling 24 to 28 tons). While larger trucks deliver a lower price per ton, a fully loaded tri-axle truck weighs over 60,000 pounds, posing significant risks of cracking standard 4-inch residential concrete driveways or breaking underground irrigation pipes if backed onto residential yards.

Tailgate Spreading, Delivery Logistics, and Site Access

When ordering a truckload of gravel, homeowners can often request a 'tailgate spread' at no additional charge or for a nominal $25 to $50 driver gratuity. An experienced dump truck driver chains the tailgate open a few inches, elevates the dump bed, and slowly drives forward down the driveway, spreading the gravel in an even 2 to 3-inch layer across the roadbed. Tailgate spreading saves homeowners dozens of hours of brutal wheelbarrow and shovel labor.

However, tailgate spreading requires unobstructed overhead clearance. The truck bed elevates 18 to 22 feet into the air during discharge; overhead electrical power lines, telephone drops, or low-hanging tree branches make dumping impossible. Furthermore, steep slopes, soft muddy soil, and sharp driveway radii can prevent heavy tandem trucks from entering, forcing the driver to dump the load in a single pile at the street apron.

How to Calculate and Order a Truckload of Gravel

Follow these five tactical steps to estimate tonnage and order bulk gravel.

  1. Calculate Cubic Yards and Convert to Tons

    Multiply length by width by depth in feet, divide by 27 to get cubic yards, and multiply by 1.4 to calculate tons required.

  2. Select the Proper Stone Grade for the Base vs. Surface

    Order dense-grade crusher run for the compacted sub-base, and clean #57 crushed stone for the top driving layer.

  3. Inspect Delivery Clearance and Overhead Power Lines

    Ensure the driveway path has 10 feet of horizontal width and 20 feet of vertical clearance free of utility lines.

  4. Request a Tailgate Spread from the Dispatcher

    Ask the quarry dispatch desk if the driver is permitted to perform a chained tailgate spread down your driveway.

  5. Rent a Plate Compactor for Crusher Run Bases

    Rent a vibratory plate compactor or roller ($75 to $120/day) to pack crusher run gravel into a rock-solid, rut-free base.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a full dump truck of gravel cost?

A standard tandem dump truck carrying 12 to 15 tons typically costs between $350 and $850 fully delivered depending on stone type.

Q2: How many tons of gravel are in a dump truck?

A standard tandem dump truck carries 12 to 16 tons; single-axle trucks hold 5 to 7 tons; tri-axles hold 18 to 22 tons.

Q3: How much area does a dump truck of gravel cover?

A 14-ton tandem load covers roughly 1,200 to 1,400 square feet at a 2-inch depth, or 800 to 900 square feet at a 3-inch depth.

Q4: What is the cheapest gravel for a driveway?

Crusher run (crushed stone with stone dust binder) is the cheapest and most stable, costing $20 to $35 per ton delivered.

Q5: Can a dump truck driver spread the gravel?

Yes, drivers frequently perform a chained tailgate spread while driving forward, provided overhead trees and power lines are clear.

Q6: Can a loaded gravel truck damage my driveway?

Yes, a loaded tandem or tri-axle truck weighs 45,000 to 65,000 lbs and can easily crack 4-inch concrete driveways or break septic pipes.

Q7: What is the difference between gravel and crushed stone?

Gravel is natural, rounded river stone shaped by water erosion; crushed stone is mechanically quarried rock with sharp angular locking edges.

Q8: How thick should a gravel driveway be?

A durable gravel driveway should be 6 to 8 inches thick total: 4 inches of compacted crusher run base topped with 2 to 3 inches of clean #57 stone.
