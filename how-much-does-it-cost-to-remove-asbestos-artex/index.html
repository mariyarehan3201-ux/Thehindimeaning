Cost to Remove Asbestos Artex Ceilings

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 23, 2026

The cost to remove asbestos Artex ceilings ranges widely depending on whether you choose complete physical scraping, wet chemical stripping, or non-destructive overboarding with new plasterboard, averaging between forty and ninety dollars per square meter.

Understanding Asbestos in Textured Artex Finishes

Artex was a dominant decorative textured wall and ceiling coating widely applied throughout residential and commercial properties from the nineteen fifties through the late nineteen eighties. Valued for its ability to disguise uneven plasterwork and produce artistic swirl or stipple patterns, Artex coatings formulated prior to nineteen eighty-five frequently incorporated chrysotile asbestos fibers to improve structural bonding and tensile durability.

Because the asbestos concentration in Artex is typically between one and four percent, the material is technically classified as non-friable when sound and undamaged. However, undertaking mechanical scraping, sanding, or aggressive drilling converts the coating into a friable hazard, liberating millions of respirable fibers into the indoor atmosphere. Homeowners evaluating renovation plans must weigh removal expenses against safer encapsulation methods.

Cost Drivers: Labor, Waste Disposal, and Clearances

When calculating the overall budget for Artex removal, several logistical factors contribute to the final invoice. Setting up a certified containment zone requires laying polythene sheeting across all floor surfaces, sealing perimeter doors with heavy tape, and operating negative air machines equipped with HEPA filtration systems to catch any fugitive dust.

Furthermore, disposal fees represent a substantial component of total abatement expenses. Because Artex debris is categorized as hazardous industrial waste, it cannot be discarded in standard domestic skips. Certified contractors must double-bag the scraped material in red UN-approved hazardous waste sacks and transport it to licensed hazardous waste disposal facilities accompanied by official consignment notes.

Overboarding as a Cost-Effective Alternative

For many property owners, overboarding offers an attractive, economical alternative to aggressive removal. Overboarding involves fixing new nine-millimeter or twelve-millimeter plasterboard directly over the existing Artex ceiling, screwing into the underlying timber joists with drywall screws, and finishing with a fresh skim coat of plaster.

This method avoids disturbing the bonded fibers, eliminating the need for expensive hazardous containment setups and waste disposal tariffs. However, building owners should maintain written records of the encapsulated Artex beneath the new ceiling to prevent future tradespeople from drilling or sawing through the substrate unaware.

How to Safely Manage an Asbestos Artex Ceiling

  1. Test the Coating Before Touching

    Take a small, wetted scrape sample of the textured plaster and send it to an accredited laboratory for polarized light microscopy.

  2. Compare Quotes from Licensed Firms

    Obtain detailed line-item estimates from certified hazardous abatement contractors for both full removal and overboarding.

  3. Ensure Proper Containment Setup

    Verify that the contractor establishes sealed polythene barriers and utilizes HEPA-filtered air scrubbers during scraping.

  4. Obtain Final Clearance Documentation

    Secure an independent air clearance test certificate and licensed waste consignment receipt before releasing final payment.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does all Artex ceiling coating contain asbestos?

No. Artex formulated after the mid-1980s is generally asbestos-free, but testing is mandatory for coatings applied before 1999.

Q2: Can I simply plaster directly over Artex?

Yes, provided the Artex is sound and well-adhered, you can coat it with a bonding agent and apply a fresh plaster skim.

Q3: Is it legal to scrape Artex yourself?

While homeowners may legally work on their own single-family homes, dry scraping Artex is extremely hazardous and strongly discouraged.

Q4: Why is Artex removal so expensive?

The cost reflects labor-intensive wet scraping, airtight plastic containment, HEPA equipment, and high hazardous landfill fees.

Q5: Can Artex be removed using a wallpaper steamer?

Steamers soften Artex, but the heat can loosen plasterboard underneath; specialized chemical poultices are generally safer.

Q6: Does overboarding Artex affect ceiling height?

Overboarding lowers the ceiling by approximately 15 millimeters (half an inch), which is barely noticeable in most residential rooms.

Q7: Do I have to disclose Artex when selling my house?

Yes. Property condition disclosure laws generally require sellers to disclose known or suspected hazardous materials to prospective buyers.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Determining how much it costs to remove asbestos Artex depends on choosing between complete hazardous scraping and economical overboarding. While full removal permanently eradicates toxic materials, overboarding provides a safe, smooth finish at roughly half the cost when executed properly.
