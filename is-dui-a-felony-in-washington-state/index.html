Is DUI a Felony in Washington State?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

In Washington State, a standard DUI is classified as a Gross Misdemeanor. However, under Revised Code of Washington (RCW) § 46.61.502(6), a DUI elevates into a severe Class B Felony punishable by up to 10 years in state prison if the driver has three or more prior qualifying DUI-related convictions within a 10-year lookback period, or has any prior conviction for Vehicular Homicide or Vehicular Assault.

The Statutory Thresholds: Gross Misdemeanor vs Class B Felony

Navigating the criminal justice system in the State of Washington following a Driving Under the Influence (DUI) arrest requires an immediate understanding of state criminal classifications. Under Washington law, DUI offenses are prosecuted aggressively under Revised Code of Washington (RCW) § 46.61.502.

For a first, second, or third offense without aggravating factors, a Washington DUI is charged as a Gross Misdemeanor under RCW § 9A.20.021. While a gross misdemeanor is not a felony, it is the most serious misdemeanor recognized under state law, carrying statutory maximum penalties of up to 364 days in county jail and fines up to $5,000.

However, Washington state lawmakers have systematically strengthened state DUI laws to target repeat offenders. Under RCW § 46.61.502(6), the state legislature established clear statutory triggers that instantly elevate a DUI arrest from a municipal or district court misdemeanor into a Superior Court Class B Felony. Once charged as a felony, the defendant faces state prison sentencing guidelines, permanent loss of civil rights, and mandatory incarceration under the Washington Department of Corrections (DOC). The table below delineates the statutory differences between misdemeanor and felony DUIs in Washington.

Class B Felony DUI (Repeat Offenses) 3 or more prior DUI convictions in 10 years County Superior Court Up to 10 years in DOC prison; $20,000 fine

The 10-Year Lookback Window and Prior Qualifying Offenses

To determine whether an impaired driving arrest is prosecuted as a felony under RCW § 46.61.502(6)(a), county prosecutors audit the defendant's driving and criminal history across a strict 10-Year Lookback Period.

Historically, Washington required four prior offenses; however, legislative statutory amendments lowered this threshold to three prior offenses to intensify repeat offender penalties. The lookback clock is measured from the date of the new arrest to the dates of the prior arrests that resulted in convictions. If a driver accumulates three prior convictions within that 120-month window, the current arrest is filed immediately as a felony.

Crucially, 'prior offenses' under Washington law (RCW § 46.61.5055) are not limited strictly to standard DUIs. Prosecutors can count deferred prosecution agreements, charges reduced from DUI to Physical Control of a Vehicle While Under the Influence (RCW § 46.61.504), Reckless Driving or Negligent Driving in the First Degree (RCW § 46.61.5249) that originated as DUIs, and equivalent impaired driving convictions from any other U.S. state. Sentencing Guidelines: The Washington State Prison Grid

Unlike misdemeanor DUIs where district court judges have discretion to suspend jail time in favor of electronic home monitoring (EHM), a Felony DUI conviction is governed strictly by the Washington State Sentencing Reform Act (SRA) felony sentencing grid.

Felony DUI is ranked as a Seriousness Level IV or Level V offense on the adult sentencing grid. A defendant's exact prison sentence is calculated by cross-referencing this seriousness level against their calculated Offender Score. Because a defendant facing a felony DUI already possesses multiple prior misdemeanor or felony convictions, their offender score is typically 4, 5, or higher.

Under Washington state guidelines, a defendant with an offender score of 5 or 6 faces a mandatory standard prison range of 60 to 84 months (5 to 7 years) in state prison, with zero eligibility for county jail alternative work crews or simple probation. Furthermore, upon release from prison, the Washington Department of Licensing (DOL) imposes an administrative driver license revocation of three to four years, followed by mandatory ignition interlock device (IID) installation for five to ten years.

How to Defend Against Washington State DUI Charges

Critical legal defense checklist if charged with a DUI in Washington State.

  1. Request an Administrative DOL Hearing Within 7 Days

    Submit a formal hearing request with the Washington Department of Licensing within 7 calendar days to prevent automated driver license suspension.

  2. Audit Prior Offenses for Constitutional Vulnerabilities

    Have your defense attorney review prior misdemeanor guilty pleas; if a prior plea lacked proper counsel, it cannot be used for felony enhancement.

  3. Challenge the Draeger Alcotest 9510 Breath Machine Accuracy

    Scrutinize Washington State Toxicology Laboratory calibration records, mouth alcohol slope detectors, and software telemetry logs.

  4. Explore Washington Felony Drug and Mental Health Court Options

    In qualifying counties, petition for diversion through specialized felony therapeutic courts to secure intensive inpatient treatment and avoid prison.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: When does a DUI become a felony in Washington State?

A DUI becomes a Class B Felony if you have three or more prior DUI-related convictions within 10 years, or any prior vehicular assault/homicide conviction.

Q2: What is the penalty for a felony DUI in Washington?

A Class B Felony carries up to 10 years in state prison and a $20,000 fine, with standard sentencing guidelines typically requiring 5 to 7 years in prison.

Q3: Does an out-of-state DUI count toward a felony in Washington?

Yes. Washington prosecutors count out-of-state convictions under the Interstate Driver License Compact if the out-of-state statute is substantially similar.

Q4: What is the lookback period for prior DUIs in Washington?

Washington enforces a strict 10-year (120-month) lookback period, calculated from arrest date to arrest date.

Q5: Can a deferred prosecution count as a prior offense?

Yes. A successfully completed or revoked deferred prosecution under RCW 10.05 is statutorily counted as a prior offense for felony enhancement.

Q6: Can you own a gun after a felony DUI in Washington?

No. A felony conviction permanently revokes your Second Amendment firearm rights under both Washington state and federal law.

Q7: Is vehicular assault always a felony in Washington?

Yes. Operating a motor vehicle while impaired and causing substantial bodily harm to another person is an automatic Class B Felony under RCW 46.61.522.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

While a first or second DUI in Washington State is prosecuted as a gross misdemeanor, accumulating three prior offenses within 10 years or having a prior vehicular assault conviction elevates the charge into a catastrophic Class B Felony. A felony conviction brings mandatory multi-year state prison sentences under the Washington Department of Corrections and lifetime loss of firearm rights. If you are arrested for DUI in Washington, exercise your right to remain silent and retain an experienced Washington criminal defense trial lawyer immediately.
