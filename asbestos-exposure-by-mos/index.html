Asbestos Exposure by Mos

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Evaluating asbestos exposure by MOS (Military Occupational Specialty) is an essential legal and medical process for veterans of the United States Armed Forces seeking disability compensation through the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA). For decades spanning World War II, the Korean War, the Vietnam War, and the Cold War, the military mandated the use of asbestos across naval warships, aircraft, combat vehicles, and military base infrastructure. Because certain military occupational specialties handled asbestos directly, the VA maintains specific exposure probabilities based on service codes.

Navy Ratings and Shipboard Service Exposures

Veterans who served in the United States Navy and Coast Guard represent the single largest group of military personnel impacted by asbestos-related diseases. Naval combat vessels were floating complexes of high-temperature steam propulsion systems, nuclear reactors, diesel engines, and miles of steam piping wrapped in amosite and chrysotile insulation. In the cramped, poorly ventilated confines of ship engine rooms, boiler spaces, and auxiliary compartments, personnel breathed dense airborne dust during daily operations and scheduled overhaul overhauls.

The VA recognizes several Navy enlisted ratings as carrying high presumptive asbestos exposure risks. Boiler Technicians (BT) and Machinist's Mates (MM) operated and repacked boiler fireboxes, steam turbines, and feed pumps using raw asbestos packing and gaskets. Hull Maintenance Technicians (HT) and Damage Controlmen (DC) welded, cut, and repaired pipe systems and thermal fire curtains, while Electrician's Mates (EM) pulled electrical cabling through asbestos-lined bulkhead transits.

Army MOS and Marine Corps Occupational Specialties

While Navy veterans faced the most concentrated maritime exposures, hundreds of thousands of soldiers and Marines serving in the US Army and Marine Corps were heavily exposed across construction, vehicle repair, and artillery specialties. Army Combat Engineers (MOS 12B) and General Construction Specialists (MOS 12W) erected military encampments, aircraft hangars, and barracks utilizing asbestos transite panels, roofing shingles, and acoustic insulation without respiratory gear.

Military vehicle mechanics faced immense exposure to friction materials. Army Wheeled Vehicle Mechanics (MOS 91B), Track Vehicle Repairers (MOS 91H), and Marine Corps Motor Transport Mechanics (MOS 3521) regularly serviced brake drums, clutch assemblies, and transmission linings on tanks, armored personnel carriers, and tactical trucks. Using compressed air hoses to blow dust out of brake housings liberated millions of respirable fibers into enclosed maintenance bays.

VA Disability Claims and Service Connection Proof

Veterans diagnosed with an asbestos-related condition—such as malignant mesothelioma, asbestos lung cancer, or asbestosis—can file for comprehensive disability compensation with the Department of Veterans Affairs. Mesothelioma is granted an automatic 100% disability rating upon proof of service connection. A 100% rating provides substantial monthly tax-free disability stipends, free medical care at specialized VA medical centers, and Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC) for surviving spouses.

To establish service connection, a veteran must submit three essential elements: a certified medical diagnosis confirming the disease, military service records (DD-214) establishing their specific MOS or naval vessel assignments, and an exposure summary detailing how their military duties involved handling asbestos. Importantly, filing a VA claim does not prevent veterans from simultaneously filing civil lawsuits or bankruptcy trust claims against the private corporations that manufactured the defective military products.

How to File a VA Asbestos Claim Based on Your MOS

A step-by-step roadmap for military veterans filing service-connected asbestos claims.

  1. Obtain Certified Military Service Records (DD-214)

    Secure your official military discharge papers, service records, and duty assignment logs showing your exact MOS, rating, and ships served.

  2. Secure Certified Medical and Pathology Documentation

    Obtain biopsy reports, CT scans, and physician notes establishing a formal diagnosis of mesothelioma, lung cancer, or asbestosis.

  3. Draft a Detailed Military Exposure Statement

    Write a chronological statement explaining how your daily MOS duties (such as repairing valves, brakes, or boilers) exposed you to asbestos.

  4. Work with an Accredited Veterans Service Officer (VSO)

    Collaborate with a certified VSO from the DAV, VFW, or American Legion to assemble and submit VA Form 21-526EZ accurately.

  5. Explore External Asbestos Bankruptcy Trust Claims

    Consult an experienced asbestos attorney to file separate claims with private corporate trust funds without affecting your VA disability benefits.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Which military MOS had the highest asbestos exposure?

Navy Boiler Technicians (BT), Machinist's Mates (MM), and Hull Technicians (HT) faced the highest shipboard exposure, alongside vehicle mechanics.

Q2: Does the VA give a 100% disability rating for mesothelioma?

Yes. The VA assigns a presumptive 100% disability rating for service-connected malignant mesothelioma, paying maximum monthly benefits.

Q3: Can Army vehicle mechanics claim VA benefits for asbestos?

Yes. Army mechanics (MOS 91B/91H) who serviced asbestos brake shoes and clutches on tactical vehicles qualify for service connection.

Q4: Can I sue the US military for asbestos exposure?

No. The Feres doctrine bars service members from suing the US military directly. However, you can sue the private manufacturers who sold products to the military.

Q5: Does filing a VA claim reduce my legal settlement money?

No. VA disability compensation and private legal trust fund payouts are completely separate; receiving VA benefits does not reduce your trust recoveries.

Q6: What evidence does the VA require for an MOS claim?

The VA requires a medical diagnosis, your DD-214 showing your MOS or ship assignment, and an exposure statement detailing asbestos contact.

Q7: Can surviving spouses receive VA benefits if a veteran died?

Yes. Surviving spouses of veterans who died from service-connected asbestos diseases are eligible for monthly Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC).

Q8: Are Air Force aircraft mechanics eligible for VA asbestos benefits?

Yes. Air Force personnel (AFSC 2A) who maintained aircraft brake pads, engine firewall insulation, and heating ducts have documented exposure.
