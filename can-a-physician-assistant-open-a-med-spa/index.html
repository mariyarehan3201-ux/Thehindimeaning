Can a Physician Assistant Open a Med Spa?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Yes, a Physician Assistant (PA) can open and own a medical spa, but the permissible legal ownership structure depends strictly on your state's Corporate Practice of Medicine (CPOM) doctrine. In states with strict CPOM laws, a PA cannot directly own 100% of a medical entity and must utilize a Management Services Organization (MSO) model alongside a licensed collaborating physician medical director.

The Regulatory Landscape: Med Spas as Medical Practices

The medical aesthetics industry has experienced explosive commercial growth over the past decade. Aesthetic procedures—such as botulinum toxin injections (Botox/Dysport), hyaluronic acid dermal fillers, PDO thread lifts, medical-grade chemical peels, and ablative laser skin resurfacing—have transformed into multi-billion-dollar wellness enterprises.

Because medical spas offer luxurious, salon-like atmospheres, non-physician healthcare professionals frequently assume that med spas are retail beauty businesses. In the eyes of state medical boards and healthcare regulatory authorities, this assumption is fundamentally false. Legally, a medical spa is an active, licensed Medical Practice.

Every aesthetic injection that penetrates the human dermis and every class-4 laser treatment is classified as the practice of medicine. Consequently, establishing a medical spa requires strict compliance with state medical board administrative codes, healthcare licensing statutes, and physician supervision frameworks. For Physician Assistants (PAs) seeking entrepreneurial independence, navigating these corporate structures is the key to compliant, profitable operation. The table below delineates how state medical regulations classify medical spa ownership.

The Corporate Practice of Medicine (CPOM) Doctrine

The single most decisive legal obstacle a Physician Assistant must navigate when founding an aesthetic medical practice is the Corporate Practice of Medicine (CPOM) doctrine.

Originating in common law and codified across more than thirty states, the CPOM doctrine is designed to prevent commercial corporate entities and non-physicians from interfering with a licensed doctor's medical judgment. The doctrine establishes that only licensed medical doctors (MDs or DOs) can own, operate, and control professional medical entities (such as Professional Corporations or Professional LLCs).

In strict CPOM jurisdictions (like New York, Texas, and Illinois), a Physician Assistant cannot simply walk into the secretary of state's office and register an LLC to practice aesthetic medicine. Doing so constitutes the Unlicensed Practice of Medicine, exposing the PA to criminal prosecution, administrative medical board license revocation, and voided malpractice insurance coverage. To establish a legally compliant venture, PAs must deploy advanced healthcare corporate structuring.

The Solution: The MSO (Friendly PC) Operational Model

To overcome CPOM restrictions while retaining primary entrepreneurial control and the vast majority of business profits, healthcare attorneys utilize the Management Services Organization (MSO) Model—widely known as the 'Friendly PC-MSO' structure.

Under this dual-entity framework, two distinct business entities are created. The Physician Assistant forms and owns 100% of an ordinary business corporation or LLC (the MSO). The MSO owns the physical real estate lease, purchases or leases the aesthetic laser equipment, manages branding and marketing campaigns, employs administrative front-desk staff, and provides billing software.

Simultaneously, a licensed physician (MD or DO) forms a Professional Medical Corporation (the Friendly PC). The PC employs the clinical providers (including the PA), maintains medical malpractice policies, purchases prescription neurotoxins and fillers from pharmaceutical distributors (such as Allergan and Galderma), and holds all clinical medical records. The PC and MSO then execute an extensive, fair-market Management Services Agreement (MSA). Under the MSA, the PC pays the PA's MSO a comprehensive management fee for administrative services, effectively channeling operational profits directly to the Physician Assistant while maintaining 100% compliance with state CPOM doctrines.

How to Legally Structure a PA-Owned Medical Spa

Step-by-step corporate workflow for Physician Assistants to launch an aesthetic med spa.

  1. Audit Your State's Corporate Practice of Medicine Laws

    Retain a specialized healthcare regulatory attorney to determine whether your state enforces strict CPOM or permits partial/direct PA ownership.

  2. Form Your Management Services Organization (MSO) Entity

    Register an LLC or C-Corporation owned 100% by you to manage marketing, facilities, laser equipment leases, and administrative operations.

  3. Partner with a Licensed Collaborating Physician Medical Director

    Contract with a board-certified dermatologist, plastic surgeon, or MD to establish the Professional Medical Entity (PC/PLLC).

  4. Draft and Execute a Formal Management Services Agreement (MSA)

    Establish fair-market administrative service fees and clinical protocol guidelines governing supervision, charting, and emergency medical transfers.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can a PA own 100% of a medical spa in California?

Under California's Moscone-Knox Act, a PA can own up to 49% of a professional medical corporation, or own 100% of an MSO that manages the clinic.

Q2: Does a PA need a medical director for a med spa?

Yes. In virtually every state, PAs practice pursuant to a collaborative or supervisory agreement with a licensed physician medical director.

Q3: Can a PA inject Botox without a doctor on site?

Yes, provided the collaborating physician has established written medical protocols and is available via telecommunications for emergency consultation.

Q4: Can a PA purchase Botox and dermal fillers directly?

Pharmaceutical manufacturers require a licensed medical doctor's DEA or state medical license to establish commercial ordering accounts.

Q5: What is an MSO in a medical spa business?

A Management Services Organization is a non-clinical business entity that handles marketing, leases, and administrative operations for a medical clinic.

Q6: How much does a medical director get paid for a med spa?

Medical directors typically charge a monthly flat retainer ranging from $1,500 to $4,000 per month, depending on supervision hours and risk.

Q7: Can a PA perform laser treatments in a med spa?

Yes, PAs can operate cosmetic lasers provided they have completed certified clinical training and operate within state medical board protocols.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

A Physician Assistant can successfully open and operate a thriving medical spa, provided the business is structured in strict alignment with state Corporate Practice of Medicine laws and supervision mandates. While autonomous states permit direct ownership, strict CPOM states require deploying the time-tested MSO model with an aligned collaborating physician medical director. Partner with an experienced healthcare regulatory attorney, secure robust aesthetic malpractice insurance, draft comprehensive collaborative agreements, and build an enduring, highly lucrative aesthetic enterprise.
