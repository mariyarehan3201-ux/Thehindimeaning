I Fell at Mcdonald's on a Wet Floor

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

If you fell at McDonald's on a wet floor, you have an actionable premises liability claim against the restaurant owner for your medical bills, lost wages, and pain. Commercial fast-food restaurants have a strict legal duty to clean up drink spills, mop grease properly, and post conspicuous yellow warning signs to protect dining customers from hazardous polished tile floors.

The Unique Hazards of Fast-Food Restaurant Floors

Slipping and falling inside a McDonald's restaurant is a startling and painful experience that impacts thousands of customers every year. Unlike retail clothing stores or office buildings, commercial quick-service restaurants present an inherently treacherous floor environment. The combination of high customer foot traffic, self-service soda fountain drink dispensers, melting ice cubes, dripping umbrellas in vestibules, and airborne cooking grease drifting from fryers onto quarry tile flooring creates friction-free surfaces resembling ice skating rinks.

When an unsuspecting customer steps from an entrance mat onto freshly mopped linoleum or a spilled soft drink puddle near the beverage station, their feet fly forward violently. The resulting blunt impact against hard commercial tile often causes devastating injuries: fractured wrists from trying to break the fall, shattered kneecaps, rotator cuff shoulder tears, severe lumbar herniations, and concussions from striking the floor or dining tables.

Under American premises liability law, customers entering a McDonald's restaurant are legally classified as business invitees. This classification imposes upon the property owner and restaurant operator the highest duty of care recognized in civil tort law. Restaurant management is legally required to inspect the dining room regularly, clean spills immediately, use grease-cutting degreasers rather than dirty mops, and place prominent yellow 'Caution Wet Floor' warning cones directly over hazardous spots. Franchisee vs. Corporate Liability: Who Do You Sue?

A critical legal complexity in filing a claim against McDonald's is understanding corporate franchise architecture. Over 90 percent of all McDonald's restaurant locations in the United States are not owned or operated directly by the McDonald's Corporation based in Chicago. Instead, they are owned and operated by independent franchisee entities (e.g., 'Smith Family Enterprises LLC d/b/a McDonald's').

When you file a personal injury claim or lawsuit, naming the correct corporate legal entity is paramount. If you sue only the global McDonald's Corporation, corporate defense attorneys will file an immediate motion for summary judgment to dismiss the lawsuit, arguing that the global franchisor does not exercise day-to-day operational control over store cleaning, employee scheduling, or mop maintenance. An experienced personal injury lawyer conducts a state corporate records search to identify the exact franchisee LLC holding the commercial property lease and operating license.

Furthermore, McDonald's franchisees carry substantial commercial general liability insurance policies, often administered by massive third-party claims administrators such as Gallagher Bassett or Sedgwick. These claims administrators employ aggressive defense adjusters whose sole job is to delay, devalue, or deny your slip and fall claim. Proving Constructive Notice and Preserving Surveillance Video

To win a slip and fall claim against a McDonald's franchisee, an injured customer must satisfy the legal burden of proving Notice. Under premises liability rules, you must prove that restaurant employees either created the hazardous condition (actual notice) or that the puddle sat on the floor for a sufficient length of time that employees should have discovered and cleaned it had they followed reasonable inspection protocols (constructive notice).

The single most powerful weapon to prove constructive notice is McDonald's in-store Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) security footage. Modern fast-food restaurants operate high-definition ceiling cameras covering the ordering counter, dining area, and drink stations. Security footage can clearly show that a customer spilled a soft drink at 12:15 PM and that multiple McDonald's employees walked past the spill over the next 35 minutes without placing a yellow cone or mopping the liquid, until you slipped at 12:50 PM.

However, commercial digital video recording systems automatically overwrite older surveillance footage within 14 to 30 days. To prevent the franchisee from erasing this video, your personal injury attorney must immediately dispatch a formal legal Spoliation Letter demanding the preservation of all interior video footage from two hours before your fall to one hour after, alongside the shift sweep inspection logs and employee timecards.

How to File a Slip and Fall Claim Against McDonald's

Step-by-step procedure to document a wet floor fall at McDonald's and secure a full personal injury settlement.

  1. Photograph the Wet Floor and Surroundings Instantly

    Use your smartphone to photograph the liquid puddle, absence of yellow warning cones, and tire marks or shoe prints through the spill.

  2. Insist on Filing a Written Store Incident Report

    Demand that the store general manager complete an official incident report, obtain the manager's name, and request a copy of the report.

  3. Identify Nearby Independent Witnesses

    Obtain names and telephone numbers of other dining patrons or delivery drivers who saw you fall or witnessed the wet floor.

  4. Serve an Immediate CCTV Video Spoliation Letter

    Have your personal injury attorney send a formal legal preservation notice to prevent the restaurant from overwriting security camera video.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I sue McDonald's if there was a yellow 'Wet Floor' sign posted?

Yes, you can still sue if the warning cone was placed in an obscure corner, hidden behind a trash can, or if the entire dining room was mopped without safe pathways.

Q2: Who pays my medical bills after a fall at McDonald's?

The franchisee's commercial liability insurance carrier pays for medical bills, lost wages, and pain, typically disbursed in a final negotiated settlement.

Q3: What is Gallagher Bassett in a McDonald's claim?

Gallagher Bassett is a major third-party claims administrator hired by McDonald's franchisees to investigate, defend, and settle customer injury claims.

Q4: Should I give a recorded statement to the McDonald's insurance adjuster?

No. You should decline recorded statements until you have consulted a personal injury attorney, as adjusters use recorded statements to shift fault to you.

Q5: How long does McDonald's keep surveillance video footage?

Most restaurant camera systems overwrite digital footage within 14 to 30 days unless an attorney serves an immediate formal legal spoliation notice.

Q6: How much is an average McDonald's slip and fall settlement worth?

Settlements range from $15,000 to $45,000 for soft tissue injuries, while cases requiring surgery (fractures, spinal disc tears) often settle between $75,000 and $250,000+.

Q7: How long do I have to file a lawsuit after falling at McDonald's?

The statute of limitations for personal injury lawsuits typically ranges from two to three years from the date of the fall depending on your state.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Suffering a slip and fall on a wet floor at McDonald's can result in painful fractures, head trauma, and expensive medical bills. You have a legal right to seek full financial compensation, but navigating franchisee corporate entities and aggressive claims administrators like Gallagher Bassett requires strategic action. Take photos of the wet tile before it is mopped, demand that the restaurant manager file an official incident report, seek emergency medical care immediately, and retain an attorney to preserve critical surveillance footage.
