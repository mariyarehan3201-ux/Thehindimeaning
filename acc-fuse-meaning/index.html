Defining the ACC Fuse: Switched 12-Volt Accessory Power

In automotive electrical engineering, ACC stands for Accessory. The ACC fuse protects the low-voltage electrical circuit that supplies power to non-essential vehicle accessories when the ignition key is turned to the ACC (Accessory) position or when the vehicle is running.

Unlike constant battery circuits (labeled BATT or B+) that deliver uninterrupted 12-volt power 24 hours a day even when the car is parked and locked, an ACC circuit is a switched circuit. Electrical current only flows through the ACC fuse when the ignition cylinder is in the ACC or ON/RUN positions. Turning the key to OFF opens the circuit, cutting power to prevent connected electronic devices from draining the vehicle starting battery overnight.

Switched ACC vs Constant BATT Power: Tapping Dashcams and Accessories

Understanding the difference between ACC and BATT fuses is essential when installing aftermarket electronics, such as two-channel dashcams with parking mode, stereo amplifiers, or radar detectors. A dashcam hardwire kit features three distinct wires: Ground (Black), ACC Switched (Red), and Battery Constant (Yellow).

Connecting the red wire to an ACC fuse ensures the dashcam powers up automatically when you start the car and shuts down when you turn the engine off. The yellow wire connects to an unswitched constant battery fuse (such as hazard lights or interior dome lights), providing trickle current so the camera can record parking security footage if G-sensors detect an impact while parked. Tapping the wrong fuse will either cause parasitic battery drain or prevent parking mode from functioning.

Compare automotive fuse circuit types found in vehicle junction blocks:

BATT / CONSTANT (Always Hot) Active 24/7 regardless of ignition position BATT, HAZARD, DOME, STOP, HORN Dashcam parking surveillance, alarm systems, stereo memory

Always use dedicated add-a-circuit fuse taps with appropriate amperage ratings to prevent electrical shorts and automotive fire hazards.

How to Test and Replace a Blown ACC Fuse

Follow this step-by-step diagnostic sequence using a 12-volt test light or multimeter.

  1. Locate the Interior Cabin Fuse Box

    Find the interior fuse panel, typically located beneath the driver side dashboard or behind the coin tray near the driver left knee.

  2. Consult the Fuse Box Diagram Lid

    Inspect the printed diagram on the inside of the fuse box cover to find the exact location of the fuse labeled 'ACC', 'CIG', or 'OUTLET'.

  3. Turn Ignition Key to the ACC Position

    Turn the ignition key to ACC so electrical current energizes the accessory bus bar.

  4. Test Both Metal Probes on Top of the Fuse

    Touch a 12V test light to both exposed metal test pins on the fuse back; if one side lights up but the other does not, the fuse is blown.

  5. Extract with Fuse Puller and Replace with Identical Amperage

    Use the plastic fuse puller clip inside the engine fuse box to remove the blown fuse; replace it with a new fuse of matching amperage rating.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does ACC fuse mean on a car?

ACC fuse stands for Accessory fuse, protecting the switched 12-volt circuit that powers radios, 12V outlets, and accessories only when the key is in ACC or RUN.

Q2: What happens when the ACC fuse blows?

Your radio, 12V cigarette lighter power sockets, power mirrors, and USB charging ports will stop working, while the engine continues to run normally.

Q3: What is the difference between ACC and BATT fuses?

ACC fuses only have power when the ignition key is on; BATT fuses have continuous power 24/7 even when the car is parked and turned off.

Q4: Can I replace a 15A ACC fuse with a 20A or 30A fuse?

Never. Installing a higher amperage fuse allows excessive current through the wires, creating a severe fire hazard; always replace with the identical rating.

Q5: Why does my ACC fuse keep blowing?

Repeated blown fuses indicate an electrical short circuit, a damaged phone charger plugged into the 12V port, or overloaded aftermarket accessories.

Q6: Which fuse should I tap for a dashcam?

Tap an ACC fuse (like CIG or RADIO) for standard driving recording, and a BATT fuse (like DOME or HAZARD) for parking surveillance mode.

Q7: Where is the ACC fuse usually located?

The ACC fuse is almost always located in the interior passenger cabin fuse panel beneath the steering column or glove compartment.

Q8: Can a blown ACC fuse cause a car not to start?

No. The ACC circuit powers non-essential convenience accessories; starter, ignition, and fuel pump circuits operate on independent high-amperage fuses.
