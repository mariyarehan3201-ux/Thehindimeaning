Sit Means Sit Prices

Meaning
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Sit Means Sit is one of the largest commercial franchised dog training corporations in the United States and Canada, operating over one hundred and forty locations. Known for its proprietary remote electronic collar (e-collar) conditioning system, Sit Means Sit promises rapid off-leash obedience, reliable recall under intense distraction, and behavioral rehabilitation for reactive dogs. Prospective pet owners frequently inquire about Sit Means Sit training costs, program structures, and proprietary methodologies when evaluating obedience options for their canine companions.

Cost Breakdown and Program Tiers at Sit Means Sit

Because Sit Means Sit operates as a franchise model where individual locations are independently owned and operated, exact pricing varies based on local market cost of living, the dog's age, and the severity of behavioral issues. However, nationwide pricing trends establish predictable fee ranges. Training begins with a mandatory, complimentary in-home or facility evaluation, during which a trainer assesses the dog and pitches tailored package options.

Entry-level private lesson packages (typically consisting of three to five private one-on-one sessions paired with a proprietary collar) generally range from $800 to $1,500. Comprehensive Day-and-Train packages (where dogs attend day school several days a week) range between $1,500 and $2,800. Their premier 'Board and Train' (immersion) programs—spanning one to three weeks of residential boarding—cost between $2,500 and $4,500+.

The Proprietary Training Methodology: Remote Collar Conditioning

The foundation of the Sit Means Sit methodology is its proprietary low-level electronic remote collar system. Unlike old-fashioned shock collars that delivered high-intensity punishment, Sit Means Sit trainers utilize micro-level electronic muscle stimulation (TENS technology) adjusted to the dog's lowest sensory perception threshold. The sensation is comparable to a gentle tapping on the shoulder.

The micro-stimulation acts as a tactile attention cue (conditioned negative reinforcement / prompt). When the dog complies with the verbal command (e.g. 'Come' or 'Sit'), the micro-tap immediately stops, and the dog is showered with enthusiastic verbal praise and food rewards. Once conditioned, dogs achieve reliable off-leash obedience even in the presence of intense distractions like running squirrels, passing bicycles, or other barking dogs.

Pet owners should always observe an active group class at their local franchise location before signing contracts to assess the trainers' patience, warmth, and handling style.

How to Evaluate and Budget for Professional Dog Training

Follow these steps when shopping for dog training programs and negotiating costs.

  1. Step 1

    Schedule the free evaluation: have all household family members attend to evaluate the trainer's communication style.

  2. Step 2

    Ask for a transparent, itemized written price quote detailing all equipment fees, boarding costs, and follow-up lessons.

  3. Step 3

    Inquire whether lifetime weekly group reinforcement classes are included in the package price.

  4. Step 4

    Ask the trainer to demonstrate the remote collar sensation on your own hand, verifying the micro-tap sensory level.

  5. Step 5

    Check online Google and Yelp reviews specifically for your local franchise branch rather than national ads.

  6. Step 6

    Confirm refund policies and health guarantees in case your dog experiences kennel illness during a board-and-train.

  7. Step 7

    Commit to practicing daily for 15 minutes at home; training fails if owners do not consistently reinforce lessons.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does Sit Means Sit cost on average?

Prices typically range from $800 to $1,500 for private lessons, and $2,500 to $4,500+ for comprehensive Board-and-Train immersion packages.

Q2: Does Sit Means Sit use shock collars?

They use proprietary remote electronic collars that deliver low-level micro-stimulation (similar to a TENS machine) used as an attention tap rather than high pain punishment.

Q3: Is the first evaluation free at Sit Means Sit?

Yes, virtually all Sit Means Sit franchise locations offer a free, no-obligation in-home or facility behavioral consultation.

Q4: How long are Sit Means Sit board and train programs?

Board-and-train immersion programs typically last between one and three weeks, depending on the dog's behavioral needs.

Q5: Do you get lifetime support with Sit Means Sit?

Most premium packages include lifetime access to weekly group refresher classes and trainer phone support for the life of the dog.

Q6: Why is professional dog training so expensive?

Costs cover round-the-clock boarding care, certified trainer labor, specialized proprietary hardware, facility overhead, and ongoing lifetime group classes.

Q7: Can Sit Means Sit fix aggressive dogs?

Yes, their trainers frequently rehabilitate dog-reactive and aggressive canines by establishing clear off-leash focus and impulse control.
