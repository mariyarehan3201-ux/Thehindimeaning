How to Replace Asbestos Shingles

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 23, 2026

Replacing asbestos shingles on residential exteriors is a common home improvement project that upgrades structural weatherproofing while eliminating hazardous building materials. Widely installed between the 1930s and 1970s under trade names like Transite, asbestos-cement shingles are rigid, brittle panels composed of Portland cement reinforced with chrysotile fibers. Successfully replacing these shingles requires careful wet detachment, compliant disposal, and the installation of modern fiber-cement or vinyl exterior cladding.

Evaluating Siding Options: Complete Abatement Versus Spot Replacement

Homeowners facing aging asbestos exterior shingles typically choose between two strategic approaches: complete exterior replacement or localized spot repairs. Complete replacement involves systematically stripping all existing siding down to the wooden wall sheathing, installing modern moisture barriers, and hanging new siding. This approach permanently eliminates asbestos from the property, resolves real estate disclosure concerns, and allows for exterior wall insulation upgrades.

Conversely, if the majority of shingles remain in good condition with only a few cracked or broken tiles from storm damage, spot replacement is a viable alternative. Specialized building supply companies manufacture non-asbestos fiber-cement replacement shingles with matching wavy edges, woodgrain embossing, and dimensions that blend seamlessly with vintage siding without necessitating whole-house remediation.

Safe Removal Techniques and Fastener Extraction

When removing individual or whole-wall asbestos shingles, the fundamental safety objective is to detach tiles whole without cracking or snapping them. Asbestos-cement shingles are classified as Category II non-friable materials; they release hazardous fibers only when fractured, sawed, or crushed. Standard claw hammers should never be used to pry against the tile surface, as the localized leverage will crack the brittle cement panel.

Instead, professionals utilize a specialized roofing ripper or siding shingle nail puller. The thin blade of the tool slides beneath the overlapping upper shingle to hook the hidden fastener nail, cutting or pulling it without exerting bending force against the asbestos tile. Technicians continuously mist the shingle edges with water mixed with dish soap to suppress any microscopic dust particles generated at the nail holes.

How to Safely Remove and Replace Damaged Asbestos Shingles

Careful, non-destructive protocol for extracting damaged exterior asbestos shingles and hanging modern replacements.

  1. Lay Ground Containment Plastic

    Spread 6-mil polyethylene plastic sheeting on the ground beneath the work area to catch any accidental chips or falling fasteners.

  2. Saturate Fasteners with Water

    Mist the damaged shingle and the overlapping upper seam with water mixed with surfactant soap to suppress dust.

  3. Slide Shingle Ripper Beneath Tile

    Slide a thin shingle nail ripper beneath the upper overlapping tile, hook the exposed roofing nail, and tap the tool handle to cut the nail.

  4. Gently Slide Out Damaged Shingle

    Carefully wiggle the loosened shingle downward and remove it whole, placing it directly into a labeled 6-mil hazardous waste bag.

  5. Install Modern Fiber-Cement Tile

    Slide a non-asbestos replica fiber-cement shingle into position, secure it with hot-dipped galvanized siding nails, and caulk nail heads.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I replace asbestos shingles myself?

Many jurisdictions permit homeowners to replace shingles on single-family homes, provided non-destructive wet methods and proper disposal are used.

Q2: What is the best replacement for asbestos siding shingles?

Non-asbestos fiber-cement shingles (such as GAF Weatherside or James Hardie) are the best replacement, matching vintage sizes and wavy edges.

Q3: Can you drill or cut replacement asbestos shingles?

Never use power saws or drills on vintage asbestos shingles; use manual guillotine-style score-and-snap cutters to avoid generating airborne dust.

Q4: Can I install vinyl siding directly over asbestos shingles?

Yes, siding over intact asbestos shingles using foam backer board is a common, cost-effective encapsulation method permitted in many areas.

Q5: How do you dispose of removed asbestos siding shingles?

Shingles must be double-bagged in 6-mil poly bags or wrapped in bundles, labeled, and taken to an authorized landfill that accepts asbestos.

Q6: Are asbestos siding shingles fireproof?

Yes, asbestos-cement shingles are completely non-combustible and fireproof, which made them popular for residential construction before 1975.

Q7: Why do asbestos shingles become brittle over time?

Decades of sun, rain, and thermal cycling dry out the Portland cement binder, making vintage shingles brittle and prone to cracking.

Q8: How much does it cost to replace asbestos siding on a whole house?

Complete removal of asbestos siding typically costs $4,000 to $10,000, plus $8,000 to $20,000 for installing new vinyl or fiber-cement cladding.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Replacing asbestos shingles modernizes your home's exterior envelope while permanently eliminating hazardous mineral siding. Whether executing localized repairs using fiber-cement replica shingles or undertaking complete exterior abatement, prioritizing gentle, non-destructive detachment methods is paramount. Always mist tiles before handling, pull fasteners carefully, seal removed panels in heavy polyethylene wrap, and verify local hazardous waste disposal regulations.
