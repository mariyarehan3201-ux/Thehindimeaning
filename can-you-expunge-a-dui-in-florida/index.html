Can You Expunge a DUI in Florida?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Under Florida law, you cannot expunge or seal a formal DUI conviction. Florida Statute § 316.193 strictly mandates that courts cannot withhold adjudication for Driving Under the Influence. However, if your DUI charge was successfully dismissed, dropped, or negotiated down to a lesser charge of Reckless Driving with adjudication withheld, you may be fully eligible to seal and expunge your record.

The Harsh Reality of Florida DUI Record Sealing Laws

Living with a permanent criminal record in the State of Florida creates crushing personal, social, and economic burdens. Prospective employers, residential apartment landlords, commercial banks, and state licensing boards routinely conduct computerized criminal background checks through the Florida Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE).

When individuals are arrested for Driving Under the Influence (DUI), their primary hope after completing probation and community service is clearing their public record. However, Florida maintains some of the most punitive and unforgiving DUI record laws in the United States.

Under Florida Statute § 943.0585 (Court-Ordered Expunction) and Florida Statute § 943.059 (Court-Ordered Sealing), eligibility to clear a criminal history record hinges entirely on one foundational legal requirement: the defendant must never have been formally 'convicted' or adjudicated guilty of the offense. In most criminal misdemeanor cases, judges have the legal discretion to 'withhold adjudication,' allowing first-time offenders to seal their records. However, under Florida Statute § 316.193, Florida state law explicitly strips judges of this power for DUIs. The 'Wet Reckless' Gateway to Clearing Your Florida Record

Because a formal DUI conviction is a permanent, non-sealable legal stain under Florida jurisprudence, the primary strategic objective of a skilled Florida DUI defense attorney is negotiating a Charge Reduction before a conviction occurs.

In Florida criminal practice, experienced defense attorneys negotiate with county prosecutors (State Attorneys) to reduce a pending DUI charge to Reckless Driving under Florida Statute § 316.192—often colloquially termed a 'Wet Reckless.' Unlike DUI charges, Florida law explicitly permits presiding judges to Withhold Adjudication on a Reckless Driving plea.

When the judge withholds adjudication, you are not formally convicted of a crime under Florida law. Once you successfully complete all negotiated terms of probation—such as paying court fines, completing a 12-hour Florida DUI Level 1 education school, performing 50 hours of community service, and submitting to random substance testing—you become legally eligible to petition the circuit court to Seal your criminal record under Fla. The Expungement Application Process with the FDLE

If your DUI charge was successfully dismissed by prosecutors, dropped via nolle prosequi, acquitted at trial, or reduced to a withhold of adjudication on reckless driving, executing the record clearing process requires strict administrative compliance.

The process begins by applying for a formal Certificate of Eligibility from the Florida Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE) in Tallahassee. The applicant must submit a certified copy of the case disposition from the county clerk of court, a complete set of official fingerprint cards taken by a law enforcement agency, a non-refundable $75 state processing fee, and a notarized state application form.

Upon receiving the Certificate of Eligibility from FDLE (which typically takes three to six months to process), your defense attorney drafts and files a formal Petition to Expunge or Seal Criminal History in the circuit court where the arrest occurred, accompanied by your sworn affidavit. The judge reviews the petition and issues a signed Court Order. This certified order is served on the arresting police department, county jail, and state agencies, commanding them to seal or physically expunge all digital booking photos, arrest blotters, and court records.

How to Seal or Expunge an Arrest Record in Florida

Step-by-step administrative roadmap to petition the FDLE and circuit court for record expunction.

  1. Obtain Certified Court Disposition from County Clerk

    Visit the clerk of court where your case was resolved to purchase a certified copy proving charges were dismissed or adjudication was withheld.

  2. Complete Official Fingerprint Card at Sheriff's Office

    Have your full fingerprints rolled onto an official FDLE applicant fingerprint card at a local police station or sheriff's office.

  3. Submit Application for Certificate of Eligibility to FDLE

    Mail your application, certified disposition, fingerprint card, and $75 money order to the Florida Department of Law Enforcement.

  4. File Formal Petition to Expunge in Circuit Court

    Once the FDLE Certificate of Eligibility arrives, have your attorney file a formal petition and proposed order before the presiding circuit judge.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can a first-time DUI conviction be expunged in Florida?

No. Florida law strictly prohibits the sealing or expungement of any formal DUI conviction, regardless of whether it is a first offense.

Q2: Why can't judges withhold adjudication on a Florida DUI?

Florida Statute Section 316.193 explicitly forbids judges from withholding adjudication for DUI offenses, making a conviction mandatory upon a guilty plea.

Q3: Can I seal my record if my DUI was reduced to reckless driving?

Yes, provided the judge granted a withhold of adjudication on the reckless driving charge and you have no prior disqualifying criminal convictions.

Q4: How long does a DUI conviction stay on your record in Florida?

A DUI conviction remains on your Florida criminal history record permanently for life, and stays on your driving record for 75 years.

Q5: Can a Florida DUI be removed through a Governor's pardon?

Yes, obtaining a full pardon from the Florida Board of Executive Clemency (chaired by the Governor) can restore rights and permit record expungement.

Q6: How long does it take to expunge a record in Florida?

The complete FDLE certification and circuit court petition process typically takes between five and nine months from start to finish.

Q7: Can I legally deny my arrest after my record is expunged?

Yes. Under Florida Statute 943.0585, once an expunction order is signed, you can legally deny or fail to acknowledge the arrest occurred on job applications.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

While a formal DUI conviction can never be sealed or expunged under Florida law, facing an arrest does not guarantee a permanent criminal record. If your charges are dismissed, acquitted, or successfully negotiated down to reckless driving with adjudication withheld, legal expungement is fully attainable. Retaining an aggressive Florida DUI defense attorney before pleading guilty is the single most critical step to protecting your public record, professional licensing, and future freedom.
