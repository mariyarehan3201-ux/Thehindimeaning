The Georgia Implied Consent Law Means

Meaning
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Under Georgia motor vehicle jurisprudence (codified in Official Code of Georgia Annotated § 40-5-67.1 and § 40-5-55), the 'Georgia Implied Consent Law' establishes that any person who operates a motor vehicle upon the public highways of the state is legally deemed to have given implied consent to chemical testing of their breath, blood, or urine for the purpose of determining alcohol or drug content if lawfully arrested for DUI. Refusing to submit to chemical testing after being read the statutory advisory notice triggers an immediate, mandatory twelve-month administrative suspension of your driver's license with zero eligibility for a limited driving permit.

The Georgia Implied Consent Notice: Exact Statutory Phrasing

A fundamental legal requirement under Georgia DUI law is that law enforcement officers must read the exact statutory Implied Consent Notice to a suspected driver at the time of, or immediately following, a lawful DUI arrest. Georgia law prescribes three distinct statutory notices: one for drivers aged twenty-one or older, one for commercial motor vehicle operators (CDL), and one for drivers under twenty-one years of age. If an officer fails to read the warning, delays reading it unreasonably, or alters the statutory wording, the chemical test results or refusal can be suppressed in court.

The statutory notice informs the driver that Georgia law requires them to submit to state-administered chemical tests designated by the officer (typically an Intoxilyzer 9000 breath test at the police station or a clinical blood draw). It explicitly warns that refusing testing will result in a one-year license suspension, that refusal can be used as evidence of guilt in a criminal trial, and that submitting to the state's test entitles the driver to an independent chemical test of their own choosing at their own expense.

The 30-Day Deadline: DDS Appeals Versus Ignition Interlock Permits

Following a DUI arrest in Georgia where a driver either refuses chemical testing or registers an unlawful breath alcohol concentration (0.08 or greater), the officer confiscates the physical plastic driver's license and issues a yellow Form DDS-1205. This document serves as a temporary forty-five-day driving permit and officially notifies the driver that the Georgia Department of Driver Services (DDS) will suspend their license on the forty-sixth day.

Under Georgia law, the driver has exactly thirty calendar days from the date of arrest to take formal administrative action. If the driver does nothing, an automatic twelve-month suspension takes effect. To avoid losing all driving privileges, the driver must choose one of two legal pathways within thirty days: file a formal $150 ALS Appeal requesting an administrative hearing before an Administrative Law Judge (ALJ) to challenge the arrest legality, or opt for an Ignition Interlock Device Limited Permit (IIDLP) requiring installation of a breath interlock device in their vehicle for twelve months.

How to Handle the Georgia Implied Consent Warning and Save Your License

Critical legal defense checklist for motorists arrested for DUI in Georgia.

  1. Request an Independent Chemical Test if You Blow

    If you agree to take the state's Intoxilyzer breath test, immediately state: 'I demand an independent blood test at a hospital of my choosing.'

  2. Secure the Yellow Form DDS-1205

    Keep the yellow carbon copy of Form DDS-1205 given to you upon release from jail; this is your temporary 45-day driving permit.

  3. Calculate Your Strict 30-Day Calendar Deadline

    Count 30 calendar days from the exact date stamped on your arrest citation; you must file your appeal or interlock paperwork before day 30.

  4. Hire a Specialized Georgia DUI Defense Attorney

    Retain a local defense lawyer who routinely handles DDS administrative license hearings in the county where you were arrested.

  5. File the Formal ALS Appeal Letter with $150 Fee

    Send a formal written appeal via certified mail with return receipt requested and a $150 check payable to the Department of Driver Services.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the Georgia Implied Consent Law?

It is a law stating that by driving on Georgia roads, you have automatically agreed to chemical testing (breath, blood, urine) if arrested for DUI.

Q2: What happens if you refuse the breath test in Georgia?

Refusing chemical testing results in an automatic 12-month administrative suspension of your driver's license with zero eligibility for a work permit.

Q3: Can you refuse field sobriety tests in Georgia without losing your license?

Yes. Implied consent only applies to chemical tests (breath/blood) after arrest. Roadside field sobriety exercises (walking a line, eye test) are completely voluntary.

Q4: What is the 30-day rule in Georgia for DUI?

You have exactly 30 calendar days from the date of arrest to file an ALS appeal with the DDS or install an ignition interlock device, or lose your license for a year.

Q5: Can police force a blood draw if you refuse in Georgia?

Under Georgia Supreme Court precedent (Elliott v. State), police cannot physically force a blood test unless they obtain an electronic search warrant signed by a judge.

Q6: Does Georgia give you a limited driving permit after a refusal?

No. Under standard refusal rules, it is a 'hard suspension' with no permit. However, eligible first offenders can now opt for a 12-month ignition interlock permit.

Q7: Can an officer read the implied consent notice late?

The law requires reading it 'at the time of arrest' or as soon as practicable. An unreasonable delay by the officer can result in evidence suppression in court.
