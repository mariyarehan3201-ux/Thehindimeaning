What Does 585 Mean on Jewellery?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

When the number 585 is stamped onto a ring shank, necklace clasp, earring post, or bracelet charm, it certifies that the piece is crafted from 14-karat gold with a precious metal purity of 58.5 percent. Stamped in accordance with the international millesimal fineness hallmarking system, 585 indicates that the jewelry contains exactly 585 parts of pure elemental gold per 1,000 parts of total alloy weight, with the remaining 415 parts consisting of base transition metals such as copper, silver, zinc, or nickel.

The Millesimal Fineness Standard and 14-Karat Chemistry

Gold has been valued for millennia for its brilliant luster and chemical resistance to corrosion. However, pure 24-karat gold is naturally soft and pliable, making it unsuitable for everyday jewelry items like rings and delicate prongs that must hold valuable diamonds securely. To create structural durability, metallurgists melt pure gold alongside hardening base metals. The hallmark 585 mathematically translates the traditional fractional karat system—14 divided by 24 equals 0.5833 (or 58.33 percent), which assay offices round up to a standardized 585 parts per thousand.

While jewelry manufactured in the United States is traditionally stamped with karat abbreviations like "14K," "14KT," or "14ct," European, British, and global assay offices mandate numerical millesimal fineness hallmarks like 585. This standardized numerical marking ensures international transparency, allowing consumers and appraisers worldwide to verify intrinsic metal value regardless of linguistic barriers or national borders.

Color Variations in 585 Jewelry: Yellow, White, and Rose

A frequent misconception among consumers is that 585 jewelry must always be yellow. In reality, 585 gold can be manufactured in rich yellow, cool silvery white, or coppery rose hues while maintaining the exact same 58.5 percent gold purity. The visual color is governed entirely by the specific metallurgical recipe of the remaining 41.5 percent alloy base.

In 585 yellow gold, the alloy metals are balanced equally between fine silver and copper, preserving the traditional warm luster of elemental gold. In 585 white gold, the alloy base incorporates bleaching transition metals such as nickel, zinc, or palladium to suppress the yellow tone, followed by an electroplated flash of ultra-hard rhodium to create a mirror-white finish. In 585 rose gold, copper is increased to approximately 33 to 38 percent of the alloy, yielding an attractive pinkish-red blush that remains resistant to tarnish.

Why 585 (14K) Is the Most Popular Jewelry Metal

In North America and Europe, 585 (14K) gold accounts for over 70 percent of all gold jewelry sales, particularly in bridal engagement rings and wedding bands. The reason for this dominance is the near-perfect balance it strikes between luxurious prestige, aesthetic beauty, and everyday physical durability. Higher karat alloys like 18K (750) and 22K (916) scratch and deform relatively easily, whereas lower karats like 10K (417) can look paler and feel brittle.

With a Vickers hardness score exceeding 150 HV, 585 gold holds intricate micropavé diamond prongs tightly in place, resisting the bending and prong lifting that causes lost gemstones. Furthermore, 585 gold delivers genuine investment value at roughly 30 to 40 percent lower retail cost than 18K gold, making fine jewelry accessible to a broader consumer market.

How to Authenticate and Test 585 Gold Jewelry

Follow these step-by-step jeweler methods to inspect, test, and authenticate a 585 hallmark on gold jewelry.

  1. Locate the 585 Hallmark Using Magnification

    Examine the interior band of rings or the flat tongue of chain clasps with a 10x jewelers loupe under strong white light.

  2. Perform a Rare-Earth Neodymium Magnet Test

    Bring a strong magnet near the jewelry; genuine 585 gold will display zero magnetic pull (excluding steel clasp springs).

  3. Inspect High-Friction Edges for Base Metal Wear

    Check clasp hooks and ring bottoms for peeling or green oxidation that indicates brass plating rather than solid 585 gold.

  4. Conduct a 14K Nitric Acid Touchstone Test

    Streak the metal across a ceramic testing stone and apply 14K nitric acid; genuine 585 gold will resist the acid without dissolving.

  5. Confirm Exact Purity with an XRF Spectrometer

    Have an appraiser scan the jewelry with an X-ray fluorescence spectrometer to verify that gold content reads 58.3% to 58.8%.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does 585 mean on gold jewelry?

585 means the jewelry is 14-karat gold, composed of 58.5% pure elemental gold and 41.5% other strengthening alloy metals.

Q2: Is 585 gold real gold?

Yes, 585 gold is genuine solid 14-karat gold, recognized internationally by assay offices and professional jewelers.

Q3: Is 585 gold better than 750 gold?

585 gold (14K) is harder and more scratch-resistant than 750 gold (18K), making it more durable for daily wear, though 750 has higher gold purity.

Q4: Will 585 gold tarnish or turn your finger green?

Genuine 585 gold will not tarnish or turn skin green under normal conditions due to its high noble metal content.

Q5: Does 585 mean gold-plated?

No. A standalone 585 stamp indicates solid 14K gold. Gold-plated items are marked with additional letters like 585 GP, HGE, or RGP.

Q6: Can 585 be white gold or rose gold?

Yes. 585 gold can be yellow, white, or rose depending on the mix of alloy metals like silver, nickel, or copper.

Q7: How much is 585 gold worth per gram?

Its scrap melt value equals exactly 58.5% of the current market spot price of pure 24K gold per gram.

Q8: Why do some countries use 585 instead of 14K?

European, British, and international assay systems use millesimal fineness (parts per thousand), whereas the US traditionally uses karat numbers.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

A 585 stamp on a piece of jewelry is the internationally recognized hallmark certifying 14-karat gold with 58.5 percent precious gold purity. Delivering outstanding resistance to daily scratches, long-lasting gemstone security, and radiant beauty across yellow, white, and rose variations, 585 gold represents the premier alloy standard for everyday fine jewelry.
