What Does Tiguan Mean?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

The name Volkswagen Tiguan is a distinctive portmanteau coined by combining two German words representing powerful natural beasts: Tiger (tiger) and Leguan (iguana). Introduced in 2007 as Volkswagen's compact crossover utility vehicle, the Tiguan was engineered to bridge urban agility with off-road tenacity. Rather than selecting an internal engineering designation or hiring an anonymous corporate naming agency, Volkswagen conducted an unprecedented public naming campaign, inviting automotive enthusiasts and readers of Germany's premier car magazine to christen the new crossover platform.

The Public Voting Campaign and Competing Name Contenders

In 2006, as Volkswagen prepared to unveil its new compact SUV built on the PQ35/PQ46 modular platform underneath the larger Touareg, marketing executives partnered with the German automotive publisher Auto Bild. More than 350,000 readers across Germany and international markets participated in an open public vote to select the model's official moniker. Voters were presented with five finalized conceptual names, each designed to capture different aspects of adventure, agility, and modern crossover styling.

The five shortlisted candidates were Tiguan, Namib (honoring the ancient Namib Desert in Southern Africa), Rockton (conveying rugged mountain durability), Samun (evoking hot Arabian desert winds), and Nanuk (an Inuit term for polar bear). Tiguan captured an overwhelming majority of public votes, capturing the public imagination by combining the predatory swiftness of the Tiger with the rugged, all-terrain adaptability of the Leguan (iguana).

Volkswagen's Naming Traditions: Winds, Myth, and Nature

Understanding the significance of the Tiguan name requires examining Volkswagen's rich history of vehicle etymology. Traditionally, Volkswagen named its passenger cars after prominent atmospheric winds and oceanic jet streams: Golf refers to the Gulf Stream (Golfstrom), Jetta honors the Pacific Jet Stream, Passat takes its name from German trade winds (Passatwinde), and Scirocco represents the Mediterranean desert wind. However, when transitioning into utility SUVs in the 2000s, Volkswagen shifted toward exotic cultural geography and animal symbolism.

The full-size Touareg derived its name from the nomadic Tuareg people of the Sahara Desert, symbolizing survival across hostile sand dunes. The smaller Tiguan followed this adventurous narrative through its biological fusion. By uniting the tiger's dynamic speed on tarmac with the iguana's tenacious grip on loose rocks, the name conveyed dual personality: civilized commuter during the workweek and capable trail explorer on weekends.

Today, the Tiguan stands as Volkswagen's best-selling model worldwide, surpassing even the iconic Golf in annual global production volume across European, Asian, and American manufacturing plants.

How to Discover the Heritage and Specifications of Your Tiguan

Follow these steps to learn more about the production history and trim heritage of your Volkswagen Tiguan.

  1. Step 1

    Locate the seventeen-digit Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) stamped on the driver-side lower windshield corner.

  2. Step 2

    Enter the VIN into an official Volkswagen decoder tool to identify whether your model was assembled in Wolfsburg, Germany or Puebla, Mexico.

  3. Step 3

    Check your window sticker or vehicle build sheet to identify whether your model features 4MOTION All-Wheel Drive or front-wheel drive.

  4. Step 4

    Examine the front bumper styling: European off-road trims feature a steeper approach angle bumper compared to street-styled R-Line trims.

  5. Step 5

    Inspect the infotainment screen software version to see whether your Tiguan is equipped with MIB2 or modular MIB3 connected services.

  6. Step 6

    Review your owner documentation to confirm engine configuration, spanning turbocharged 2.0L TSI gasoline or TDI diesel engines.

  7. Step 7

    Consult official Volkswagen enthusiast registries and heritage clubs to explore model generation differences between MK1 and MK2 platforms.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does the name Tiguan mean?

Tiguan is a German portmanteau created by merging Tiger (tiger) and Leguan (iguana), representing agility and all-terrain adaptability.

Q2: Who came up with the name Tiguan?

The name was chosen by over 350,000 readers of the German automotive magazine Auto Bild in an open public vote organized by Volkswagen in 2006.

Q3: What were the other name choices for the Tiguan?

The competing naming finalists were Namib, Rockton, Samun, and Nanuk.

Q4: Why didn't Volkswagen name the Tiguan after a wind?

While VW historically named sedans after winds (Golf, Jetta, Passat), it created a new naming lineage for SUVs focusing on adventure, geography, and animal strength.

Q5: How do you pronounce Tiguan correctly?

It is correctly pronounced TEE-gwahn, placing emphasis on the first syllable.

Q6: Is the Volkswagen Tiguan built on a car chassis?

Yes, modern Tiguan models are built on Volkswagen's modular transverse matrix (MQB platform), shared with the Golf, Audi Q3, and Jetta.

Q7: What is the largest market for the Volkswagen Tiguan?

China and North America represent the largest individual sales markets for long-wheelbase Tiguan variants, while Europe favors standard-wheelbase models.
