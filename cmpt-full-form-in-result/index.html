CMPT Full Form in Result: Compartment Exam Guide

Full Form
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 18, 2026

The full form of CMPT in examination results stands for Compartment (or Compartmental Examination). Used across secondary and senior secondary school examination report cards—most prominently by the Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE), the Council for the Indian School Certificate Examinations (CISCE), and various State Boards of Education—CMPT indicates that a student has passed the majority of their subjects but failed to achieve the minimum qualifying pass mark in one (or occasionally two) specific subjects, granting them an opportunity to appear for a supplementary compartmental examination without repeating the entire academic year.

Understanding Academic Abbreviations in Board Examination Results

Receiving annual board examination results is an important milestone for secondary and senior secondary school students across India. While the majority of students see the welcome status 'PASS' printed on their scorecards, others encounter standardized administrative acronyms that can cause initial confusion. Abbreviations like CMPT, RT, ER, and RW are used by educational boards to communicate specific academic outcomes.

The abbreviation CMPT indicates Compartment (or Compartmental Examination). Rather than failing a student who stumbled in a single difficult subject—such as Mathematics, Physics, or Chemistry—educational boards use the compartment mechanism as an academic safety net. CMPT status gives students a second chance to prepare, retake the examination in that single subject, and proceed with their higher education without losing a year.

Overview of Standard Examination Result Abbreviations

Educational boards, including CBSE and state councils, utilize standardized shorthand abbreviations on digital scorecards to convey official results. The table below details common result abbreviations and their academic meanings.

Result Abbreviation Full Expansion Academic Standing Next Action Required from Student
CMPT Compartment Passed majority; failed 1 subject (Class 12) or up to 2 (Class 10) Apply for supplementary compartment re-test
ER Essential Repeat Failed 3 or more subjects; year not passed Must re-enroll and repeat the entire academic year
RT Repeat Theory Failed theory examination paper while passing practicals Re-appear only for the theory examination paper
RP Repeat Practical Failed the internal school lab/practical assessment Must re-attend and pass the laboratory practical test
RW Result Withheld Result paused due to administrative check or verification Contact school principal or board regional office
ABST Absent Student was officially marked absent on examination day Apply for supplementary or next year's examination cycle

Eligibility Rules: Compartment in Class 10 vs. Class 12 (CBSE Standards)

The rules governing eligibility for compartmental examinations differ between middle secondary (Class 10) and senior secondary (Class 12). The table below outlines standard CBSE criteria governing CMPT placement.

Educational Level Maximum Allowed Failing Subjects for CMPT Passing Mark Threshold Required Practical Mark Carry-Forward Policy
Class 10 (Secondary) Failing in up to 2 subjects out of 5/6 subjects Combined 33% overall (Theory + Internal Assessment) Internal assessment scores carry forward automatically
Class 12 (Senior Secondary) Failing in exactly 1 subject out of 5 subjects Strictly 33% in Theory AND 33% in Practical separately Passed practical scores carry forward; only theory is re-tested

Strategic Preparation to Clear a Compartment Examination

Facing a compartment examination requires a focused, strategic approach to revision. Students typically have four to six weeks between the release of annual results in May and the supplementary examinations held in July. Because the student only needs to prepare for a single subject, they can dedicate all their daily study hours to addressing that specific subject.

Effective preparation includes obtaining a photocopy of the evaluated answer script from the board to understand why marks were deducted. By working through previous years' question papers, understanding step-marking criteria, and practicing time-management on full-length mock exams, students routinely clear their compartment examinations, turn their CMPT status into a clean PASS, and proceed smoothly with university college admissions.

How to Apply for and Clear a CMPT Compartment Board Exam in 5 Steps

  1. Verify Official Result Status and Failing Subject Code

    Check your official online score card to confirm the 'CMPT' notation and identify the specific subject where theory marks fell below the passing cutoff.

  2. Submit Online Compartment Application via School Portal

    Complete the compartmental examination registration form through your school or the board's official portal, paying the required examination fee.

  3. Analyze Question Paper Blueprint and Previous Weaknesses

    Obtain your original answer script photocopy to identify question mistakes, focusing revision on high-weightage chapters and marking schemes.

  4. Appear for the Compartmental Re-Examination in July/August

    Download your compartmental admit card and sit for the single-subject re-test at the designated examination center with thorough preparation.

  5. Collect Updated Consolidated Pass Marks Sheet

    Upon clearing the exam, receive a revised, consolidated mark sheet from the educational board, removing the compartment notation to proceed with admissions.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the full form of CMPT in board examination results?

In school board results, CMPT stands for Compartment (or Compartmental Examination).

Q2: Does receiving CMPT mean a student has completely failed the academic year?

No, CMPT means the student has a temporary conditional status, allowing them to clear the single failed subject via a supplementary re-test.

Q3: How many subjects can a student fail and still receive CMPT status in CBSE Class 12?

In CBSE Class 12, a student who fails in exactly one subject is placed in the Compartment category.

Q4: How many subjects qualify for compartment in Class 10?

In CBSE Class 10, a student failing in up to two subjects is eligible to appear for the Compartment examination.

Q5: What is the difference between CMPT and ER on a CBSE scorecard?

CMPT allows a re-test in 1-2 subjects, while ER (Essential Repeat) means the student failed three or more subjects and must repeat the entire year.

Q6: Do practical marks carry over during a compartment re-exam?

Yes, if a student passed the practical/internal assessment, those marks carry forward, and they only need to re-write the theory examination.

Q7: How many attempts does an educational board give to clear a compartment?

Boards typically offer three consecutive chances: the immediate supplementary exam (July/August), the next year's main exam, and the following compartment.

Q8: Can a student with CMPT status secure provisional university admission?

Many universities grant provisional admission, provided the student clears the compartment exam and submits the updated mark sheet by a deadline.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The abbreviation CMPT in examination results stands for Compartment, representing an academic opportunity rather than a permanent academic failure. Established by educational boards like CBSE and state councils, the compartmental examination system allows students who underperformed in one or two subjects to prove their competence through a focused supplementary re-test without losing an entire school year. Approaching a CMPT result with a clear revision plan, focused study, and diligent preparation enables students to clear their pending subjects and continue their educational journeys with confidence.
