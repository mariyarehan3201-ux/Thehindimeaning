How Much Does It Cost to Remove a Hot Tub?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Removing a hot tub costs between $300 and $800 on average for a standard freestanding backyard spa, with complex or custom installations running from $800 to over $1,500. Factors influencing your total invoice include the physical accessibility of your yard, whether the unit can be rolled out intact or must be sliced into pieces with reciprocating saws, electrical disconnection requirements, and local municipal landfill dump fees. Evaluating your site layout and understanding professional junk removal pricing empowers you to clear valuable backyard patio space safely and affordably.

Removal Methods, Site Access, and Price Influences

The primary determinant of hot tub removal pricing is how easily junk hauling crews can access and maneuver the spa. A standard acrylic hot tub weighs between 500 and 1,000 pounds empty, and several thousand pounds when waterlogged from decayed internal polyurethane insulation. If the spa sits on an open concrete patio with wide gate access, two to three workers can tilt the drained shell onto specialized heavy-duty dollies and wheel it directly to a flatbed trailer within an hour, keeping labor costs around $300 to $500.

Conversely, when a hot tub is recessed into an elevated wooden deck, installed in a screened sunroom, or trapped behind steep terraced retaining walls with narrow gate clearance, removal becomes far more complex. In these situations, crew members must use reciprocating saws, angle grinders, and sledgehammers to slice the fiberglass shell, wood-plastic composite skirt, and PVC piping into dozens of manageable chunks. This intensive manual demolition takes three to five hours and pushes removal fees to $750 to $1,400.

Electrical Disconnection and Hazardous Waste Disposal

Safe hot tub removal involves more than just hauling away fiberglass; high-voltage electrical disconnections must be performed properly. Most full-sized hot tubs run on dedicated 220V/240V circuits pulling 40 to 60 amps through a dedicated outdoor ground-fault circuit interrupter (GFCI) disconnect box. Unless your junk removal provider carries a licensed electrician on staff, you may need to pay an independent electrician $100 to $250 to disconnect, cap, and safely terminate the 240V subpanel lines before demolition begins.

Disposal logistics also factor directly into contractor costs. Modern hot tubs are lined with thick expanding polyurethane foam insulation, fiberglass resin, acrylic shells, and electronic circuit boards. Many municipal transfer stations prohibit uncrushed spas or charge heavy environmental surcharge fees ranging from $80 to $200 per ton. Professional junk hauling services bundle these tipping fees and fuel surcharges directly into their upfront quotes, saving homeowners the headache of transport permits.

DIY Demolition vs Professional Junk Removal Services

Homeowners seeking to minimize expenses often contemplate cutting up and disposing of a defunct hot tub themselves. While a do-it-yourself demolition requires only a reciprocating saw with heavy-duty carbide demolition blades, eye protection, and a respirator mask, the physical effort and mess are immense. The cutting process produces thousands of microscopic airborne fiberglass particles that irritate lungs and skin, and filling personal pickup trucks for multiple landfill runs can quickly tally $150 to $250 in dump fees and vehicle wear.

In contrast, hiring professional junk removal specialists like 1-800-GOT-JUNK, College Hunks, or local independent haulers provides swift, insured execution. Professional teams carry specialized dollies, commercial flatbed trucks, and protective tarps to capture debris without scratching patio pavers or damaging landscaping. The peace of mind, physical safety, and rapid turnaround make professional removal well worth the investment for most property owners.

How to Prepare Your Hot Tub for Professional Removal

Follow these five straightforward steps to prepare your spa, streamline crew labor, and avoid unexpected surcharges.

  1. Turn Off Main Breaker and Disconnect 240V Power

    Shut off the dedicated breaker at your home main panel and ensure the exterior GFCI subpanel is powered down safely.

  2. Drain All Water Completely from the Spa Shell

    Hook up a garden hose to the bottom drain bib or drop a submersible utility pump inside to empty all water into an approved sewer cleanout.

  3. Remove and Pack Loose Accessories and Covers

    Fold up the heavy vinyl cover, remove headrests, take out filter cartridges, and unhook clip-on side steps to reduce total bulk.

  4. Clear a Wide, Obstacle-Free Pathway to the Street

    Trim back hanging tree branches, prop open backyard gates, move patio furniture, and ensure pets are securely indoors.

  5. Confirm All-Inclusive Quotes Before Work Begins

    Verify with the hauling crew that their price quote covers labor, demolition, truck loading, and municipal transfer station tipping fees.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a junk company charge to remove a hot tub?

National and local junk removal franchises generally charge between $350 and $850 for standard freestanding residential hot tub haul-aways.

Q2: Can you cut up a hot tub with a Sawzall?

Yes, using a reciprocating saw equipped with carbide-tipped demolition or pruning blades easily cuts through fiberglass, acrylic, and wood frames.

Q3: How do you drain a hot tub if the pump is broken?

You can drain it using an inexpensive submersible utility pump from a hardware store, or siphon the water out using an ordinary garden hose.

Q4: Do scrap metal collectors take old hot tubs for free?

Rarely, because hot tubs contain minimal scrap metal (copper motor windings) buried under hundreds of pounds of worthless foam and fiberglass.

Q5: How heavy is an empty hot tub?

An average dry hot tub weighs between 500 and 1,000 pounds, depending on whether the internal polyurethane insulation is dry or water-saturated.

Q6: Will hot tub removal damage my lawn or patio pavers?

Experienced crews use pneumatic-tire dollies and protective plywood runways to prevent rutting soft grass or cracking decorative concrete patio stones.

Q7: Can a hot tub be lifted over a house with a crane?

Yes, when townhomes or zero-lot-line properties lack side yard access, mobile crane services can hoist the spa over the roof for $400 to $900.

Q8: What should I do with the empty concrete pad afterward?

You can repurpose the sturdy reinforced concrete pad for an outdoor dining patio, fire pit seating area, storage shed foundation, or pergola.
