How to Become a Substitute Teacher in Mississippi?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Substitute teachers play a vital role in maintaining educational consistency across Mississippi's public school districts. Whether stepping into a classroom for a single day or leading instruction for several months during a teacher's medical leave, substitute educators ensure students stay on track academically. Governed by Mississippi Department of Education (MDE) regulations and local school board policies, qualifying as a substitute teacher is an accessible, flexible, and deeply fulfilling career choice.

Mississippi Substitute Teaching Tiers and Minimum Educational Standards

In Mississippi, public education is overseen by the Mississippi Department of Education (MDE), while daily personnel management is delegated to local school boards across the state's 140 public school districts. Because school districts face differing enrollment demands, substitute teacher requirements vary slightly depending on whether you apply in metropolitan areas like Jackson, DeSoto County, and Rankin County, or in rural Delta communities. However, all districts maintain distinct tiers based on educational attainment.

The baseline educational requirement for a short-term substitute teacher in Mississippi is a high school diploma or equivalent General Educational Development (GED) credential, alongside reaching the minimum age of 18 or 21 depending on district policy. Candidates possessing 60 or more accredited college credits, an associate degree, or a bachelor's degree qualify for higher daily compensation and eligibility for long-term substitute assignments. Fully certified retired educators holding an active or expired MDE teaching license command the highest pay rates across all districts.

Application Pathways: Direct District Hiring vs. Staffing Partners

Prospective substitute educators in Mississippi can apply through two primary avenues: applying directly through a school district's human resources department or registering with an authorized third-party educational staffing agency. Many prominent Mississippi school districts partner with educational staffing agencies such as Kelly Education or ESS (Educational Staffing Solution) to handle recruitment, compliance tracking, and daily scheduling through automated management software like Frontline Absence Management.

Regardless of whether you apply directly or through a staffing partner, all applicants must pass strict background clearances mandated by Mississippi Code Section 37-9-17. Applicants submit biometric digital fingerprints for review by the Mississippi Department of Public Safety (State Police) and the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI). Candidates must also clear the Mississippi Child Abuse Central Registry and submit a negative tuberculosis (TB) skin test performed within the past twelve months.

Classroom Strategies, Daily Expectations, and Career Development

Once approved, substitute teachers gain access to automated online dispatch portals where they can accept daily assignments that fit their schedules and geographic preferences. A typical school day begins by arriving 20 to 30 minutes before the morning bell, checking in at the school's administrative office, collecting classroom keys, and reviewing lesson plans left by the permanent teacher. The primary duties include taking accurate attendance, executing provided lesson activities, maintaining positive behavioral discipline, and leaving comprehensive summary notes for the returning educator.

Substitute teaching serves as a proven launching pad for individuals interested in pursuing a permanent teaching career in Mississippi. Working across diverse grade levels and subject areas provides valuable real-world classroom experience. Those who hold a bachelor's degree in any field can leverage their substitute experience to transition into full-time teaching through Mississippi's Alternate Route Teacher Certification programs, such as the Master of Arts in Teaching (MAT), Teach Mississippi Institute (TMI), or American Board (ABCTE) programs.

How to Apply to Become a Mississippi Substitute Teacher

Follow these simple steps to complete your background checks, attend orientation, and begin substitute teaching in Mississippi.

  1. Confirm Minimum Educational and Age Criteria

    Verify you are at least 18 or 21 years old and possess a high school diploma, GED, or official college transcripts.

  2. Submit Application to School District or Staffing Partner

    Apply online through your local Mississippi school district or its staffing vendor like Kelly Education or ESS.

  3. Complete Fingerprint and Background Clearance

    Complete state and FBI fingerprint checks and obtain clearance from the Mississippi Child Abuse Central Registry.

  4. Obtain Tuberculosis Health Screening

    Visit a local health clinic to complete a standard tuberculosis (TB) skin test and obtain a signed clearance form.

  5. Attend Mandatory Substitute Teacher Orientation

    Complete training modules covering classroom management strategies, emergency procedures, and FERPA privacy regulations.

  6. Create Your Profile on the Absence Management Portal

    Log into the district's automated dispatch platform (such as Frontline) to begin selecting classroom assignments.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What qualifications are needed to substitute teach in Mississippi?

Most Mississippi school districts require at least a high school diploma or GED, passing criminal background checks, and attending orientation.

Q2: How much do substitute teachers make per day in Mississippi?

Daily pay typically ranges from sixty-five to eighty-five dollars for non-degreed substitutes, and up to one hundred forty-five dollars for long-term degreed substitutes.

Q3: Does Mississippi require a state substitute teaching license?

No, Mississippi does not issue a statewide substitute teaching license; hiring criteria and approvals are handled by local school boards.

Q4: Can college students substitute teach in Mississippi?

Yes, college students who are at least 18 years old and have completed a high school diploma can substitute teach during semester breaks.

Q5: What background checks are required in Mississippi?

Applicants must undergo FBI and state fingerprinting, a Mississippi Child Abuse Central Registry check, and drug screening if required by the district.

Q6: Do substitute teachers in Mississippi get benefits?

Standard day-to-day substitutes generally do not receive health or retirement benefits, though some long-term substitutes or agency employees may qualify.

Q7: Can substitute teaching lead to a full teaching license in Mississippi?

Yes, candidates holding a bachelor's degree can utilize substitute experience while completing an approved Alternate Route teacher certification program.

Q8: How do substitute teachers choose their work schedules in Mississippi?

Substitutes use online scheduling platforms like Frontline to accept or decline assignments on days that fit their availability.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Becoming a substitute teacher in Mississippi is a meaningful way to support local communities, guide young learners, and enjoy remarkable schedule flexibility. By completing the requisite background checks, attending district orientation sessions, and demonstrating enthusiasm and patience in the classroom, you establish an impactful presence across Mississippi schools.
