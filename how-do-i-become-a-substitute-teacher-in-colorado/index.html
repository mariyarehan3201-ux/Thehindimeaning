How Do I Become a Substitute Teacher in Colorado?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Learning how do i become a substitute teacher in colorado provides a flexible, community-focused pathway to inspire K-12 students while earning dependable daily compensation across the Centennial States diverse school districts. Whether you are an aspiring full-time educator, a career switcher, or an active community member, the Colorado Department of Education (CDE) offers streamlined authorization pathways that allow qualified candidates with varying educational backgrounds—including those with a high school diploma or an associate degree—to step into the classroom as valued guest teachers.

Colorado Department of Education (CDE) Authorization Tiers

The Colorado Department of Education issues three primary types of substitute authorizations based on an applicants formal educational credentials and professional experience. The 1-Year Substitute Authorization is specifically designed for candidates holding a high school diploma or equivalent who have completed an approved district training module or possess relevant youth-oriented experience. This authorization provides critical flexibility, enabling suburban and rural districts facing educator shortages to staff classrooms effectively.

For applicants holding a bachelors degree or higher from a regionally accredited college, the CDE offers the 3-Year Substitute Authorization. This credential allows educators to substitute across any K-12 classroom and school district statewide without subject-matter restrictions. Additionally, experienced educators who already hold a valid Colorado professional teacher license can obtain a 5-Year Substitute Authorization, which grants maximum professional latitude and expedited district hiring privileges.

CBI / FBI Fingerprinting and Background Clearance Protocols

Student safety is paramount across all Colorado educational institutions. Prior to submitting an authorization application via the CDE eLicensing online portal, every prospective substitute teacher must complete a comprehensive fingerprint-based criminal history record check conducted jointly by the Colorado Bureau of Investigation (CBI) and the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI). Fingerprinting must be scheduled through an approved state vendor, such as IdentoGO or Colorado Fingerprinting.

Applicants schedule an in-person biometrics appointment, present a government-issued photo ID, and submit digital fingerprints coded to the specific CDE educator licensing agency code. The background check screens for felony convictions, child welfare offenses, and violent misdemeanors. Processing times typically range from three to ten business days, after which the digital clearance status automatically attaches to your CDE profile.

District Hiring, Automated Sub Systems, and Classroom Success

Once your CDE substitute authorization is officially approved and visible in your eLicensing dashboard, you must apply directly to individual school districts or educational boards of cooperative services (BOCES). Colorado districts do not automatically assign you jobs; you must complete a district human resources application, submit official transcripts, provide references, and attend an orientation session. Many substitutes choose to register with multiple adjacent districts to ensure a full calendar of assignments.

Most Colorado school districts utilize automated substitute dispatch platforms such as Frontline Absence Management (formerly Aesop) or SmartFind Express. These platforms allow you to set your availability, choose preferred school campuses, designate subject interests (such as STEM, physical education, or language arts), and accept jobs via a mobile app either days in advance or on the morning of instruction. Punctuality, strong classroom management, following lesson plans, and leaving detailed teacher feedback guarantee repeat booking requests.

How to Become a Substitute Teacher in Colorado

Follow these five clear steps to obtain your CDE authorization and secure substitute teaching jobs in Colorado.

  1. Determine Your CDE Authorization Eligibility

    Select the 1-Year (high school diploma/training) or 3-Year (bachelors degree) authorization track based on your education.

  2. Complete CBI / FBI Fingerprinting

    Schedule an appointment with IdentoGO or Colorado Fingerprinting using the CDE agency licensing code.

  3. Submit Your CDE eLicensing Online Application

    Create an account on the CDE portal, upload official college transcripts or diploma, and pay the state processing fee.

  4. Apply Directly to Colorado School Districts

    Submit employment applications to school districts like Denver, Cherry Creek, or Jeffco through their online careers portals.

  5. Configure Automated Dispatch Software

    Set up your Frontline or SmartFind account to select your favorite schools, grade bands, and accept teaching assignments.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Do you need a bachelor's degree to substitute teach in Colorado?

No, Colorado offers a 1-Year Substitute Authorization for candidates holding a high school diploma or GED who complete district training.

Q2: How much does a substitute teacher make per day in Colorado?

Daily rates range between $150 and $190 per day for standard day-to-day assignments, with long-term rates reaching $210 to $240 daily.

Q3: How long does it take for CDE to process a substitute authorization?

CDE processing typically takes 2 to 6 weeks, depending on seasonal volume and how quickly fingerprint background results arrive.

Q4: Can I substitute teach in multiple Colorado school districts simultaneously?

Yes, once you have your state CDE authorization, you can apply and work across as many separate school districts or charter schools as you wish.

Q5: What is the fee for a Colorado substitute authorization?

The state fee is $60 for a 1-Year authorization and $90 for a 3-Year authorization, plus fingerprinting vendor fees (around $50).

Q6: Do Colorado substitute teachers receive employee benefits?

Day-to-day substitutes generally do not receive health benefits, though Colorado PERA retirement contributions are accrued in public districts.

Q7: What software do Colorado schools use to post substitute jobs?

Most districts use automated management systems like Frontline Absence Management (Aesop) or SmartFind Express.

Q8: Can a retired teacher substitute teach in Colorado without losing PERA benefits?

Yes, Colorado PERA allows retirees to work up to 110 or 120 days per calendar year under specific post-retirement employment provisions.
