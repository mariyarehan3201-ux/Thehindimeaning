How to Determine If Your Popcorn Ceiling Is Asbestos in 5 Steps

Follow these five recommended steps to safely determine whether your textured popcorn ceiling contains asbestos.

  1. Check the Property Construction and Renovation Dates

    Look at property records; if the home was built before 1990, the popcorn ceiling carries a high probability of containing asbestos.

  2. Assess the Ceiling Physical Condition

    Visually check whether the ceiling is sealed with paint or if it is unpainted, flaky, or crumbling onto floor surfaces.

  3. Don a NIOSH P100 Respirator and Protective Gloves

    Protect your airways before approaching the ceiling with personal protective gear; do not rely on simple paper masks.

  4. Collect a Safe Wet Laboratory Core Sample

    Mist an inconspicuous corner with soapy water, scrape a teaspoon of texture into a sealed Ziploc bag, and patch the spot.

  5. Submit the Sample to an NVLAP-Accredited Lab

    Send the sample to a certified laboratory for Polarized Light Microscopy (PLM) to receive an official percentage report.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is popcorn ceiling always asbestos?

No, popcorn ceilings installed after 1990 are virtually all asbestos-free, but ceilings installed between 1950 and 1985 frequently contain it.

Q2: Can you tell if a popcorn ceiling has asbestos just by looking?

No, asbestos fibers are microscopic; modern styrofoam popcorn ceilings look identical to legacy asbestos formulations.

Q3: Is an asbestos popcorn ceiling dangerous if left alone?

No, if the ceiling is intact, painted, and undisturbed, it poses minimal health risk to building occupants.

Q4: What year did they stop putting asbestos in popcorn ceilings?

The EPA banned manufacturing in 1978, but existing inventories were legally installed until the mid-to-late 1980s.

Q5: What happens if you scrape an asbestos popcorn ceiling dry?

Dry scraping releases billions of invisible carcinogenic fibers that contaminate the entire home and remain airborne for days.

Q6: Can you paint over an asbestos popcorn ceiling?

Yes, carefully spray-painting the ceiling encapsulates the fibers and prevents them from shedding into indoor air.

Q7: How much does it cost to test a popcorn ceiling for asbestos?

Sending a sample directly to an accredited laboratory typically costs between $30 and $75 per test.

Q8: What is the best way to cover an asbestos popcorn ceiling?

Screwing a new layer of 1/4-inch or 1/2-inch drywall directly over the old ceiling is the cleanest, most durable encapsulation method.
