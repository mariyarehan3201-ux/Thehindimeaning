How to Get a Bonded Title in Mississippi?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Obtaining a bonded title in Mississippi is a vital statutory process for vehicle owners who have purchased or inherited an automobile, truck, motorcycle, or recreational vehicle without a valid, assignable Certificate of Title. Whether the original document was lost in transit, destroyed by previous owners, incorrectly endorsed, or abandoned without legal paperwork, state law prohibits driving or selling an un-titled vehicle. Through the Mississippi Department of Revenue (DOR) Title Bureau, vehicle owners can establish legal ownership by purchasing a state surety bond backed by a licensed insurance carrier. This surety bond indemnifies the State of Mississippi, lienholders, and any potential prior owners against financial loss. Following the DOR guidelines ensures you receive a clear, legally recognized Mississippi title.

Mississippi Department of Revenue Statutory Title Bond Regulations

In the State of Mississippi, vehicle titling is administered under the Mississippi Motor Vehicle Title Act and governed by the Mississippi Department of Revenue (DOR). Under Mississippi Code Annotated Section 63-21-23, when an applicant is unable to produce satisfactory evidence of ownership—such as an original manufacturer certificate of origin (MCO) or properly executed prior certificate of title—the state may issue a title supported by a continuous surety bond.

The state surety bond must be executed by a reputable surety company licensed and admitted by the Mississippi Insurance Department. Crucially, Mississippi law mandates that the penal sum of the surety bond must equal exactly one and one-half times (150%) the fair market retail value of the vehicle, as objectively determined by the Mississippi Department of Revenue using official vehicle valuation guides such as the National Automobile Dealers Association (NADA) Official Used Car Guide or the state property tax schedule. The bond must remain in full active force for a statutory period of three consecutive years, after which the bonded brand is cleared and a clean title is issued upon application.

Vehicle Valuation, Physical VIN Inspections, and Criminal Checks

The bonded title application begins with verifying the vehicle identification number (VIN) and establishing fair market value. The vehicle cannot have active stolen records registered on the National Crime Information Center (NCIC) or active total loss salvage brands that disqualify it from standard titling. If the vehicle is titled in another state, the Mississippi DOR requires a certified National Motor Vehicle Title Information System (NMVTIS) vehicle history report.

An applicant must schedule an official physical inspection of the vehicle. In Mississippi, inspections are conducted by certified law enforcement officers, such as the Mississippi Highway Patrol (MHP) or local county sheriff deputies, who examine the public VIN plate on the driver side dashboard, the Federal Safety certification decal on the B-pillar door jamb, and secondary hidden stamping marks on the frame. The officer completes and signs the Mississippi Inspection of Vehicle Identification Number form, certifying that the VIN matches the chassis and shows zero signs of mechanical tampering, cutting, or fraudulent re-stamping.

Submitting the Application and Securing License Plates

Once you have secured the executed surety bond, the signed law enforcement VIN inspection certificate, a notarized bill of sale, and a detailed sworn affidavit explaining how you acquired the vehicle and why the original title is missing, compile the full packet for the Mississippi Department of Revenue Title Bureau in Clinton, Mississippi. Applications can be submitted through your local county tax collector office or mailed directly to the central DOR Title Bureau.

For individuals who need to register and drive the vehicle urgently, Mississippi provides an expedited "Fast Track" title service for an additional statutory fee of thirty dollars. Under Fast Track processing, the state reviews the bonded documentation and issues the physical title within seventy-two hours of receiving a clean file. Once the Certificate of Title is officially issued, visit your local county tax collector to pay county ad valorem personal property taxes, purchase your state license plate tags, and legally operate the vehicle on Mississippi roadways.

How to Apply for a Mississippi Vehicle Title Surety Bond

A step-by-step procedural guide to purchasing a surety bond and receiving an official bonded vehicle title through the Mississippi Department of Revenue.

  1. Contact County Tax Collector to Determine Official NADA Value

    Visit your local Mississippi County Tax Collector office. Present your vehicle make, model, year, and VIN to obtain the official state-recognized NADA fair market retail valuation.

  2. Complete Law Enforcement Physical VIN Verification Inspection

    Contact the Mississippi Highway Patrol or county sheriff to perform a physical inspection of the VIN plates, door jamb decals, and frame stamps, obtaining a certified inspection form.

  3. Purchase a 3-Year Mississippi Vehicle Title Surety Bond

    Contact a licensed surety agency and purchase a bond equal to 150 percent of the NADA vehicle value. Ensure the bond is executed on the official Mississippi DOR bond form with an attached power of attorney.

  4. Draft a Notarized Affidavit of Ownership and Bill of Sale

    Write a comprehensive affidavit describing the transaction history, purchase price, seller contact info, and detailed reasons why the previous certificate of title could not be produced.

  5. Submit Application to Mississippi DOR Title Bureau

    Submit the completed application packet, fees, and bond to the Mississippi Department of Revenue Title Bureau in Clinton, MS, opting for Fast Track processing if rapid 72-hour turnaround is needed.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is a bonded title in Mississippi?

A bonded title is a legal certificate of vehicle title backed by an active surety bond, issued by the Mississippi DOR when the original ownership documents are missing, lost, or defective.

Q2: How much does a Mississippi title surety bond cost?

The bond must equal 150% of the vehicle NADA value, but the actual premium paid to the surety company is typically a one-time fee of $100 to $150 for vehicles valued under $8,000.

Q3: How long does a bonded title remain in effect in Mississippi?

The surety bond must remain active for three consecutive years. If no prior owner or lienholder makes a valid financial claim during this period, the owner can apply for a clear, unbonded title.

Q4: Can you get a bonded title for an abandoned vehicle in Mississippi?

No, Mississippi has separate statutory abandoned motor vehicle auction procedures (Title 85, Chapter 7). You cannot simply obtain a bonded title for a vehicle abandoned on private property without following auction protocols.

Q5: Who conducts vehicle VIN inspections in Mississippi?

Official VIN inspections are conducted by sworn law enforcement officers, including the Mississippi Highway Patrol (MHP), county sheriff deputies, or municipal police departments.

Q6: What is Mississippi Fast Track title processing?

Fast Track is an expedited Mississippi DOR title service that processes and prints a Certificate of Title within seventy-two hours of receiving a clean application for an additional fee of thirty dollars.

Q7: Can I sell a vehicle with a Mississippi bonded title?

Yes, a bonded title grants full legal ownership rights, allowing you to register, insure, finance, or sell the vehicle to a new buyer just like a standard title.

Q8: Can you get a bonded title on a salvage or parts-only vehicle?

No, vehicles branded as parts-only, non-repairable, or dismantled cannot receive a bonded title. Rebuilt salvage vehicles must complete a separate salvage rebuilding inspection process.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Securing a bonded title in Mississippi provides a clear, lawful avenue to rehabilitate undocumented vehicles and obtain clean ownership rights. By calculating the required 150 percent bond value, completing a certified law enforcement VIN inspection, and submitting a thorough application package to the Department of Revenue, you protect your investment from ownership disputes. After holding the bonded title for three consecutive years without third-party claims, you can apply to have the bond notation permanently removed, leaving you with an unencumbered standard Mississippi vehicle title.
