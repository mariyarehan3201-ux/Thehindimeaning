How to Get Lights with a Switch Hold?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Moving into a new Texas apartment or home only to discover that the electricity cannot be turned on due to an active switch hold is an extraordinarily frustrating roadblock. In the deregulated Texas electric market overseen by the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT), a switch hold is an administrative block placed on an Electric Service Identifier (ESI ID) that prevents changing retail electric providers until specific debts or meter issues are resolved. Fortunately, state regulations ensure that incoming occupants are not held responsible for prior residents' debts, providing a clear legal process to remove the hold and activate your power.

The Texas Deregulated Electric Market and Switch Hold Mechanics

Under the statutory framework established by the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT Substantive Rule §25.480), electric service in most Texas metropolitan areas—including Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth, Corpus Christi, and West Texas—is deregulated. Service delivery is bifurcated between Transmission and Distribution Utilities (TDUs like Oncor, CenterPoint Energy, AEP Texas, and TNMP), which maintain physical wires and meters, and competitive Retail Electric Providers (REPs), which sell electricity plans and bill consumers.

When an existing customer enters into a deferred payment arrangement for past-due electric bills, or when a utility discovers meter tampering or unauthorized electricity diversion, a switch hold is electronically applied to that property's unique ESI ID number. The primary purpose of a switch hold is to prevent a delinquent customer from simply jumping to a new electric company to evade unpaid debts while continuing to reside at the same address. However, when that resident moves out, the switch hold remains physically attached to the electric meter until formally cleared.

Documentation Required to Clear a Switch Hold as a New Occupant

If you are moving into a residential rental property or purchasing a home encumbered by a switch hold left by the previous resident, PUCT regulations strictly protect you from paying that stranger's debts. Under state rules, Retail Electric Providers and TDUs must process switch hold removals promptly once the incoming consumer establishes lawful possession of the premises through verifiable documentation.

The centerpiece of this verification process is the official PUCT New Occupant Statement (NOS). This standardized legal affidavit requires you to declare under penalty of perjury that you are a newly arrived occupant possessing no familial or financial association with the defaulting prior tenant. Alongside the NOS form, you must furnish a complete, signed copy of your residential lease agreement, closing disclosure, or property deed, accompanied by proof of utility service or rent payment at your prior residence.

Step-by-Step Resolution Process with Your New Electric Provider

To initiate the switch hold removal, you must first select a new Retail Electric Provider and enroll in a service plan for your new home. During the automated enrollment sequence, the REP's computerized system will flag the active switch hold and generate a notification pausing the order. At this juncture, do not cancel your order or contact the prior occupant's electric company; your chosen new provider is legally obligated to manage the hold removal paperwork with the TDU on your behalf.

Immediately email or upload your completed New Occupant Statement, signed lease agreement, and supporting utility documents directly to your new provider's specialized switch hold or exceptions processing department. Upon receipt, the provider's back-office specialists review the documents for authenticity and transmit an electronic Texas Standard Electronic Transaction (TX SET) request to the regional TDU (such as Oncor or CenterPoint). By PUCT mandate, once the TDU receives compliant documentation, they must release the switch hold within one to two business days, allowing your new provider to complete the move-in order and energize your lights.

How to Remove an Electric Switch Hold in Texas

A step-by-step procedural guide for new occupants to clear a switch hold and get electric service connected in Texas.

  1. Enroll with Your Chosen Retail Electric Provider (REP)

    Select your desired electric plan and complete the enrollment process; the provider system will alert you to the active switch hold on the ESI ID.

  2. Download the Official PUCT New Occupant Statement

    Obtain the New Occupant Statement form from your new electric provider customer service portal or directly from the Public Utility Commission of Texas website.

  3. Gather Supporting Proof of Occupancy Documents

    Assemble your complete signed lease agreement, landlord contact details, closing disclosure statement, and a recent utility bill from your previous address.

  4. Submit the Documentation Packet to Your Provider

    Email or upload the completed New Occupant Statement and supporting lease documentation to your new electric provider specialized switch hold department.

  5. Confirm Switch Hold Release and Service Connection

    Follow up with your provider within 24 to 48 hours to confirm the TDU has removed the hold and finalized the electronic move-in order to energize your power.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can an electric company make me pay the previous tenant bill to get lights?

No. Under PUCT Substantive Rule §25.480, electric providers are strictly prohibited from requiring a new, unrelated occupant to pay a prior tenant unpaid bills.

Q2: How long does it take to remove a switch hold in Texas?

Once your new electric provider submits compliant proof of new occupancy to the TDU, the switch hold is typically cleared within one to two business days.

Q3: Can I clear a switch hold myself if the debt belongs to me?

If you incurred the debt under a deferred payment plan, you must pay off the remaining balance owed to your current electric company before the hold is lifted.

Q4: What is an ESI ID number?

An Electric Service Identifier (ESI ID) is a unique 17- or 22-digit number assigned by Texas electric utilities to identify a specific physical meter location.

Q5: Who actually places the switch hold on the electric meter?

A switch hold is placed electronically either by the Retail Electric Provider for payment arrangements or by the regional TDU for meter tampering issues.

Q6: What happens if my landlord refuses to sign the New Occupant Statement?

Your signed lease agreement accompanied by proof of prior address and rent payment is generally accepted by electric providers if the landlord is unavailable.

Q7: Can prepaid electric companies bypass a switch hold?

No. Prepaid electric providers must comply with the same ERCOT and PUCT switch hold regulations and cannot bypass an active meter block without documentation.

Q8: What should I do if my electric provider rejects my lease agreement?

Contact customer service to identify the exact deficiency, ensuring that all pages are included, signatures are clear, and effective lease dates are fully visible.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

While encountering a switch hold when moving into a new home is an unwelcome complication, Texas utility law ensures that innocent new residents are never forced to pay someone else's electric debt. By choosing your preferred retail electric provider, accurately preparing the New Occupant Statement, and submitting your signed lease promptly, you can rapidly clear the administrative block and enjoy reliable electric power in your new residence.
