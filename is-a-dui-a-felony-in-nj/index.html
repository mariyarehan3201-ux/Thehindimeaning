Is a DUI a Felony in NJ?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Under New Jersey state law, a standard first, second, or third Driving While Intoxicated (DWI) or Driving Under the Influence (DUI) offense is not classified as a felony, nor is it classified as a misdemeanor. Instead, New Jersey prosecutes standard motor vehicle impaired driving charges under Title 39 of the New Jersey Statutes (specifically N.J.S.A. 39:4-50) as serious quasi-criminal traffic violations. These offenses are adjudicated in local Municipal Courts without a jury. However, an impaired driving incident in New Jersey immediately elevates into an indictable felony crime if aggravating factors—such as causing serious bodily injury or vehicular homicide—occur during the event.

New Jersey Statutory Classification: Quasi-Criminal Traffic Offenses

Unlike most American jurisdictions that categorize impaired driving under state penal codes as either misdemeanors or felonies, New Jersey treats driving while intoxicated as a motor vehicle violation under Title 39: Motor Vehicles and Traffic Regulation. The legal terminology in New Jersey distinguishes non-indictable traffic violations from indictable crimes, which are the statutory equivalent of common law felonies. Because standard DUI charges are heard exclusively in municipal courts before a single judge, defendants do not have a constitutional right to a jury trial or an expungable criminal arrest record under standard traffic filings.

Nevertheless, referring to a New Jersey DUI as a traffic ticket minimizes its severe legal repercussions. New Jersey treats Title 39 offenses as quasi-criminal proceedings with stringent burdens of proof, state-mandated minimum financial fines, mandatory driver license suspensions, mandatory installation of ignition interlock devices (IID), compulsory attendance at the Intoxicated Driver Resource Center (IDRC), and statutory county jail terms for repeat violations.

When Does an Impaired Driving Incident Become an Indictable Felony in NJ?

While standard DUI citations remain in municipal court, specific aggravating circumstances instantly trigger prosecution under Title 2C of the New Jersey Code of Criminal Justice. When criminal charges are filed under Title 2C, the case is transferred out of municipal court to the County Superior Court Law Division, where grand jury indictments, formal felony records, state prison sentences, and trial by jury apply.

The primary statutory vehicle for felony prosecution is N.J.S.A. 2C:12-1(c), Assault by Auto. If an impaired motorist causes bodily injury or serious bodily injury to another driver, passenger, or pedestrian, the offense transforms from a motor vehicle violation into a fourth-degree, third-degree, or second-degree indictable crime. In tragic instances involving fatality, the driver faces prosecution for Vehicular Homicide under N.J.S.A. 2C:11-5, a first-degree or second-degree crime carrying mandatory prison terms under the No Early Release Act (NERA).

Long-Term Collateral Consequences of a New Jersey DUI Conviction

Even though a standard municipal DUI does not produce a state criminal record on fingerprint databases, its collateral consequences are profound and permanent. Crucially, under current New Jersey expungement statutes (N.J.S.A. 2C:52-1 et seq.), motor vehicle traffic convictions cannot be expunged or sealed. A Title 39 DWI conviction remains permanently stamped on your New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission (MVC) certified driver abstract for life.

This permanent driving record can severely impair commercial driver's license (CDL) holders, lead to automatic cancellation of standard auto insurance policies (forcing drivers onto high-risk residual risk plans), and trigger international travel restrictions. For instance, Canadian immigration authorities view a New Jersey Title 39 DWI conviction as equivalent to an indictable criminal offense under the Criminal Code of Canada, rendering convicted individuals inadmissible at borders without specialized rehabilitation waivers.

How to Navigate a New Jersey DUI Charge

Step-by-step guidance on understanding court proceedings and legal rights following a New Jersey DUI stop.

  1. Identify the Issuing Summons and Municipal Court Venue

    Review the traffic complaint summons issued by the arresting police department. Identify the specific municipal court jurisdiction and scheduled initial arraignment appearance date listed at the bottom of the summons.

  2. Retain Qualified Legal Counsel Specializing in Title 39 Defense

    Retain a licensed New Jersey defense attorney experienced in municipal court DWI litigation and certified in Alcotest breathalyzer operation protocols and Standardized Field Sobriety Testing (SFST).

  3. File Formal Demands for State Discovery Packages

    Your attorney must submit formal discovery demands to the municipal prosecutor, requesting dashboard/body-worn camera footage, police calibration logs for the breath instrument, and officer observation narratives.

  4. Examine Potential Pre-Trial Motions and Flaws

    Evaluate constitutional grounds to challenge the reasonable articulable suspicion for the initial vehicle stop, compliance with the mandatory 20-minute continuous pre-test observation period, or technical instrument tolerances.

  5. Comply with Mandatory Post-Sentence Protocols

    If convicted, promptly complete mandatory screening hours at your local county Intoxicated Driver Resource Center (IDRC), schedule certified ignition interlock installation within specified deadlines, and pay court-ordered state surcharges.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is a DUI considered a misdemeanor or felony in New Jersey?

Neither. New Jersey does not use the common terms misdemeanor or felony for traffic offenses. Standard DUIs are classified as Title 39 quasi-criminal motor vehicle violations.

Q2: Can a New Jersey DUI be expunged from your record?

No. Under New Jersey law, motor vehicle traffic convictions cannot be expunged. A DWI conviction remains on your certified New Jersey driving record permanently.

Q3: Do you have the right to a jury trial for a DUI in NJ?

No. Standard Title 39 DWI cases are heard and decided solely by a municipal court judge without a jury. Jury trials only occur if aggravated indictable felony charges are filed in Superior Court.

Q4: Can you go to jail for a first-offense DUI in New Jersey?

While the municipal judge possesses statutory authority to impose up to 30 days in county jail for a first offense, jail time is rarely imposed for first offenses absent serious reckless driving circumstances.

Q5: How long must an ignition interlock device remain installed?

For a first offense with low BAC, 3 months. For first offenses with higher BAC (0.10% to 0.14%), 7 to 12 months. For high BAC (0.15%+), 9 to 15 months following license reinstatement. Repeat offenses require 2 to 4 years.

Q6: What makes a DUI a felony in New Jersey?

A DUI escalates into an indictable felony crime (such as Assault by Auto or Vehicular Homicide) whenever the impaired driver causes bodily injury, serious injury, or death to another individual.

Q7: Does New Jersey offer hardship or work licenses during suspension?

No. New Jersey does not issue conditional, work, or hardship driver licenses. If your driving privileges are suspended, driving for any purpose is illegal and carries severe mandatory jail penalties.

Q8: Can you refuse the breathalyzer test in New Jersey?

Under New Jersey implied consent laws (N.J.S.A. 39:4-50.4a), refusing to submit to chemical breath testing is a separate serious violation that carries penalties virtually identical to a high-BAC DWI conviction.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

To conclude, a standard DUI in New Jersey is technically classified as a serious quasi-criminal traffic infraction adjudicated in municipal court, not a felony or misdemeanor. Nevertheless, treating the citation lightly is a grave mistake. With mandatory ignition interlock requirements, massive insurance surcharges, potential municipal jail terms, and permanent un-expungable entries on state driving abstracts, standard DWIs inflict heavy long-term burdens. Most critically, any accident involving bodily harm immediately transfers the case to Superior Court as an indictable felony crime, carrying severe multi-year state prison sentences.
