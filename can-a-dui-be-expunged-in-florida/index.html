Can a DUI Be Expunged in Florida?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 30, 2026

Under Florida law, individuals who have been convicted of driving under the influence encounter exceptionally strict statutory restrictions regarding criminal record relief. Pursuant to Florida Statute 943.0585 and Florida Statute 943.059, the state strictly prohibits sealing or expunging any formal DUI conviction under any circumstances. However, individuals whose charges were dropped, dismissed, or successfully negotiated down to reckless driving with a withholding of adjudication may qualify to seal or expunge their public criminal record through the Florida Department of Law Enforcement.

Florida Statute 943.0585 and the Prohibition on Expunging DUI Convictions

Navigating the criminal justice system in the Sunshine State requires understanding Florida's exceptionally strict stance regarding impaired driving convictions. Under Florida Statute 943.0585, which governs court-ordered expungements, and Florida Statute 943.059, which governs the sealing of criminal records, any criminal offense that results in a formal conviction cannot be sealed or expunged. This statutory prohibition applies universally across all criminal misdemeanor and felony offenses where a finding of guilt was entered.

Compounding this general restriction is Florida Statute 316.656, a dedicated statutory provision that strictly forbids Florida judges from withholding adjudication in driving under the influence prosecutions. In many other misdemeanor offenses, judges possess the equitable discretion to withhold adjudication, allowing a defendant to complete probation without receiving a formal criminal conviction on their public record. However, because Florida Statute 316.656 legally mandates that every guilty plea or verdict for driving under the influence must result in an adjudication of guilt, a DUI automatically becomes a permanent criminal conviction that remains on an individual's lifetime criminal record forever.

Understanding these severe legal consequences is crucial when evaluating initial court proceedings. Defendants evaluating legal defense strategies should examine is a DUI a misdemeanor in Florida to understand standard statutory penalties, community service obligations, and probation terms. Furthermore, motorists should review penalties and costs of a Florida DUI to appreciate the long-term financial ramifications of mandatory court fines, mandatory vehicle impoundments, and extreme automobile insurance surcharges.

Because an adjudication of guilt carries lifetime repercussions, engaging an experienced criminal defense practitioner immediately after arrest is vital. Motorists considering how hiring defense counsel to secure release can influence their broader defense will find that early legal representation provides the best opportunity to negotiate reduced charges before a formal conviction is permanently recorded.

Dismissals, Acquittals, and Withholding of Adjudication Rules

While a formal DUI conviction can never be expunged or sealed in Florida, an arrest for driving under the influence does not automatically bar criminal record relief. The critical legal factor is the ultimate disposition of the court case. If the criminal charges did not culminate in an adjudication of guilt, the defendant remains eligible to seek court-ordered record sealing or expungement through the Florida Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE).

There are two distinct categories of record relief under Florida law: record sealing and record expungement. When a criminal record is sealed pursuant to Florida Statute 943.059, the public, private employers, landlords, and commercial background check companies can no longer see or access the arrest records. However, government entities, criminal justice agencies, and specialized licensing boards (such as the Florida Bar, Department of Education, and Department of Health) maintain continued confidential access. When a record is expunged pursuant to Florida Statute 943.0585, the physical criminal records held by arresting agencies and court clerks are physically destroyed, with only a single confidential master record retained by the FDLE.

To qualify for either sealing or expungement, an applicant must satisfy strict statutory prerequisites. The applicant must have never been previously convicted of any criminal offense in Florida or any other state, must have never previously sealed or expunged a criminal record in Florida, must not have any pending criminal charges, and must have fully satisfied all terms of probation and court financial obligations.

Plea Bargaining: Reducing Charges to Reckless Driving

Given that a DUI conviction cannot be cleared, the primary objective of a skilled Florida criminal defense attorney is negotiating a reduction of the charges. The most common and beneficial plea negotiation involves reducing the charge from driving under the influence to reckless driving, commonly referred to as a 'wet reckless' under Florida Statute 316.192. A reckless driving charge carries significantly less social stigma and avoids mandatory driver license revocations.

Crucially, Florida Statute 316.656 does not apply to reckless driving charges. This statutory distinction means that a judge is legally permitted to withhold adjudication on a reckless driving charge. When a judge withholds adjudication, the defendant is placed on probation, required to pay fines, and may be directed to complete substance education classes, but they are not formally convicted of a crime. Once the defendant successfully completes probation and pays all court costs, they maintain a clean record free of any criminal conviction, preserving their statutory right to petition to seal the entire arrest record.

Defense attorneys achieve charge reductions by aggressively challenging prosecution evidence during pre-trial discovery. Common defense avenues include filing motions to suppress evidence based on unlawful traffic stops, challenging the accuracy and maintenance history of breath testing equipment, exposing improper administration of standardized field sobriety exercises, or highlighting medical conditions that mimic physical impairment.

Securing an FDLE Certificate of Eligibility and Petitioning the Court

For individuals who had their charges dismissed or pleaded to reckless driving with adjudication withheld, sealing or expunging the record requires navigating a formal two-stage legal procedure. The first stage requires obtaining a Certificate of Eligibility from the Florida Department of Law Enforcement in Tallahassee. The applicant must complete an official FDLE application form, submit a complete set of fingerprints taken by a law enforcement agency, provide a certified copy of the final court disposition from the county clerk of court, and remit a non-refundable seventy-five dollar state processing fee.

The FDLE conducts an exhaustive criminal history audit across state and federal databases to ensure the applicant has no prior disqualifying criminal convictions. Processing times for an FDLE Certificate of Eligibility typically range from three to six months. Once the certificate is issued, it remains valid for twelve months from the date of issuance.

The second stage involves filing a formal legal petition in the county court where the original arrest occurred. The defendant's attorney files a Petition to Seal or Expunge, an accompanying sworn affidavit affirming eligibility, and the original FDLE Certificate of Eligibility. Copies must be formally served upon the State Attorney's Office and the original arresting police agency. If the State Attorney does not file a written objection within twenty days, the judge will typically grant the petition and sign a formal Order to Seal or Expunge without requiring a hearing. Once signed, certified copies of the judicial order are distributed to all law enforcement agencies, court clerks, and commercial background check entities to seal the record permanently.

How to Petition to Seal or Expunge an Eligible Driving Record in Florida

The complete legal procedure to obtain an FDLE Certificate of Eligibility and petition the county court to seal eligible driving records.

  1. Confirm Case Disposition Did Not Result in a Formal Conviction

    Review your court docket to verify that your case ended in a dismissal, nolle prosequi, or a plea to a reduced charge such as reckless driving with adjudication withheld.

  2. Obtain a Certified Case Disposition from the County Clerk of Court

    Visit the clerk of the circuit court in the county where the arrest occurred and purchase an official certified copy of the final judgment and sentence demonstrating successful case completion.

  3. Submit Fingerprints and Application to FDLE for Certificate of Eligibility

    Get fingerprinted at a local sheriff office, complete the FDLE application, attach the certified disposition, and submit the package with the required state processing fee to the FDLE in Tallahassee.

  4. Draft and File the Petition and Sworn Affidavit in County Court

    Upon receiving your Certificate of Eligibility from the FDLE, file a formal Petition to Seal or Expunge alongside a notarized affidavit and the certificate in the appropriate county court.

  5. Serve Legal Notice to the State Attorney and Obtain the Judicial Order

    Serve certified copies of the petition upon the State Attorney and arresting agency. Once the statutory objection window expires, submit the final judicial order for the judge's signature and distribute certified copies to all agencies.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can a first-time DUI conviction ever be expunged in Florida?

No. Under Florida Statutes 943.0585 and 943.059, any charge resulting in a formal conviction cannot be sealed or expunged. Because Florida Statute 316.656 strictly forbids judges from withholding adjudication on a DUI, a DUI conviction remains on your record permanently.

Q2: What is the difference between sealing and expunging a criminal record in Florida?

Sealing a record makes it confidential and hides it from the general public, private employers, and landlords, though select government and law enforcement entities retain access. Expunging physically destroys the arrest records held by courts and police, leaving only a confidential FDLE master file.

Q3: Can I seal my record if my DUI was reduced to reckless driving?

Yes, provided the judge withheld adjudication and you have no prior criminal convictions on your lifetime record. Once you complete probation and satisfy all court costs, you are legally eligible to petition to seal the entire arrest record.

Q4: How long does it take to obtain an FDLE Certificate of Eligibility?

The Florida Department of Law Enforcement typically takes three to six months to process applications and issue a Certificate of Eligibility. Once issued, the certificate remains legally valid for twelve months from the issue date.

Q5: Will a sealed reckless driving record show up on a private employer background check?

No, once a court order sealing the record is entered and processed by the FDLE, private employers, commercial background check agencies, and landlords cannot view or access the sealed record during standard background screenings.

Q6: Can I seal a DUI if I successfully completed a pre-trial diversion program?

Yes. When you complete a court-approved pre-trial diversion program, the State Attorney's Office files an official nolle prosequi (dismissal) of the charges. Because the case was dismissed without a conviction, you can immediately apply to seal or expunge the record.

Q7: Does a sealed record restore my commercial driver license driving privileges?

While sealing hides the criminal record from public view, it does not erase administrative driving history suspensions from the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (FLHSMV), which governs commercial driver licenses under strict federal standards.

Q8: Can law enforcement and government agencies still see an expunged record in Florida?

Yes, criminal justice agencies, federal security clearance investigators, and select state regulatory licensing boards (such as the Florida Bar, Department of Education, and Department of Health) retain statutory access to confidential sealed and expunged records.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

While Florida law firmly bars the expungement or sealing of any driving under the influence conviction, individuals facing impaired driving charges are not without hope. By intervening early with aggressive criminal defense representation, challenging prosecution evidence, and negotiating charges down to reckless driving with adjudication withheld, motorists can completely avoid a permanent conviction. Taking proactive control of your legal defense preserves your statutory eligibility to seal your criminal record, protecting your professional reputation and future career opportunities.
