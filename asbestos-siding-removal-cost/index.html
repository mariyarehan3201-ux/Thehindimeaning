Asbestos Siding Removal Cost

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Asbestos siding removal cost is a major financial and architectural consideration for owners of vintage homes constructed between the 1920s and early 1970s. During this era, cementitious asbestos shingles—commonly known by the trade name Transite—were extensively installed as exterior cladding due to their fireproof nature, rot resistance, and exceptional durability. When renovating, stripping, or siding over these exterior surfaces, property owners typically face removal expenses ranging from $7 to $15 per square foot, translating to $8,000 to $18,000 for an entire single-family home.

Cost Breakdown: Pricing Variables and Project Estimations

The overall cost of removing exterior asbestos siding is primarily calculated on a square-footage basis, with rates fluctuating between $7 and $15 per square foot. For a standard 1,500-square-foot single-story ranch home with approximately 1,200 square feet of exterior wall area, professional removal generally totals between $8,400 and $15,000. For large two-story or multi-level historic homes, expenses can escalate beyond $20,000 due to complex scaffolding requirements, elevated heights, and multi-day labor schedules.

Several distinct operational components dictate the final estimate. Because transite siding is heavy and brittle, removal crews must unfasten each individual shingle by hand using specialized nail pullers rather than aggressively prying or smashing boards. Technicians continuously mist shingles with amended water, wrap removed tiles in heavy poly bundles on ground tarps, and transport the dense cementitious waste in lined hazardous roll-off containers, incurring substantial landfill weight surcharges.

Removal versus Siding Overlay (Capping with Vinyl)

Faced with high abatement expenditures, many property owners evaluate the practical alternative of siding overlayment, commonly known as capping or residing. Because exterior transite siding is classified as Category II non-friable asbestos, building codes and environmental agencies universally permit leaving intact shingles on the wall and installing new vinyl, fiber cement, or engineered wood siding directly over them. This technique completely eliminates the hazardous removal and disposal fees.

During an overlay installation, siding contractors install a layer of rigid foam insulation backer board directly over the transite shingles, securing it with extended fasteners driven through the shingles into the wall studs, or by furring out the wall with wood strapping. New modern siding is then hung over the insulation. Overlayment typically costs between $4 and $9 per square foot—saving homeowners 40 to 60 percent compared to total removal. However, future siding alterations will still require addressing the encapsulated asbestos underneath.

Regulatory Mandates, Contractor Selection, and Safety Protocols

Executing asbestos siding removal requires strict adherence to federal EPA NESHAP and OSHA construction standards. Even though siding is non-friable while intact, snapping, cutting, or dropping shingles can create hazardous friable dust plumes. Contractors must spread heavy 6-mil polyethylene drop cloths around the entire perimeter of the home extending at least ten feet from the walls to catch any chipped fragments or dislodged dust.

Workers must utilize wet-mist spraying while extracting fasteners, wear NIOSH-approved P100 respirators and protective coveralls, and package the shingles directly into lined, labeled wooden pallets or hazardous waste bags on site. Hiring a licensed C-22 asbestos abatement contractor guarantees that mandatory 10-day state environmental agency notifications are filed, worker safety is monitored, and waste manifests are delivered to certified hazardous landfills without municipal code violations.

How to Plan and Execute an Asbestos Siding Removal Project

Follow these five operational steps to budget, contract, and safely manage exterior asbestos siding removal.

  1. Calculate Total Exterior Wall Square Footage

    Measure the height and perimeter of your home, subtracting major windows and doors, to determine accurate surface area.

  2. Decide Between Removal and Vinyl Siding Overlay

    Weigh your remodeling budget and long-term property goals to choose between complete abatement or residing over the shingles.

  3. Obtain Multiple Quotes from Licensed Abatement Contractors

    Request detailed, itemized bids that include ground drop cloths, wet methods, waste transport, and hazardous landfill fees.

  4. Verify State Regulatory Notifications

    Ensure your chosen contractor submits mandatory 10-day advance notifications to your state environmental protection department.

  5. Coordinate New Cladding Installation Immediately

    Schedule your siding replacement contractor to install weather-resistant house wrap and new siding right after abatement.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to remove asbestos siding on average?

The national average cost ranges from $8,000 to $18,000 for a typical single-family home, or $7 to $15 per square foot.

Q2: Is it legal to put new siding over asbestos siding?

Yes, building codes and environmental agencies allow installing rigid foam backer board and new vinyl or fiber cement siding over intact transite shingles.

Q3: Can I remove asbestos siding myself to save money?

While legal for single-family homeowners in some states, it is physically demanding, hazardous, and requires strict wet methods and disposal permits.

Q4: Why is asbestos siding removal so expensive?

Costs stem from licensed hazardous labor rates, careful hand-unfastening of fragile tiles, ground drop cloth containment, and heavy landfill disposal fees.

Q5: What is transite siding?

Transite is a brand name for rigid exterior shingles made of Portland cement reinforced with 12% to 25% chrysotile asbestos fibers.

Q6: Does asbestos siding pose an immediate health danger?

No, intact transite siding is dense and non-friable; it poses virtually zero health risk unless it is broken, sawed, sanded, or power-washed.

Q7: Can you pressure wash asbestos siding?

No, power washing transite siding is strictly prohibited by environmental regulations because high-pressure water pulverizes shingles into hazardous airborne slurry.

Q8: How long does it take to remove asbestos siding from a house?

A professional certified abatement crew of three to four technicians typically completes removal in three to six working days.
