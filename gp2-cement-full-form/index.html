GP2 Cement Full Form: Non-Shrink Free Flow Grout

Full Form
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 17, 2026

In civil engineering, heavy industrial construction, structural machinery installation, and structural rehabilitation, the full form of GP2 cement is General Purpose Non-Shrink Free Flow Grout (widely manufactured as Conbextra GP2 by Fosroc). GP2 is a pre-bagged, high-performance cementitious precision grout formulated from graded quartz silica aggregates, portland cement, specialized expanding chemical admixtures, and plasticizing polymers. Designed to provide positive dual-phase expansion that counteracts plastic and drying shrinkage, GP2 achieves high early and ultimate compressive strength (exceeding 65 N/mm²), ensuring full, void-free bearing contact under heavy industrial machine baseplates, crane rails, and structural steel stanchions.

The Engineering Science of Precision Machinery Grouting

Industrial manufacturing plants, petrochemical refineries, power generation stations, and heavy metallurgical steel mills operate mammoth mechanical equipment. Industrial air compressors, reciprocating turbines, heavy stamping presses, and overhead gantry cranes generate enormous dynamic loads, multi-directional rotational torques, and violent cyclic vibrations. Transferring these destructive dynamic forces safely down into reinforced concrete foundation beds requires a flawless, load-bearing interface.

When heavy steel machine baseplates are positioned over cast concrete foundations, microscopic surface irregularities make direct metal-to-concrete contact impossible; the baseplate touches only a few isolated high spots, creating localized stress points that would crack the foundation under load. For decades, builders attempted to fill this gap with standard site-mixed sand and Portland cement mortar. However, conventional cement naturally shrinks as it cures and dries, pulling away from the underside of the steel baseplate and leaving hollow air voids. General Purpose Non-Shrink Free Flow Grout (GP2 cement) was engineered to overcome this structural limitation.

Chemical Formulation and Dual-Expansion Mechanism of GP2

Conbextra GP2 is an advanced composite cementitious formulation manufactured under factory-controlled quality standards. It combines graded spheroidal quartz silica aggregates (which provide exceptional compressive resistance and fluidity), high-early-strength Portland cement, special water-reducing polycarboxylate plasticizers, and proprietary gas-generating expanding agents. The fundamental secret behind GP2's performance is its dual-phase non-shrink expansion mechanism.

During the initial plastic state (within the first few hours of pouring), micro-gaseous expansion occurs, generating controlled positive volume expansion that pushes the fluid grout tightly against the underside of the steel baseplate, ensuring 100% load-bearing surface contact. Subsequently, during the secondary hardened state, specialized chemical hydration compounds expand, completely counteracting normal drying shrinkage. Site Preparation and Pouring Protocols for Flawless Execution

Achieving the rated compressive strength and non-shrink performance of GP2 demands disciplined site execution. Before erecting formwork, the cast concrete foundation must be mechanically prepared using light pneumatic breakers or scabblers to remove all weak cement laitance, exposing clean, rough aggregate stone. The concrete must be continuously soaked with clean water for twenty-four hours to achieve a Saturated Surface Dry (SSD) condition; if dry concrete is left unsoaked, it will pull mixing water out of the grout, causing localized cracking.

Formwork must be erected rigidly, sealed with silicone caulking to prevent water leakage, and designed with a 45-degree inclined pouring hopper on one side. Grout must always be poured continuously from a single side only. Pouring from multiple sides traps air pockets beneath the center of the baseplate. The fluid grout front advances smoothly across the baseplate cavity, pushing all air ahead of it until clean, bubble-free grout emerges on the opposite overflow side.

Curing Protocols and Vibration Dampening Longevity

The final critical phase of GP2 installation is moist curing. Because high-strength cementitious grouts undergo rapid early hydration, premature moisture loss will trigger surface hairline cracks. As soon as the grout achieves its initial set (typically two to four hours after pouring), all exposed formwork shoulders must be covered with clean, soaked hessian burlap sacks kept continuously moist for seven full days.

When mixed and cured correctly, GP2 provides a permanent, monolithic structural bridge between rotating steel machinery and earth foundations. It dampens mechanical vibration harmonics, prevents anchor bolt fatigue failure, resists lubricating oil absorption, and ensures that critical industrial manufacturing equipment operates with smooth, vibration-free precision for decades.

How Civil Engineers Mix and Pour Conbextra GP2 Non-Shrink Grout

  1. Mechanically Scabble and Saturate the Concrete Substrate

    Chop away weak laitance from the concrete foundation using pneumatic scabblers, blow away dust, and saturate the concrete with clean water for 24 hours (SSD condition).

  2. Erect Rigid, Watertight Formwork with a Pouring Hopper

    Construct leak-proof timber formwork sealed with silicone mastic, building a 45-degree angled pouring hopper on one side to facilitate continuous gravity head flow.

  3. Measure Clean Water Accurately and Mechanically Mix the Grout

    Measure exactly 4.0 to 4.5 liters of potable water per 25kg bag into a mechanical grout mixer, slowly adding GP2 powder while mixing for 5 continuous minutes.

  4. Pour Grout Continuously from One Direction to Prevent Air Voids

    Pour the mixed grout slurry from one side only to maintain a continuous fluid front, allowing air to escape freely from the opposite side without using mechanical vibrators.

  5. Execute Wet Curing for a Minimum of Seven Days

    Cover exposed grout edges with wet hessian burlap sacks or apply certified curing compounds immediately after initial set to prevent premature surface moisture loss.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does GP2 stand for in construction grouting materials?

GP2 stands for General Purpose Non-Shrink Free Flow Grout, an engineered cementitious precision grout (Conbextra GP2).

Q2: Why is regular site-mixed cement sand mortar unsuitable under heavy machinery?

Regular cement shrinks as it dries, leaving air gaps beneath the baseplate that cause localized stress points, vibration loosening, and structural failure.

Q3: What compressive strength does GP2 grout achieve at 28 days?

GP2 achieves a massive 28-day compressive strength of 65 to 70 N/mm² (MPa), vastly exceeding standard M25 or M30 construction concrete.

Q4: What is the water-powder ratio for mixing Conbextra GP2?

For a standard 25kg bag, 4.0 to 4.5 liters of clean potable water is added to achieve a self-leveling, free-flowing consistency.

Q5: How does GP2 achieve its 'non-shrink' characteristic?

It incorporates gaseous expanding agents that generate controlled micro-expansion during both the initial plastic state and hardened drying phases.

Q6: Can mechanical vibrators be used when pouring GP2 grout?

No, mechanical vibration must never be used as it causes aggregate segregation; GP2 is self-compacting and relies solely on gravity flow.

Q7: What is the recommended grouting gap thickness for GP2?

Standard GP2 is optimized for gap thicknesses ranging from 10 mm to 100 mm beneath baseplates and structural anchor pockets.

Q8: What primary industrial equipment uses GP2 grout foundations?

Heavy diesel generators, industrial air compressors, rolling mills, overhead crane rails, bridge bearing plates, and wind turbine towers.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Conbextra GP2 Non-Shrink Free Flow Grout (GP2 cement) is an indispensable, precision-engineered material in modern structural and industrial construction. By delivering guaranteed non-shrink volumetric expansion, rapid high-strength development, and effortless self-leveling flowability, GP2 provides the rock-solid foundation upon which modern industrial machinery and civil infrastructure depend.
