Asbestos Cement Siding Replacement

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: October 1, 2026

Managing an asbestos cement siding replacement is an essential exterior remodeling project for owners of mid-century residential homes. Known commercially as transite, these rigid exterior shingles combined Portland cement with chrysotile asbestos to achieve exceptional fire resistance and durability, but aging materials, broken shingles, and structural updates now necessitate careful removal or siding overlay solutions.

Exterior transite siding was installed on millions of suburban residences constructed between 1940 and the late 1970s. When planning an upgrade or reviewing professional guides on asbestos siding replacement, homeowners must understand that uncracked cement shingles remain classified as Category II non-friable materials, posing minimal airborne risk until they are subjected to mechanical impact, drilling, or sawing.

Replacement Materials and Modern Cladding Alternatives

Homeowners replacing vintage transite shingles can choose from several modern high-performance materials that replicate the historical aesthetic without hazardous minerals. Leading manufacturers produce non-asbestos fiber-cement replacement shingles engineered with cellulose wood pulp and Portland cement, specifically designed to match the wavy-edge and woodgrain textures of vintage exterior wall assemblies.

Alternatively, complete facade retrofits often incorporate insulated vinyl cladding or engineered composite wood siding. Rather than tearing off entire wall planes, many contractors suggest modern replacement for asbestos siding options that utilize foam underlayment backing boards, allowing fresh siding to be fastened directly over stable transite shingles, eliminating disposal costs while improving thermal insulation.

Safe Shingle Removal and Wet Deconstruction Protocols

When full teardown is necessary due to widespread cracking, workers must follow strict wet deconstruction practices. Finding compatible replacement asbestos siding tiles for localized patches requires carefully uncoupling existing fasteners without shattering adjacent shingles, utilizing specialized siding shingle ripper tools inserted under the overlap.

Contractors spray each shingle with amended water containing surfactant before gently backing out nails using pry bars and pliers. Shingles must never be dropped from ladders or tossed into open debris dumpsters; instead, they are lowered to ground level and stacked directly onto heavy 6-mil polyethylene plastic sheeting for double-wrapping and labeling.

Permits, Disposal Regulations, and Exterior Overlays

Local municipal building codes regulate whether non-friable transite siding can be removed by homeowners or requires licensed abatement crews. In projects where owners select asbestos replacement siding systems, building inspectors often permit encapsulating intact transite under furring strips and foam board, provided the old siding is not cut, planed, or power-drilled during the fastening process.

If full removal is undertaken, bagged waste must be transported by licensed haulers to permitted non-friable construction demolition landfills. Maintaining full documentation of disposal tickets shields homeowners from future municipal citations and satisfies structural disclosures during eventual home sales.

How to Safely Replace or Remove Asbestos Cement Exterior Siding

A comprehensive five-step protocol for safely uncoupling, packaging, and replacing vintage exterior transite cement siding shingles.

  1. Establish Ground Containment and Perimeter Controls

    Lay continuous 6-mil polyethylene drop cloths extending ten feet out from the foundation walls around the entire work perimeter to capture any falling debris or mineral particles.

  2. Equip Technicians with P100 PPE and Wet Sprayers

    Ensure all personnel wear NIOSH-certified P100 elastomeric half-mask respirators, disposable Tyvek coveralls, safety glasses, and carry garden sprayers filled with amended surfactant water.

  3. Saturate Shingles and Cut Concealed Fasteners

    Thoroughly mist each siding shingle with surfactant water, slide a specialized siding shingle ripper tool underneath the overlapping edge, and gently sever fasteners without snapping the brittle shingle.

  4. Carefully Lower and Double-Wrap Transite Shingles

    Remove intact shingles by hand, lower them directly to ground level, stack them neatly on polyethylene sheeting, and wrap bundles in double 6-mil plastic sealed hermetically with duct tape.

  5. Install Modern Non-Asbestos Fiber-Cement Replacement Cladding

    Inspect and repair the underlying vapor barrier tar paper, install modern non-asbestos fiber-cement shingles matching historical dimensions, and fasten with corrosion-resistant exterior ring-shank nails.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is it safe to live in a house with asbestos cement siding?

Yes, intact transite siding on a home exterior is non-friable and completely safe; health risks only arise if shingles are broken, sawed, sanded, or power washed.

Q2: Can new siding be installed directly over existing asbestos shingles?

Yes, encapsulating intact transite under rigid foam insulation backer boards and installing vinyl or composite siding over it is a recognized, cost-effective practice.

Q3: What modern shingle matches the look of vintage asbestos siding?

GAF WeatherSide and James Hardie produce authentic fiber-cement replacement shingles made with non-hazardous cellulose fibers that mirror vintage transite profiles.

Q4: How can I tell if my exterior house shingles contain asbestos?

Rigid cement shingles manufactured before 1978 measuring 12 by 24 inches with wavy or straight bottom edges generally contain twenty to forty percent chrysotile.

Q5: Can homeowners legally remove asbestos siding themselves?

In many states, single-family homeowners are permitted to remove exterior siding provided they follow wet removal methods and transport waste to an approved landfill.

Q6: How much does it cost to remove asbestos siding from an entire home?

Complete professional removal typically costs between $7.00 and $12.00 per square foot of wall area, totaling $8,000 to $16,000 for an average two-story residence.

Q7: Can I cut asbestos siding with a circular saw?

Never use power saws or abrasive cutting wheels on asbestos siding; high-speed blades pulverize the cement matrix, releasing dangerous respirable dust clouds.

Q8: How should waste transite siding shingles be disposed of?

Shingles must be double-bagged in certified 6-mil hazardous waste bags or wrapped in heavy plastic sheeting and delivered to a landfill permitted to receive non-friable asbestos.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Executing an asbestos cement siding replacement successfully requires weighing the cost and labor of complete removal against the practical advantages of protective exterior encapsulation. By using authentic fiber-cement replacement shingles for localized repairs, adhering to disciplined wet removal methods, and engaging qualified exterior contractors, property owners can modernize home exteriors while ensuring lasting safety and curb appeal.
