How Do I File for a Legal Separation in Illinois?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how do i file for a legal separation in illinois offers spouses a legally binding mechanism to formalize property rights, child support, spousal maintenance, and parenting arrangements while remaining legally married. Governed by the Illinois Marriage and Dissolution of Marriage Act (750 ILCS 5/402), a Judgment for Legal Separation allows couples who object to divorce for religious, moral, or practical healthcare reasons to live physically apart with full judicial protections, eliminating ambiguity over financial and familial responsibilities.

Legal Separation vs. Divorce Under the Illinois IMDMA

The Illinois Marriage and Dissolution of Marriage Act establishes a distinct boundary between a Petition for Dissolution of Marriage (divorce) and a Petition for Legal Separation. In an Illinois divorce, the marital bond is completely severed, allowing both parties to remarry and permanently dividing marital assets and debts under equitable distribution laws. Conversely, a legal separation leaves the marital status intact; neither spouse may legally remarry another person while legally separated.

However, a legal separation grants the circuit court full authority to enter binding court orders regarding spousal support (maintenance), parental allocation of responsibilities (child custody), parenting time schedules, and child support obligations. Crucially, under Illinois law, the court cannot divide marital property without the mutual written agreement and consent of both spouses. If spouses cannot agree on asset distribution, an action for dissolution of marriage must be pursued.

Statutory Eligibility: Residency and Living Separate and Apart

To file for legal separation in an Illinois Circuit Court, specific statutory criteria must be satisfied. First, at least one spouse must be a resident of Illinois (or stationed in the state as a member of the armed forces) for at least 90 continuous days immediately preceding the filing of the petition. Venue is typically established in the county where either spouse resides.

Second, the petitioning spouse must allege and prove that the parties are living separate and apart without fault. Under Illinois jurisprudence, living separate and apart does not strictly require maintaining two separate residential addresses. If financial constraints or childcare needs compel spouses to reside under the same roof, courts recognize separation if they sleep in separate bedrooms, cease sexual intimacy, maintain separate finances, and do not hold themselves out as a romantic couple.

Filing Workflow: Service of Process, Temporary Relief, and Final Decree

The legal separation process begins when the petitioning spouse files the formal Petition for Legal Separation along with a Summons at the county circuit clerks office (or electronically through the states mandatory e-filing system, Odyssey eFileIL). The non-filing respondent must be formally served by the county sheriff or a licensed private process server. Once served, the respondent has 30 days to file an Appearance and a formal Answer.

During the pendency of the case, either spouse may file a motion for temporary relief (temporary maintenance, child support, or exclusive possession of the marital home). If the spouses successfully negotiate a comprehensive Separation Agreement addressing support and parenting time, the court schedules an uncontested prove-up hearing. Upon verifying statutory residency and voluntariness, the judge enters a final Judgment for Legal Separation.

How to File for Legal Separation in Illinois

Follow these five strategic steps to prepare, file, and finalize a legal separation in Illinois.

  1. Confirm Illinois Residency and Separation Status

    Ensure at least one spouse has resided in Illinois for 90 days and that you are living separate and apart.

  2. Draft the Petition for Legal Separation

    Complete the statewide standardized Petition for Legal Separation specifying desired support and child custody terms.

  3. File Documents Electronically with Circuit Clerk

    Submit your petition, summons, and financial documents through the Odyssey eFileIL portal in your county.

  4. Serve Your Spouse with Formal Summons

    Have the county sheriff or a certified private process server officially deliver the paperwork to your spouse.

  5. Attend Prove-Up Hearing for Final Judgment

    Present your negotiated Separation Agreement and Parenting Plan before a family court judge to enter the final decree.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can you live in the same house and still be legally separated in Illinois?

Yes, Illinois courts recognize living separate and apart under the same roof if you maintain separate bedrooms, finances, and cease marital relations.

Q2: Can an Illinois judge divide marital property in a legal separation?

No, under 750 ILCS 5/402, the court cannot divide property in a legal separation unless both spouses execute a voluntary written agreement.

Q3: Can I stay on my spouse's health insurance after a legal separation in Illinois?

In many cases yes, because the marriage is not dissolved; however, you must review the employer's specific plan summary definition of 'spouse.'

Q4: How long does a legal separation last in Illinois?

A legal separation lasts indefinitely until either party petitions the court to modify the decree or files for a full dissolution of marriage (divorce).

Q5: Can a legal separation be converted into a divorce in Illinois?

Yes, either spouse can file a Petition for Dissolution of Marriage at any time, which supersedes the separation action.

Q6: How much does it cost to file for legal separation in Illinois?

Circuit court filing fees typically range between $250 and $380, plus sheriff service fees (around $50-$75) unless a fee waiver is granted.

Q7: Is child support calculated the same way in separation as in divorce?

Yes, child support in Illinois legal separations follows the exact same Income Shares model established under state statutory guidelines.

Q8: Do you need a lawyer to file for legal separation in Illinois?

No, you can represent yourself pro se using standardized forms, though consulting a family law attorney is strongly advised for complex assets.
