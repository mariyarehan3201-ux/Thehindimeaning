Kelly Moore Asbestos Lawsuit

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

The Kelly Moore asbestos lawsuit litigation represents one of the most prominent chapters in corporate toxic tort history, culminating in the complete shutdown and liquidation of a historic American paint manufacturer after seven decades of operation. Founded in 1946 in San Carlos, California, the Kelly-Moore Paint Company grew into a beloved regional paint powerhouse across the western United States. However, between 1960 and 1978, Kelly-Moore manufactured and distributed drywall joint compounds, taping muds, and texture products that incorporated raw asbestos fibers purchased from Union Carbide and other suppliers. The resulting wave of personal injury lawsuits, multi-million-dollar jury verdicts, and unmanageable legacy liabilities ultimately drove the company out of business in January 2024.

Historic Product Line: Paco Joint Compound and Chrysotile Formulations

While Kelly-Moore was famous for its architectural paints, its catastrophic asbestos liability stemmed from its dry and ready-mix drywall joint compound lines, marketed heavily under the brand name 'Paco' and Kelly-Moore packaging. Throughout the 1960s and 1970s, the company formulated its taping compounds, texture plasters, and bedding muds with chrysotile asbestos fibers—most notably Union Carbide proprietary 'Calidria' asbestos mined in California.

Asbestos was incorporated into Paco joint compounds because the silky, microscopic mineral fibers acted as exceptional thickening agents and crack-resisting binders. When applied over gypsum drywall seams, the compound feathered smoothly, resisted shrinking, and produced seamless walls. However, drywall tapers, painters, carpenters, and DIY homeowners routinely dry-sanded the dried Paco joint mud before painting, releasing dense clouds of respirable chrysotile dust that workers inhaled daily without warnings or respiratory protection.

Litigation Evolution, Landmark Verdicts, and the Union Carbide Battle

Beginning in the late 1980s and accelerating through the 2000s, Kelly-Moore was named as a co-defendant in tens of thousands of asbestos personal injury and wrongful death lawsuits filed by tradespeople diagnosed with mesothelioma and asbestosis. Trial courts across California, Texas, Washington, and Nevada heard testimonies from former drywallers who described how sanding Kelly-Moore joint compounds filled construction sites with thick white dust. Over forty years, Kelly-Moore paid out an estimated $600 million in settlements and jury verdicts.

In response to the mounting legal onslaught, Kelly-Moore launched aggressive legal counterclaims against Union Carbide Corporation. Kelly-Moore alleged that Union Carbide knew Calidria asbestos was deadly when inhaled, yet actively misled customer companies by marketing the mineral as safer and less hazardous than other commercial asbestos fibers. While Kelly-Moore secured a landmark $55 million fraud verdict against Union Carbide in Texas state court in 2004, the verdict was subsequently overturned on appeal, leaving Kelly-Moore solely responsible for its compounding liabilities.

The 2024 Corporate Shutdown and Current Rights of Claimants

In January 2024, after exhausting insurance assets and facing an estimated $332 million in future unresolved asbestos liabilities, Kelly-Moore officially ceased all business operations and shuttered its manufacturing plant in Hurst, Texas, alongside its 157 retail stores. The company announced it could no longer secure commercial operating capital or insurance due to unending legacy litigation. The closure resulted in the immediate layoff of approximately 700 employees and the liquidation of inventory.

For individuals diagnosed with mesothelioma or surviving family members, the shutdown of Kelly-Moore does not extinguish legal rights to financial recovery. Asbestos litigation routinely targets multiple solvent entities responsible for a victim's total exposure, including suppliers like Union Carbide, raw mineral distributors, building material retailers, and jobsite general contractors. Furthermore, if Kelly-Moore formalizes a Chapter 11 bankruptcy trust under Section 524(g), future claims will be processed through structured administrative trust payouts.

How to Pursue a Claim Involving Kelly-Moore Asbestos Products

Follow these five legal steps to document historic exposure to Kelly-Moore Paco products and seek compensation.

  1. Verify Usage of Paco or Kelly-Moore Products

    Collect purchase invoices, union dispatch records, or witness statements confirming you handled Paco joint compound.

  2. Obtain Certified Mesothelioma Pathology

    Secure official biopsy medical reports confirming an asbestos-related diagnosis such as malignant mesothelioma.

  3. Retain a Qualified Toxic Tort Attorney

    Partner with an experienced asbestos litigator who has actively litigated claims against Kelly-Moore and Union Carbide.

  4. Identify Co-Liable Equipment and Mineral Suppliers

    Ensure your legal team names the raw fiber suppliers, distributors, and other co-occurring jobsite manufacturers.

  5. File Claims with Applicable Bankruptcy Trusts

    Submit administrative claims to active asbestos trust funds representing other asbestos products present on your jobsites.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Did Kelly-Moore paint itself contain asbestos?

No, Kelly-Moore liquid architectural paint did not contain asbestos; the hazardous asbestos was in its Paco drywall joint compounds and textures.

Q2: Why did Kelly-Moore go out of business in 2024?

Kelly-Moore ceased operations due to unmanageable legacy asbestos liabilities, having paid over 600 million dollars with hundreds of millions in pending claims.

Q3: What was the brand name of Kelly-Moore asbestos drywall mud?

The products were primarily marketed under the 'Paco' brand name, including Paco Ready-Mix and Paco All-Purpose Joint Compound.

Q4: Where did Kelly-Moore purchase its asbestos fibers?

Kelly-Moore purchased substantial quantities of chrysotile asbestos fibers from Union Carbide, which marketed its California-mined fiber as Calidria.

Q5: Can I still file a lawsuit for Kelly-Moore asbestos exposure?

Yes, you can pursue compensation through remaining insurance policies, bankruptcy proceedings, and lawsuits against co-liable suppliers like Union Carbide.

Q6: Who was most exposed to Kelly-Moore asbestos products?

Drywall tapers, painters, sheetrock installers, plasterers, and general carpenters who sanded joint compounds faced the highest exposure.

Q7: What years did Kelly-Moore manufacture asbestos products?

Kelly-Moore manufactured asbestos-containing joint compounds, taping muds, and ceiling textures between 1960 and 1978.

Q8: How much did Kelly-Moore pay in total asbestos settlements?

Over its forty-year litigation history, Kelly-Moore paid out an estimated 600 million dollars in asbestos-related settlements and legal defense costs.
