Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does the VA consider mesothelioma a service-connected disability?

Yes. If a veteran can demonstrate that at least 50% of their asbestos exposure occurred during military service, the VA grants an automatic 100% disability rating.

Q2: How much does the VA pay monthly for mesothelioma?

A 100% service-connected disability rating pays over $3,737 per month tax-free for a single veteran, with higher amounts for veterans with spouses or dependents.

Q3: Can a veteran sue the U.S. Navy for asbestos exposure?

No. Veterans cannot sue the military or U.S. government due to the Feres Doctrine. However, veterans CAN sue the private commercial manufacturers that sold the products to the military.

Q4: Will filing a private lawsuit affect my VA disability benefits?

No. Filing civil lawsuits or bankruptcy trust claims against private equipment manufacturers will not reduce or eliminate your VA disability payments or VA healthcare.

Q5: Which Navy jobs had the highest asbestos exposure?

Boiler Technicians, Machinist Mates, Pipefitters, Shipfitters, Damage Controlmen, and Electricians had the highest exposure to steam pipe lagging, gaskets, and boiler insulation.

Q6: Can surviving spouses of veterans receive benefits?

Yes. Surviving spouses are eligible for monthly Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC) from the VA and can pursue private wrongful death settlements.

Q7: Does the PACT Act cover asbestos exposure?

Yes. The PACT Act expanded VA healthcare and benefits for veterans exposed to toxic substances, streamlining the claims process for service-related respiratory conditions.

Q8: How long does it take to get a VA decision for mesothelioma?

Mesothelioma claims are flagged for expedited processing by the VA and are typically decided within 30 to 90 days when accompanied by complete medical records.
