I Got Hit by a Car While Crossing the Street

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

If you got hit by a car while crossing the street, you are entitled to comprehensive financial compensation for your medical care, lost wages, and pain. Because pedestrians have zero structural protection against multi-ton vehicles, drivers owe a strict legal duty of care to yield right-of-way and scan intersections for pedestrians at all times.

The Vulnerability of Pedestrians and Legal Duties of Drivers

Stepping off a curb to cross a public street should never result in catastrophic trauma. Yet, pedestrian collisions are among the most lethal and devastating events on modern roadways. When an unprotected human body is struck by a 4,000-pound passenger sedan or a 6,000-pound pickup truck traveling at 30 to 45 miles per hour, the physics of impact inflict violent multi-stage trauma: primary impact against the vehicle bumper and hood, secondary impact as the pedestrian's torso and head smash against the rigid windshield or A-pillar, and tertiary impact as the pedestrian is launched through the air onto concrete pavement.

Common catastrophic injuries include traumatic brain injuries (TBIs), shattered pelvises, compound tibia-fibula fractures, torn knee ligaments (ACL/MCL tears), and spinal cord fractures. Under American motor vehicle jurisprudence across all fifty states, motor vehicle operators bear an elevated legal duty of care toward pedestrians. Motorists are legally required to maintain a continuous lookout, obey all traffic control signals, yield the right-of-way to pedestrians within marked and unmarked crosswalks, and exercise heightened caution in school zones, residential neighborhoods, and urban commercial districts.

When a driver breaches this legal duty—whether through smartphone distraction, speeding, rolling through a red light on a right turn, or operating under the influence—their automobile liability insurance is legally responsible for paying all resulting damages. What If You Were Jaywalking? Comparative Fault Realities

One of the most frequent defense tactics deployed by insurance adjusters is attempting to deny a pedestrian claim by alleging that the victim was 'jaywalking' or crossing outside a designated crosswalk. Many injured pedestrians mistakenly assume that because they were crossing mid-block, they have surrendered all legal rights to compensation. This assumption is legally incorrect in virtually every jurisdiction.

Under the universal doctrine of Due Care, drivers are never granted a legal license to run down pedestrians simply because they are outside a painted crosswalk. State vehicle codes (such as California Vehicle Code § 21954 or New York VTL § 1154) explicitly mandate that every driver of a vehicle shall exercise due care to avoid colliding with any pedestrian upon any roadway, sounding the horn when necessary. If a driver was traveling 15 miles per hour over the speed limit, texting on a mobile device, or driving with dirty headlights, their negligent conduct contributed directly to the disaster.

In states following pure comparative negligence or modified comparative negligence rules, an injured pedestrian who crossed mid-block can still recover substantial financial compensation. If a jury determines that the pedestrian was 25 percent at fault for crossing outside a crosswalk and the speeding, distracted driver was 75 percent at fault, the pedestrian recovers 75 percent of their total damages. Accessing Tiered Insurance Coverages for Pedestrians

Because pedestrians do not have physical automotive insurance attached to their sneakers, a critical operational question is: which insurance company pays for your hospital bills and lost wages while you recover? The insurance recovery framework for pedestrians involves multiple coordinated coverage layers.

First, the at-fault driver's Bodily Injury Liability coverage is the primary recovery source for overall pain, suffering, long-term disability, and total medical reimbursement. Second, in no-fault states (such as New York, Florida, and New Jersey), the insurance policy of the car that struck you is legally required to pay your immediate emergency room and rehabilitation bills up to statutory Personal Injury Protection (PIP) limits, regardless of who caused the accident.

Third, if the driver fled the scene in an illegal hit-and-run or possessed only minimal liability coverage, an injured pedestrian can tap into their own household automobile insurance policy's Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM) coverage. In nearly all jurisdictions, your own auto policy's UM/UIM benefits follow you anywhere as a pedestrian, providing a crucial financial safety net if struck by an uninsured motorist.

How to Protect Your Rights After Being Hit by a Car

Step-by-step emergency checklist for a pedestrian struck by a motor vehicle while crossing a street.

  1. Ensure Police Dispatch and Paramedic Transport

    Ensure emergency 911 is dispatched immediately so paramedics can immobilize fractures and police can draft an official collision report.

  2. Photograph the Scene and Vehicle Impact Marks

    Have someone take photos of the vehicle's dented hood, shattered windshield, crosswalk markings, and your torn footwear and clothing.

  3. Collect Driver and Independent Witness Information

    Obtain the driver's license, vehicle registration, and insurance policy details, along with phone numbers of witnesses who saw you crossing.

  4. Retain a Dedicated Pedestrian Injury Attorney

    Consult an attorney before speaking with the driver's insurance adjuster to prevent comparative fault arguments and identify all insurance layers.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I get compensation if I was hit while crossing outside a crosswalk?

Yes. Drivers still have a legal duty of due care to avoid hitting pedestrians; under comparative negligence rules, you can recover damages reduced by your share of fault.

Q2: Who pays my medical bills if I was hit as a pedestrian?

Initial medical bills are paid by PIP insurance (from the driver's car or your own auto policy) or private health insurance, and reimbursed in the final liability settlement.

Q3: What if the driver who hit me fled the scene (hit-and-run)?

You can file an Uninsured Motorist (UM) claim through your own household auto insurance policy, which covers hit-and-run pedestrian injuries.

Q4: Does my own car insurance cover me when I am walking?

Yes. In most states, your personal auto insurance policy's PIP, MedPay, and Uninsured Motorist coverages extend to protect you when walking as a pedestrian.

Q5: How much is an average pedestrian accident settlement worth?

Due to severe bodily trauma, pedestrian settlements frequently range from $50,000 to over $500,000+, depending on driver policy limits and injury severity.

Q6: What is an unmarked crosswalk?

An unmarked crosswalk is the invisible continuation of sidewalk boundary lines across an intersection where no painted stripes are present; pedestrians have right-of-way.

Q7: How long do I have to file a lawsuit after being hit by a car?

The personal injury statute of limitations is typically two to three years from the date of the accident depending on your state.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Surviving a pedestrian collision requires focused medical rehabilitation and aggressive legal representation. Drivers have an inescapable statutory duty to watch for pedestrians and avoid impacts. Do not let an insurance adjuster intimidate you with allegations of jaywalking. Seek urgent medical care, preserve all clothing and digital evidence, and retain a personal injury lawyer to secure the complete financial recovery you need to heal.
