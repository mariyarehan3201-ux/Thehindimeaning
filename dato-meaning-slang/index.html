Dato Meaning Slang

Meaning
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

In the Spanish language, the word dato fundamentally translates to fact, piece of data, datum, or piece of information. However, across modern colloquial Spanish, contemporary Latin American slang, and digital communication in countries like Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, and Spain, dato has evolved into a vibrant, versatile conversational colloquialism. In everyday spoken vernacular, saying 'buen dato' or 'pasame el dato' means 'good tip', 'insider recommendation', or 'give me the inside scoop', while phrases like 'tirar la data' mean sharing juicy gossip, secret information, or valuable street knowledge.

Etymological Evolution: From Statistical Datum to Insider Intel

Etymologically, dato derives from the Latin datum, the neuter past participle of dare (to give), literally meaning 'something given' or an established premise. In formal Spanish, un dato is a statistical point, historical fact, or piece of technical data used in scientific research. Over the late twentieth century, everyday speakers began using dato colloquially to describe practical, high-value everyday information—such as a recommendation for an affordable mechanic, the phone number of a trusted plumber, or the secret recipe for a regional salsa.

Today, if a friend tells you about an underground rooftop coffee shop with extraordinary views and reasonable prices, responding with '¡Que buen dato!' expresses enthusiastic gratitude for an exceptional insider tip. Dato bridges the formal world of facts with the casual street world of local hacks and recommendations.

Regional Variations Across Latin America and Spain

The nuance and grammatical styling of dato vary pleasantly across the Hispanic world. In Argentina and Uruguay (Rioplatense Spanish), speakers frequently feminize the term into 'la data', utilizing it as an umbrella noun for the current vibe, exclusive intel, or background scoop on an upcoming social event (e.g., '¿Tenes la data de la fiesta?' meaning 'Do you have the details on the party?').

In Colombia and Chile, 'el dato' frequently refers to a direct telephone contact or professional recommendation. Asking '¿Tienes el dato de un electricista?' is the standard natural way to ask if someone can recommend a reliable electrician. In Mexico and Spain, 'dar por sentado un dato' means taking an unverified claim for granted, while digital TikTok influencers popularize '#ElDatoDelDia' to share rapid-fire historical and life-hack trivia.

Fluency in modern conversational Spanish requires recognizing when dato means clinical statistics versus when it represents warm personal networking and enthusiastic recommendations.

How to Use 'Dato' Naturally in Spoken Spanish

Follow these tips to integrate colloquial uses of dato into your Spanish conversations.

  1. Step 1

    Use '¡Buen dato!' when a local gives you helpful advice on where to eat, park, or find great deals in a new city.

  2. Step 2

    Say 'Pasame el dato' when asking a friend for the phone number or name of a trusted professional (hairdresser, mechanic).

  3. Step 3

    Adopt 'la data' when speaking with friends in Buenos Aires or Montevideo to sound culturally fluent with youth slang.

  4. Step 4

    Introduce interesting conversation trivia during dinners by saying 'Les comparto un dato curioso...' (Let me share a fun fact...).

  5. Step 5

    Use 'Tengo el dato' when you want to signal that you have exclusive insider knowledge about an upcoming opportunity.

  6. Step 6

    Distinguish between 'dato' (a single actionable tip or fact) and 'informacion' (broad general knowledge).

  7. Step 7

    Listen to regional podcasts and conversational street vloggers to observe how native speakers pace and inflect the word.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does 'dato' mean in Spanish slang?

In Spanish slang, 'dato' means an insider tip, a helpful recommendation, a secret lead, or the 'inside scoop' on something.

Q2: What does '¡Buen dato!' mean?

It is a popular expression meaning 'Great tip!' or 'Thanks for the helpful advice!' used across Latin America and Spain.

Q3: What does 'pasame el dato' mean?

It means 'hook me up with the contact' or 'give me the details' when asking for a recommendation like a good doctor or mechanic.

Q4: What is 'la data' in Argentine slang?

In Argentina, 'la data' refers to exclusive gossip, insider news, or the specific details about an upcoming event or party.

Q5: Is 'dato' formal or informal in Spanish?

It is both; formally it means a data point or scientific fact, while colloquially it means a handy tip or recommendation.

Q6: What does 'dato curioso' mean?

It means 'fun fact' or 'interesting trivia', commonly used by educators, podcasters, and storytellers.

Q7: What is the origin of the word dato?

It comes from the Latin 'datum', meaning 'something given', which originally formed the basis for mathematical calculations.
