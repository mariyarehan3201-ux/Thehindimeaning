If a Tow Truck Damages Your Car

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: October 1, 2026

If a tow truck damages your car during hookup, transit, or impound lot storage, the towing company is legally liable under common law principles of bailment and tort negligence. When a towing operator assumes physical control of your vehicle, a legal bailment for mutual benefit or involuntary bailment is established, imposing an affirmative legal duty of ordinary care upon the company. If the tow operator uses inappropriate towing hardware, fails to disengage the drivetrain on an all-wheel-drive vehicle, or scrapes body panels, you are entitled to full compensation for repair costs and diminished vehicle value.

Legal Principles of Towing Liability and Bailment Law

When a motor vehicle is towed—whether through a voluntary roadside assistance dispatch or an involuntary municipal parking impound—the legal relationship between the vehicle owner and the towing service is governed by the law of bailment. Under bailment doctrine, the owner is the bailor and the towing company is the bailee. The bailee takes lawful temporary custody of the personal property and owes an absolute duty of reasonable care to protect that property from foreseeable physical harm. If the vehicle is returned in a damaged condition that did not exist prior to the hookup, the law creates a legal presumption of bailee negligence.

To overcome this legal presumption, the towing company must prove that the structural, mechanical, or cosmetic damage was caused by pre-existing vehicle wear or unavoidable circumstances rather than improper towing methods. Many towing companies attempt to shield themselves from financial accountability by printing sweeping liability waivers on the back of impound release slips. Courts routinely void these boilerplate disclaimers as unconscionable contracts of adhesion, holding that commercial transport operators cannot legally contract away their liability for negligent conduct.

Common Towing Errors: AWD Transmission Failure and Body Damage

Among all mechanical failures resulting from negligent towing, drivetrain destruction on all-wheel-drive (AWD) and four-wheel-drive (4WD) vehicles is the most catastrophic. Modern AWD vehicles (such as Subaru, Audi Quattro, BMW xDrive, or dual-motor electric vehicles) feature permanently engaged center differentials or viscous couplings. Vehicle manufacturer specifications mandate that these automobiles must be transported exclusively on a flatbed carrier or with secondary tow dollies installed under the rolling wheels.

When a negligent or rushed tow operator hooks an AWD vehicle using a standard wheel-lift and drags the opposite two wheels along the asphalt at highway speeds, the spinning wheels rotate the driveshaft while the locked wheels keep internal gears stationary. This mechanical friction generates extreme heat within minutes, boiling gear oil, shredding planetary gears, and seizing the transfer case and transmission. The resulting repair requires an entire factory transmission replacement that routinely costs between four thousand and ten thousand dollars.

Filing Claims Against Tow Company Garagekeepers liability claims

Legitimate towing companies maintain specialized commercial liability claims known as Garagekeepers Liability Coverage and On-Hook Cargo liability claims. On-hook coverage specifically insures against physical damage sustained by a customer vehicle while it is being loaded, transported, or unloaded. When initiating a claim, bypass verbal arguments with tow yard attendants and request the towing company's commercial certificate of liability claims, including their policy number and claims adjuster contact information.

If the towing company refuses to cooperate or claims your vehicle was damaged prior to hookup, immediately contact your own comprehensive and collision auto risk claims representative. Your commercial risk representative will dispatch an independent adjuster to inspect the damage, pay for repairs minus your out-of-pocket threshold, and pursue aggressive subrogation against the towing company's commercial liability claims policy to recover your out-of-pocket threshold in full. For smaller claims under local jurisdictional limits, filing a civil action in small claims court is a powerful option, as tow companies frequently settle rather than sending corporate representatives to defend obvious negligence before a judge.

How to File a Property Damage Claim Against a Towing Company

Follow this step-by-step procedural workflow to document damage at the impound yard, demand commercial liability information, and secure repair payments.

  1. Inspect the Vehicle Thoroughly Before Signing Release Documents

    Before signing any paperwork or driving away from the impound lot, inspect the front bumper, rocker panels, wheels, tires, and undercarriage with a flashlight in plain daylight.

  2. Capture Photographic and Video Evidence on the Tow Lot

    Take detailed photographs and wide-angle video showing all scratches, dents, crushed exhaust pipes, or leaking transmission fluids while the vehicle remains physically parked inside the tow facility.

  3. Write a Damage Notation on the Impound Release Form

    If the tow company insists you sign a release receipt, clearly write 'Vehicle accepted under protest; subject to existing towing damages documented on-site' directly above your signature.

  4. Obtain an Immediate Certified Mechanical Inspection

    Drive or flatbed the car to a certified dealership or body shop to conduct a diagnostic scan, suspension alignment check, and transmission fluid inspection to document internal mechanical failure.

  5. Submit a Formal Claim to the Tow Company's On-Hook claims department

    Send a formal demand letter including mechanic repair estimates, pre-tow photos, and towing receipts to the towing company's commercial claims management office, initiating subrogation through your own tow management if necessary.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is a towing company liable if they damage my car?

Yes. Towing companies have a legal duty of care under bailment law. If their negligence during hookup, transit, or drop-off causes cosmetic, structural, or mechanical damage, they are legally liable for repair costs.

Q2: Can an all-wheel-drive car be towed with two wheels on the ground?

No. Towing an AWD car with two wheels rolling on the pavement forces the differential gears to spin against stationary components, destroying the transmission, transfer case, and viscous couplings within miles.

Q3: Does signing a tow release form waive my right to sue for damage?

No. Boilerplate liability waivers on tow receipts are commonly deemed unenforceable contracts of adhesion by courts. You retain the legal right to sue for negligent property damage.

Q4: What kind of claims covers damage caused by a tow truck?

Towing companies carry commercial On-Hook Cargo claims coverage and Garagekeepers Legal Liability claims coverage specifically designed to pay for customer vehicles damaged during transport and storage.

Q5: What should I do if the tow company refuses to accept responsibility?

File a claim with your own auto commercial tow entity under your collision coverage. Your claims department will cover repairs and use subrogation to collect compensation directly from the tow company.

Q6: Can I take a towing company to small claims court for car damage?

Yes. Small claims court is an efficient legal avenue for claims up to state limits (typically $5,000 to $12,500) where you can present repair estimates, photos, and mechanic reports without hiring a lawyer.

Q7: How do I prove the damage was caused by the tow truck and not pre-existing?

Proof includes timestamped photos taken prior to towing, dispatch condition reports from the tow driver, fresh metal scrapes with paint transfer, and mechanic reports detailing trauma consistent with tow hooks.

Q8: Can electric vehicles (EVs) be towed behind a standard tow truck?

No. Electric vehicles do not have a true neutral gear that disconnects the electric motors. Towing an EV with wheels rolling generates electrical current that can destroy inverters or cause catastrophic battery fires.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

When a towing company damages your car through negligent strapping, improper flatbed angles, or towing an AWD vehicle on two wheels, you are not powerless against boilerplate disclaimers. Under bailment law, towing companies are strictly accountable for delivering vehicles in the exact physical condition in which they were received. By capturing immediate photographs, having a certified mechanic complete a comprehensive diagnostic inspection, and asserting claims against their commercial garagekeepers liability claims policy, you can secure complete property restitution for repair costs and diminished vehicle value.
