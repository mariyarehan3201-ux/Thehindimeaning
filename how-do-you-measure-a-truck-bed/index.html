How Do You Measure a Truck Bed?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how do you measure a truck bed is an essential skill for pickup owners purchasing vital aftermarket accessories such as tonneau covers, camper shells (truck caps), drop-in bedliners, cargo racks, or crossbed toolboxes. Commercial accessory manufacturers do not manufacture universal, one-size-fits-all components; every cover and shell is engineered to exact, millimeter-precise bed dimensions. Measuring from the incorrect reference points—such as measuring the exterior body panels or keeping the tailgate open—inevitably leads to buying incompatible, expensive truck accessories.

The Golden Rule: Inside Bulkhead to Inside Tailgate

The single most critical rule when measuring a pickup truck bed is that all commercial bed lengths are calculated based on the interior cargo footprint rather than exterior body styling. Automotive manufacturers (Ford, Chevrolet, GMC, Ram, Toyota, Nissan) design flared outer fenders and wrap-around bumpers that add several inches to the truck's overall length, which are completely irrelevant for interior accessory fitment.

To measure bed length accurately, you must always close the tailgate completely. Position the metal hook of a rigid steel measuring tape flat against the inside wall of the front bulkhead (the metal wall of the bed located directly behind the cab glass). Extend the tape measure down the center of the bed floor straight back until it contacts the inside surface of the closed tailgate. Record this measurement in total inches.

Measuring Width: Bed Rails vs. Wheel Wells

Depending on the specific aftermarket accessory you plan to install, measuring truck bed width requires two distinct reference points. If you are purchasing a crossbed toolbox, bed rail caps, or a tonneau cover, you need the inside bed rail width. Measure across the width of the bed from the inside edge of the driver-side top bed rail to the inside edge of the passenger-side top bed rail at both the front (near the cab) and rear (near the tailgate), noting any subtle tapers.

Conversely, if you intend to load flat 4x8-foot plywood sheets, pallets, or install slide-out bed drawer systems, the critical measurement is the Width Between Wheel Wells. Extend your tape measure horizontally across the narrowest constriction point on the floor between the inner metal wheel housings. On virtually all standard full-size half-ton and heavy-duty pickups, this distance is engineered to be at least 48 to 50 inches wide to accommodate standard 48-inch construction materials.

Converting Inches to Nominal Feet and Cab Style Variations

When browsing retail truck accessory websites, you will rarely see products listed as '78.5 inches.' Instead, manufacturers categorize products by nominal marketing designations—such as a '6.5-Foot Bed.' To determine your truck's nominal category, divide your total measured inches by 12. For instance, a bed measuring 78 inches equals 6.5 feet, whereas a bed measuring 67 inches corresponds to a nominal 5.5-foot short bed.

Furthermore, truck bed lengths are intrinsically tied to vehicle cab configurations. Automotive manufacturers pair shorter beds (5.5-foot) with massive four-door Crew Cabs (SuperCrew, CrewMax) to keep overall vehicle wheelbases manageable for residential suburban garages. In contrast, extended cabs (SuperCab, Double Cab) standardly feature 6.5-foot beds, while commercial two-door Regular Cabs are frequently paired with 8-foot long beds.

How to Measure Your Truck Bed for Accessories

Follow these five simple steps to calculate the exact dimensions of your pickup truck bed.

  1. Close the Tailgate Completely

    Ensure the tailgate is latched shut so you measure true usable internal cargo space.

  2. Measure Inside Length from Front Bulkhead to Tailgate

    Hook the tape measure to the inner front wall and measure straight back to the inner tailgate surface.

  3. Measure Inside Width Between the Top Bed Rails

    Measure across the cargo opening from the inside lip of one bed rail to the inside lip of the opposite rail.

  4. Measure Floor Width Between the Wheel Wells

    Measure the narrowest distance on the bed floor between the two protruding inner wheel arches.

  5. Convert Total Inches to Nominal Bed Size

    Divide total inches by 12 to find your truck's standard category (e.g., 67 inches = 5.5-foot bed).

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Do you measure a truck bed with the tailgate open or closed?

Always measure with the tailgate closed; all commercial truck accessories are engineered to fit inside the closed bed perimeter.

Q2: Where do you start measuring a truck bed?

Start from the inside metal bulkhead wall directly behind the cab glass and extend the tape to the inside of the tailgate.

Q3: Why does my 6-foot bed only measure 5 feet 8 inches?

Modern midsize crew cab pickups (Tacoma, Colorado) standardly feature 5-foot short beds (around 60 inches) rather than 6-foot beds.

Q4: Can a standard 4x8 plywood sheet fit flat in a truck bed?

In full-size pickups (F-150, Silverado, Ram), 4x8 sheets fit flat between the wheel wells (48+ inches wide); an 8-foot bed allows closing the tailgate.

Q5: Are all truck beds the same width?

No, midsize trucks have bed widths between wheel wells around 41 to 44 inches, while full-size trucks feature widths of 48 to 51 inches.

Q6: What is the difference between a short bed and a standard bed?

A short bed is typically 5.5 feet long (on crew cabs), a standard bed is 6.5 feet long, and a long bed is 8.0 feet long.

Q7: Does a spray-in bedliner change truck bed measurements?

A spray-in bedliner (Line-X, Rhino) adds only about 1/8 to 1/4 inch of thickness, which does not interfere with tonneau cover fitment.

Q8: How do I know my truck's exact bed size without measuring?

You can check the original window sticker, look up your VIN on a manufacturer portal, or check the driver's door jamb placard.
