Assault on a Pregnant Woman Texas

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Under Texas Penal Code § 22.01(b)(5), committing an assault against an individual known to be pregnant is an elevated Third-Degree Felony punishable by 2 to 10 years in Texas state prison and fines up to $10,000. Furthermore, under the Texas Prenatal Protection Act, inflicting harm that injures or terminates an unborn child triggers separate, severe felony homicide or aggravated assault charges.

Texas Statutory Framework: Texas Penal Code § 22.01(b)(5)

In the State of Texas, the criminal justice system enforces exceptionally strict statutory protections for pregnant women and unborn children. While a standard domestic assault involving minor physical contact or pain is generally prosecuted as a Class A misdemeanor, Texas law significantly elevates the offense when the victim is pregnant.

Under Texas Penal Code § 22.01(b)(5), a person commits a Third-Degree Felony if they intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly cause bodily injury to another person, and the actor knows that the victim is pregnant at the time of the offense. In Texas criminal jurisprudence, 'bodily injury' is defined broadly under Tex. Penal Code § 1.07(a)(8) as 'physical pain, illness, or any impairment of physical condition.' This means that prosecutors do not need to prove broken bones or hospitalization; causing physical bruising or pain to a woman known to be pregnant satisfies the statutory threshold for felony prosecution.

Knowledge of the pregnancy can be established circumstantially or directly. Evidence such as visible physical pregnancy, verbal statements made by the victim, text message conversations discussing obstetric appointments, ultrasound photographs displayed in the home, or prenatal vitamins confirms the perpetrator was aware of the pregnancy. The Texas Prenatal Protection Act: Dual Victim Prosecution

A profound dimension of Texas criminal law is the Texas Prenatal Protection Act, enacted into state law in 2003 under Senate Bill 319. This statute amended the Texas Penal Code (specifically Tex. Penal Code § 1.07(a)(26) and Chapter 19) to redefine an 'individual' to include an unborn child at every stage of gestation, from fertilization until birth.

Under this legal doctrine, an assailant who strikes, kicks, or batters a pregnant woman does not commit an offense against a single person; in the eyes of Texas law, there are two distinct legal victims: the expectant mother and the unborn child. If an act of domestic violence or physical battery inflicts trauma that causes placental abruption, emergency premature delivery, severe fetal trauma, or miscarriage (fetal demise), the perpetrator faces separate criminal homicide or aggravated assault charges.

Prosecutors routinely indict defendants on simultaneous counts: one count of Third-Degree Felony Assault on a Pregnant Female, and a second count of First-Degree Murder or Manslaughter for the death of the unborn fetus. This legal mechanism prevents assailants from escaping severe accountability by claiming they only intended to harm the mother. Emergency Protective Orders and Mandatory Arrest Protocols

When Texas law enforcement officers respond to a 911 domestic violence call involving a pregnant victim, statutory mandates strictly remove officer discretion.

Under Article 14.03 of the Texas Code of Criminal Procedure, peace officers are legally mandated to execute an immediate warrantless arrest if they establish probable cause that an assault causing bodily injury occurred. The defendant is handcuffed and booked into county jail without bond pending formal magistration. Under Article 17.292 of the Texas Code of Criminal Procedure, the magistrate routinely issues a Magistrate's Order for Emergency Protection (MOEP).

In cases involving felony assault or assault on a pregnant female, magistrates frequently issue MOEPs sua sponte (on their own motion) lasting between 61 and 91 days. The protective order legally bars the defendant from approaching within 500 or 1,000 feet of the victim's residence, workplace, or healthcare clinic, prohibits all electronic communications, and orders the immediate surrender of all firearms. Violating this protective order constitutes a separate crime under Texas Penal Code § 25.07.

How to Seek Safety and Legal Protection in Texas

Step-by-step emergency legal and safety protocol for pregnant victims of domestic violence.

  1. Call 911 Immediately to Trigger Mandatory Arrest

    Dial 911; inform the dispatcher that you are pregnant and experiencing physical assault to dispatch emergency police and medical paramedics.

  2. Obtain Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric Care

    Go to an emergency labor and delivery trauma center immediately to perform fetal heart monitoring, ultrasound checks, and document maternal bruising.

  3. Request a Magistrate's Order for Emergency Protection (MOEP)

    Ask the arresting police officer or victim services advocate to request an emergency protective order from the magistrate within 24 hours of booking.

  4. Connect with the Texas Crime Victims' Compensation Fund

    Apply through the Texas Office of the Attorney General (OAG) for financial grants covering medical bills, obstetric care, and emergency relocation.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is assault on a pregnant woman always a felony in Texas?

Yes. Under Texas Penal Code 22.01(b)(5), causing bodily injury to someone known to be pregnant is an automatic Third-Degree Felony.

Q2: What if the attacker did not know the woman was pregnant?

The state must prove the defendant knew about the pregnancy; if lack of knowledge is established, the charge drops to a Class A misdemeanor.

Q3: Can an attacker be charged with murder if the baby dies?

Yes. Under the Texas Prenatal Protection Act, causing the death of an unborn fetus through assault is prosecuted as manslaughter or murder.

Q4: How long is the prison sentence for this charge in Texas?

A Third-Degree Felony carries 2 to 10 years in prison; if strangulation occurred, it is a Second-Degree Felony carrying 2 to 20 years.

Q5: Can the victim drop the charges against the defendant?

No. In Texas, only the District Attorney has the legal power to dismiss criminal charges; the victim cannot drop felony charges.

Q6: What is an MOEP in a Texas domestic violence case?

A Magistrate's Order for Emergency Protection (MOEP) is a criminal court order prohibiting the defendant from contacting or approaching the victim.

Q7: Can an assailant be sued in civil court for pregnant assault?

Yes. The victim can file a civil personal injury lawsuit to recover medical expenses, emotional trauma damages, and substantial punitive damages.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Assault on a pregnant woman in Texas is treated as an egregious felony crime carrying severe, multi-year state prison sentences. With the Texas Prenatal Protection Act recognizing the unborn child as an independent legal victim, assailants face dual-charge indictments that can escalate to capital murder if the pregnancy is terminated. Expectant mothers subjected to domestic abuse must reach out immediately to law enforcement, seek emergency obstetric medical care, and secure judicial protective orders to protect their lives and their unborn children.
