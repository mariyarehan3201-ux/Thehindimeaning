How Much Is a Bonded Title in Ga?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Acquiring ownership documentation for a vehicle missing its legal certificate of title in Georgia requires establishing a surety bond through the state Department of Revenue (DOR). If you want to know how much is a bonded title in ga, the total out-of-pocket cost generally ranges from $175 to $450 for standard passenger vehicles. The exact expense depends primarily on the calculated fair market value of your automobile, the premium quoted by your surety bond underwriter, statutory state filing fees, vehicle inspection charges, and mandatory Title Ad Valorem Tax (TAVT).

Determining Your Georgia Surety Bond Value and Premium

In the state of Georgia, motor vehicle ownership bonds must be executed for an amount equal to exactly two times the average retail value of the vehicle. The Georgia Department of Revenue determines this valuation based on official state vehicle assessment schedules or reputable industry guides like the National Automobile Dealers Association (NADA). For instance, if the state assesses your vehicle retail value at $4,000, your required surety bond face amount will be exactly $8,000. This legally binding financial instrument protects the state and any prior rightful owners or lienholders against fraudulent title claims.

The price you actually pay for this bond is known as the surety premium, which is a small fraction of the total bond amount rather than the full face value. For bond values under $6,000 to $10,000, most licensed Georgia surety companies charge a flat minimum premium ranging from $100 to $150. For higher-value bonds exceeding $10,000, premiums generally cost between 1 percent and 2 percent of the bond value for applicants with good credit scores. If an applicant has severe credit blemishes, underwriters may quote slightly higher rates between 3 percent and 5 percent.

Mandatory Government Fees, Taxes, and County Inspections

Aside from the commercial surety bond premium, titling an automobile through Georgia bonded procedures requires paying several fixed county tag office and state processing fees. First, you must have your vehicle physically inspected by a certified Georgia law enforcement officer or state-approved tag agent using Form T-22B (Certification of Inspection). While many county sheriff departments or local municipal police stations perform this verification free of charge at their precinct, some jurisdictions assess an administrative inspection fee between $5 and $20.

When filing your completed packet at your local County Tag Office, the official Georgia certificate of title application fee is legally set at $18. In addition, you must submit a National Motor Vehicle Title Information System (NMVTIS) history report, which typically costs $5 to $15 through an approved online provider. Crucially, applicants must also remit Georgia Title Ad Valorem Tax (TAVT), calculated as a state-mandated percentage (currently 6.6 percent in most standard cases) of the fair market value, unless valid exemptions apply based on prior registration.

Eligibility Rules, Required Paperwork, and Long-Term Conversion

To obtain a bonded title in Georgia, strict legal conditions must be met. The vehicle must be a model year 1986 or newer, as older vehicles in Georgia can frequently be registered on a bill of sale alone. Furthermore, the vehicle cannot be branded as junk, scrap, parts-only, or stolen, and it must not have an active unresolved mechanic lien or financial lien attached to the VIN. You must also prove legal Georgia residency and establish that you made reasonable, documented efforts to obtain the original certificate of title from the prior registered owner.

The bonded title issued by the Georgia Department of Revenue remains bonded for a statutory duration of exactly four years. During this 48-month window, if no third party files a valid legal ownership challenge or claim against your bond, the bond condition is formally fulfilled. At the conclusion of this four-year period, you can return to the county tag office to apply for a clean, unbonded standard Georgia certificate of title, fully freeing your vehicle asset from bonding restrictions.

How to Complete the Georgia Bonded Title Process

Follow these five straightforward steps to obtain your surety bond and secure your Georgia bonded vehicle title.

  1. Establish Vehicle Value and Check NMVTIS

    Run an approved NMVTIS vehicle history report to verify clean branding and check DOR valuation schedules for the vehicle fair market value.

  2. Schedule a Law Enforcement Physical Inspection

    Have a certified Georgia police officer or county tag inspector complete Form T-22B verifying your VIN and odometer reading.

  3. Purchase a 4-Year Georgia Motor Vehicle Title Bond

    Buy an official Form MV-46 surety bond for twice the vehicle retail value from an insurance agency authorized in Georgia.

  4. Assemble the Complete Application Packet

    Compile Form MV-1, Form MV-46, Form T-22B, the NMVTIS report, bill of sale, and proof of Georgia residency.

  5. Submit at Your Local County Tag Office

    Deliver your packet to your County Tax Commissioner office and pay the $18 title fee along with required TAVT taxes.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a Georgia title bond premium usually cost?

Most standard passenger vehicles qualify for the minimum surety bond premium of $100 to $150 through licensed insurance agencies.

Q2: How is the required bond amount calculated in Georgia?

Georgia law requires the surety bond face amount to be exactly twice (200%) the fair market retail value assessed by the state DOR.

Q3: Can I get a bonded title for any vehicle in Georgia?

Bonded titles are only issued for vehicles from 1986 or newer that are not classified as scrap, parts-only, or reported stolen.

Q4: How long does a Georgia bonded title remain bonded?

A Georgia bonded title carries the bonded status for four years, after which the owner can apply for a standard clean title.

Q5: Can I sell a vehicle with a Georgia bonded title?

Yes, you can legally sell, transfer, or register a vehicle holding a valid Georgia bonded title, as the bond protects future purchasers.

Q6: What happens if someone claims ownership against my bond?

The surety company investigates the claim; if legitimate, the surety compensates the claimant and pursues reimbursement from you.

Q7: Do I have to pay TAVT on a bonded title in GA?

Yes, Georgia Title Ad Valorem Tax (TAVT) must be paid at the time of titling unless you qualify for an explicit statutory exemption.

Q8: Where do I submit my Georgia bonded title paperwork?

All completed paperwork, inspections, and fees must be submitted directly to your local County Tag Office in your county of residence.
