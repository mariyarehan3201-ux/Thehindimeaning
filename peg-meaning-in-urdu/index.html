Peg Meaning in Urdu

Meaning
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

In South Asian colloquial vernacular, contemporary Urdu and Hindi slang, and regional beverage culture across Pakistan and India, a peg (پیگ) refers to a standardized volumetric unit of measure for alcoholic spirits, particularly whiskey, rum, and brandy. While the word 'peg' fundamentally originated from British colonial naval and railway measurement traditions, it was fully assimilated into South Asian urban vocabulary, films, and literature. In everyday Urdu social parlance, asking for 'chhota peg' (چھوٹا پیگ) or 'bada peg' (بڑا پیگ) specifies the exact volume of liquor poured into a tumbler.

Colonial Etymology: From Wooden Tent Stakes to Liquor Measures

The linguistic journey of 'peg' into Urdu and Hindi dates back to the British East India Company and the British Raj in the nineteenth century. In English, a peg originally described a tapered wooden pin or stake used to secure canvas military tents or fasten ship timber joints. British colonial officers, soldiers, and railway engineers stationed across the hot plains of Punjab and Bengal faced immense physical exhaustion and tropical diseases like malaria.

At the end of a grueling workday, British officers would enjoy whiskey to unwind. The phrase 'taking a peg' emerged from the nautical idiom 'taking someone down a peg', while medical folklore held that a stiff drink drove another 'peg' into one's coffin or anchored one down after a hard day. South Asian household staff, bartenders, and local troops serving in the British Indian Army adopted the term, phoneticizing it into Urdu as پیگ (peg) to describe a measured glass of spirits.

The Legend of the Patiala Peg: Royal Punjabi Hospitality

Among all variations of the peg in South Asian lore, none carries greater cultural fame than the legendary Patiala Peg (پٹیالہ پیگ). According to celebrated historical legend, the term originated with Maharaja Bhupinder Singh of Patiala (1891–1938), the lavish and charismatic ruler of the princely state of Patiala in Punjab. Renowned for his formidable stature and legendary hospitality, the Maharaja hosted a friendly polo match against the elite British 'Viceroy's Pride' polo team.

Aware that the British officers were exceptional polo riders, the Maharaja's team served them extraordinarily generous drinks the evening before the match. Instead of standard 30ml measures, the royal bartenders poured double and quadruple measures—measured as the height of the index finger and little finger held against the side of the glass (roughly 90 to 120ml). When the British guests commented on the intoxicating size of the drinks, the Maharaja smiled and proudly declared: 'In Patiala, this is a normal peg!'

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does 'peg' mean in Urdu and Hindi?

In Urdu and Hindi, a peg refers to a measured pour of alcoholic spirits, usually whiskey, rum, or brandy.

Q2: How many ml is a small peg (chhota peg)?

A chhota peg is standardly 30 milliliters (equivalent to approximately one fluid ounce or a single shot).

Q3: How many ml is a large peg (bada peg)?

A bada peg is 60 milliliters (equivalent to two fluid ounces or a standard double shot).

Q4: What is a Patiala peg?

A Patiala peg is a legendary, extraordinarily generous pour of 90ml to 120ml (roughly two to four fingers high), named after the Maharaja of Patiala.

Q5: Where did the word peg come from?

It came from 19th-century British colonial officers and soldiers in India, derived from nautical wooden pins and military pegs.

Q6: Is alcohol allowed in Pakistan?

Alcohol is legally prohibited for Muslim citizens in Pakistan under the 1977 Prohibition Ordinance, though non-Muslims and foreign tourists can obtain permits.

Q7: Who was the Maharaja of Patiala?

Maharaja Bhupinder Singh of Patiala was a charismatic 20th-century Indian royal known for his lavish lifestyle, cricket teams, and legendary hospitality.
