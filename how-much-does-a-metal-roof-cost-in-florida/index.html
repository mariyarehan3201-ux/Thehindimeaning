How Much Does a Metal Roof Cost in Florida?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a metal roof cost in Florida requires evaluating metal profile types, Florida Building Code wind uplift ratings, and roof geometry. On average, installing a metal roof on a typical 2,000-square-foot home in Florida costs between $14,000 and $36,000. Basic exposed-fastener 5V Crimp or corrugated metal roofs range from $6.00 to $10.50 per square foot installed ($12,000 to $21,000), while premium concealed-fastener Standing Seam systems cost $12.00 to $18.50 per square foot ($24,000 to $37,000).

Standing Seam vs. 5V Crimp and Exposed-Fastener Systems in Florida

The choice of metal panel profile is the primary determinant of both cost and longevity under Florida's punishing subtropical climate. Standing Seam metal roofs represent the gold standard in coastal architectural roofing, featuring raised vertical interlocking ribs that conceal all fasteners beneath the seams. With zero exposed screw penetrations, standing seam eliminates water leakage risks from gasket degradation, rated to withstand Category 5 hurricane winds (150 to 170+ MPH). Standing seam costs $12.00 to $18.50 per square foot installed.

Traditional 5V Crimp and corrugated agricultural metal panels are popular, cost-effective alternatives, particularly across Central and rural Florida. Priced at $6.00 to $10.50 per square foot installed, 5V Crimp utilizes exposed screws driven through the face of the metal panel with neoprene rubber washers. While economical, Florida's intense UV radiation degrades rubber washer gaskets over 10 to 15 years, requiring complete screw replacement to prevent roof leaks.

Material Substrates: Galvalume Steel vs. Coastal Aluminum

In Florida's maritime environment, selecting the appropriate metal substrate is critical. Galvalume steel—carbon steel hot-dipped in an alloy of 55% aluminum and 45% zinc—is the most widely installed material. Coated with premium Kynar 500 (PVDF) resin finishes, Galvalume resists corrosion, reflects 70% of solar thermal energy, and carries 30 to 40-year manufacturer warranties, making it ideal for inland Florida properties.

However, for homes located within 1,500 feet of breaking saltwater or ocean coastlines, Galvalume steel is susceptible to edge creep and salt-spray corrosion that voids manufacturer warranties. Coastal properties in Florida require heavy-gauge coastal aluminum (0.032 or 0.040-inch thickness). Marine-grade aluminum naturally forms an impervious aluminum oxide film that never rusts, commanding a 20% to 30% price premium over Galvalume steel ($15 to $21 per square foot installed).

Florida Building Code, Insurance Discounts, and Energy Efficiency

Installing a metal roof in Florida requires strict adherence to the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) requirements of the Florida Building Code (mandatory in Miami-Dade and Broward counties). The code mandates high-temperature self-adhering secondary water barrier underlayment (peel-and-stick membrane) applied directly to re-nailed roof decking with ring-shank nails. These mandatory mitigation measures ensure that even if metal panels are torn away in a hurricane, rainwater cannot penetrate the home.

The long-term return on investment for a Florida metal roof is substantial. Homeowners routinely qualify for substantial wind mitigation credits on their homeowner's insurance policies, often saving $800 to $2,500 annually. Furthermore, reflective 'cool metal roof' coatings reduce attic temperatures by 20 to 30 degrees Fahrenheit, lowering summer air conditioning electrical bills by 15% to 25%.

How to Choose and Install a Florida Metal Roof

Follow these five critical steps to ensure your Florida metal roof survives hurricanes and yields maximum insurance savings.

  1. Verify Coastal Saltwater Distance

    Determine if your property is within 1,500 feet of open saltwater to decide between Galvalume steel and marine-grade aluminum.

  2. Demand Florida Product Approval Codes

    Ensure the specific metal roofing panel system has an active Florida Product Approval Number or Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA).

  3. Specify Peel-and-Stick Secondary Water Barrier

    Insist on a high-temperature self-adhering polymer underlayment across the entire roof deck for maximum water barrier protection.

  4. Re-Nail the Roof Deck with Ring-Shank Nails

    Ensure the roofing crew re-nails the plywood roof decking with 8d ring-shank nails spaced at 6-inch intervals per Florida code.

  5. Schedule a Wind Mitigation Inspection After Installation

    Hire a licensed home inspector to complete a Florida Uniform Wind Mitigation Inspection report to secure insurance discounts.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a metal roof cost on a 2,000 sq ft house in Florida?

A 2,000-square-foot home typically costs between $14,000 and $21,000 for 5V Crimp, and $24,000 to $36,000 for Standing Seam.

Q2: Which metal roof is best for Florida hurricanes?

Concealed-fastener mechanically seamed Standing Seam metal roofs provide the highest wind uplift resistance (rated up to 180 MPH).

Q3: How much can a metal roof save on Florida home insurance?

A metal roof with secondary water barrier and certified ring-shank deck re-nailing can lower annual insurance premiums by 15% to 40%.

Q4: How long does a metal roof last in Florida?

Standing seam Galvalume and aluminum metal roofs last 40 to 60+ years, compared to traditional asphalt shingles that last 12 to 18 years in Florida.

Q5: Is a metal roof loud when it rains in Florida?

No, when installed over solid plywood decking with thick peel-and-stick underlayment and attic insulation, interior rain sound is barely audible.

Q6: Can a metal roof be installed over existing asphalt shingles in Florida?

While permitted under certain building codes, Florida contractors strongly recommend tearing off old shingles to inspect and re-nail roof decking.

Q7: What is 5V Crimp roofing?

5V Crimp is a classic Florida exposed-fastener metal panel with small V-shaped ribs, widely used for residential and agricultural roofs.

Q8: Does a metal roof lower air conditioning bills in Florida?

Yes, reflective cool-roof coatings reflect solar heat, reducing attic heat buildup and lowering summer cooling costs by 15% to 25%.
