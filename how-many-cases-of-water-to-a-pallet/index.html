How Many Cases of Water to a Pallet?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how many cases of water to a pallet requires examining the logistics formulas used by beverage manufacturers and wholesale distributors. In standard commercial supply chains, a full 48x40-inch pallet holds between 48 to 84 cases of bottled water. The exact count is dictated by the bottle packaging configuration: standard 24-packs of 500 mL (16.9 oz) water bottles are stacked 72, 78, or 84 cases per pallet, while bulk wholesale club 35-pack or 40-pack cases are configured at 48 cases per pallet.

The 'Ti/Hi' Calculation: Determining Pallet Geometry

In the commercial shipping industry, pallet configurations are planned using the 'Ti/Hi' calculation method. The 'Tie' (Ti) refers to how many cases fit on a single horizontal tier or layer across the surface of a standard Grocery Manufacturers Association (GMA) 48x40 pallet. The 'High' (Hi) indicates the number of vertical layers stacked on top of each other before stretch wrapping.

For standard 24-count cases of 16.9-ounce water bottles, the cases are arranged in an interlocking brick-pattern with 12, 13, or 14 cases per tier. Stacked 6 layers high, these Ti/Hi combinations yield standard totals of 72 cases (12x6), 78 cases (13x6), or 84 cases (14x6). Interlocking the cases between layers prevents load shifting during rapid highway braking and sharp transport turns.

Weight Distribution and DOT Highway Regulations

The governing limitation that dictates how many cases of water can sit on a pallet is gross shipping weight. Water has a physical density of 8.34 pounds per gallon, translating to roughly 27 to 28 pounds per 24-pack case. An 84-case pallet weighs approximately 2,350 to 2,400 pounds when including the wooden pallet base and plastic strapping.

Federal Department of Transportation (DOT) gross vehicle weight limits enforce an 80,000-pound maximum for standard commercial tractor-trailers. After accounting for the tare weight of the semi-truck tractor and empty 53-foot trailer (typically 34,000 to 36,000 pounds), a motor carrier can legally transport only 44,000 to 45,000 pounds of freight. Because of this, a carrier reaches legal weight capacity with just 18 to 20 pallets of water—filling only about 60% of the trailer's available floor area.

Safe Handling, Storage Guidelines, and Packaging Integrity

When managing bulk water deliveries, facility managers and relief agencies must adhere to strict handling procedures. Moving a 2,400-pound water pallet requires certified material handling machinery, such as an electric forklift or a hydraulic pallet jack rated for 3,000+ pounds. Never attempt to drag a water pallet across asphalt or uneven surfaces using light utility equipment.

Furthermore, plastic bottles made from polyethylene terephthalate (PET) are microscopically semi-permeable. While completely safe for potable water containment, PET bottles can absorb ambient chemical vapors over prolonged storage. Water pallets must always be stored in clean, well-ventilated, pest-controlled indoor facilities away from vehicle exhaust, gasoline fumes, cleaning solvents, and pesticides.

How to Order and Calculate Water Pallet Needs

Follow these five steps to estimate, order, and stage the correct quantity of water pallets for your organization.

  1. Calculate Total People and Required Daily Gallons

    Estimate 1 gallon of potable drinking water per person per day for emergency preparedness planning.

  2. Divide Total Water Volume by Gallons Per Pallet

    Divide your required gallon target by 270 (the approximate liquid gallon content of an 84-case pallet).

  3. Confirm Receiving Facility Forklift or Dock Access

    Ensure your delivery site has a loading dock or request a liftgate-equipped truck with a pallet jack.

  4. Inspect Stretch Wrap and Banding Upon Freight Delivery

    Verify that cases are shrink-wrapped tightly without crushed corner cases before signing the delivery receipt (BOL).

  5. Rotate Pallets Using First-In First-Out (FIFO) Protocols

    Date incoming water pallets and consume older inventory first to maintain fresh, crisp bottled water quality.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How many cases of water are on a standard 24-pack pallet?

Most commercial 24-pack pallets contain either 72, 78, or 84 cases, with 84 cases being the most common.

Q2: How many cases of water are on a 40-pack pallet?

A pallet of 40-pack club cases (like Kirkland) holds 48 cases, totaling 1,920 bottles.

Q3: How many cases of water can fit on a 53-foot semi-trailer?

A 53-foot truckload carries 18 to 20 pallets, totaling roughly 1,500 to 1,680 cases before reaching legal weight limits.

Q4: How tall is a pallet of water?

A full pallet of bottled water stands between 50 and 58 inches high from floor to top layer.

Q5: How many gallons of water are on an 84-case pallet?

An 84-case pallet of 16.9 oz bottles contains approximately 266 gallons of drinking water.

Q6: Can you fit a pallet of water in a pickup truck?

Most half-ton pickups (F-150, Silverado 1500) have payload limits of 1,500 to 2,000 lbs; a 2,400 lb water pallet requires a 3/4-ton (F-250) truck.

Q7: Why are water pallets only stacked 6 layers high?

Stacking more than 6 layers causes lower plastic bottles to crush under hydrostatic weight and exceeds road weight limits.

Q8: What does Ti/Hi mean for water pallets?

'Ti' is cases per horizontal layer; 'Hi' is the number of vertical layers. A 14x6 Ti/Hi equals 84 cases per pallet.
