How Much to Repair a Keyed Car?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Discovering that your car has been intentionally keyed is infuriating, and the expense to restore your vehicle finish depends directly on the depth of the gouge and the number of damaged body panels. On average, repairing a keyed car costs between $150 and $2,500. A shallow surface scratch restricted to the outer clear coat layer can often be buffed and polished out for $150 to $300. In contrast, a deep malicious key gouge that slices through the color basecoat and primer down to the bare sheet metal across multiple panels frequently requires complete panel sanding, repainting, and clear-coat blending, pushing professional collision repair bills well past $1,500 to $3,000.

Depth Classification and Cost Determinants for Key Scratches

Automotive paint finishes consist of four distinct strata: the electrocoat e-primer applied to bare metal or composite panels, the surfacer primer layer, the pigmented color basecoat, and the protective acrylic polyurethane clear coat. When evaluating keyed damage, professional auto body estimators use the fingernail drag test. If your fingernail glides over the scratch without catching, the damage is isolated within the clear coat and can be eliminated through multi-stage compound rotary buffing, micro-abrasive wet sanding, or paint correction. This non-invasive detailing work averages between $150 and $350 per affected panel.

However, perpetrators of vehicular keying typically exert substantial downward force, slicing through the basecoat and exposing bare steel or plastic substrates. Once the paint film is penetrated to the primer or metal, compounding cannot fix the defect because physical paint pigment is missing. Professional repair necessitates feathering the gouge with graded sandpaper, applying filler primer, spraying computer-matched basecoat pigment, and clearing the entire body panel from seam to seam. If a vandal keyed an entire side of the vehicle—spanning the front fender, front door, rear door, and quarter panel—each adjacent panel must be detrimmed, prepped, and sprayed, driving labor hours up rapidly.

Insurance Deductibles vs Out-of-Pocket Paint Restoration

Keying damage falls under the comprehensive coverage portion of an automotive insurance policy rather than collision coverage, because it is classified legally as vandalism or criminal mischief. Filing a comprehensive claim generally will not cause your insurance premium rates to spike as aggressively as an at-fault collision accident would. However, your decision to file a claim hinges entirely on your comprehensive deductible. If your deductible is $500 or $1,000 and the estimated repair for a single door panel is $650, paying out of pocket avoids an unnecessary claim entry on your Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange CLUE report.

Conversely, when a vehicle suffers 360-degree vandalism involving the hood, trunk lid, and both sides, repair estimates routinely exceed $3,500 to $5,000. In severe scenarios, comprehensive insurance coverage is essential. Before filing, motorists must file an official police report documenting criminal vandalism, take high-resolution photographs under direct sunlight and diffuse lighting, and secure estimates from both independent auto body shops and certified dealer collision centers. Insurance adjusters will calculate paint labor hours, paint materials charges per hour, and remove-and-install R&I labor for door handles, mirrors, and belt moldings.

Critical Factors That Escalate Professional Paint Costs

Several specialized vehicle characteristics can double or triple paint repair estimates. Metallic, pearlescent, and tri-coat finishes require significantly more skilled labor and expensive pigment materials than non-metallic flat colors such as solid gloss white or gloss black. Pearlescent finishes incorporate microscopic ceramic or mica crystals that refract sunlight; body technicians must spray blend adjacent undamaged panels to ensure the light reflection matches seamlessly from door to quarter panel, adding hundreds of dollars in blending labor.

Furthermore, modern vehicle construction incorporates numerous delicate sensors, cameras, and electrical modules embedded directly into bumpers, door handles, and exterior trim. Removing and reinstalling R&I these components to prevent overspray adds substantial mechanical labor hours at standard body shop rates of $60 to $120 per hour. If a side-view mirror housing or blind-spot monitoring radar must be disconnected and recalibrated after panel repainting, digital ADAS calibration fees ranging from $250 to $600 may be added to your final collision invoice.

How to Evaluate and Address a Keyed Car Scratch

Follow these five practical steps to assess scratch depth, protect against corrosion, and choose the most cost-effective repair path.

  1. Clean and Decontaminate the Scratched Surface

    Wash the keyed panel thoroughly using automotive shampoo and isopropyl alcohol to remove road grime, dirt particles, and wax from the gouge.

  2. Perform the Fingernail Drag Test for Depth Assessment

    Gently run the tip of your fingernail across the scratch perpendicular to the line to verify whether it catches on the bare primer or metal beneath.

  3. Apply Clear Automotive Wax or Primer Sealant Immediately

    If bare sheet metal or raw steel is exposed, apply temporary clear coat or wax immediately to prevent atmospheric moisture from causing surface rust oxidation.

  4. Collect Multiple Professional Collision Shop Estimates

    Visit at least two reputable independent collision repair facilities and one dealership body shop to obtain itemized written estimates for labor and paint.

  5. Compare Your Comprehensive Deductible Against Out-of-Pocket Costs

    Check your auto policy comprehensive deductible; file a claim if repair costs far surpass the deductible or choose out-of-pocket payment to avoid a claims record.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does auto insurance cover car keying vandalism?

Yes, car keying is covered under comprehensive auto insurance policies because it is classified as vandalism or malicious mischief, subject to your comprehensive deductible.

Q2: Can WD-40 or toothpaste fix a keyed car scratch?

No, neither WD-40 nor toothpaste can fix paint scratches; WD-40 merely temporarily masks minor scuffs with oil, while toothpaste lacks uniform abrasives to level clear coat.

Q3: Will buffing compound remove a deep key scratch?

Buffing compound only smooths imperfections within the clear coat layer; if the key scratch penetrates through to the colored basecoat or metal, compounding cannot restore the missing pigment.

Q4: How long does a body shop take to fix a keyed car?

A single panel spot repair or buffing takes 1 to 2 days, while multi-panel deep scratch repairs requiring full respraying, clear blending, and curing usually take 3 to 6 business days.

Q5: Will filing an insurance claim for keying raise my monthly rates?

Comprehensive vandalism claims typically do not raise insurance premiums as much as at-fault collision claims, though frequent claims within a short window can affect overall risk tiers.

Q6: Can touch-up paint pens make a key scratch completely invisible?

Touch-up pens can conceal the contrasting white primer or bare metal color and prevent rust, but they leave an uneven raised edge that remains visible upon close inspection.

Q7: Why do body shops have to repaint the entire panel for a scratch?

Automotive clear coat must be applied from panel edge to panel edge to ensure proper chemical bonding and prevent the clear coat from peeling or showing a visible blend line over time.

Q8: What happens if you leave a deep key scratch unrepaired?

Leaving bare steel exposed allows atmospheric moisture and road salt to initiate galvanic rust, which creeps beneath the surrounding paint film and causes widespread bubbling and rot.
