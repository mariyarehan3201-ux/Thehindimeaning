How Can You Get Rid of Asbestos?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: October 1, 2026

Discovering asbestos in your residential home or commercial property often prompts immediate questions regarding safe disposal and legal containment methods. Because microscopic silicate fibers can linger airborne for days and cause severe respiratory diseases when inhaled, getting rid of asbestos demands certified engineering controls rather than conventional demolition practices.

Professional Abatement Versus In-Place Management Options

When property owners realize they have hazardous building materials on their premises, the most urgent question is determining the safest disposal methodology. A comprehensive assessment of how do you get rid of asbestos demonstrates that complete physical removal is not always the only or even the best immediate solution. Federal environmental guidelines consistently emphasize that undamaged, non-friable materials are generally safest when left undisturbed or sealed behind impermeable physical barriers.

Before making any disposal decisions, homeowners must understand the biological exposure pathways by investigating how can you get asbestos fibers into the lungs. Inhalation occurs almost exclusively when dry materials are crushed, cut, abraded, or sanded, converting solid building components into microscopic respirable dust. If materials are crumbling, shedding loose dust, or blocking unavoidable remodeling work, complete professional abatement becomes an absolute legal and health necessity.

Step-by-Step Engineering Controls in Professional Removal

Eliminating hazardous materials from an occupied structure requires rigorous environmental containment. Licensed contractors begin by sealing every supply vent, return air grill, window, and doorway using two independent layers of heavy six-mil polyethylene plastic sheeting. Industrial negative-air machines equipped with true HEPA filtration operate continuously throughout the removal, exhausting scrubbed air outdoors to ensure that any dislodged fibers are drawn into the filtration system rather than drifting into adjacent living quarters.

Specialized procedures are also required for exterior building envelopes. Property owners evaluating exterior wall renovations can review protocols for how do you get rid of asbestos siding to discover how careful unfastening and wet handling prevent brittle cement shingles from cracking and releasing hazardous particulates. Wetting materials with amended surfactant water solutions keeps fibers bound to moisture droplets, dramatically minimizing airborne dispersion.

Packaging, Transport Manifests, and Hazardous Waste Landfills

Getting rid of the waste generated during abatement is subject to strict environmental tracking laws. Removed materials must be wetted, sealed inside double six-mil leak-tight plastic bags printed with bold hazard warnings, and placed into rigid, lined transport containers. Reviewing the legal roadmap in how can I get rid of asbestos safe steps clarifies why hazardous waste cannot be left for standard curbside trash collection or taken to general municipal transfer stations.

Licensed hazardous waste transporters must haul the sealed containers under a formal Uniform Hazardous Waste Manifest to an EPA-permitted landfill. Upon arrival, landfill operators place the bags into dedicated asbestos disposal cells and cover them immediately with clean fill dirt to prevent accidental puncture. Retaining copies of these signed disposal manifests is vital for property owners to prove code compliance during future real estate transactions.

How to Safely Get Rid of Asbestos from Your Property

Follow these five certified procedural steps to inspect, contain, and legally dispose of hazardous building materials without contaminating your home.

  1. Schedule an Accredited Laboratory Testing Survey

    Retain a state-licensed asbestos inspector to collect bulk material samples and confirm fiber concentration using polarized light microscopy (PLM) analysis.

  2. Determine if Complete Removal or Encapsulation Is Best

    Review the inspector's condition report to establish whether the material is friable and requires immediate removal or if it can be safely encapsulated in place.

  3. Contract a State-Licensed Asbestos Abatement Company

    Hire an insured, licensed abatement contractor who provides verifiable proof of state certification, worker training records, and comprehensive liability insurance.

  4. Establish Negative Air Containment and Wet Stripping

    Ensure the contractor erects airtight poly containment, installs HEPA negative-air units, and thoroughly saturates materials with amended water before removal.

  5. Conduct Independent Aggressive Air Clearance Testing

    Require a third-party environmental technician to conduct aggressive optical air clearance testing and provide certified disposal manifests before dismantling containment.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I remove asbestos from my house myself?

While single-family homeowners are permitted to perform removal in certain jurisdictions, attempting DIY removal without negative air scrubbers, HEPA vacuums, and proper respirators almost always causes severe household contamination and dangerous fiber inhalation.

Q2: What is the safest way to get rid of asbestos?

The safest method is hiring a licensed environmental remediation contractor who establishes airtight negative-pressure plastic containment, utilizes continuous wet stripping, and disposes of waste at permitted landfills.

Q3: Where can you legally throw away asbestos waste?

Asbestos waste can only be disposed of at specialized, EPA-permitted solid waste landfills that operate certified hazardous waste trenches with mandatory daily soil cover.

Q4: How do professionals keep asbestos dust from spreading during removal?

Contractors maintain negative air pressure inside sealed plastic enclosures using HEPA air scrubbers, saturate materials with amended water spray, and use multi-stage decontamination airlocks.

Q5: How much does it typically cost to get rid of asbestos?

Professional removal typically costs between $5 and $15 per square foot for flooring or ceilings, with average residential projects ranging from $1,500 to $4,500 depending on material type and accessibility.

Q6: Is it legal to bury asbestos waste in your own backyard?

No. Burying asbestos on private property is strictly illegal under federal and state environmental protection statutes and carries severe criminal penalties and massive civil fines.

Q7: What should you do if you accidentally break an asbestos tile?

Immediately keep people away from the area, turn off your central HVAC heating and cooling systems, avoid vacuuming or sweeping the debris, and gently mist the area with water before calling a certified professional.

Q8: What is the difference between encapsulation and removal?

Encapsulation coats or encloses intact asbestos to lock fibers in place without moving the material, whereas removal extracts the material entirely from the structure for permanent disposal.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Knowing how can you get rid of asbestos safely requires prioritizing accredited professional guidance over hazardous DIY shortcuts. Whether you opt for complete negative-pressure abatement, professional encapsulation, or structural enclosure, following certified engineering standards guarantees that your living environment remains completely safe. By partnering with licensed testing inspectors and accredited abatement contractors, you permanently resolve building hazards while protecting the long-term respiratory health of your household.
