How to Become a Private Investigator in Colorado?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Launching a career as a private investigator in Colorado requires a clear understanding of the state's evolving regulatory landscape, investigative techniques, legal boundaries, and business setup. While Colorado previously regulated private detectives through the Department of Regulatory Agencies (DORA), the mandatory state licensing program officially sunsetted in September 2020. Today, prospective investigators can establish investigative agencies under general state business and local municipal frameworks, provided they operate with strict ethical, civil, and criminal compliance.

The Regulatory Landscape: Colorado DORA Sunset and Current Rules

To understand how to become a private investigator in Colorado, one must examine the state's recent legislative history. In 2011, the Colorado General Assembly enacted a voluntary licensing program under the Department of Regulatory Agencies (DORA) Division of Professions and Occupations, which was converted to a mandatory licensing mandate in 2015. However, following a sunset review analyzing cost-benefit ratios and consumer harm metrics, Colorado lawmakers chose not to renew the statute, resulting in the full sunset and deregulation of state-level private investigator licensing as of September 1, 2020.

Because there is no longer a centralized DORA state license, individuals can enter the investigative profession without having to complete state-mandated classroom hours or pass a centralized state examination. However, deregulation does not mean the absence of rules. Private investigators in Colorado must strictly adhere to state and federal criminal laws, privacy statutes, wiretapping legislation, Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) rules, and local municipal business licensing requirements throughout cities such as Denver, Colorado Springs, Aurora, and Fort Collins.

Core Investigative Disciplines, Skills, and Modern Surveillance Tactics

Successful private investigators in Colorado offer a wide range of specialized services to law firms, insurance corporations, commercial enterprises, and private citizens. Major practice areas include insurance fraud investigation, workers' compensation claims defense, family law and domestic infidelity surveillance, child custody evaluations, corporate intellectual property theft, missing persons locating, and criminal defense mitigation for public defenders and private criminal counsel.

Modern private investigation blends traditional field surveillance with digital open-source intelligence (OSINT). In the field, investigators conduct mobile and stationary surveillance, capturing high-definition, date-stamped video evidence from specialized surveillance vans or discreet vantage points. In the digital realm, investigators utilize proprietary skip-tracing databases (such as TLOxp, Tracers, and Delvepoint) to unearth public records, property deeds, vehicle registrations, and social media footprints while respecting the Driver's Privacy Protection Act (DPPA) and Fair Credit Reporting Act.

Launching Your PI Business in Colorado: Insurance, Ethics, and Setup

To establish a legitimate investigative practice in Colorado, an entrepreneur must register an official business entity—such as a Limited Liability Company (LLC) or Corporation—through the Colorado Secretary of State website. Next, obtain an Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the IRS and secure local municipal business licenses where required. For instance, certain municipalities like Denver maintain local mercantile or security licenses that apply to independent private detectives.

Acquiring comprehensive commercial general liability and specialized Errors and Omissions (E&O) insurance is indispensable. A robust policy covering at least one million dollars shields the investigator from claims alleging invasion of privacy, trespass, libel, slander, or bodily injury. Furthermore, joining recognized professional associations such as the Professional Private Investigators Association of Colorado (PPIAC) connects new investigators with experienced mentors, continuous professional development seminars, and valuable peer sub-contracting networks.

How to Establish a Private Investigation Practice in Colorado

Follow these practical steps to develop investigative skills, register your business, and operate compliantly in Colorado.

  1. Acquire Foundational Investigative Training and Skills

    Gain practical experience through criminal justice coursework, military or police service, or working under an established investigator.

  2. Master State and Federal Privacy Laws

    Study Colorado statutes on trespassing, stalking, wiretapping, and harassment alongside federal FCRA, DPPA, and GLBA regulations.

  3. Register Your Business Entity with the Colorado Secretary of State

    Establish a formal LLC or Corporation, secure an Employer Identification Number (EIN), and obtain local municipal business licenses.

  4. Obtain Comprehensive Professional Liability Insurance

    Purchase errors and omissions and general liability insurance with minimum coverage of one million dollars to shield against tort claims.

  5. Procure Professional Surveillance and Digital Tools

    Invest in high-magnification optical video cameras, covert vehicle recording rigs, secure storage, and licensed skip-tracing databases.

  6. Join the Professional Private Investigators Association of Colorado (PPIAC)

    Become an active member of PPIAC to access industry mentorship, standardized contracts, ethics resources, and business referrals.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does Colorado require a state license to be a private investigator?

No. The state-level mandatory licensing program under the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies (DORA) officially sunsetted on September 1, 2020.

Q2: Can anyone call themselves a private investigator in Colorado?

While state licensing is sunsetted, investigators must still comply with all criminal laws, privacy rights, and local municipal business regulations.

Q3: Do private investigators in Colorado carry guns?

Investigators can carry concealed firearms only if they obtain an official Concealed Handgun Permit (CHP) from their local county sheriff.

Q4: Can a private investigator in Colorado tap a phone?

No. Wiretapping without consent is a serious felony under federal law and Colorado state law, which operates under one-party consent rules.

Q5: What is the PPIAC in Colorado?

The Professional Private Investigators Association of Colorado (PPIAC) is the premier professional trade organization promoting ethical standards and continuing education.

Q6: How much does it cost to start a PI business in Colorado?

Startup costs typically range from three thousand to ten thousand dollars for business registration, professional insurance, cameras, and database subscriptions.

Q7: Can a private investigator run license plates in Colorado?

Investigators can only access registered motor vehicle records if they have an authorized permissible use under the federal Driver's Privacy Protection Act.

Q8: What salary do private investigators earn in Colorado?

Private investigators in Colorado generally earn between fifty-five thousand and eighty-five thousand dollars annually, with specialized agency owners earning more.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Entering the private investigation field in Colorado presents exciting entrepreneurial freedom combined with critical professional responsibility. While the state no longer enforces a bureaucratic DORA licensing framework, thriving in this competitive field requires mastering legal surveillance, upholding impeccable ethical standards, securing adequate commercial insurance, and delivering accurate, unassailable evidence for your clients.
