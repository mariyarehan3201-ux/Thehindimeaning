Can You Stop a Divorce After Filing in California?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: October 1, 2026

Couples navigating marital distress frequently wonder: can you stop a divorce after filing in California? Fortunately, the California legal system provides clear procedural mechanisms to dismiss or pause a marital dissolution action prior to the entry of a final judgment. Whether spouses choose reconciliation, structured marital therapy, or informal negotiation, filing Judicial Council Form CIV-110 allows parties to dismiss their petition without prejudice.

Legal Mechanisms for Stopping a Divorce in California Courts

Under California Family Code regulations, a divorce proceeding does not instantly dissolve a marriage upon filing. Spouses seeking to pause or withdraw legal proceedings often ask whether can you cancel a divorce after filing, and the procedural answer depends entirely on whether the responding spouse has formally submitted a response. If the respondent has not filed Form FL-120, the petitioner holds the unilateral legal right to file a Request for Dismissal using mandatory Judicial Council Form CIV-110.

Once both spouses have entered formal court appearances, canceling the dissolution requires mutual consent. Both parties or their legal counsel must sign the dismissal request before the court clerk will process it. Understanding state court procedural delays is essential, especially when assessing how long does a divorce take in California, because California enforces a mandatory six-month waiting period from the date of initial service before marital status can be legally terminated.

Financial Implications and Filing Fee Recovery in California

Dismissing a divorce in California does not entitle either party to a refund of their initial court filing fees, which currently stand at $435 per initial petition and response in most California counties. Couples evaluating legal expenditures often examine how much is a simple divorce in California costs to determine whether halting litigation preserves marital assets compared to ongoing contested trial preparation.

Furthermore, marital agreements and temporary restraining orders issued under California Family Law must be formally dissolved to avoid accidental legal non-compliance. Similar to dealing with automated administrative administrative sanctions, such as wondering is your license suspended immediately after a DUI in CA, court orders remain fully enforceable by law enforcement until the judge signs a formal dismissal order.

Navigating Temporary Orders and Reconciliation Strategies

If temporary orders regarding child custody, spousal support, or exclusive home possession were issued during the initial proceedings, filing a dismissal automatically vacates those orders unless an independent child support action exists through the Department of Child Support Services. Spouses seeking reconciliation are encouraged to execute post-nuptial agreements if they wish to resolve property disputes while remaining legally married.

How to Stop a Divorce Action in California Courts

A practical guide for petitioners and respondents wishing to dismiss an active marital dissolution case in California.

  1. Confirm Current Procedural Case Status

    Check the California county court docket to confirm whether the respondent has filed Form FL-120 and whether temporary orders have been issued.

  2. Obtain Judicial Council Form CIV-110

    Download Form CIV-110 (Request for Dismissal) from the California Courts judicial website or obtain it from the court self-help center.

  3. Complete the Dismissal Form Accurately

    Mark the box for dismissal 'without prejudice' so the action can be refiled in the future if reconciliation efforts prove unsuccessful.

  4. Secure Required Signatures

    If a response was filed, ensure both spouses or their attorneys sign the consent section of Form CIV-110 before filing.

  5. File and Serve the Dismissal Order

    Submit the completed form to the county court clerk, pay any minor processing fees, and retain file-stamped copies for personal records.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I stop a divorce if my spouse does not want to stop it?

If your spouse has already filed a response (Form FL-120), you cannot unilaterally dismiss the case; both parties must sign the dismissal request.

Q2: Can a divorce be canceled after the six month waiting period?

Yes, you can cancel the case anytime before the court officially signs and enters the final judgment of dissolution (Form FL-180).

Q3: What does dismissal without prejudice mean in California?

Dismissal without prejudice means the case is closed without legal penalty, allowing either spouse to file a fresh divorce petition in the future if necessary.

Q4: Do we get our initial filing fees refunded by the court?

No, California county superior courts do not refund the initial filing fees paid upon submitting the petition or response.

Q5: What happens to temporary child custody orders upon dismissal?

When a divorce action is dismissed, all temporary court orders regarding custody and spousal support are automatically dissolved.

Q6: Can we put our divorce on hold instead of completely dismissing it?

Yes, spouses can simply agree not to advance the case or request a formal stay from the judge while attending marital counseling.

Q7: How long does the court take to process Form CIV-110?

Most California county clerk offices process and stamp a Request for Dismissal within one to two business weeks.

Q8: Can an attorney prevent a couple from dismissing their divorce?

No, the legal decision to dismiss belongs solely to the clients, although attorneys must be compensated for any outstanding legal work performed.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Stopping a divorce after filing in California is entirely feasible provided that the court has not yet entered a final judgment of dissolution. Utilizing Form CIV-110 provides an accessible, dignified pathway for couples choosing reconciliation or alternative dispute resolution. Consulting with a family law specialist guarantees that all temporary orders are properly vacated and financial protections remain intact.
