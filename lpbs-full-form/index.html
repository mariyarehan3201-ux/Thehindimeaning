LPBS Full Form: Local Push Button Station Guide

Full Form
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 17, 2026

In industrial electrical engineering, factory automation, and process plant design, the acronym LPBS stands for Local Push Button Station (also commonly designated as a Local Control Station or LCS). It is an industrial-grade, enclosed electrical control station installed in immediate physical proximity to a driven machine—such as an electric motor, pump, compressor, or conveyor belt. Equipped with start pushbuttons, stop pushbuttons, emergency mushroom switches, and selector switches, an LPBS allows field operators and maintenance technicians to safely control and instantly shut down machinery during localized emergencies.

Industrial Automation and the Functional Role of Local Push Button Stations

In heavy industrial facilities—including chemical refineries, cement plants, thermal power stations, and steel mills—electric motors drive continuous high-energy equipment like slurry pumps, grinding mills, and massive overhead conveyor belts. While primary electrical power switchgear sits securely inside remote Motor Control Center (MCC) rooms, personnel working on the plant floor must have immediate physical control over moving machinery. The Local Push Button Station (LPBS) provides this indispensable frontline control.

If a technician inspecting a conveyor detects a jammed belt or an operator spots a leaking pump seal, running hundreds of meters to a remote electrical room to shut off power is impossible. The LPBS, mounted directly adjacent to the machine within clear line of sight, allows the operator to slap the emergency mushroom button and stop the equipment in milliseconds.

Every industrial engineering standard, from OSHA to IEC and Indian Standards, mandates local isolating and stop mechanisms to protect human life from rotating machinery accidents.

Lockout-Tagout (LOTO) Compliance, Remote Motor Starters, and Safe Machine Maintenance

Electrical safety codes require that emergency stop buttons adhere to strict fail-safe wiring design. By utilizing Normally Closed (NC) contacts wired in series, current must continuously flow through the circuit to keep the motor contactor energized.

If a wire becomes loose, a cable is severed, or the push button is depressed, the circuit breaks instantly, causing the magnetic contactor to drop out and bringing the motor to a safe standstill.

How to Install and Wire an Industrial Local Push Button Station (LPBS)

  1. Select Enclosure IP Rating and Material

    Choose an enclosure suited to site conditions: die-cast aluminum or stainless steel with IP65/IP66 ingress protection for wet chemical environments.

  2. Mount Within Direct Line of Sight

    Position the LPBS firmly on a structural pedestal or wall within 1 to 2 meters of the driven motor, ensuring unhindered emergency access.

  3. Wire the Normally Closed (NC) Emergency Stop

    Connect the red mushroom emergency push button using Normally Closed contacts wired in series with the motor contactor holding circuit.

  4. Wire the Normally Open (NO) Start Circuit

    Connect the green start button with Normally Open contacts in parallel with the motor starter auxiliary seal-in contact.

  5. Verify Grounding and Test Interlocks

    Bond the enclosure to plant earth with copper ground straps, verify cable gland sealing, and perform live trip tests prior to commissioning.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the full form of LPBS in electrical engineering?

LPBS stands for Local Push Button Station.

Q2: Why is an LPBS installed locally next to a motor?

To provide field personnel immediate local visibility and physical emergency stop control during equipment maintenance or mechanical jams.

Q3: Why are Stop and Emergency push buttons always Normally Closed (NC)?

For fail-safe safety; if a control wire is accidentally cut or disconnected, the circuit opens immediately, shutting down the running motor.

Q4: What is a "Lock-Off" or "Stay-Put" emergency push button?

A twist-to-release or key-release mushroom button that stays mechanically latched in the open position once pressed, preventing accidental restarts.

Q5: What does a Local/Remote selector switch do on an LPBS?

It determines whether the machine is controlled locally by the field operator via the LPBS or remotely by a central PLC/DCS automation system.

Q6: What ingress protection (IP) rating is standard for outdoor LPBS units?

IP65 or IP66 is standard, preventing heavy rain, dust, and washdown jets from penetrating the electrical contacts.

Q7: What materials are used for LPBS enclosures?

Cast aluminum, sheet steel, polycarbonate, glass-reinforced polyester (GRP), or stainless steel for corrosive petrochemical sites.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The Local Push Button Station (LPBS) is a vital frontline safety and control device in industrial electrical engineering. Mounted within direct sight of rotating motors and heavy machinery, the LPBS provides field operators with immediate start, stop, and emergency trip capabilities, preventing mechanical catastrophes and protecting worker lives.
