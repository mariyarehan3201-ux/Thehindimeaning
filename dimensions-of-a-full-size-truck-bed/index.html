Dimensions of a Full Size Truck Bed

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 12, 2026

Whether you are hauling four-by-eight-foot sheets of plywood from the lumberyard, loading an all-terrain vehicle (ATV), packing camping equipment, or ordering an aftermarket tonneau cover or truck bed camper, knowing the exact dimensions of a full-size pickup truck bed is critical. Across America's dominant full-size truck platforms—the Ford F-150, Chevrolet Silverado 1500, GMC Sierra, and Ram 1500—truck beds are manufactured in three standardized length categories: short bed, standard bed, and long bed.

The Three Standard Bed Lengths: Short, Standard, and Long

Full-size pickup truck beds are categorized into three distinct length classes: short beds (typically 5.5 to 5.8 feet), standard beds (typically 6.5 to 6.6 feet), and long beds (an exact 8.0 feet). Short beds (roughly 66 to 70 inches long) are the most popular configuration on modern half-ton crew cab trucks, balancing family passenger cabin room with garage parking practicality.

Standard beds (measuring 76 to 80 inches long) are the preferred choice for contractors and outdoor enthusiasts who require balanced cargo room for toolboxes and camping gear without extending overall truck wheelbase excessively. Long beds (measuring 96 to 98 inches long) represent the commercial workhorse configuration, available primarily on regular cab or heavy-duty (three-quarter ton and one-ton) work trucks.

Truck Bed Width and the Golden 4-Foot Plywood Standard

While bed lengths vary, truck bed widths are remarkably standardized across all full-size pickup manufacturers. The most important dimensional benchmark in the American pickup truck industry is the width between wheel wells. Every full-size pickup truck sold today (Ford F-150, Chevy Silverado, Ram 1500, GMC Sierra, and Toyota Tundra) features a minimum width between the interior wheel arches of at least fifty to fifty-one inches.

This fifty-plus-inch clearance is deliberately engineered to accommodate standard four-by-eight-foot (48" x 96") sheets of construction plywood, drywall, and OSB subflooring lying completely flat between the wheel tubs. Overall maximum interior bed wall-to-wall width (measured above the wheel wells) ranges from sixty-four to seventy-one inches, providing expansive volume for mulch, gravel, and bulky equipment.

Tailgate Open Lengths, Tonneau Sizing, and Payload Capacities

When hauling oversized cargo like eight-foot lumber or motorcycles in a 5.5-foot short bed, lowering the tailgate extends the usable flat floor length by nineteen to twenty-two inches. With the tailgate lowered flat, a 5.5-foot short bed provides roughly 7.5 feet of continuous flat support, while a 6.5-foot standard bed expands to over eight feet of continuous floor length, easily supporting eight-foot building supplies when properly strapped.

When ordering aftermarket bed accessories—such as fiberglass camper shells, hard-folding tonneau covers, or drop-in bedliners—never rely solely on model year or trim names. Always use a steel measuring tape to measure your truck bed floor from the inside front bulkhead to the inside of the closed tailgate. Furthermore, be mindful of payload ratings: a full-size half-ton truck bed typically carries one thousand five hundred to two thousand two hundred pounds of cargo safely.

Accurate bed measurements ensure hassle-free accessory fitment and safe cargo hauling.

How to Measure a Truck Bed Accurately in 5 Steps

Follow these practical steps to measure your truck bed for tonneau covers, toppers, or plywood cargo.

  1. Close the Tailgate Securely

    Ensure the tailgate is completely closed and latched before measuring interior length for toppers or tonneau covers.

  2. Measure Interior Bed Length Along the Floor

    Hook a tape measure against the inside front bulkhead wall and measure straight back to the inside edge of the closed tailgate.

  3. Measure Width Between Wheel Wells

    Measure the narrowest clearance between the two interior wheel well arches to confirm 48-inch plywood sheet fit.

  4. Measure Maximum Wall-to-Wall Width

    Measure the widest distance between the upper bed rails from driver side to passenger side near the cab.

  5. Measure Bed Wall Depth Vertically

    Measure vertically from the lowest corrugated floor groove up to the top flat surface of the bed rail cap.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What are the dimensions of a standard full-size truck bed?

A standard full-size truck bed measures approximately 6.5 feet long (76 to 79 inches), 50 to 51 inches wide between wheel wells, and 21 to 22 inches deep.

Q2: What is the length of a short bed on an F-150?

The short bed on a modern Ford F-150 SuperCrew measures approximately 5.5 feet long (67.1 inches of interior floor length).

Q3: Can an 8-foot sheet of plywood fit in a 5.5-foot truck bed?

Yes, an 8-foot sheet of plywood will fit flat between the wheel wells, but will overhang the lowered tailgate by approximately twelve to eighteen inches and must be strapped down.

Q4: What is the width between wheel wells on full-size trucks?

Full-size pickup trucks (Ford, Chevy, Ram, GMC) have fifty to fifty-one inches of clear width between the interior wheel wells to fit four-foot wide sheet goods flat.

Q5: How long is an 8-foot long bed full-size truck?

An 8-foot work truck bed measures ninety-six to ninety-eight inches of interior floor length from bulkhead to closed tailgate.

Q6: How do you measure a truck bed for a tonneau cover?

Measure from the inside of the front bulkhead wall to the inside of the closed tailgate at bed rail height; do not measure the outside body sheet metal.

Q7: What truck brand has the widest bed?

The Chevrolet Silverado and GMC Sierra Durabed features the widest maximum bed width, measuring 71.4 inches wall-to-wall above the wheel arches.

Q8: How deep are full-size truck bed walls?

Modern full-size truck bed walls are typically twenty-one to twenty-two and a half inches deep from floor to rail top.
