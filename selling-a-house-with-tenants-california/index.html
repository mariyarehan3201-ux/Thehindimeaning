Selling a House with Tenants California

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Selling a house with tenants in California requires navigating complex state and municipal tenant protection laws, specifically the California Tenant Protection Act of 2019 (AB 1482). Under California law, a fixed-term lease survives the sale of real property, passing automatically to the buyer, while terminating month-to-month tenancies often requires statutory 'Just Cause' and mandatory relocation assistance payments.

The Legal Reality: Leases Transfer with Property Title

Residential real estate investors and homeowners in the Golden State face one of the most heavily regulated landlord-tenant environments in the nation. When marketing a tenant-occupied single-family home, duplex, or multi-family property for sale, property owners frequently assume they can simply issue a 30-day notice to vacate. In California, this assumption can result in costly administrative fines and wrongful eviction lawsuits.

In California real property law, a residential lease agreement is an interest in real estate that attaches to the property title. Under established common law and California Civil Code, the sale of the real estate does not void an existing lease. The buyer of the property steps into the exact legal shoes of the seller, inheriting all contractual obligations, rental rates, and security deposits held under the lease.

If tenants occupy the home under an active fixed-term lease (such as a 12-month lease with five months remaining), the owner cannot evict them simply because the house is being sold. The tenants hold the absolute legal right to remain in peaceful possession until the lease expiration date. Sellers must decide whether to sell the property tenant-occupied to fellow real estate investors, or negotiate lawful vacancy prior to listing. The California Tenant Protection Act (AB 1482) and Just Cause

The defining statutory framework governing tenant-occupied real estate sales across California is the California Tenant Protection Act of 2019 (codified under California Civil Code §§ 1946.2 and 1947.12, widely known as AB 1482).

Under AB 1482, if tenants have continuously occupied the residential property for twelve months or more, a landlord cannot terminate the tenancy without establishing statutory 'Just Cause.' Just Cause is divided into two categories: At-Fault Just Cause (non-payment of rent, lease violations, or nuisance) and No-Fault Just Cause.

Selling a house is NOT, by itself, recognized as a statutory No-Fault Just Cause under AB 1482. However, an owner can terminate a month-to-month tenancy under No-Fault Just Cause if the buyer enters into a contract to purchase the home with the explicit intent that the buyer (or their immediate family) will occupy the property as their primary residence. Under AB 1482, exercising a No-Fault termination legally mandates that the landlord pay the tenant Relocation Assistance equal to one month of the tenant's current rent, provided via direct payment or rent waiver within 15 days of service. Showing Tenant-Occupied Property: California Civil Code § 1954

One of the most contentious friction points when marketing an occupied California property is coordinating open houses, broker showings, home appraisals, and physical property inspections with existing tenants.

Under California Civil Code § 1954, landlords and listing agents possess the legal right to enter an occupied rental dwelling to exhibit the property to prospective purchasers or mortgagees. However, this statutory right is strictly conditioned upon providing proper written notice. The landlord must provide the tenant with at least 24 hours of advance written notice stating the date, approximate time window (during normal business hours, typically 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM), and purpose of entry.

If the landlord has notified the tenant in writing within the previous 120 days that the property is actively for sale, subsequent showing notices may be delivered verbally in person or via telephone, provided at least 24 hours advance notice is given. Furthermore, listing agents must leave a written note or business card inside the home confirming entry occurred. Demanding unannounced drop-in showings or weekend-long open houses without tenant cooperation violates the tenant's statutory Covenant of Quiet Enjoyment and can lead to civil harassment restraining orders.

How to Execute a Cash for Keys Agreement in California

Step-by-step roadmap to negotiate voluntary tenant relocation prior to a home sale.

  1. Verify Property AB 1482 and Local Rent Control Status

    Check whether your property is subject to statewide AB 1482 rules or local municipal rent stabilization ordinances (such as Los Angeles RSO or San Francisco).

  2. Communicate Openly with Tenants Regarding Sale Intentions

    Hold an in-person meeting with tenants to explain sales plans respectfully; gauge whether they prefer staying for an investor or receiving a buyout.

  3. Draft a Written California Cash for Keys Agreement

    Prepare a formal contract specifying the agreed buyout amount ($3,000 to $10,000+), the exact move-out date, and property condition requirements.

  4. Conduct Final Walkthrough and Disburse Certified Funds

    Perform a final walkthrough on moving day, collect all door keys and garage remotes, and deliver the certified cashier's check upon complete surrender.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can a California landlord evict a tenant just to sell the house?

No. Selling a house is not a recognized Just Cause under AB 1482; an eviction is permitted only if the buyer contractually intends to move in as an owner-occupant.

Q2: Does an existing lease end when a house is sold in California?

No. The lease survives the sale and transfers automatically to the buyer, who must honor all existing terms, rent amounts, and expiration dates.

Q3: How much advance notice is required to show a tenant-occupied house?

Under California Civil Code 1954, landlords must provide at least 24 hours of advance written notice before showing the property to buyers.

Q4: What is the relocation assistance fee under AB 1482?

Under AB 1482 No-Fault just cause terminations, landlords must pay the tenant relocation assistance equal to one month of current rent within 15 days.

Q5: Are single-family homes exempt from AB 1482 in California?

Yes, single-family homes owned by individuals (not corporations or REITs) are exempt, provided the landlord served written statutory notice of exemption.

Q6: What is a Cash for Keys agreement in California?

A voluntary, legally binding contract where a landlord pays a tenant a negotiated financial sum in exchange for vacating the home by a set date.

Q7: What happens to the tenant's security deposit when the house sells?

Under California law, the seller must transfer the full security deposit to the buyer through escrow, and notify the tenant in writing of the transfer.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Selling a house with tenants in California demands a disciplined understanding of property title succession, AB 1482 just cause eviction requirements, and Civil Code 1954 entry protocols. While selling tenant-occupied provides seamless passive rental cash flow to investor buyers, securing vacancy often requires executing formal Cash for Keys buyout agreements or paying statutory relocation assistance. Consult an experienced California real estate attorney and licensed Realtor to craft a compliant sales strategy that protects your equity.
