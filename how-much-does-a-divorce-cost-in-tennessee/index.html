How Much Does a Divorce Cost in Tennessee?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a divorce cost in Tennessee depends heavily on whether spouses qualify for an irreconcilable differences divorce or must litigate fault grounds in Circuit or Chancery Court. An agreed uncontested divorce in Tennessee typically costs between $1,000 and $2,800 total. In contrast, a contested divorce with significant disputes over alimony, business assets, or parenting time averages $11,000 to $18,000 per spouse, with protracted courtroom battles reaching $30,000 or more.

Tennessee Court Filing Fees and County Surcharges

Divorce petitions in Tennessee can be filed in Circuit Court or Chancery Court depending on county practice. Court filing fees vary by county and are bifurcated based on whether the marriage produced minor children. For divorces without minor children, filing fees typically range from $180 to $260. For couples with minor children, filing fees increase to $250 to $380, reflecting administrative surcharges, parenting plan docketing, and state vital statistics fees.

In counties like Davidson (Nashville), Shelby (Memphis), Knox (Knoxville), and Hamilton (Chattanooga), local court technology fees and litigation taxes can slightly increase these totals. If the divorce is contested, service of process by a county sheriff adds $28 to $45, or $65 to $125 for a private process server. Furthermore, Tennessee enforces strict statutory cooling-off periods: 60 days for divorces without children, and 90 days for divorces with minor children.

Attorney Retainers and Hourly Rates Across Tennessee

Attorney hourly rates in Tennessee vary significantly between major metropolitan areas and rural regions. In Nashville, Franklin (Williamson County), and Memphis, experienced domestic relations attorneys bill between $325 and $525 per hour, requiring initial retainer deposits of $4,000 to $8,500. In Chattanooga, Knoxville, and Murfreesboro, rates generally range from $250 to $400 per hour. In rural East and West Tennessee communities, hourly rates typically fall between $200 and $300 per hour.

Most attorneys require a retainer deposit held in a trust account, which is billed against as work is performed. When cases involve complex financial discovery, such as auditing medical practices, music industry royalties, real estate portfolios, or military pensions, parties must also budget for financial experts. Certified divorce financial analysts and business valuation appraisers in Tennessee routinely bill between $250 and $450 per hour.

Tennessee Parenting Plans and Mandatory Rule 31 Mediation

Tennessee Code Annotated Title 36 mandates that any divorce involving minor children must include a detailed Permanent Parenting Plan. This statutory document outlines day-to-day residential schedules, major decision-making authority for education and healthcare, and child support calculations following Tennessee Child Support Guidelines. Both parents must also attend a mandatory four-hour parent education seminar costing $40 to $70 per person.

Furthermore, Tennessee courts routinely order contested cases into mandatory mediation under Tennessee Supreme Court Rule 31. Under Rule 31, a listed family mediator conducts settlement sessions to help the couple resolve property distribution and custody disputes before a trial date is scheduled. Mediation successfully resolves over 75% of Tennessee domestic disputes, preventing tens of thousands of dollars in protracted trial expenses.

How to Reduce Tennessee Divorce Legal Fees

Follow these five proven steps to minimize litigation costs and streamline your Tennessee divorce.

  1. Establish Grounds for Irreconcilable Differences

    Work with your spouse to agree on no-fault irreconcilable differences to avoid costly fault-based claims.

  2. Draft an Agreed Marital Dissolution Agreement (MDA)

    Document the equitable split of marital assets, real estate, bank accounts, and personal debt in a signed agreement.

  3. Complete the Mandatory Parenting Class Promptly

    Attend the required four-hour parenting seminar immediately after filing if minor children are involved.

  4. Engage a Rule 31 Listed Family Mediator Early

    Participate in structured mediation to resolve parenting schedule conflicts before attorneys engage in formal depositions.

  5. Submit Agreed Documents for Judge Approval

    File your signed MDA and Permanent Parenting Plan to finalize the divorce upon completion of the mandatory waiting period.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the average cost of divorce in Tennessee?

An agreed uncontested divorce averages $1,000 to $2,800, while a contested divorce typically costs between $11,000 and $18,000 per spouse.

Q2: What is the filing fee for divorce in Tennessee?

Court filing fees range from $180 to $260 without minor children, and $250 to $380 with minor children depending on the county.

Q3: What is the waiting period for divorce in Tennessee?

Tennessee imposes a mandatory statutory cooling-off period of 60 days for divorces without children, and 90 days for divorces with minor children.

Q4: What is an MDA in a Tennessee divorce?

An MDA is a Marital Dissolution Agreement, a legally binding contract defining how spouses divide marital assets, real property, and debts.

Q5: What is Rule 31 mediation in Tennessee?

Rule 31 mediation is a court-sponsored dispute resolution process conducted by a trained, neutral family mediator to resolve issues prior to trial.

Q6: Is Tennessee a 50/50 property division state?

No, Tennessee is an equitable distribution state where judges divide marital assets fairly according to statutory factors, not automatically 50/50.

Q7: Can you get a divorce without a lawyer in Tennessee?

Yes, couples without contested issues can use Tennessee Supreme Court-approved self-represented litigant forms for an agreed divorce.

Q8: How much does a divorce lawyer cost in Nashville?

Divorce attorneys in the Nashville and Williamson County areas generally charge between $325 and $525 per hour with retainers between $4,000 and $8,500.
