Mckinney Asbestos Legal Question?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Residents and industrial workers diagnosed with mesothelioma, asbestosis, or asbestos-related lung cancer in McKinney, Texas often face urgent legal questions regarding their rights to financial compensation, medical recovery, and justice. McKinney—the county seat of Collin County—sits within the bustling Dallas-Fort Worth metropolitanplex, an economic hub characterized by heavy commercial construction, aerospace defense manufacturing, petrochemical supply pipelines, and historic railway operations. Addressing an asbestos legal question in McKinney requires a thorough understanding of Texas civil practice codes, strict state multidistrict litigation (MDL) procedures, asbestos bankruptcy trust claims, and veteran disability benefits.

Collin County Industrial Context and Historical Exposure Sites

While McKinney is known today as a rapidly expanding suburban community, its historical and regional economic connections exposed thousands of local residents and tradespeople to heavy occupational asbestos. Historically, the Missouri-Kansas-Texas (MKT) Railroad operated key maintenance lines through McKinney, where boilermakers, machinists, and brake technicians handled asbestos locomotive lagging, refractory cements, and friction materials without respiratory protection.

Furthermore, many McKinney residents commuted to industrial, aerospace, and petrochemical job sites across Greater Dallas-Fort Worth. Major historical exposure sites included Texas Instruments semiconductor fabrication plants, Bell Helicopter and Lockheed Martin aircraft facilities in Fort Worth, power generation stations operated by TU Electric and Texas Utilities, and major commercial high-rise construction sites across downtown Dallas. Workers in these facilities were exposed to spray-applied structural fireproofing, high-pressure steam pipe insulation, and industrial gaskets produced by negligent manufacturers.

Texas Asbestos Statutes: Chapter 90 Medical Criteria and MDL 2004

Navigating an asbestos lawsuit in McKinney requires adhering strictly to the Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code, specifically Chapter 90, which governs asbestos and silica claims. Enacted in 2005, Chapter 90 establishes stringent medical criteria that plaintiffs must satisfy before their lawsuits can proceed. For non-malignant claims such as asbestosis, plaintiffs must provide a comprehensive medical report from a board-certified pulmonary specialist confirming specific pulmonary function impairment and an ILO-classified chest X-ray interpreted by a certified NIOSH B-reader.

In contrast, claims involving malignant mesothelioma or primary bronchogenic lung cancer are exempt from certain restrictive functional criteria, allowing malignant cases to move forward rapidly. In Texas, all asbestos personal injury and wrongful death lawsuits filed across Collin County and other regional courts are consolidated before a specialized pre-trial court: the Texas Asbestos Multidistrict Litigation Court (MDL 2004) in Harris County. Under Texas law (CPRC Section 16.003), plaintiffs must file their lawsuits within two years from the date of diagnosis or discovery of injury.

Compensation Avenues: Bankruptcy Trusts, Lawsuits, and VA Benefits

Claimants in McKinney diagnosed with asbestos-induced diseases possess three distinct legal avenues for financial recovery. The first avenue is filing civil personal injury or wrongful death lawsuits against solvent defendants, such as equipment manufacturers, valve producers, and chemical plant owners. Many of these lawsuits reach multi-million-dollar negotiated settlements prior to trial, compensating victims for past and future medical bills, lost wages, physical pain, and loss of spousal consortium.

The second avenue is submitting administrative claims to national Asbestos Bankruptcy Settlement Trusts. More than sixty major manufacturing corporations—including Johns-Manville, Armstrong World Industries, Owens Corning, and Babcock & Wilcox—established court-supervised trusts containing over thirty billion dollars to pay injured workers. McKinney claimants can receive significant trust compensation without ever appearing in court. Finally, military veterans exposed during active duty can secure 100 percent tax-free disability compensation and free healthcare through the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA).

How to Pursue an Asbestos Legal Claim in McKinney, Texas

Follow these five legal steps to protect your statutory rights and secure financial compensation following an asbestos diagnosis in McKinney.

  1. Secure Complete Pathology and Medical Records

    Obtain certified pathology tissue biopsy reports and diagnostic CT scans confirming a definitive diagnosis of mesothelioma or asbestosis.

  2. Retain a Qualified Texas Toxic Tort Attorney

    Engage an experienced lawyer with a proven record in Texas asbestos multidistrict litigation (MDL) and Collin County venues.

  3. Document Complete Employment and Job Site History

    Compile an itemized chronological record of all McKinney rail lines, Dallas-Fort Worth job sites, military bases, and materials handled.

  4. File Administrative Claims with Asbestos Trusts

    Submit certified exposure affidavits and medical proof to active bankruptcy settlement trusts to secure fast financial relief.

  5. Initiate Civil Action Under Texas Chapter 90 Rules

    File formal civil product liability claims against solvent equipment manufacturers to recover full economic and compensatory damages.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the statute of limitations for an asbestos lawsuit in McKinney, Texas?

Under Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code Section 16.003, you have strictly two years from the date of formal diagnosis (or date of death in wrongful death cases) to file a lawsuit.

Q2: What is Texas Chapter 90 in asbestos litigation?

Texas Chapter 90 is a state law requiring plaintiffs to provide specific objective medical reports from qualified pulmonary experts verifying asbestos-induced impairment before lawsuits can proceed.

Q3: Do I have to travel to court to receive asbestos compensation?

No. The vast majority of asbestos claims are resolved out of court through attorney negotiations or administrative bankruptcy trust claims without the victim ever testifying in a courtroom.

Q4: Can Navy veterans in McKinney file asbestos claims?

Yes. Veterans can file for Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) disability compensation (rated at 100% for mesothelioma) and simultaneously sue the civilian companies that supplied asbestos to the Navy.

Q5: How much does it cost to hire an asbestos attorney in McKinney?

Asbestos attorneys work on a contingency fee basis, meaning there are zero upfront fees or out-of-pocket costs; the attorney receives a percentage fee only if they successfully recover compensation.

Q6: What if the company that exposed me went out of business?

If the manufacturer went bankrupt, you can still recover substantial compensation by filing claims against their dedicated court-ordered Asbestos Bankruptcy Settlement Trust.

Q7: How long does it take to receive compensation from asbestos trusts?

Most asbestos bankruptcy trust claims are reviewed and paid within three to nine months after submitting certified work history and medical records.

Q8: Can family members file a claim if a loved one passed away from mesothelioma?

Yes. Surviving spouses, children, and legal estate representatives can file a wrongful death lawsuit within two years of the decedent's death to recover medical bills, lost income, and funeral costs.
