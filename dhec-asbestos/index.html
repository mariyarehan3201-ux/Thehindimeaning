DHEC Asbestos

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

DHEC asbestos regulations govern the inspection, permitting, abatement, and lawful disposal of asbestos-containing materials across South Carolina to protect public health and the environment. Overseen by the South Carolina Department of Health and Environmental Control (now transitioned to the Department of Environmental Services), these administrative rules mandate stringent licensing for abatement professionals, strict demolition notifications, and certified disposal manifests.

South Carolina Regulatory Framework and DHEC Oversight

In South Carolina, the management and abatement of hazardous mineral fibers is governed by the state environmental regulatory apparatus historically known as DHEC (Department of Health and Environmental Control), which has transitioned its environmental divisions into the South Carolina Department of Environmental Services (SCDES). Under South Carolina Regulation 61-86.1, titled Standards of Performance for Asbestos Abatement Operations, the state exercises comprehensive regulatory authority over all commercial, industrial, institutional, and qualifying residential projects that disturb asbestos.

The primary mission of South Carolina's asbestos rules is preventing the release of hazardous mineral dust into ambient air currents. Regulation 61-86.1 establishes rigorous guidelines that mirror and in several areas exceed federal EPA NESHAP and OSHA mandates. All commercial facility owners and contractors are legally obligated to conduct thorough hazardous material inspections prior to undertaking any demolition, renovation, or structural modification, ensuring that friable and non-friable materials are cataloged and addressed in accordance with state law.

Operational Compliance, Permitting, and Disposal Workflows

One of the most critical operational requirements under DHEC asbestos governance is the mandatory ten-working-day advance notification requirement. Prior to initiating any demolition operation—or any renovation project that disturbs more than 160 square feet, 260 linear feet, or 35 cubic feet of regulated asbestos-containing material—the facility owner or general contractor must submit a formal notification using DHEC Form 3454. Work cannot legally commence until the state acknowledges receipt and the statutory waiting period has elapsed.

Licensing and personnel accreditation represent another core pillar of South Carolina's environmental protections. DHEC certifies professionals across distinct operational disciplines, each requiring specific initial training, written examinations, and recurring annual continuing education courses. Supervisors, abatement workers, building inspectors, management planners, project designers, and air sampling professionals must hold valid South Carolina credentials issued under Regulation 61-86.1 before setting foot inside a regulated containment area.

Disposal of asbestos waste generated within South Carolina is governed under rigorous environmental controls. All friable asbestos debris must be thoroughly wetted, double-bagged inside leak-tight six-mil polyethylene disposal bags bearing standardized OSHA and EPA warning labels, and accompanied by a completed Waste Shipment Record (WSR). Waste haulers must transport materials exclusively to municipal solid waste landfills or industrial landfills permitted by the state to receive regulated asbestos waste, where operators immediately apply clean daily soil cover to prevent airborne fiber dispersion.

How to Comply with South Carolina DHEC Asbestos Regulations

A comprehensive compliance roadmap for building owners, managers, and contractors conducting renovation or demolition in South Carolina.

  1. Commissioning a DHEC-Licensed Asbestos Survey

    Retain an environmental consultant holding a valid South Carolina DHEC Asbestos Building Inspector license to conduct a comprehensive structural survey and bulk sampling.

  2. Developing a Project Design and Selecting Licensed Contractors

    Engage a certified project designer for large projects and contract an abatement firm holding an active South Carolina DHEC Asbestos Contractor license.

  3. Submitting Form DHEC 3454 Ten Working Days in Advance

    File the formal written notification alongside required state review fees at least 10 working days prior to starting any demolition or major renovation work.

  4. Erecting Regulated Negative Pressure Containment

    Construct containment enclosures utilizing double-layer 6-mil poly, functional decontamination showers, and certified HEPA-filtered negative air equipment.

  5. Executing Air Clearance and Archiving Waste Shipment Records

    Secure independent final air clearance testing from a licensed air sampler, obtain signed landfill waste shipment records, and archive all documentation for state inspection.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is South Carolina Regulation 61-86.1?

Regulation 61-86.1 is South Carolina's comprehensive administrative code governing asbestos abatement, establishing strict standards for contractor licensing, training, work practices, notifications, and disposal.

Q2: Do I have to notify DHEC before demolishing a building in South Carolina?

Yes, under both state and federal NESHAP regulations, all facility demolitions require a written notification submitted at least 10 working days prior to demolition, even if no asbestos is present.

Q3: Are single-family residential homes subject to DHEC asbestos rules?

Individual residential homeowners renovating their own private primary residence may have certain exemptions, but commercial residential demolitions, multi-family housing, or planned community projects must fully comply.

Q4: What happens if a contractor conducts asbestos abatement without a DHEC license?

Operating without a state license is a severe violation resulting in immediate stop-work orders, substantial administrative monetary penalties, and potential civil or criminal litigation.

Q5: How long is a South Carolina DHEC asbestos license valid?

DHEC asbestos licenses are valid for one year from the date of issue and require successful completion of an accredited annual refresher training course prior to license renewal.

Q6: Can asbestos waste be disposed of in any South Carolina landfill?

No, asbestos waste must be deposited exclusively in designated municipal solid waste or industrial landfills specifically permitted by the state to accept regulated asbestos-containing material.

Q7: What form is required for submitting an asbestos notification to DHEC?

Property owners and contractors must complete and submit Form DHEC 3454 (Notification of Demolition and Renovation) via mail or through the state's online environmental permitting portal.

Q8: Who can perform final air clearance testing in South Carolina?

Final air clearance testing must be performed by an independent DHEC-licensed asbestos air sampler who is not an employee of the abatement contracting firm executing the physical removal.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

South Carolina DHEC asbestos regulations provide a structured, enforceable framework that protects public health, construction workers, and building occupants from hazardous environmental contamination. Facility managers, developers, and homeowners must ensure full compliance with pre-renovation inspections, mandatory state notifications, licensed contractor execution, and certified disposal manifests, ensuring all projects remain safe, lawful, and environmentally sound.
