Can You Insure a Car with Expired Registration?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Yes, you can insure a car with an expired registration. In fact, insurance companies routinely issue auto insurance policies on vehicles with expired tags because insurance is legally required as a prerequisite before the Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) will permit you to renew your vehicle registration.

The Order of Operations: Insurance Before Registration

A widespread misconception among motorists is that an automobile must possess valid, up-to-date license plates and registration tags before an insurance company will sell you an auto policy. In reality, state regulatory frameworks operate in the exact reverse order. State motor vehicle departments universally mandate that a motor vehicle owner must establish continuous financial responsibility—verified through active auto liability insurance—before the DMV or BMV will issue, reinstate, or renew registration stickers.

Auto insurance underwriters care primarily about your vehicle's Vehicle Identification Number (VIN), proof of ownership (such as a vehicle title or purchase bill of sale), the garaging location, and the driving records of all named household operators. Whether the registration sticker on the metal license plate is active, expired by six months, or entirely non-existent has zero bearing on the insurer's legal willingness to issue comprehensive, collision, and liability coverage.

Insurance companies recognize that motorists frequently purchase used vehicles from private sellers where registration has lapsed, retrieve project cars from prolonged garage storage, or inherit vehicles with expired state documentation. Underwriting systems allow consumers to bind immediate coverage on these vehicles so that the owner can drive the car directly to an emissions testing station, state inspection bay, or DMV branch office to finalize official registration. Risks and Limitations of Driving with Expired Tags

While an insurance company will readily collect your premium and issue a valid insurance card, you cannot legally drive the vehicle on public roadways with expired registration tags. Operating an automobile on public streets or highways without current registration violates state motor vehicle codes and exposes you to immediate law enforcement action.

If a police officer stops you while driving an insured vehicle with expired tags, you will receive a traffic citation carrying fines that typically range between $100 and $500 depending on the jurisdiction and how long the registration has lapsed. In aggressive jurisdictions, law enforcement officers have statutory authority to impound and tow your vehicle on the spot if tags have been expired for over six months, triggering heavy towing fees, storage daily charges, and administrative impound release fees.

From an insurance standpoint, however, having an accident while driving with expired registration will not automatically void your insurance coverage. Because registration is an administrative motor vehicle requirement rather than a condition precedent to insurance validity, your insurer must still honor third-party liability claims and repair collision damages subject to your policy deductible. Nevertheless, repeated citations for driving an unregistered vehicle will negatively impact your driving record and cause insurers to reclassify you as a non-standard high-risk driver. How to Legally Drive an Unregistered Insured Car to the DMV

Once you secure your insurance policy, you face a logistical paradox: how do you get the car to an emissions inspection station or DMV office without getting pulled over for expired tags? Most state motor vehicle departments have established simple, legal solutions designed specifically for this transition.

You can visit your state DMV website or walk into a local branch office with your newly printed proof of insurance and vehicle title to request a Temporary Moving Permit (often called a one-trip permit or temporary operating permit). These temporary paper placards are valid for anywhere from 24 hours to 30 days and cost between $5 and $20. Displaying this valid temporary permit in your rear windshield grants you full legal authorization to drive the car for state inspections, mechanical repairs, and final registration renewals without fear of police citations or impoundment.

How to Insure and Register an Unregistered Vehicle

Step-by-step instructions to insure a car with expired registration and renew tags at the DMV.

  1. Locate Vehicle Title and 17-Digit VIN

    Ensure you possess clear title documentation or the bill of sale showing your legal ownership and verify the exact VIN on the dashboard tag.

  2. Purchase Auto Insurance Coverage Online or via Agent

    Contact an insurance company or broker to bind a policy meeting your state minimum liability requirements, adding collision and comprehensive if desired.

  3. Obtain a State DMV Temporary Moving Permit

    Download or purchase a temporary trip permit from your state DMV portal so you can legally drive the car to inspection and renewal stations.

  4. Complete Inspection and Submit DMV Renewal

    Take your vehicle through required safety and emissions testing, then present your inspection certificate, proof of insurance, and payment to renew your registration.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Do insurance companies verify registration when writing a policy?

No. Insurance carriers verify the vehicle VIN, title ownership status, and driver history, but they do not require an active DMV registration sticker to issue coverage.

Q2: Can I drive my car once I buy insurance if tags are expired?

No. You cannot legally drive on public streets until you renew the registration or obtain a state temporary moving permit, even if insurance is fully active.

Q3: Will my insurance cover an accident if my registration is expired?

Yes. An expired registration is an administrative code violation and does not invalidate your insurance policy or void liability protection after a crash.

Q4: Can police impound my car for expired registration?

Yes, in many jurisdictions police can impound and tow a vehicle if the registration stickers are expired by more than six months.

Q5: What is a DMV temporary trip permit?

It is a short-term paper permit (valid for 1 to 30 days) that allows you to legally drive an unregistered vehicle for testing, repairs, and registration appointments.

Q6: Can I insure an unregistered car that has been in storage?

Yes. You can purchase standard liability or comprehensive-only storage insurance for a parked car with expired registration.

Q7: Do I need insurance before renewing my registration online?

Yes. State DMV electronic registration renewal systems are linked to insurer databases and will reject renewals without active electronic proof of insurance.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Insuring a car with an expired registration is not only completely possible but is a mandatory legal prerequisite before you can restore your vehicle to legal standing. Insurers require only your vehicle VIN, title proof, and personal details to bind coverage. Once your insurance binder is in hand, obtain a temporary moving permit, pass any required smog or safety inspections, and submit your registration renewal to the DMV promptly to enjoy safe, uninterrupted driving on public roads.
