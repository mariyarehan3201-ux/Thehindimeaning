How Much Does a Living Trust Cost in Arizona?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a living trust cost in Arizona involves evaluating attorney specialization, estate complexity, and whether you are planning as an individual or married couple. On average, a comprehensive revocable living trust package prepared by an Arizona estate planning attorney costs between $1,500 and $3,500 for a single person, and $2,000 to $4,500 for a married couple. Certified legal document preparers (AZCLDP) offer lower-cost document drafting ranging from $600 to $1,200.

Estate Planning Attorney Fees vs. Certified Legal Document Preparers (AZCLDP)

In Arizona, consumers have two primary professional avenues for drafting estate planning documents: licensed estate planning attorneys or Certified Legal Document Preparers (AZCLDP) regulated by the Arizona Supreme Court. An experienced estate planning attorney in Phoenix, Scottsdale, Tucson, or Mesa typically charges a flat fee ranging from $1,800 to $3,800 for an entire comprehensive trust package. Attorneys provide customized legal advice, tax shelter strategies, asset protection structuring, and personalized trust drafting.

Arizona is unique in licensing non-lawyer document preparers through the Arizona Supreme Court Code of Judicial Administration Section 7-208. An AZCLDP can prepare living trust documents for $600 to $1,200. While document preparers can format legal forms and record deeds, state law strictly prohibits them from giving legal advice, analyzing tax consequences, or customizing fiduciary language for complex family dynamics.

What Is Included in a Comprehensive Arizona Estate Planning Package?

A properly constructed living trust is not a standalone document; it is the centerpiece of an integrated estate plan. A reputable Arizona attorney bundles multiple essential statutory documents into a single flat fee. These include a Pour-Over Will (directing un-transferred assets into the trust at death), Durable Financial Power of Attorney, and an Arizona Health Care Power of Attorney complying with ARS Section 36-3221.

Additionally, the package includes a Living Will (end-of-life medical directive), Mental Health Care Power of Attorney (specifically recognized under Arizona law), HIPAA medical privacy waivers, a Final Disposition Directive (burial/cremation instructions), and preparation of a Special Warranty Deed to convey real property into the trust.

Trust Funding, Real Estate Deeds, and Avoiding Arizona Probate

The true value of an Arizona living trust lies in avoiding probate court. In Arizona, if an individual dies owning more than $75,000 in personal property or more than $100,000 in real estate equity held solely in their name, the estate must undergo formal probate in Superior Court. Probate in Maricopa or Pima County typically takes 6 to 12 months and incurs legal fees, court costs, and publication fees ranging from $3,500 to $8,000.

However, a living trust only avoids probate if it is correctly 'funded.' Funding involves re-titling assets into the name of the trust. Transferring real estate requires preparing and executing a deed (warranty deed or quitclaim deed) and recording it with the County Recorder ($30 statutory recording fee in Arizona). Neglecting to deed your home into your trust nullifies its primary probate-avoidance benefit.

How to Create and Fund an Arizona Living Trust

Follow these five tactical steps to establish and fund a legally sound Arizona revocable living trust.

  1. Inventory All Real Estate, Accounts, and Assets

    List primary homes, rental properties, brokerage accounts, vehicles, and business ownership interests with estimated valuations.

  2. Designate Successor Trustees and Beneficiaries

    Select trusted individuals or corporate fiduciaries to manage and distribute trust assets upon your incapacity or passing.

  3. Draft Custom Trust and Ancillary Directives with Counsel

    Work with an Arizona attorney to customize distribution terms, minor sub-trusts, and mental health powers of attorney.

  4. Execute Documents with a Notary and Witnesses

    Sign the trust agreement, pour-over will, and powers of attorney before an Arizona notary public and two independent adult witnesses.

  5. Fund the Trust by Re-Titling Real Estate and Bank Accounts

    Record a deed transferring your real estate to the trust and update beneficiary designations on non-retirement accounts.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the average cost of a living trust in Arizona?

An attorney-prepared living trust package averages $1,500 to $3,500 for individuals, and $2,000 to $4,500 for married couples.

Q2: Can a document preparer create a living trust in Arizona?

Yes, Arizona Certified Legal Document Preparers (AZCLDP) can draft documents for $600 to $1,200, but they cannot provide legal advice.

Q3: What is the probate threshold in Arizona?

Probate is required in Arizona if personal property exceeds $75,000 or real estate equity exceeds $100,000 in the decedent's sole name.

Q4: Does a living trust need to be recorded in Arizona?

No, the trust agreement itself remains private and is not recorded; only deeds transferring real estate into the trust are recorded publicly.

Q5: Is an Arizona living trust revocable?

Yes, standard revocable living trusts can be amended, modified, or completely revoked at any time while the grantor retains mental capacity.

Q6: What is an Arizona Mental Health Power of Attorney?

It is a specialized statutory document in Arizona allowing your designated agent to consent to inpatient psychiatric or behavioral health treatment.

Q7: Does a living trust protect assets from nursing homes in Arizona?

A revocable living trust does not protect assets from Medicaid (ALTCS); irrevocable trusts are required for long-term care asset protection.

Q8: How much does it cost to deed a house into a trust in Arizona?

Attorneys typically charge $150 to $350 to prepare the deed, plus the county recorder's statutory filing fee of approximately $30.
