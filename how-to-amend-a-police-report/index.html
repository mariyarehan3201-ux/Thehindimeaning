How to Amend a Police Report?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Learning how to amend a police report is crucial if you discover inaccurate information, misspelled names, or missing evidence after a traffic collision or criminal incident. Legally, law enforcement agencies will not erase, white-out, or rewrite an original approved police report once it has been logged into the municipal or state records management system. Instead, the process of amending a report involves submitting official documentation to request that the investigating officer draft a formal 'Supplemental Police Report' or attach an official written statement of dispute to the permanent case file.

Factual Errors vs. Disputed Subjective Opinions

The first step in amending a police report is identifying whether the error is objective and factual or subjective and disputed. Objective factual errors include verifiable clerical mistakes such as misspelled names, inaccurate driver's license numbers, incorrect insurance policy information, transposed vehicle identification numbers (VIN), or the wrong date, time, and street intersection. Police departments routinely correct factual errors quickly once you present valid supporting documentation like vehicle registrations, insurance cards, or GPS timestamps.

Subjective errors, on the other hand, involve an officer's personal judgment, witness credibility assessments, vehicle speed estimations, or fault determinations. For instance, if an officer notes that you failed to yield or seemed distracted, you cannot simply demand that the officer delete their professional opinion. To challenge subjective conclusions, you must provide compelling corroborating evidence—such as dashcam footage, third-party surveillance video, traffic camera data, or independent witness affidavits—and request that a formal supplemental narrative be added.

Step-by-Step Procedure for Requesting a Supplemental Report

To initiate an amendment, you must obtain an official, certified copy of the original report from the police department's records division or online portal. Carefully review the entire document, highlighting every inaccuracy, omitted witness, or misattributed statement. Note the investigating officer's name, badge number, and the unique case or incident number located at the top of the form.

Next, contact the investigating officer directly rather than arguing with civilian clerks at the front desk. Call the precinct or dispatch center during the officer's scheduled shift and politely explain that you noticed factual discrepancies you would like to help resolve. Approach the conversation professionally and courteously; treating the officer respectfully as an ally rather than an adversary drastically increases their willingness to review your evidence and reopen the file to draft an official supplemental report.

What to Do If the Investigating Officer Refuses to Amend

If the investigating officer reviews your materials but refuses to modify their narrative or issue a supplemental report, you still have legal avenues to protect your record. Every citizen has the statutory right in most jurisdictions to submit a formal written 'Statement of Disagreement' or 'Addendum of Dispute'. Draft a concise, factual, and unemotional statement detailing the exact points where you disagree with the officer's report, attach your copies of photographs, video stills, or repair invoices, and submit it to the precinct records commander with a request for permanent attachment.

Additionally, remember that a police accident report is generally considered inadmissible hearsay in a civil personal injury or property damage trial in many states. Insurance adjusters and civil juries evaluate the underlying physical evidence, witness depositions, and accident reconstruction rather than treating an officer's opinion as infallible truth. Providing your comprehensive evidence packet directly to your insurance claims adjuster and personal injury attorney ensures your interests remain thoroughly protected regardless of police department revisions.

How to File an Amendment Request for a Police Report

Follow these five tactical steps to correct errors and submit supplemental documentation to law enforcement.

  1. Obtain the Certified Original Police Incident Report

    Acquire an official copy of the report from the precinct records unit or state portal, locating the incident number and investigating officer's badge number.

  2. Identify and Highlight Specific Inaccuracies

    Create an itemized list distinguishing verifiable factual mistakes (dates, VINs, names) from subjective narrative disputes.

  3. Gather Unimpeachable Documentary Evidence

    Assemble supporting materials including digital dashcam files, medical discharge paperwork, vehicle title copies, or photos of the scene.

  4. Contact the Investigating Officer During Their Shift

    Reach out via professional phone call or email, present your documented evidence politely, and request a formal supplemental report.

  5. Submit a Formal Written Statement of Dispute if Denied

    If the officer declines to draft a supplement, submit a notarized statement of disagreement to the records division for permanent attachment to the docket.

Frequently Asked Questions (9 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can a police officer change an accident report after it is filed?

An officer cannot erase or rewrite the original report, but they can write and file an official Supplemental Police Report that updates or corrects the record.

Q2: How long do you have to amend a police report?

There is no universal federal deadline, but requests should be submitted within days or weeks of the incident before memories fade and the case is closed.

Q3: Can you change who was found at fault in a police report?

It is exceptionally difficult to get an officer to reverse a fault finding unless you provide undeniable visual evidence such as crystal-clear dashcam video.

Q4: What if the police officer refuses to amend the report?

If an officer refuses, you can submit a formal written Statement of Disagreement to the department's records supervisor to be attached to the file.

Q5: Does an incorrect police report affect insurance claims?

Insurance adjusters conduct their own independent investigations; while an incorrect report hurts, providing your adjuster with photos, videos, and witness contacts can overcome police errors.

Q6: Are police accident reports admissible in court?

In many states, police accident reports are considered inadmissible hearsay regarding fault in civil trials, though the officer can testify based on their personal observations.

Q7: Can you add witness statements after a police report is finished?

Yes, if a new witness comes forward, you can submit their contact details and a signed, notarized affidavit to the officer for a supplemental report.

Q8: How much does it cost to amend a police report?

Police departments do not charge a fee to correct factual errors or submit supplemental narratives, though there may be a nominal $5 to $15 fee for printed copies.

Q9: Should you hire a lawyer to amend a police report?

For minor clerical errors, you can easily handle it yourself; if contested fault threatens you with severe civil liability or criminal charges, consulting an attorney is recommended.
