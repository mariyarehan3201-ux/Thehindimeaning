How Do You Know If You Have a Home Warranty?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 30, 2026

Locating evidence of an active home warranty policy is a frequent dilemma for recent property purchasers facing an unexpected mechanical breakdown in an HVAC compressor, water heater, or major kitchen appliance. Because home warranties are commonly purchased by home sellers or real estate brokerages as an introductory incentive during closing negotiations, new homeowners often inherit one-year coverage plans without receiving an explicit policy booklet or onboarding email. Determining whether your residence is protected requires systematic review of your real estate closing documentation, escrow disclosures, and title company records.

Deconstructing Real Estate Closing Paperwork and Escrow Documents

When you complete a residential real estate purchase, the title company or closing attorney provides a comprehensive closing packet containing dozens of legal and financial instruments. The single most authoritative document for verifying the existence of a home warranty is the Closing Disclosure (often referred to as the CD), or the HUD-1 Settlement Statement used in select transactions. This standardized multi-page document details every penny transferred between buyer, seller, lending institution, and third-party service providers.

To determine if a home warranty was funded at settlement, navigate to Page 2 of your Closing Disclosure under Section H, labeled Other, or occasionally Section F under Prepaids. If a home warranty plan was purchased, you will find an explicit line item stating Home Warranty, accompanied by the name of the warranty corporation (such as American Home Shield, First American Home Warranty, Choice Home Warranty, or Old Republic Home Protection). The ledger will display the exact premium paid—typically ranging from $450 to $950—and indicate whether the payment was remitted by the seller, the buyer, or the real estate brokerage as a closing concession. Reviewing real estate contract disclosures illustrates how seller credits and repair agreements are structured during transactions.

Beyond the Closing Disclosure, your Purchase and Sale Agreement (PSA) and post-inspection repair addenda contain vital confirmation. In competitive housing negotiations, buyers frequently request a one-year home warranty to offset aging mechanical equipment discovered during home inspections. Direct Inquiry Channels: Real Estate Agents, Title Companies, and Providers

If you cannot locate your physical closing folder or purchased the property digitally without saving printed settlement statements, several professional inquiry channels can resolve the question within minutes. Your first call should be to the buyer real estate agent who represented you during the transaction. Real estate brokerages are legally required to archive complete transaction files—including digital contracts, repair invoices, and warranty enrollment confirmations stored within platforms like Dotloop or SkySlope—for three to seven years depending on state real estate commission rules.

The second authoritative inquiry channel is the title company or closing escrow office where transaction settlement occurred. The escrow closing officer maintains direct financial records of all checks cut from escrow proceeds on closing day. By calling the escrow officer and providing your property address and closing date, they can inspect their disbursement ledger, locate the copy of the check sent to the warranty company, and provide the exact policy enrollment number. Learning about standard home sale transaction paperwork highlights the archival responsibilities title companies uphold.

How to Verify Policy Status and Expiration Timelines

Once you identify the name of the warranty provider from your closing documents or escrow officer, you can verify your active coverage status directly. Major national warranty corporations maintain centralized property registries. By calling their customer service hotline and reciting your property address, your full legal name, and your closing settlement date, a representative can locate your policy record even if you never received an official welcome card or member account number.

Understanding the coverage timeline is vital for scheduling service before protections expire. Seller-funded home warranties almost universally initiate on the exact calendar day of closing and remain active for precisely 365 calendar days. Reviewing your plan details early clarifies what appliances are included, whether optional coverage was added (such as pool pumps, septic systems, or detached guest suites), and what trade call service fee applies to service requests. Staying proactive with home plumbing maintenance obligations ensures warranty adjusters cannot deny claims based on lack of routine homeowner upkeep.

How to Verify If Your Home Has an Active Warranty Policy

Follow this systematic investigative checklist to locate policy documents, confirm active coverage, and identify your warranty provider.

  1. Examine Section H of Your Closing Disclosure or HUD-1 Statement

    Review Page 2 of your Closing Disclosure under Section H (Other) or Section F (Prepaids) to see if a home warranty premium was paid to a specific provider.

  2. Inspect the Purchase Agreement for Seller Warranty Concessions

    Check your original sales agreement and inspection repair addenda to confirm whether the seller contractually agreed to purchase a one-year warranty.

  3. Contact Your Buyer Real Estate Agent for Transaction Archives

    Reach out to your real estate agent, who maintains permanent digital transaction archives containing warranty confirmation receipts and plan brochures.

  4. Call the Escrow Title Officer to Retrieve Payment Vouchers

    Contact the title company that closed your transaction and request the check number, date, and provider name from their settlement disbursement ledger.

  5. Query Major Warranty Companies Using Your Property Street Address

    Call the customer service departments of prominent warranty providers, providing your property address and closing date to locate active accounts.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Where is a home warranty listed on closing paperwork?

A home warranty is listed on Page 2 of your Closing Disclosure (CD) or HUD-1 Settlement Statement under Section H, labeled Other, or occasionally Section F under Prepaids, detailing the company name and fee paid.

Q2: Does a seller automatically provide a home warranty when you buy a house?

No, a seller is not legally required to provide a home warranty. It is an optional contractual concession negotiated during the purchase offer or home inspection resolution to give buyers peace of mind regarding older mechanical systems.

Q3: How long does a seller-paid home warranty last after closing?

A standard seller-paid home warranty lasts for exactly one year (365 calendar days) starting on the official day of closing and title transfer.

Q4: Can I find out if I have a home warranty by calling my title company?

Yes. The title company or escrow closing officer maintains complete records of all settlement disbursements. They can search their ledger, confirm if a check was sent to a warranty provider, and give you the company name and check details.

Q5: What is the difference between a home warranty and homeowners insurance?

Homeowners insurance covers major property damage caused by sudden, accidental perils like storms, fires, and theft. A home warranty covers the repair or replacement of domestic systems and appliances (HVAC, plumbing, electrical) that break down from normal wear and tear.

Q6: What happens if an appliance breaks and I do not know my warranty policy number?

You can call the warranty provider's customer service line and provide your property street address, full name, and home closing date. Their database links contracts to the physical home address, allowing them to open a service claim without the account number.

Q7: How do I transfer an existing home warranty into my name as the new homeowner?

If the seller or their agent ordered the warranty during closing, it is usually registered in your name automatically upon title transfer. If not, you can call the warranty company with your Closing Disclosure to update primary account holder details.

Q8: Can I renew a seller-provided home warranty after the first year expires?

Yes. As your initial one-year warranty approaches expiration, the warranty provider will contact you via mail or email with renewal options, allowing you to extend coverage on an annual or monthly premium basis.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Finding out whether your home has an active warranty policy should never wait until an air conditioner fails on a sweltering summer afternoon. By reviewing Section H of your Closing Disclosure, contacting your buyer real estate agent, and confirming payment records with your escrow title officer, you can quickly establish your coverage status. Knowing your warranty company, policy number, and coverage exclusions empowers you to utilize your seller-funded benefits effectively, protecting your household budget from unexpected mechanical repair costs.
