What Does It Cost to Remove Asbestos Siding?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 30, 2026

Understanding what does it cost to remove asbestos siding is an essential budgeting step for homeowners remodeling mid-century residential exteriors. Because exterior cement-asbestos shingles, commonly termed transite, are durable yet brittle, certified contractors must manually unfasten and lower panels without shattering them, balancing hazardous disposal fees, regulatory permit costs, and exterior wall cladding replacements.

Exterior siding manufactured between 1930 and 1975 commonly integrated chrysotile mineral fibers into a dense portland cement matrix. These transite siding shingles provided outstanding fire resistance, weatherproofing, and pest protection. However, when siding panels crack, weather, or require replacement during energy-efficiency retrofits, owners must weigh certified abatement against safe over-cladding. Evaluating overall health risks alongside epidemiological data on what causes mesothelioma other than asbestos reinforces the necessity of preventing hazardous mineral dust dispersion.

National Average Pricing and Per-Square-Foot Costs

Nationwide, removing exterior cement-asbestos siding costs between $7.00 and $15.00 per square foot for abatement, transport, and hazardous landfill disposal. For an average single-story 1,500-square-foot home with roughly 1,200 square feet of exterior wall area, homeowners typically invest between $8,400 and $15,000 for certified abatement alone. When factoring in the installation of contemporary vinyl, fiber cement, or engineered wood replacement siding, total project expenditures range from $16,000 to $32,000.

Comparing these exterior costs with interior remediation figures detailed in how much does asbestos removal cost analyses reveals that exterior siding removal is often less complex than interior pipe or ceiling abatement. Because the work occurs outdoors, elaborate negative-pressure indoor enclosures are unnecessary, although ground containment plastic and strict drop cloths are mandatory.

Primary Cost Drivers in Exterior Siding Projects

Several critical variables govern final estimates for exterior siding remediation. Architectural height and roof pitch dictate whether extensive pipe scaffolding or mechanical bucket lifts are needed. Multistory residences incur higher labor rates because technicians must carefully lower unfastened shingles to ground drop sheets rather than dropping materials. Identifying distinctive surface textures, as explained in visual guides on what asbestos looks like in building exteriors, helps estimators calculate shingle density and weight.

Disposal tariffs also vary significantly by geographic region. Municipal landfills charge specialized tipping fees for regulated non-friable construction debris, often ranging from $80 to $220 per ton. Transport manifests, specialized double-bagging consumables, and local air quality agency notification fees add fixed administrative costs to the contract.

Removal Versus Siding Encapsulation

Homeowners seeking to reduce initial renovation expenses frequently consider over-cladding rather than complete removal. In this approach, rigid foam insulation board is secured over intact transite shingles, followed by new vinyl or metal siding. While over-cladding costs roughly 50 percent less up front, complete abatement permanently eliminates hazardous minerals, resolves real estate disclosure hurdles, and facilitates future wall modifications.

How to Plan and Budget for Asbestos Siding Removal

Follow these five professional steps to evaluate your home siding, obtain competitive bids, and oversee safe certified removal.

  1. Verify Material Composition via Testing

    Collect a core shingle sample or hire a certified inspector to conduct PLM analysis confirming asbestos content before soliciting bids.

  2. Measure Exterior Wall Surface Square Footage

    Calculate gross exterior wall dimensions minus window and door openings to determine exact net square footage for accurate contractor estimates.

  3. Solicit itemized Bids from Licensed Abatement Firms

    Request detailed estimates specifying manual unfastening, wetting protocols, ground plastic containment, and hazardous disposal manifesting.

  4. Confirm Municipal Permits and Environmental Notices

    Ensure the selected contractor files state environmental notifications and secures local building department demolition permits.

  5. Execute Safe Removal and Install Weather Barrier

    Supervise wet unfastening of shingles, inspect bare sheathing, and install breathable weather-resistant house wrap prior to new siding.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to remove asbestos siding per square foot?

Certified abatement contractors typically charge between $7.00 and $15.00 per square foot for manual unfastening, bagging, and hazardous landfill disposal.

Q2: Can I simply install vinyl siding over existing asbestos shingles?

Yes, over-cladding with vinyl siding over rigid insulation is legal in many areas, but driving nails through shingles can break them, creating minor fiber risks.

Q3: Is asbestos siding hazardous if left undisturbed?

No, intact cement-asbestos siding is non-friable and binds fibers securely, posing no health risk as long as it is not broken, sawed, sanded, or pulverized.

Q4: Can I remove asbestos siding myself to save money?

While some jurisdictions allow homeowners to remove siding from single-family dwellings, DIY removal carries high risks of cracking shingles and violating disposal laws.

Q5: How long does professional siding removal take on an average home?

A certified four-person crew typically completes exterior siding removal on a standard single-story home within two to four business days.

Q6: What happens to the removed shingles after abatement?

Shingles are wrapped in double-layer 6-mil polyethylene, placed in sealed lined dumpsters, and transported with waste manifests to an authorized landfill.

Q7: Does removing asbestos siding increase home property value?

Yes, completely removing hazardous materials eliminates real estate disclosure liabilities, improves buyer confidence, and allows modern energy retrofits.

Q8: Do power washers release fibers from asbestos siding?

Yes, pressure washing asbestos siding is strictly prohibited by environmental agencies because high-pressure water pulverizes the surface and aerosolizes fibers.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Investing in the certified removal of asbestos siding permanently rids your residential exterior of hazardous mineral materials, enhances property resale appeal, and facilitates modern insulation upgrades. By obtaining multiple licensed contractor quotes, verifying proper waste manifesting, and selecting high-performance modern replacement cladding, homeowners achieve a safe and visually stunning home exterior transformation.
