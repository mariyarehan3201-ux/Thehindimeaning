How to Become a Social Worker in Virginia?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 30, 2026

Becoming a licensed social worker in the Commonwealth of Virginia requires fulfilling rigorous educational, clinical, and examination standards established by the Virginia Board of Social Work under the Department of Health Professions. Aspiring professionals must graduate from a Council on Social Work Education accredited bachelor or master of social work program, register their supervisory arrangement with the state board, complete up to three thousand hours of supervised clinical experience, and achieve a passing score on the national Association of Social Work Boards examination.

Licensing Tiers Under the Virginia Board of Social Work

The practice of social work in the Commonwealth of Virginia is regulated by the Virginia Board of Social Work, operating under the Virginia Department of Health Professions in Henrico. Virginia recognizes two distinct tiers of professional licensure: the Licensed Social Worker (LSW) and the Licensed Clinical Social Worker (LCSW). Understanding the legal scope, educational prerequisites, and clinical responsibilities associated with each tier is essential for mapping an effective career path in community advocacy, healthcare administration, or clinical psychotherapy.

The Licensed Social Worker (LSW) designation is accessible to candidates who have earned either a Bachelor of Social Work (BSW) or a Master of Social Work (MSW) from an educational institution accredited by the Council on Social Work Education (CSWE). LSW professionals engage in generalist social work, case management, psychosocial assessments, resource navigation, and supportive counseling within schools, hospice organizations, and community agencies. Unlike non-credentialed human services workers discussed in overviews of alternative pathways in social human services, an LSW holds a protected professional title recognized by Virginia state statutes.

In contrast, the Licensed Clinical Social Worker (LCSW) represents the highest level of clinical licensure in Virginia. An LCSW must hold an MSW or doctorate in social work with a demonstrated clinical concentration. Virginia law authorizes LCSWs to operate as independent mental health practitioners capable of diagnosing mental, emotional, and behavioral disorders, formulating clinical treatment plans, providing specialized psychotherapy, and billing commercial insurance, Medicaid, and Medicare. Comparing this level of clinical training with advanced clinical mental health training highlights the rigorous preparation required. Supervised Clinical Practice Requirements for Virginia LCSW Candidates

For Master of Social Work graduates pursuing clinical practice, completing post-graduate supervised residency represents the most rigorous phase of the licensing journey. Under Virginia Administrative Code (18 VAC 140-20-50), LCSW candidates must complete three thousand hours of post-graduate, supervised clinical social work experience. These hours must be accumulated in no less than one year (twelve months) and no more than four years (forty-eight months). Candidates practicing in community service boards, hospital systems, outpatient clinics, or private group practices must maintain meticulous documentation of their clinical activities.

A critical statutory mandate that candidates must respect is the requirement to formally register their supervisory contract with the Virginia Board of Social Work BEFORE initiating any clinical hours. Clinical hours accrued prior to the Board written approval of the supervisory registration are completely forfeited and will not count toward the three thousand hour total. If a resident changes employers, changes job duties, or switches clinical supervisors, an updated supervisory registration packet must be submitted to and approved by the Board before resuming hours under the new arrangement.

The three thousand required hours are structured into precise regulatory sub-categories. At least one thousand three hundred eighty hours must consist of direct face-to-face client contact delivering clinical social work services, including biopsychosocial assessments, diagnostic evaluations, individual psychotherapy, couples counseling, or group therapy. Furthermore, the candidate must receive a minimum of one hundred hours of direct supervision from a Virginia Board-approved LCSW supervisor, provided at a ratio of at least one hour of supervision per forty hours of clinical practice. Understanding regulatory enforcement, similar to standards in professional licensing disciplinary procedures, ensures strict compliance. The ASWB Examination, Criminal Background Checks, and Licensure Maintenance

Upon satisfactory completion and verification of the three thousand supervised clinical hours, the candidate submits their comprehensive licensure application packet to the Virginia Board of Social Work. After the Board reviews and approves the application, supervisory verification affidavits, and official university transcripts, the Board grants the candidate official authorization to register for the Association of Social Work Boards (ASWB) Clinical Examination. Candidates cannot register directly with the ASWB without preliminary approval from the state licensing board.

The ASWB Clinical Examination is a computerized four-hour examination comprising one hundred seventy multiple-choice questions, of which one hundred fifty are scored. The test assesses knowledge across four primary domains: Human Development, Diversity, and Behavior in the Environment; Assessment, Diagnosis, and Treatment Planning; Psychotherapy, Clinical Interventions, and Case Management; and Professional Values and Ethics. Preparation typically involves completing practice exams through the ASWB portal and reviewing the NASW Code of Ethics and DSM-5 diagnostic criteria. Additionally, all applicants must undergo electronic fingerprinting through Fieldprint Virginia for state and FBI criminal history background checks.

Once issued, a Virginia LCSW license expires biennially on the licensee birth month. Licensees must complete thirty hours of continuing education (CE) during each two-year renewal cycle. Mandated coursework includes at least two hours in professional ethics and standards of practice and at least two hours in cultural competence or health equity. Virginia has also enacted legislation to participate in the interstate Social Work Licensure Compact, facilitating multistate practice mobility and telehealth privileges for qualified Virginia clinical social workers.

How to Obtain Your LCSW License from the Virginia Board of Social Work

A comprehensive procedural guide covering graduate education, supervisory registration, clinical residency, and board exam passage in Virginia.

  1. Complete a CSWE-Accredited Master of Social Work Degree Program

    Graduate from a Council on Social Work Education accredited MSW program, ensuring your curriculum includes dedicated coursework in psychopathology, clinical assessment, and psychotherapy interventions.

  2. Secure Employment in an Approved Clinical Social Work Setting

    Obtain a clinical role within a hospital, community mental health center, outpatient counseling clinic, or residential behavioral facility that guarantees direct client diagnostic and psychotherapeutic contact.

  3. Select a Board-Approved LCSW Supervisor and File Supervision Registration

    Contract with a qualified LCSW supervisor and submit the formal Registration of Supervision package to the Virginia Board of Social Work, awaiting written approval before logging hours.

  4. Complete 3,000 Hours of Post-Graduate Supervised Clinical Experience

    Accumulate three thousand hours over one to four years, including at least one thousand three hundred eighty direct client contact hours and one hundred hours of documented clinical supervision.

  5. Submit the Full Licensure Application and Complete Criminal Background Checks

    Submit the completed LCSW application with supervisor verification forms and official transcripts to the Board, and complete required electronic fingerprinting through Fieldprint Virginia.

  6. Pass the ASWB Clinical Examination and Receive Your Virginia License

    Obtain Board approval to register for the national ASWB Clinical Exam, achieve a passing score, and receive your official Virginia Licensed Clinical Social Worker credential.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the difference between an LSW and an LCSW in Virginia?

An LSW (Licensed Social Worker) in Virginia focuses on generalist social work, case management, and supportive services with a BSW or MSW, while an LCSW (Licensed Clinical Social Worker) requires an MSW, three thousand supervised clinical hours, and authorizes independent mental health diagnosis and private psychotherapy.

Q2: Can I count clinical hours in Virginia before my supervision is approved by the board?

No. Virginia regulations strictly stipulate that no post-graduate clinical hours will be credited toward licensure until the candidate has received written approval of their Registration of Supervision from the Virginia Board of Social Work.

Q3: How long does it take to complete the three thousand clinical hours for Virginia LCSW licensure?

Virginia law requires the three thousand hours to be completed in no less than one year (twelve months) and no more than four years (forty-eight months). Most full-time clinicians complete the requirement in approximately two to three years.

Q4: What happens if I fail the ASWB Clinical Examination in Virginia?

If you do not pass the ASWB Clinical Exam on your first attempt, you must wait ninety days before retaking the test under ASWB retake policies, and you must maintain an active application file with the Virginia Board.

Q5: Can clinical social work supervision in Virginia be completed remotely through telehealth?

Yes. The Virginia Board of Social Work permits clinical supervision to occur via real-time synchronous audiovisual technology, provided patient privacy and security are maintained and the supervisor is licensed in Virginia.

Q6: Does Virginia grant social work license reciprocity for out-of-state social workers?

Virginia offers licensure by endorsement for clinical social workers licensed in another jurisdiction who have met substantially equivalent requirements, including a CSWE-accredited MSW, supervised clinical hours, and passing the ASWB Clinical exam.

Q7: What degree programs qualify for clinical social work licensure in Virginia?

Applicants must hold a master or doctoral degree in social work from a program accredited by the Council on Social Work Education (CSWE) that includes clinical coursework in psychopathology, clinical assessment, and intervention methods.

Q8: How many continuing education hours are required to maintain a Virginia social work license?

Virginia LCSWs must complete thirty hours of continuing education every two years, which must include at least two hours in ethics and standards of practice and at least two hours in cultural competence or health equity.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Achieving social work licensure in Virginia represents a profound commitment to mental health advocacy, clinical excellence, and ethical human service. While navigating CSWE graduate education, the three thousand hour clinical residency, and the ASWB national board exam requires immense perseverance, the professional reward is unparalleled. Licensed clinical social workers serve as indispensable pillars across Virginia healthcare networks, schools, veteran medical centers, and private therapy practices, providing compassionate, evidence-based care to individuals and communities across the Commonwealth.
