What to Do After a Slip and Fall?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

After a slip and fall accident, your immediate actions dictate both your physical recovery and the legal viability of your injury claim. You must inspect the area for hazards, photograph the scene and your footwear, report the fall to management to create an official incident report, seek urgent medical treatment, and consult a premises liability attorney before giving statements to insurance adjusters.

The Immediate Trauma: Shock, Adrenaline, and Hidden Physical Injuries

Experiencing a violent slip and fall on a hard commercial surface—such as polished terrazzo tile, vinyl composition tile (VCT), or wet concrete—is a shocking, disorienting event. The instantaneous loss of traction causes the human body to twist uncontrollably before crashing down with blunt, bone-shattering force.

In the immediate aftermath, biological adrenaline surges through your bloodstream. This natural 'fight-or-flight' hormonal surge masks acute pain, frequently leading victims to mistakenly believe they are uninjured. Embarrassment often takes over, prompting victims to quickly stand up, apologize to onlookers, and rush away. This is the single most common mistake in slip and fall incidents.

Severe musculoskeletal and neurological trauma—such as hairline wrist fractures, rotator cuff tears, herniated lumbar spinal discs, and traumatic brain concussions—frequently manifest hours or days after the incident once adrenaline subsides and inflammatory swelling peaks. Remaining seated for a moment to assess pain, requesting immediate assistance, and refusing to minimize your discomfort protects your health and establishes undeniable clinical proof of injury. Scene Evidence Preservation: The 15-Minute Critical Window

In American premises liability law, commercial property owners and retail businesses owe patrons a high duty of care. However, the legal burden rests entirely on the injured victim to prove that a dangerous, transitory hazard caused the fall and that the property owner had notice of it.

The physical evidence at a slip and fall scene is fleeting. The moment you leave the area, store employees will immediately deploy mops, wipe up liquid puddles, sweep away broken glass, and adjust floor mats. Once the puddle is dried, proving what caused your fall becomes vastly more complex.

If you are physically able—or by directing a companion or bystander—utilize your smartphone camera immediately. Capture wide-angle photographs showing the surrounding environment and the absence of yellow warning cones, as well as close-up photos of the liquid puddle, grease, wax residue, or structural transition. Photograph the soles of your shoes to prove they had adequate tread and were free of preexisting defects. Secure contact information from civilian shoppers who witnessed your fall. Navigating Insurance Claims and the Spoliation Letter

Within days of reporting your fall, you will receive telephone calls from corporate risk management adjusters representing the property owner's Commercial General Liability (CGL) insurer (such as Travelers, Liberty Mutual, or third-party administrators like Sedgwick).

Insurance adjusters are skilled negotiators trained to elicit damaging admissions. They will ask leading questions such as: 'Were you looking down while walking?' or 'Did you see the spill before you stepped?' They may offer an immediate $500 or $1,000 'goodwill' check accompanied by a multi-page document. Never sign this document; it is a full legal Release of All Claims that permanently extinguishes your right to seek compensation for surgeries or medical treatments.

Instead, retain an experienced personal injury attorney who will immediately serve the property owner with a formal legal Spoliation Letter. This legal notice commands the business under penalty of court sanctions to preserve all electronic digital surveillance video (CCTV), employee shift timecards, and maintenance sweep logs from the date of your injury.

How to Handle the Aftermath of a Slip and Fall Accident

Step-by-step post-accident crisis protocol to document injuries and preserve legal rights.

  1. Assess Physical Injuries and Call for Immediate Aid

    Remain still for a moment to evaluate spine, head, and limb pain; ask store staff or bystanders to summon emergency medical paramedics if injured.

  2. Photograph the Unsafe Hazard, Puddle, and Surroundings

    Take clear photos and video of the liquid puddle, food debris, or uneven floor transition, capturing the complete lack of warning cones or caution signs.

  3. Notify On-Site Management and Insist on an Incident Report

    Report the fall directly to the store general manager; confirm they complete a written incident report and request the report reference number.

  4. Seek Urgent Medical Evaluation and Consult Legal Counsel

    Visit an emergency room or urgent care center within 24 hours to document trauma, and retain a personal injury lawyer to preserve security video.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Should I sign an incident report presented by the store manager?

You may sign a factual statement documenting what happened, but never sign any document containing waiver or liability release language.

Q2: How long after a slip and fall should I see a doctor?

You should seek medical evaluation immediately or within 24 hours; waiting longer gives insurance adjusters grounds to argue injuries were not caused by the fall.

Q3: What should I do with the shoes I wore during the fall?

Place the unwashed shoes in a sealed plastic bag and store them safely; your attorney will preserve them as physical proof of proper shoe tread.

Q4: Can I sue if there were no witnesses to my slip and fall?

Yes. Store ceiling security cameras, emergency room intake records, footwear inspection, and employee testimony can conclusively prove your claim.

Q5: Who pays my medical bills while my slip and fall case is pending?

Bills are submitted to your health insurance or paid via medical liens; the business's liability insurance pays a lump-sum settlement at conclusion.

Q6: What is Med-Pay coverage in a commercial property policy?

Med-Pay is a no-fault commercial insurance rider that covers immediate medical expenses (typically $5,000 to $10,000) regardless of who was at fault.

Q7: How much does a slip and fall lawyer charge?

Premises liability attorneys work on a contingency fee basis (typically 33.3%), meaning you pay zero upfront fees and pay only if your case is won.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Knowing what to do after a slip and fall protects your health and secures your legal rights against negligent commercial property owners. Never let embarrassment prevent you from documenting the hazard, filing an official manager report, and seeking immediate emergency medical treatment. Preserve your footwear and clothing, decline to give recorded statements to insurance adjusters, and partner with a dedicated premises liability attorney to recover maximum financial compensation.
