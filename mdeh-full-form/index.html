MDEH Full Form: Master Diploma in Electrohomeopathy

Full Form
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 17, 2026

The full form of MDEH in alternative medicine and holistic healthcare education is Master Diploma in Electrohomeopathy (also referred to in clinical institutions as Doctor of Medicine in Electrohomeopathy, MD-EH). It is an advanced post-graduate professional qualification awarded by alternative medical councils and institutes practicing Electrohomeopathy, a natural therapeutic discipline founded in the nineteenth century by Italian physician Count Cesare Mattei using botanical extracts to balance blood and lymph.

What Is MDEH and What Does It Stand For in Alternative Medicine?

In holistic therapeutics, botanical pharmacology, and complementary healthcare, MDEH stands for Master Diploma in Electrohomeopathy (or MD in Electrohomeopathy). Electrohomeopathy is a distinctive alternative healthcare discipline formulated in 1865 by Count Cesare Mattei of Bologna, Italy. The therapeutic philosophy rests upon the principle that all pathological human diseases originate from imbalances or impurities in two fundamental vital circulating fluids: blood (sanguis) and lymph (lympha).

Unlike conventional allopathic pharmacology that utilizes synthetic chemical compounds, or classical homeopathy that incorporates animal and mineral tinctures, Electrohomeopathy utilizes exclusively non-toxic plant extracts prepared through a specialized spagyric distillation technique known as 'cohobation.' The prefix 'Electro' refers not to electrical physical devices, but to the bio-energetic, rapid-acting kinetic botanical force extracted from medicinal plants.

Curriculum Structure and Core Syllabi in MDEH

The MDEH diploma program is designed for practitioners who have completed basic diplomas such as BEMS (Bachelor of Electro Medicine & Surgery) or related alternative medical qualifications. The advanced curriculum delves deeply into herbal pharmacognosy, pathology, symptom diagnosis, and therapeutic formulation.

Students study the 114 medicinal plants cataloged by Count Mattei, learning how these botanical species interact with human physiology to restore cellular homeostasis. The table below delineates the core academic syllabus modules covered in an advanced MDEH course.

Remedy Classifications in Electrohomeopathy

Electrohomeopathic therapeutics organizes botanical remedies into functional remedy groups tailored to specific anatomical systems and cellular tissues. Mattei classified these into distinct series, each addressing constitutional weaknesses or organ dysfunctions.

For example, the Scrofoloso remedies target the lymphatic system and metabolic glands, while the Angiotico series supports circulatory vascular health. The table below outlines the core functional groups taught in MDEH coursework.

Remedy Group Therapeutic Target / Organ System Common Clinical Indications
Scrofoloso (S1 - S12) Lymphatic vessels, lymph nodes, glandular metabolism General debility, immune deficiency, skin eruptions
Angiotico (A1 - A3) Blood vascular system, arterial and venous circulation Circulatory sluggishness, varicose veins, vascular headaches
Canceroso (C1 - C17) Cellular tissue regeneration, chronic inflammation Chronic ulcers, benign tumors, glandular indurations
Pettorale (P1 - P9) Respiratory tract, bronchial mucosa, lungs Coughs, bronchitis, asthma, catarrhal congestion

Febbrifugo (F1 - F2) Thermoregulatory system, sympathetic nervous system Fever, periodic chills, neuralgic pain paroxysms

Legal Status and Recognition in India

In India, Electrohomeopathy holds a specialized legal and educational status. While not currently recognized under the National Commission for Indian System of Medicine (NCISM) or the central Ministry of AYUSH as a registered medical system alongside Ayurveda, Unani, Siddha, and Sowa-Rigpa, several judicial pronouncements by high courts and the Supreme Court of India have affirmed the right of alternative medicine practitioners to practice and dispense non-toxic natural remedies.

Various government expert committees have examined Electrohomeopathy for potential central recognition. Holders of MDEH diplomas typically practice in private holistic wellness clinics, dispensing botanical formulations while educating communities on natural dietetics, hygiene, and lifestyle health.

How to Pursue and Complete an MDEH Diploma Course

  1. Complete Basic Foundation Coursework (BEMS)

    Earn a Bachelor of Electro Medicine and Surgery (BEMS) or equivalent diploma in alternative medical systems from an accredited institution.

  2. Select a Registered Electrohomeopathy Institute

    Choose a recognized training council or college offering the advanced MDEH curriculum affiliated with national alternative medical associations.

  3. Undertake Theoretical and Spagyric Studies

    Master the 114 medicinal plants, spagyric essence cohobation processes, and the therapeutic indications of the Scrofoloso and Angiotico remedy groups.

  4. Complete Clinical Internship and Case Study Logs

    Attend supervised outpatient clinics to observe patient evaluation, temperament analysis, and customized remedy compounding.

  5. Clear Examination and Receive Council Certification

    Pass the written theory and practical viva examination to receive your diploma certificate and council membership credentials.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does MDEH stand for?

MDEH stands for Master Diploma in Electrohomeopathy (or Doctor of Medicine in Electrohomeopathy).

Q2: Who founded the system of Electrohomeopathy?

Electrohomeopathy was founded in 1865 by Italian physician and researcher Count Cesare Mattei in Bologna, Italy.

Q3: What is the primary principle of Electrohomeopathy?

The primary principle is that all diseases stem from impurities or imbalances in the two vital fluids: blood and lymph, which are restored using plant essences.

Q4: Are chemical or animal products used in Electrohomeopathy?

No, remedies in Electrohomeopathy are derived 100% from non-toxic medicinal plants prepared using spagyric extraction methods.

Q5: What does the term 'Electro' mean in this system?

It refers to the dynamic, bio-energetic, rapid kinetic healing power extracted from medicinal plants, not electrical machines.

Q6: What are the eligibility requirements for MDEH?

Candidates must typically hold a BEMS (Bachelor of Electro Medicine and Surgery) or an equivalent foundational alternative medicine credential.

Q7: What are the five fluidic electricities in Electrohomeopathy?

They are Red, Blue, Green, Yellow, and White electricities, representing subtle bio-energetic botanical extracts used internally and externally.

Q8: Is Electrohomeopathy recognized by the Ministry of AYUSH?

It is under governmental committee review for central recognition, while practitioners operate legally under various state court rulings for natural health practice.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The Master Diploma in Electrohomeopathy (MDEH) represents specialized training in Count Cesare Mattei's botanical therapeutic system. By focusing exclusively on non-toxic, plant-derived spagyric essences designed to restore balance to blood and lymph, MDEH graduates carry forward a distinct tradition of natural, gentle, and holistic health promotion.
