RLA Meaning HVAC: Rated Load Amps Compressor Guide

The Electrical Engineering Definition of RLA in HVAC

In heating, ventilation, air conditioning, and refrigeration (HVAC/R) engineering, the acronym rla meaning hvac stands for Rated Load Amps (historically termed Running Load Amps). Prominently stamped on the electrical rating data plate of every residential condensing unit, commercial chiller, and heat pump compressor, RLA represents the maximum continuous electrical current in amperes that the hermetic compressor motor can safely draw under full-load design operating conditions.

Unlike standard external electric motors that carry a fixed Full Load Amps (FLA) rating based on free-air shaft horsepower, hermetic refrigeration compressors are cooled directly by suction refrigerant gas passing over internal motor windings. Because cooling efficiency depends directly on the refrigeration cycle, safety certification standards (such as UL 1995 and NEC Article 440) establish RLA as a calculated mathematical baseline: RLA is established by dividing the compressor's Maximum Continuous Current (MCC) by a safety factor, typically 1.56 (or multiplied by 0.64).

Comparing Critical HVAC Electrical Ratings: RLA, LRA, and FLA

HVAC service technicians utilize three foundational amperage ratings stamped on compressor data plates to diagnose electrical failures, size circuit breakers, and evaluate motor winding health. The table below delineates the functional differences between RLA, LRA, and FLA.

Electrical Rating Code Full Technical Term Operational Definition Typical Measurement Benchmark
RLA Rated Load Amps Maximum continuous running current under full cooling load. Actual running amps should typically be 60% to 80% of stamped RLA on mild days.
LRA Locked Rotor Amps Massive instantaneous inrush current drawn when the motor starts from dead stop. Typically 5 to 7 times higher than RLA; lasts for only a fraction of a second.
FLA Full Load Amps Continuous current rating for external open motors (e.g., outdoor condenser fan motor). Used for conventional fan motors cooled by surrounding ambient air.
MCC Maximum Continuous Current The absolute threshold where internal motor overload switches trip. RLA is calculated directly from MCC (MCC = RLA × 1.56).

How Technicians Use RLA for Compressor Diagnostics

When an air conditioning unit fails to cool or trips circuit breakers, technicians clamp a true-RMS digital multimeter around the compressor's common run wire to measure operating current. Comparing measured amperage against stamped RLA diagnoses severe mechanical and thermodynamic problems.

The table below summarizes common diagnostic findings when analyzing measured compressor running amps against stamped RLA.

Amperage Reading Scenario Physical Diagnostic Condition Common System Fault Required Service Action
Amps Significantly Above RLA (> 110%) Severe electrical or mechanical overload Dirty condenser coils, overcharged refrigerant, failing start capacitor, failing bearings. Clean outdoor coils immediately; verify refrigerant subcooling and test capacitor.
Amps Equal to LRA Continuously Locked rotor state; compressor cannot turn Mechanical seizure, seized scroll plates, broken internal crankshaft. Attempt hard-start kit installation; if still locked, compressor replacement is required.
Amps Abnormally Low (< 40% of RLA) Compressor is running with zero load Severe refrigerant leak, empty system, broken internal compressor discharge valves. Conduct nitrogen pressure decay leak test; repair piping leak and recharge.
Normal Running Amps (60% to 80% of RLA) Compressor operating within design parameters Proper refrigerant charge, clean heat exchangers, balanced airflow. Standard annual preventative maintenance and electrical terminal inspection.

Electrical inspectors and HVAC installers also rely on RLA to comply with the National Electrical Code (NEC). Circuit wire sizing (Minimum Circuit Ampacity or MCA) and maximum fuse or breaker ratings (Maximum Overcurrent Protection or MOCP) are calculated directly from the combined RLA of the compressor and FLA of the fan motor.

Additionally, modern inverter-driven variable-speed compressors utilize pulse-width modulation (PWM) drives that continuously modulate running amps well below rated RLA, delivering superior seasonal energy efficiency (SEER2) ratings.

How to Measure and Check Compressor Running Amps Against RLA

A certified HVAC technician's step-by-step procedure for safely measuring compressor operating amperage.

  1. Locate the Electrical Data Plate on Outdoor Unit

    Read and record the stamped RLA and LRA values printed on the metal nameplate riveted to the condenser cabinet.

  2. Ensure Safe High-Voltage Testing Setup

    Wear rated electrical personal protective equipment, remove the control panel access cover, and ensure all wiring connections are clean.

  3. Clamp True-RMS Multimeter Around Common Run Lead

    Place the jaws of an inductive clamp meter around the single insulated wire leading from the run capacitor or contactor to the compressor 'C' terminal.

  4. Compare Running Amps to RLA at Peak Operating Load

    Allow the system to run for fifteen minutes; verify that operating amps remain between 60% and 80% of stamped RLA under normal summer ambient temperatures.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does RLA stand for in HVAC?

RLA stands for Rated Load Amps, representing the maximum continuous running current a compressor motor can safely draw under full design load.

Q2: What is the difference between RLA and LRA?

RLA is the steady running current under load, while LRA (Locked Rotor Amps) is the massive initial surge current drawn when the motor starts from rest.

Q3: Should compressor running amps equal RLA?

No, under normal mild to warm operating conditions, running amps should typically hover between 60% and 80% of the stamped RLA rating.

Q4: What causes a compressor to draw higher amps than RLA?

Causes include dirty outdoor condenser coils, an overcharged refrigerant system, high ambient heat, a bad capacitor, or tight mechanical bearings.

Q5: What does FLA mean compared to RLA?

FLA (Full Load Amps) applies to open electric motors like outdoor fan motors, while RLA applies specifically to hermetic refrigerant-cooled compressors.

Q6: How is RLA calculated by manufacturers?

Manufacturers calculate RLA by dividing the Maximum Continuous Current (MCC) by a safety factor of 1.56, complying with UL standards.

Q7: Why is RLA important for sizing electrical wire and breakers?

Electrical codes (NEC) use RLA to calculate the Minimum Circuit Ampacity (MCA) for wire gauge selection and Maximum Overcurrent Protection (MOCP) for breakers.

Q8: Can a low refrigerant charge cause low running amps?

Yes, because the compressor is doing less physical work pumping vapor, a low refrigerant charge results in amperage readings far below RLA.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The RLA meaning in HVAC stands for Rated Load Amps, an indispensable metric for equipment sizing, electrical safety, and system troubleshooting. By indicating the maximum safe continuous running current of a refrigeration compressor, RLA enables technicians to diagnose mechanical seizures, identify refrigerant overcharges, and ensure wiring satisfies electrical safety codes. Monitoring RLA protects expensive HVAC equipment from premature motor failure.
