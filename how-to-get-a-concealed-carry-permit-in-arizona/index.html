How to Get a Concealed Carry Permit in Arizona?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Arizona is widely recognized for its strong constitutional carry framework, which permits law-abiding citizens aged twenty-one and older to carry concealed firearms without a government-issued license. However, thousands of residents actively choose to obtain an official Arizona Concealed Weapons Permit issued by the Arizona Department of Public Safety Concealed Weapons Permit Unit. Holding an official permit grants critical legal advantages, including multi-state reciprocity across more than thirty-five states, exemption from federal NICS background checks when purchasing firearms, and legal authorization to carry within establishments serving alcohol for consumption on premises under specific statutory conditions.

Constitutional Carry Versus Holding an Official Arizona Permit

In 2010, Arizona enacted constitutional carry under Arizona Revised Statutes Title 13, establishing that any United States citizen or lawful permanent resident aged twenty-one or older who is not a prohibited possessor may legally carry a concealed weapon without a license. While this statute provides broad personal protection freedom within state lines, relying exclusively on permitless carry presents distinct legal constraints. For example, permitless carriers cannot legally carry concealed weapons across state lines into jurisdictions that require recognized credentials, nor can they bypass federal background checks at licensed firearms dealers.

Acquiring an Arizona Concealed Weapons Permit (CWP) bridges these jurisdictional and legal gaps. An Arizona CWP holder enjoys reciprocity in over thirty-five states, enabling seamless travel throughout large portions of the country. Furthermore, an Arizona permit satisfies the requirements of the federal Brady Handgun Violence Prevention Act, allowing permit holders to bypass the FBI National Instant Criminal Background Check System (NICS) when purchasing firearms from federal firearms licensees (FFLs). Additionally, permit holders may carry inside licensed restaurant premises serving alcohol, provided the individual does not consume alcoholic beverages and the business has not posted statutory prohibition signage.

Statutory Eligibility and Approved Firearms Safety Training

To qualify for an Arizona Concealed Weapons Permit under Arizona Revised Statutes Section 13-3112, an applicant must be a United States citizen or lawful resident alien, at least twenty-one years of age (or at least nineteen years of age for active-duty military personnel or honorably discharged veterans), and not under indictment or convicted of a felony offense. Applicants must not be adjudicated mentally incompetent, have active domestic violence protection orders, or be classified as prohibited possessors under state or federal criminal statutes.

Demonstrating firearm competence is a mandatory prerequisite for Arizona permit issuance. Arizona law accommodates diverse methods for satisfying this requirement. An applicant can complete any standard concealed carry firearms safety course taught by an instructor certified by the National Rifle Association or the Arizona Department of Public Safety. Alternatively, competence can be proven by submitting evidence of current or prior military service (such as a DD-214 showing honorable discharge), completion of a law enforcement firearms training course, or an active hunter education safety certificate from an authorized state fish and wildlife agency.

Application Submission, Fingerprinting, and Renewal Cycles

The Arizona Department of Public Safety Concealed Weapons Permit Unit processes applications through an efficient online portal as well as traditional mail. Applicants must complete the official CWP application form, pay the non-refundable state processing fee of sixty dollars, and submit proof of firearms competence. Additionally, applicants must provide two completed standard FBI FD-258 fingerprint cards. Fingerprints can be obtained electronically via approved LiveScan vendors or rolled in black ink at local county sheriff's offices or municipal police departments.

Upon receipt of the complete application packet, Arizona DPS conducts state and national criminal history record checks. State law mandates that DPS process applications within sixty days of receiving a completed packet. Once approved, the physical permit card is issued by postal mail and remains valid for a period of five years. Renewing an Arizona permit requires submitting a renewal application and a forty-three dollar fee prior to expiration; refresher training courses are not statutorily mandated for standard renewal.

How to Apply for an Arizona Concealed Weapons Permit

A step-by-step process for satisfying DPS requirements, submitting fingerprints, and receiving an Arizona Concealed Weapons Permit.

  1. Confirm Age and Legal Eligibility

    Ensure you are at least 21 years old (or 19 with active military status), a U.S. citizen or legal resident, and free from any felony convictions, domestic violence charges, or mental health commitments.

  2. Complete an Approved Firearms Training Course

    Attend an NRA or DPS-certified concealed carry safety class, or gather documentation of military service (DD-214) or prior law enforcement firearms qualification.

  3. Obtain Two Sets of Fingerprints on FD-258 Cards

    Visit a local law enforcement agency or approved electronic fingerprinting vendor to have your fingerprints rolled onto two official FBI FD-258 cards.

  4. Complete the Arizona DPS Application

    Fill out the online application via the Arizona DPS Concealed Weapons Permit portal or complete the standard physical paper application packet.

  5. Submit Application, Documents, and State Fee

    Submit your training certificate, two fingerprint cards, and the $60 application fee by money order, cashier's check, or approved online payment.

  6. Receive Your Five-Year Arizona Permit

    Wait approximately 30 to 60 days for background vetting; upon approval, Arizona DPS will mail your laminated permit card directly to your home address.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Do you need a permit to carry a concealed gun in Arizona?

No, Arizona is a constitutional carry state. Anyone 21 or older who is legally allowed to own a firearm can carry concealed without a permit within the state of Arizona.

Q2: Why should I get an Arizona CCW if permitless carry is legal?

An Arizona permit provides reciprocity in over 35 other states, exempts you from NICS background checks when buying firearms, and allows you to carry inside restaurants serving alcohol as long as you do not drink.

Q3: How much does an Arizona concealed carry permit cost?

The initial Arizona DPS application fee is 60 dollars, which covers background processing and a five-year permit card. Five-year renewals cost 43 dollars.

Q4: Can non-residents get an Arizona concealed carry permit?

Yes, Arizona issues Concealed Weapons Permits to non-residents. Non-residents must meet the exact same training, age, and background criteria as Arizona residents.

Q5: Does military service count for the Arizona firearms training requirement?

Yes, active duty military personnel or veterans who provide a copy of their DD-214 showing an honorable discharge satisfy the state firearms training qualification without attending a civilian class.

Q6: How long does it take for Arizona DPS to issue a permit?

By Arizona statute, the Department of Public Safety must process and issue or deny the permit within 60 days of receiving a complete application packet, though many applicants receive permits within 3 to 4 weeks.

Q7: Can I carry concealed in restaurants that serve alcohol in Arizona?

Only if you hold an official Arizona Concealed Weapons Permit. You must not consume any alcoholic beverages, and the establishment must not have posted legal prohibition signs forbidding firearms.

Q8: How long is an Arizona concealed carry permit valid?

An Arizona Concealed Weapons Permit is valid for five years from the date of issuance. You can renew your permit up to 90 days before it expires by submitting a renewal form and fee.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

While Arizona allows constitutional carry within its state lines, obtaining an official Arizona Concealed Weapons Permit provides substantial benefits that enhance legal peace of mind and travel flexibility. From interstate reciprocity across thirty-seven states to skipping background check delays at gun counters and safely navigating federal school buffer zones, the permit remains an invaluable asset for responsible firearm owners committed to comprehensive personal defense.
