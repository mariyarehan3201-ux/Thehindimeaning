How Much Does a Divorce Cost in WA?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a divorce cost in WA requires evaluating court filing fees, county mandatory programs, and legal representation options. For an agreed uncontested dissolution, couples in Washington typically spend between $1,200 and $3,000 total. In contrast, a contested divorce involving child custody disputes, spousal maintenance (alimony), and community property division averages $11,000 to $18,000 per spouse, with complex litigation in King and Snohomish counties reaching $30,000 to $50,000.

Washington State Superior Court Filing Fees and Surcharges

In Washington State, domestic relations petitions are filed in the Superior Court of the county where either spouse resides. The statutory baseline filing fee for a Petition for Dissolution of Marriage is approximately $314 to $374 statewide. This fee includes state court improvement assessments, law library fees, dispute resolution surcharges, and county judicial administration funds. If formal service by a county sheriff or private process server is necessary, it adds $40 to $100.

Washington operates under a mandatory statutory 90-day waiting period established by RCW 26.09.030. Even if spouses agree on every aspect of property division and parenting schedules, a Superior Court commissioner or judge cannot sign the final dissolution decree until 90 days have elapsed from the date the petition was filed and served. For families with minor children, attending a mandatory co-parenting seminar adds approximately $40 to $70 per parent.

Community Property Division and Legal Billing in Western vs. Eastern WA

Washington is one of nine community property states. Under RCW 26.16, all assets and debts acquired during the marriage are presumed community property, subject to a 'just and equitable' division. While many assume this means an automatic 50/50 division, the court considers economic circumstances, duration of marriage, and separate property contributions. Litigating these issues with forensic accountants and property appraisers significantly escalates legal bills.

Attorney billing rates in the Puget Sound metropolitan area (Seattle, Bellevue, Tacoma, and Everett) are among the highest in the Pacific Northwest, typically ranging from $350 to $600 per hour with upfront retainers of $5,000 to $10,000. In Eastern Washington (Spokane, Tri-Cities, Yakima), family law attorneys generally bill between $225 and $350 per hour with retainers ranging from $3,000 to $5,000.

Streamlining Parenting Plans and Child Support Calculations

In Washington, child custody is formalized through a mandatory state Permanent Parenting Plan form. This comprehensive document sets out residential schedules, holiday schedules, decision-making protocols, and dispute resolution methods. Washington child support is determined via the state's economic table guidelines, which account for both parents' net incomes, health insurance costs, and work-related childcare expenses.

Spouses can dramatically reduce divorce costs by engaging an accredited mediator or using an agreed joint petition. In an agreed dissolution, parties resolve all property, debt, maintenance, and parenting terms cooperatively. One attorney can draft the finalized pleadings for a flat fee, or each spouse can retain independent counsel for an unbundled document review, keeping overall costs under $3,500.

How to Minimize Divorce Costs in Washington

Follow these five strategic steps to reduce legal expenses during a Washington State divorce.

  1. File a Joint Petition for Dissolution

    File jointly with your spouse to eliminate summons costs and process server fees while establishing a cooperative posture.

  2. Construct an Agreed Permanent Parenting Plan

    Work out residential time, holiday rotations, and decision-making authority collaboratively on standard state forms.

  3. Calculate Child Support Accurately Using State Worksheets

    Complete the Washington State Child Support Schedule Worksheets with transparent W-2 and paystub data.

  4. Engage a Professional Family Mediator

    Resolve disputed asset divisions or maintenance disagreements through private mediation rather than court hearings.

  5. Observe the Mandatory 90-Day Waiting Period

    Prepare final decrees and findings of fact to submit to the court commissioner immediately after the 90-day clock elapses.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the average cost of divorce in WA?

An uncontested divorce averages $1,200 to $3,000, while a contested divorce typically costs between $11,000 and $18,000 per spouse.

Q2: What is the filing fee for divorce in Washington State?

Superior Court filing fees in Washington typically range between $314 and $374 depending on county surcharges.

Q3: What is the 90-day waiting period in WA divorce?

Under RCW 26.09.030, Washington mandates a 90-day cooling-off period from filing and service before a judge can enter a final decree.

Q4: Is Washington a community property state?

Yes, Washington is a community property state where marital assets and debts are divided equitably, though not strictly required to be 50/50.

Q5: Can you get a divorce in Washington without going to court?

Yes, if the divorce is completely uncontested, final paperwork can often be presented via ex parte administrative review without a courtroom hearing.

Q6: How much does a divorce lawyer cost in Seattle?

Divorce attorneys in Seattle typically bill between $350 and $600 per hour with upfront retainers ranging from $5,000 to $10,000.

Q7: Is alimony guaranteed in Washington State?

No, spousal maintenance is not automatic; courts evaluate marriage duration, standard of living, and financial need versus ability to pay.

Q8: Can court filing fees be waived in Washington?

Yes, low-income litigants can submit a Motion and Declaration for Waiver of Civil Fees and Surcharges to waive filing costs.
