How to Become a Substitute Teacher in Oklahoma?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Serving as a substitute teacher in Oklahoma provides a flexible, rewarding opportunity to support local public schools and foster student achievement. Governed by Oklahoma State Department of Education (OSDE) statutory guidelines and local school district policies, substitute educators ensure continuous classroom instruction when full-time teachers are absent. Whether you are a college graduate, an aspiring educator, or a community member looking to make a difference, this guide outlines the eligibility rules, background checks, daily expectations, and district hiring processes.

Oklahoma State Regulations and Substitute Teaching Tiers

In Oklahoma, public school staffing is regulated under Title 70 of the Oklahoma Statutes and administrative rules established by the Oklahoma State Department of Education (OSDE). Because the state grants significant operational autonomy to local boards of education, individual school districts determine specific hiring requirements, orientation procedures, and compensation schedules. However, state law establishes important parameters regarding the qualifications of substitutes and the maximum duration an individual can teach in a single classroom.

Oklahoma distinguishes between certified substitute teachers (individuals holding an active or valid standard Oklahoma educator certificate) and non-certified substitute teachers. Under Oklahoma law, non-certified substitutes who hold a high school diploma or college credits are generally restricted to a maximum number of instructional days per semester in the same classroom to preserve educational standards. In contrast, certified substitutes face fewer statutory restrictions and are actively recruited for lucrative long-term assignments covering extended medical or maternity leaves.

Application Pathways, Background Screenings, and District Hiring

Prospective substitutes in Oklahoma can apply through two primary channels depending on the school district. Larger districts like Oklahoma City Public Schools (OKCPS), Tulsa Public Schools, Edmond Public Schools, and Moore Public Schools often process applications directly through their internal human resources departments. Other suburban and rural districts partner with educational staffing agencies such as Kelly Education or ESS to manage onboarding, payroll, and automated shift dispatching.

Regardless of the application channel, state law strictly mandates thorough background vetting. Under Oklahoma Statutes Title 70 Section 5-142, every applicant must submit biometric digital fingerprints for criminal history reviews conducted by the Oklahoma State Bureau of Investigation (OSBI) and the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI). Applicants must also provide official high school or university transcripts and complete district-mandated training modules on child abuse reporting, bloodborne pathogens, and student privacy under FERPA.

Classroom Success Strategies and Alternative Certification Pathways

Once your background checks clear and you receive your district onboarding approval, you will gain access to automated scheduling platforms like Frontline Education or SmartFind Express. These platforms allow you to browse and accept substitute teaching assignments across selected campuses, grade levels, and subjects. Arriving early, dressing in professional business-casual attire, introducing yourself to adjacent classroom teachers, and following lesson instructions meticulously ensures positive administrative feedback and steady booking requests.

For individuals interested in pursuing education as a lifelong career, substitute teaching provides an ideal stepping stone into full-time teaching in Oklahoma. The Oklahoma State Department of Education offers multiple Alternative Placement teacher certification pathways for individuals holding a bachelor's degree in non-education fields. By demonstrating competence as a reliable substitute teacher, you build valuable relationships with campus principals who can recommend you for alternative certification teaching contracts.

How to Apply for Substitute Teaching in Oklahoma

Follow these clear steps to complete your application, background checks, and orientation to start substitute teaching in Oklahoma.

  1. Confirm Minimum Age and Educational Eligibility

    Ensure you are at least 18 years old and hold a high school diploma, GED, or accredited college transcripts.

  2. Select Your Preferred School District or Staffing Partner

    Apply online through your local school district website or an educational staffing vendor such as Kelly Education.

  3. Complete Fingerprint-Based Criminal Background Checks

    Undergo biometric Livescan fingerprinting for mandatory OSBI and FBI criminal record checks as required by Oklahoma law.

  4. Submit Official Transcripts and Identification

    Provide sealed academic transcripts, valid government-issued photo identification, and Social Security documentation.

  5. Complete District Orientation and Safety Training

    Attend substitute orientation modules covering classroom management, mandatory child abuse reporting, and FERPA regulations.

  6. Activate Your Profile on the Absence Management System

    Set up your availability on Frontline or SmartFind Express and begin accepting daily substitute assignments.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the minimum qualification to substitute teach in Oklahoma?

The baseline state requirement is a high school diploma or GED, though individual districts may prefer candidates with college coursework.

Q2: How much do substitute teachers make per day in Oklahoma?

Pay generally ranges from sixty-five to eighty-five dollars per day for non-certified substitutes, and up to one hundred forty dollars for certified educators.

Q3: Does Oklahoma require a state substitute teaching certificate?

No, Oklahoma does not issue a specific state substitute license; substitute teachers are approved and hired directly by local school districts.

Q4: Are non-certified substitutes limited in how many days they can teach?

Yes, Oklahoma law places limits on consecutive teaching days per semester in the same classroom for non-certified substitutes.

Q5: What background check is required for Oklahoma substitutes?

Applicants must undergo fingerprint-based criminal history screenings through the Oklahoma State Bureau of Investigation (OSBI) and the FBI.

Q6: Can substitute teaching lead to full-time certification in Oklahoma?

Yes, candidates holding a bachelor's degree can utilize substitute teaching experience while pursuing an OSDE Alternative Placement teaching license.

Q7: Can college students substitute teach in Oklahoma?

Yes, college students who are at least 18 years of age and meet district background requirements can substitute teach.

Q8: How do Oklahoma substitute teachers find available jobs?

Most districts use automated digital platforms such as Frontline Education, allowing substitutes to accept assignments via phone or mobile app.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Becoming a substitute teacher in Oklahoma is an accessible, deeply meaningful way to contribute to local communities while enjoying outstanding professional flexibility. By clearing required OSBI and FBI background checks, completing district orientation sessions, and demonstrating patient, enthusiastic leadership in the classroom, you establish yourself as an indispensable educational resource across Oklahoma schools.
