Fort Worth Industrial Facilities and Historical Exposure Sites

Workers and tradesmen across Fort Worth and Tarrant County encountered heavy occupational asbestos exposure throughout the twentieth century across diverse heavy industries. A major local source was the aerospace and defense sector, including General Dynamics (later Lockheed Martin) and Bell Helicopter, where aircraft assemblers, machinists, and mechanics handled asbestos brake assemblies, engine firewalls, and high-temperature electrical wire insulation.

Additionally, major railway repair yards operated by the Texas & Pacific Railway (T&P), Santa Fe, and Burlington Northern in Fort Worth utilized immense volumes of asbestos boiler lagging, brake shoes, and steam pipe packings. Power generation plants operated by Texas Electric Service Company (TESCO), including the Handley Power Plant on Lake Arlington, exposed boilermakers, pipefitters, and electricians to dense clouds of friable amosite and chrysotile insulation.

Texas Asbestos Statutes: Chapter 90 and the Two-Year Discovery Rule

A primary legal question for Fort Worth residents centers on the Texas statute of limitations governing asbestos personal injury claims. Under Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code Section 16.003, personal injury actions must be filed within two years from the date the cause of action accrues. Texas strictly applies the 'discovery rule,' meaning the statutory clock begins on the day a patient receives a formal medical diagnosis.

Furthermore, Texas enacted landmark tort reform under Chapter 90 of the Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code, which established the centralized Texas Asbestos Multidistrict Litigation (MDL) court. Chapter 90 imposes strict medical criteria requiring qualifying pulmonary function testing and board-certified physician reports before a case can proceed. For wrongful death claims, surviving family members have two years from the date of death to file suit.

Third-Party Product Liability vs Workers' Compensation in Fort Worth

Many workers in Fort Worth mistakenly assume they are barred from seeking financial recovery because Texas workers' compensation laws provide immunity to direct employers. However, seasoned asbestos attorneys emphasize that asbestos claims are third-party product liability lawsuits brought against the commercial manufacturers, distributors, and suppliers of defective asbestos products.

For example, an aircraft mechanic who worked at General Dynamics does not sue the aerospace company; instead, legal claims target the third-party manufacturers that supplied the unbonded asbestos brakes, thermal blankets, and electrical insulation without warning labels. Furthermore, Fort Worth residents diagnosed with mesothelioma can file claims with national bankruptcy trusts holding over thirty billion dollars, securing substantial compensation without courtroom trials.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the statute of limitations for an asbestos claim in Fort Worth, Texas?

Under Texas law (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code Section 16.003), you have two years from the date of formal medical diagnosis to file a personal injury claim, or two years from death for wrongful death.

Q2: What is Texas Chapter 90 in asbestos litigation?

Chapter 90 is a Texas statute requiring asbestos plaintiffs to submit a detailed medical report from a qualified physician establishing specific impairment criteria before proceeding to trial.

Q3: Did aerospace workers in Fort Worth face asbestos exposure?

Yes. Aircraft manufacturing at facilities like General Dynamics and Bell Helicopter involved extensive handling of asbestos brake assemblies, engine firewalls, and thermal wire wraps.

Q4: Can I file an asbestos claim if my former employer in Fort Worth closed down?

Yes. Asbestos lawsuits target the third-party manufacturers of the asbestos products, not your former employer, so the status of your employer does not prevent recovery.

Q5: Can family members in Fort Worth file a wrongful death lawsuit?

Yes. Surviving spouses, children, and personal representatives of the estate can file wrongful death actions within two years of a loved one's passing from an asbestos illness.

Q6: Do I have to travel to Houston or Dallas to resolve my claim?

No. Over 95% of asbestos claims settle out of court, and depositions can be conducted at your home in Fort Worth or via secure video conference.

Q7: Will an asbestos settlement reduce my Social Security benefits?

No. Compensatory personal injury settlements are private tort recoveries and do not reduce Social Security retirement or disability benefits.

Q8: How much compensation can a Fort Worth mesothelioma claimant recover?

Total recoveries typically range between $1 million and $2.4 million through combined bankruptcy trust claims and negotiated civil settlements.
