Should I Get a Lawyer After an Airplane Accident?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Yes, you should retain a specialized aviation accident lawyer immediately after an airplane accident. Aviation litigation is one of the most complex, federally regulated fields of law, governed by National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) investigations, international treaties like the Montreal Convention, and multi-billion-dollar aerospace corporate defense teams.

The Unique Complexity of Aviation Disaster Litigation

Surviving an aviation disaster?"whether a commercial airline crash, regional commuter turboprop accident, private general aviation incident, or helicopter collision?"is a catastrophic life event. Survivors frequently suffer severe polytrauma, including third-degree thermal burns from jet fuel combustion, multiple compound spinal fractures, traumatic brain injuries (TBIs), and severe post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). Unlike standard automobile accidents, aircraft collisions involve extreme speeds, catastrophic structural failures, and federal regulatory agencies.

Hiring an experienced aviation attorney is critical because airplane accidents are subject to dual layers of federal and international law. General practice personal injury lawyers rarely possess the aeronautical engineering knowledge or familiarity with Federal Aviation Regulations (FARs) necessary to challenge major aerospace corporations like Boeing, Airbus, or commercial airline carriers. Aviation litigation requires understanding aircraft maintenance manuals, Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) airworthiness directives, air traffic control (ATC) radar transcripts, and cockpit voice recorder (CVR) audio data.

Immediately following any domestic aviation incident, the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) launches an official investigation. The NTSB secures the crash site, impounds the wreckage, downloads the Flight Data Recorder (FDR) black box, and performs metallurgical tests on engine components. Under federal statute (49 U.S.C. 1154), formal NTSB probable-cause reports are technically inadmissible as direct evidence in civil liability trials. An aviation lawyer must conduct an independent parallel investigation using private aeronautical experts to establish legal fault. Identifying Potentially Liable Parties in Airplane Crashes

Aviation crashes are rarely caused by a single isolated mistake; they typically stem from a cascading chain of mechanical, operational, and organizational failures. Identifying every liable entity in the chain of commerce is essential to access full financial compensation for catastrophic medical bills, burn care, and wrongful death.

The commercial airline carrier is held to the highest legal standard of care as a common carrier, making it liable for pilot error, pilot fatigue, failure to avoid extreme weather, or deficient crew training. Beyond the airline, liability frequently extends to the aircraft manufacturer under product liability laws for defective design or manufacturing flaws (such as faulty automated flight control software or rudder malfunctions). Third-party contract maintenance providers, component manufacturers, and even the Federal Aviation Administration (for air traffic controller negligence under the Federal Tort Claims Act) can be joined as co-defendants. The Montreal Convention: International Passenger Protections

If your airplane accident occurred during an international itinerary?"even on a domestic segment of an international ticket?"your legal rights are governed by the Montreal Convention of 1999. The Montreal Convention provides extraordinary consumer protections by establishing a two-tiered liability system that eliminates the requirement to prove airline fault for initial damages.

Under Tier 1 of the treaty, the airline carrier is held strictly liable for passenger bodily injuries or death up to approximately 128,821 Special Drawing Rights (SDRs)?"equivalent to approximately $175,000 USD. The passenger does not need to prove the pilot or airline made a mistake; they need only prove that an accident occurred on board or during embarking/disembarking that caused physical injury. For damages exceeding this threshold (Tier 2), liability is unlimited unless the airline can prove that the incident was entirely caused by a third party or that the airline was 100 percent free of negligence. A specialized aviation attorney is essential to maximize recoveries under these treaty formulas.

How to Take Legal Action After an Airplane Accident

A step-by-step procedural roadmap for passengers and families to secure evidence and file an aviation accident claim.

  1. Undergo Immediate Comprehensive Medical Triage

    Seek immediate medical treatment for physical trauma, inhalation injuries from burning jet fuel, thermal burns, and psychological shock. Document all injuries thoroughly.

  2. Retain a Dedicated Aviation Accident Law Firm

    Retain an attorney with a proven track record in federal aviation litigation and NTSB investigations rather than a general local personal injury lawyer.

  3. Launch an Independent Private Aeronautical Investigation

    Your legal team will retain certified aeronautical engineers, former NTSB investigators, and metallurgical experts to conduct a private parallel investigation alongside federal probes.

  4. Determine Applicable Jurisdiction and International Treaties

    Your attorney will evaluate flight ticket contracts to determine whether claims fall under state common carrier law, the Federal Tort Claims Act, or the international Montreal Convention.

  5. File Formal Lawsuits Against All Liable Aerospace Entities

    File civil complaints in appropriate federal or state courts against the airline, aircraft manufacturer, and maintenance providers before statutory deadlines expire.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Why do I need a specialized lawyer for an airplane accident?

Aviation law involves Federal Aviation Regulations (FARs), NTSB investigations, complex aeronautical engineering, international treaties, and multi-billion dollar aerospace defense teams.

Q2: What is the Montreal Convention in airline accidents?

The Montreal Convention is an international treaty that holds airlines strictly liable for passenger injury or death on international flights up to approximately $175,000, with unlimited damages beyond that unless the airline proves zero fault.

Q3: Can I sue the aircraft manufacturer like Boeing or Airbus?

Yes. If an aircraft crash was caused by a mechanical failure, design defect, or faulty automated flight control system, victims can file strict product liability lawsuits against the manufacturer.

Q4: Can NTSB accident reports be used in a lawsuit?

By federal law (49 U.S.C. 1154), the NTSB's formal probable-cause conclusions cannot be admitted as evidence in civil liability trials. Your attorney must conduct an independent expert investigation.

Q5: Who pays for injuries in a private plane crash?

Private general aviation aircraft carry specialized aircraft hull and liability insurance policies covering pilot negligence, mechanical failure, or hangar maintenance errors.

Q6: Can you sue the FAA for air traffic controller negligence?

Yes, under the Federal Tort Claims Act (FTCA). However, suing the federal government requires filing formal administrative notices within strict statutory deadlines.

Q7: How long do I have to file an airplane accident lawsuit?

For domestic flights, state statutes of limitations range from 1 to 3 years. For international flights under the Montreal Convention, there is a strict, non-extendable 2-year deadline from the date of arrival.

Q8: How much does an aviation accident lawyer cost?

Aviation accident attorneys work on a contingency fee basis, advancing all expert witness and investigation costs, taking a percentage only after securing a settlement or court verdict.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Surviving an airplane accident places you in direct confrontation with massive international airline carriers, aerospace manufacturers, and federal regulatory bodies. Aviation law involves technical complexity and international treaties found nowhere else in the legal world. Retaining a dedicated aviation accident trial lawyer ensures that an independent forensic investigation is launched immediately, your rights under the Montreal Convention are protected, and you receive the full financial compensation necessary for lifetime rehabilitation.
