Barbados Flag Broken Trident Meaning

Meaning
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

The national flag of Barbados—affectionately termed the Broken Trident flag—is one of the most symbolically profound national banners in the Caribbean. Adopted on November 30, 1966, the day Barbados achieved full independence from the United Kingdom, the flag features three equal vertical bands: two outer stripes of deep ultramarine blue flanking a central golden yellow stripe emblazoned with a black broken trident head. Designed by Barbadian art educator Grantley Prescod through an open national competition, the broken trident represents a bold, defiant break from British colonial subjugation and the birth of self-determined sovereignty.

Colonial Etymology and the Broken Shaft Symbolism

To grasp the symbolic magnitude of the broken trident, one must examine Barbados's pre-independence colonial coat of arms. Under British imperial rule dating back to 1627, Barbados was represented on colonial seals by the mythical sea deity Britannia riding across the ocean in a conch-shell chariot, holding an unbroken, intact golden trident. The intact trident symbolized the undisputed maritime dominion of the British Empire over the seas and the island colony.

When designing the new sovereign flag in 1966, Prescod took this colonial weapon of imperial dominance and deliberately severed the staff. By omitting the handle and showing only the trident head with a jaggedly broken base, the symbol proclaimed that Britannia's imperial hold was broken forever. The three pointed prongs of the trident were simultaneously re-imagined to represent the three pillars of democratic governance: a government of the people, by the people, and for the people.

National Identity and Transition to a Parliamentary Republic

The broken trident permeates every aspect of Barbadian civic identity and national pride. It serves as the official watermark on currency notes issued by the Central Bank of Barbados, decorates military and police badges, and features prominently on the national coat of arms flanked by a pelican and a dolphinfish. Barbadians celebrate November 30 as Independence Day, dressing in vibrant ultramarine and gold garments while hoisting the national banner.

The symbol gained renewed international prominence on November 30, 2021, when Barbados officially severed its final constitutional ties with the British monarchy, removing Queen Elizabeth II as head of state and inaugurating its first native President, Dame Sandra Mason. Throughout this historic republican transition, the Broken Trident remained the supreme emblem of a mature, self-governing Caribbean civilization.

Flag protocol in Barbados dictates that the banner must be treated with utmost reverence, flown from sunrise to sunset on official government buildings, and never allowed to touch the ground or water.

How to Display and Respect the Flag of Barbados

Follow these official national flag etiquette guidelines when hoisting or displaying the Barbadian banner.

  1. Step 1

    Ensure the flag is flown with the trident prongs pointing straight upward toward the sky, never inverted or tilted.

  2. Step 2

    Position the flag to its own right (observer's left) when flown alongside state flags or corporate banners.

  3. Step 3

    Display the flag prominently from public buildings on national holidays, especially National Heroes Day and Independence Day.

  4. Step 4

    Fly the flag at half-mast during designated periods of national mourning upon formal proclamation by the Governor-General or President.

  5. Step 5

    Replace frayed, soiled, or faded flags immediately, retiring worn banners through private, dignified ceremonial burning.

  6. Step 6

    Refrain from printing commercial advertisements across the face of the flag or using the banner as temporary drapery.

  7. Step 7

    Maintain the exact chromatic shades of ultramarine blue and golden yellow specified in official government color standards.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the meaning of the broken trident on the Barbados flag?

The broken trident symbolizes Barbados's historic break from British colonial rule and the establishment of independent democratic self-governance in 1966.

Q2: What do the three points of the trident represent?

The three prongs represent the democratic triad: government of the people, by the people, and for the people.

Q3: What do the colors of the Barbados flag stand for?

The ultramarine blue stripes represent the sea and sky, while the central golden yellow stripe represents the island's golden sands and tropical sun.

Q4: Who designed the national flag of Barbados?

The flag was designed by Grantley Prescod, a Barbadian art educator, who won a nationwide design competition prior to independence in 1966.

Q5: Is the intact trident linked to the British Empire?

Yes, colonial Barbados was depicted with Britannia holding an unbroken trident; breaking the shaft symbolized shattering colonial dominance.

Q6: When did Barbados become an independent nation?

Barbados gained independence from the United Kingdom on November 30, 1966.

Q7: Did the flag change when Barbados became a republic in 2021?

No, the Broken Trident flag was proudly preserved as the national flag when Barbados transitioned from a constitutional monarchy to a republic.
