Asbestos Products List

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

The historical asbestos products list encompasses more than three thousand distinct commercial, industrial, and consumer products manufactured throughout the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. Revered by manufacturers as the 'miracle mineral' for its extraordinary tensile strength, chemical resistance, acoustic deadening, and fireproofing properties, asbestos was integrated into every sector of modern infrastructure. Today, identifying legacy asbestos products across residential buildings, industrial facilities, and naval vessels is vital for property owners, remodeling contractors, and environmental inspectors working to prevent catastrophic toxic exposures.

Thermal System Insulation (TSI) and Boiler Materials

Thermal System Insulation represents the most hazardous and friable category on the asbestos products list. Widely installed between 1880 and the late 1970s across steam locomotives, power plants, oil refineries, and residential basements with gravity boiler heating, TSI products contained exceptionally high concentrations of chrysotile and amosite asbestos—frequently ranging from fifty to ninety percent by weight.

The most prevalent TSI product was corrugated asbestos paper pipe wrap, commercially known as 'aircell' insulation. Manufactured to resemble layered, corrugated cardboard wrapped concentrically around steam and hot water lines, aircell was sealed at elbows and joints with wet-mixed asbestos insulating mud or plaster. Other widespread thermal products included rigid 85% Magnesia blocks (a blend of magnesium carbonate and amosite fibers) used to jacket high-pressure boilers, and pre-formed amosite block insulation (such as Unibestos) utilized in high-temperature industrial turbines.

Surfacing, Construction Plaster, and Drywall Finishing Products

Surfacing materials represent another extensive, highly friable category on the asbestos products list. In commercial high-rises and public schools built between 1945 and 1973, spray-applied structural fireproofing was applied directly to structural steel beams, corrugated steel floor decking, and concrete ceilings. Formulated with up to thirty percent loose asbestos fibers mixed with gypsum and mineral wool binders, these coatings degrade over time, flaking and contaminating suspended ceiling return-air plenums.

In residential construction, drywall joint compounds, spackling pastes, and acoustic ceiling textures represent the most common exposure sources. Major manufacturers such as United States Gypsum, National Gypsum, and Georgia-Pacific formulated ready-mixed and dry powder joint compounds with five to fifteen percent chrysotile asbestos to prevent shrinkage and cracking. When drywall tapers, painters, and homeowners sanded these compounds flush, massive clouds of respirable asbestos dust filled interior rooms, creating chronic inhalation hazards.

Flooring, Roofing, Transite Cement, and Friction Materials

The asbestos products list extends deeply into non-friable construction materials, where fibers were tightly bound into polymer, asphalt, or cement matrices. The most widely encountered example is Vinyl Asbestos Tile (VAT), manufactured primarily in 9-inch by 9-inch (9x9) squares between 1920 and 1980 by companies like Armstrong, Kentile, and Flintkote. The tiles contained ten to twenty-five percent chrysotile, while the black cutback asphalt adhesive used to glue them down contained five to fifteen percent asbestos.

Other durable products included transite asbestos-cement panels, shingles, and municipal water pipes, which combined Portland cement with twenty to forty percent chrysotile or crocidolite asbestos to create weather-proof, rot-proof conduit. In transportation and industrial machinery, friction products—including automotive brake shoes, heavy truck clutch facings, elevator brake bands, and industrial crane friction discs—utilized compressed chrysotile to withstand extreme kinetic friction and heat.

How to Identify Items on the Asbestos Products List

Follow these five professional steps to identify and safely assess items on the historical asbestos products list.

  1. Check the Property Age and Construction History

    Determine if the home, building, or vessel was constructed or renovated prior to 1981, when asbestos use peaked.

  2. Conduct a Visual Survey for Hallmark Materials

    Look for 9x9 floor tiles, corrugated cardboard pipe wrap, acoustic popcorn ceilings, and transite cement panels.

  3. Do Not Disturb, Cut, or Sand Suspect Products

    Leave materials intact; non-friable products like intact floor tiles pose minimal risk if left completely undisturbed.

  4. Commission Certified Bulk Sampling by an Inspector

    Hire an EPA-certified asbestos building inspector to collect representative bulk samples under wet protocols.

  5. Review Certified NVLAP Laboratory Analytical Reports

    Verify asbestos mineral type and percentage using Polarized Light Microscopy (PLM) reports from an accredited lab.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How many products were made with asbestos historically?

Over three thousand different commercial, industrial, and domestic products were manufactured using asbestos throughout the twentieth century.

Q2: What are the most common asbestos products found in homes?

The most common residential products are 9x9 vinyl floor tiles, acoustic popcorn ceilings, drywall joint compound, and corrugated pipe wrap.

Q3: Are 9x9 floor tiles always asbestos?

Nearly all 9-inch by 9-inch resilient floor tiles manufactured between 1920 and 1980 contain ten to twenty-five percent chrysotile asbestos.

Q4: What is transite asbestos?

Transite is a brand name for a rigid, weather-resistant building material made of Portland cement reinforced with twenty to forty percent asbestos fibers.

Q5: Did automotive brakes contain asbestos?

Yes, automobile and truck brake shoes, pads, and clutch facings contained up to sixty percent chrysotile asbestos until late-1990s phase-outs.

Q6: Is popcorn ceiling texture always asbestos-containing?

Popcorn ceiling textures installed before 1985 frequently contain between five and twenty-five percent chrysotile asbestos.

Q7: How can you tell if a product has asbestos just by looking at it?

You cannot definitively identify asbestos by visual inspection alone; certified laboratory analysis using Polarized Light Microscopy is the only reliable method.

Q8: Are products on the asbestos list dangerous if they are not damaged?

Undisturbed, undamaged asbestos products—especially non-friable materials like floor tiles—are safe; hazards arise only when materials are cut, sanded, or broken.
