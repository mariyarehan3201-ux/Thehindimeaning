How to Form a PA in Florida?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 30, 2026

Forming a Professional Association (P.A.) in Florida offers licensed professionals a robust corporate structure that shields personal assets from commercial liabilities, streamlines business taxes, and enhances institutional credibility. Governed by Florida Statutes Chapter 621, known as The Professional Service Corporation and Limited Liability Company Act, this legal entity is tailored specifically for practitioners such as physicians, attorneys, accountants, dentists, and architects. Navigating the formation process through the Florida Department of State Division of Corporations (Sunbiz) requires strict adherence to statutory naming rules, shareholder licensing requirements, and corporate governance mandates.

Statutory Foundations of a Florida Professional Association (Chapter 621)

Licensed professionals operating private practices face unique commercial and regulatory challenges. In standard business ventures, entrepreneurs can form standard commercial corporations or general limited liability companies without professional restrictions. However, licensed practitioners—including physicians, lawyers, certified public accountants, dentists, and engineers—are subject to statutory professional codes that hold them personally accountable for their clinical and ethical duties. To accommodate these specialized professions while providing modern corporate protections, the Florida Legislature enacted Florida Statutes Chapter 621, entitled The Professional Service Corporation and Limited Liability Company Act.

Under Florida Statute § 621.03, a professional service is defined as any type of personal service to the public that requires as a condition precedent to rendering such service the obtaining of a license or other legal authorization. Eligible professionals include physicians licensed under Chapter 458 or 459, attorneys admitted to The Florida Bar, certified public accountants licensed under Chapter 473, chiropractic physicians, podiatrists, dentists, veterinarians, architects, and professional engineers. Forming a Professional Association allows these practitioners to conduct business under a recognized corporate umbrella, open corporate commercial banking accounts, enter into commercial real estate leases, and employ clinical and administrative staff.

The primary benefit of a Florida P.A. is the commercial liability shield it provides to its shareholders. In an unincorporated sole proprietorship or general partnership, practitioners are personally liable for all practice obligations, including office lease defaults, business loan breaches, vendor payment failures, and slip-and-fall premises accidents. Under Chapter 621, the P.A. acts as an independent legal person, insulating the personal wealth, homes, and private bank accounts of shareholders from ordinary commercial debts. However, practitioners must understand that forming a P.A. does not shield an individual practitioner from personal liability for their own medical malpractice, legal malpractice, or professional misconduct. Each professional remains personally answerable for their direct negligence and the acts of subordinates under their direct supervision.

Furthermore, Florida law imposes strict ownership rules on Professional Associations. Under Florida Statute § 621.09, no corporation organized under Chapter 621 may issue capital stock to anyone other than an individual who is duly licensed or legally authorized in Florida to render the same specific professional service. Non-licensed investors, private equity groups, or family members cannot hold shares or serve as corporate voting officers. Practitioners frequently evaluate the P.A. framework alongside strategies for converting an existing practice into a corporate entity and obtaining mandatory state business entity registration.

Choosing between a Professional Association (P.A.), a Professional Limited Liability Company (PLLC), and a standard commercial entity requires evaluating personal liability, ownership rules, and tax efficiency. Step-by-Step Filing Procedures with Sunbiz and Ongoing Compliance

Forming a Professional Association in Florida requires navigating the official filing processes of the Florida Department of State Division of Corporations, commonly accessed via the online Sunbiz portal. The initial legal document required is the Articles of Incorporation for a Professional Association. This document must state the precise legal name of the entity, the specific professional service to be rendered, the street address of the principal office, the registered agent details, and the number of shares the corporation is authorized to issue.

Selecting a compliant entity name requires adherence to Florida Statute § 621.12. The corporate name must contain the word "chartered", the phrase "professional association", or the abbreviation "P.A." It is strictly unlawful to include words such as "Company", "Incorporated", "Corporation", or their abbreviations ("Co.", "Inc.", "Corp."), as these terms imply a general commercial business rather than a specialized professional entity. Furthermore, the name must be distinguishable from all other active corporate names on record with the Division of Corporations.

Every Florida P.A. must designate a Registered Agent with a physical street address within the state of Florida (P.O. boxes are prohibited). The registered agent is the individual or authorized corporate service designated to receive service of process, official legal notices, and state correspondence on behalf of the corporation. The registered agent must formally execute a statutory acceptance statement agreeing to comply with Florida law. The state filing fee for the Articles of Incorporation is thirty-five dollars, combined with a mandatory thirty-five dollar fee for designating the Registered Agent, resulting in a baseline state filing fee of seventy dollars. Organizers can optionally purchase a certified copy for eight dollars and seventy-five cents or a Certificate of Status for eight dollars and seventy-five cents.

Once Sunbiz issues the corporate charter and document number, the organizers must execute organizational corporate governance steps. These include drafting corporate bylaws, issuing stock certificates to licensed shareholders, electing a Board of Directors, and applying for an Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the Internal Revenue Service. By default, a P.A. is classified by the IRS as a C-Corporation, which may subject practice income to corporate tax rates. Most solo practitioners and small professional groups file IRS Form 2553 within seventy-five days of incorporation to elect S-Corporation tax treatment, allowing practice earnings to pass through to personal tax returns and optimizing self-employment taxes. Structuring agreements in alignment with principles used by medical practice legal counsel ensures both corporate integrity and professional board compliance.

How to Incorporate a Professional Association (P.A.) in Florida

A comprehensive procedural roadmap for licensed professionals establishing a Florida Professional Association through the Division of Corporations.

  1. Verify Professional Licensure and Shareholder Eligibility under Chapter 621

    Confirm that all intended shareholders, directors, and officers possess active, unencumbered Florida professional licenses in the exact discipline for which the association is formed.

  2. Choose a Distinguishable Legal Entity Name Ending in P.A.

    Select a unique corporate name incorporating the words chartered, professional association, or the abbreviation P.A., ensuring it is distinguishable on the Sunbiz corporate records.

  3. Designate a Qualified Florida Registered Agent with a Physical Street Address

    Appoint a Florida resident or registered corporate service with a physical street address in Florida to accept legal service of process and execute the statutory acceptance statement.

  4. Draft and File Articles of Incorporation Electronically on Sunbiz

    Complete the online incorporation form on the Sunbiz portal, pay the seventy-dollar filing and registered agent fee, and download your official corporate filing acknowledgment.

  5. Acquire an Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the Internal Revenue Service

    Apply online through the IRS portal to obtain your corporate FEIN, which is mandatory for opening commercial bank accounts, paying staff, and obtaining vendor accounts.

  6. Adopt Formal Corporate Bylaws, Hold Organizational Meeting, and File Form 2553

    Draft corporate bylaws governing practice operations, issue stock certificates to licensed shareholders, and file IRS Form 2553 within seventy-five days if electing S-Corporation tax status.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is a PA in Florida business entity law?

In Florida, a P.A. stands for Professional Association, a specialized corporate entity established under Florida Statutes Chapter 621 for licensed professionals such as doctors, attorneys, and CPAs.

Q2: Can anyone form a Professional Association in Florida?

No, under Florida Statute Chapter 621, only individuals who hold active, valid professional licenses issued by the state of Florida in the specific field of practice are legally permitted to own shares in a P.A.

Q3: Does a Florida PA protect me from malpractice lawsuits?

No, a P.A. protects shareholders from commercial business debts, vendor breach claims, and leases, but practitioners remain personally liable for their own professional malpractice and negligence.

Q4: What is the difference between a PA and a PLLC in Florida?

A P.A. is governed by corporate law with a board of directors, officers, and corporate bylaws, while a PLLC is governed by limited liability company law with members and an operating agreement.

Q5: How much does it cost to incorporate a PA on Sunbiz in Florida?

The total baseline filing fee is seventy dollars, consisting of a thirty-five dollar Articles of Incorporation fee and a mandatory thirty-five dollar Registered Agent designation fee.

Q6: What naming rules apply to a Florida Professional Association?

The name must contain the word chartered, the phrase professional association, or the abbreviation P.A., and cannot contain words like Company, Incorporated, or Corp.

Q7: When is the Florida Annual Report due for a PA?

The Florida Annual Report must be filed online through Sunbiz between January 1 and May 1 each calendar year, accompanied by a one hundred fifty dollar state filing fee.

Q8: How is a Florida Professional Association taxed by the IRS?

By default, a P.A. is taxed as a C-Corporation, but most licensed practitioners elect S-Corporation status by filing IRS Form 2553 to pass income through to their individual tax returns.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Forming a Professional Association in Florida is a strategic move that provides licensed practitioners with essential commercial liability insulation, enhanced tax planning mechanisms, and an established framework for multi-practitioner partnerships. By segregating business debt liabilities from personal family assets, a P.A. allows physicians, attorneys, and accountants to build thriving practices without risking personal solvency over commercial contracts. To ensure long-term operational and regulatory compliance, practitioners must remain vigilant regarding statutory mandates. Filing the mandatory Sunbiz annual report on time, maintaining active professional board licenses, upholding corporate formalities, and coordinating with experienced legal and tax counsel will ensure that your Florida P.A. continues to protect your practice for years to come.
