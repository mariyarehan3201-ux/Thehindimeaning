Does Misdemeanor Warrant Show on Background Check?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 12, 2026

Facing an unresolved misdemeanor charge or missing a routine court hearing can lead a judge to issue an arrest warrant or bench warrant. Individuals entering the job market or applying for housing frequently wonder whether a misdemeanor warrant—given its minor statutory grading compared to a felony—will appear on a background check. The answer is unequivocally yes: misdemeanor warrants are active judicial orders recorded in public court dockets that routinely surface on criminal background checks.

Public Court Dockets and Misdemeanor Warrant Indexing

When a municipal or county judge issues a warrant for a misdemeanor offense, the order is entered into the court's public case management database. Common causes for misdemeanor warrants include missing a court date for a traffic citation (Failure to Appear, or FTA), failing to pay mandatory court assessments, violating probationary conditions, or facing new misdemeanor charges such as petty theft or trespass.

Professional Consumer Reporting Agencies (CRAs) specialize in extracting these records during employment screening. When conducting county-level criminal record searches, screening investigators examine the active docket status of every case associated with the candidate's name and date of birth. An active warrant flags the case as an open, unresolved criminal matter, immediately alerting the background screening vendor.

County Searches, Statewide Repositories, and the NCIC

The likelihood of a misdemeanor warrant appearing depends heavily on the geographic scope of the background check. The vast majority of corporate employers conduct county criminal searches covering every jurisdiction where the applicant has resided over the past seven to ten years. If the misdemeanor warrant was issued in a county where you lived, it will almost certainly be discovered.

However, misdemeanor warrants differ from felony warrants when it comes to national law enforcement databases like the FBI's National Crime Information Center (NCIC). Because county sheriffs face budgetary limits on out-of-state extraditions, many jurisdictions do not enter non-violent misdemeanor warrants into national multi-state databases, designating them as 'in-state or county-only extradition.' Consequently, an out-of-state database check may miss a local misdemeanor warrant, whereas a local county courthouse search will uncover it immediately.

The Devastating Impact on Job Offers and How to Quash It

Having an active misdemeanor warrant appear on an employment background check is almost universally disqualifying. Employers view active warrants as severe red flags: it indicates a disregard for legal obligations and introduces the immediate risk that the employee could be arrested at work during business hours. Under federal Fair Credit Reporting Act guidelines, the employer will issue a Pre-Adverse Action notice, giving the candidate five business days to dispute or resolve the finding.

The only effective solution is to resolve the warrant before submitting to screening. In most misdemeanor bench warrant scenarios, retaining a local criminal defense attorney allows you to file a Motion to Quash the Warrant. In many jurisdictions, the attorney can appear on your behalf to recall the warrant and schedule a new court date without you ever spending a night in jail, converting an active warrant into a pending court case.

How to Clear a Misdemeanor Warrant in 5 Steps

Follow these five disciplined steps to discover, quash, and resolve a misdemeanor warrant safely.

  1. Confirm the Warrant Discretely Online

    Check your local municipal or county court website docket search to confirm the case number and warrant status without calling police.

  2. Retain a Local Defense Attorney

    Hire a criminal lawyer who practices in that municipal court; attorneys can often petition to recall warrants without client incarceration.

  3. File a Motion to Quash and Recall the Warrant

    Have your counsel file an emergency motion explaining the missed court date (such as illness or address change) and requesting a new hearing.

  4. Appear in Court and Satisfy Bench Fees

    Attend the recalled hearing with your lawyer, pay any administrative warrant fees or bond, and re-establish your active defense.

  5. Obtain a Certified Warrant Clearance Certificate

    Request a certified docket sheet showing the warrant is quashed from the court clerk to keep as proof for background screeners.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Will a misdemeanor warrant show up on a pre-employment background check?

Yes, active misdemeanor warrants are public court records that appear on county and state criminal background searches.

Q2: Can you be hired with an active misdemeanor warrant?

Virtually no employer will hire a candidate with an active arrest warrant due to the ongoing legal liability and threat of sudden arrest.

Q3: Will police show up at my job interview if I have a warrant?

Private employers do not call the police for interviewees, but government or security clearance checks can trigger law enforcement alerts.

Q4: Do misdemeanor warrants expire after a certain number of years?

No, arrest warrants and bench warrants never expire; they remain active in court computers until resolved by a judge or through an arrest.

Q5: What is the difference between a bench warrant and an arrest warrant?

A bench warrant is issued by a judge for failing to appear or disobeying court orders, while an arrest warrant is requested by police for suspected criminal conduct.

Q6: Can an attorney quash a misdemeanor warrant without me going to jail?

Yes, in many jurisdictions, defense attorneys can file a motion to quash and recall the warrant without the client facing jail time.

Q7: Does an unpaid speeding ticket create a misdemeanor warrant?

Yes, ignoring a speeding citation leads to a charge of Failure to Appear (FTA), which causes a judge to issue an active bench warrant.

Q8: Will a misdemeanor warrant from another state show up?

It will show up if the employer conducts nationwide database checks or searches the specific out-of-state county where you previously resided.
