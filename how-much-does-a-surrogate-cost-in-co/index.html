How Much Does a Surrogate Cost in CO?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a surrogate cost in CO requires examining gestational surrogate compensation, IVF clinical embryo transfer fees, surrogacy agency coordination, and legal parentage contracts under Colorado's progressive surrogacy statutes. On average, a gestational surrogacy journey in Colorado costs between $115,000 and $190,000. Gestational carrier base compensation typically ranges from $45,000 to $65,000, with medical, agency, legal, and escrow expenses comprising the remainder.

Colorado Surrogacy Laws: The Colorado Surrogacy Agreement Act

Colorado is widely recognized as one of the most surrogacy-friendly states in the nation, governed by the Colorado Surrogacy Agreement Act (C.R.S. § 19-4.5-101 et seq.) enacted in 2021. The statute establishes a clear legal framework protecting intended parents (regardless of marital status, gender, or sexual orientation) and gestational carriers. It explicitly authorizes pre-birth parentage orders, ensuring intended parents are placed directly on the original birth certificate without requiring post-birth adoptions.

Because Colorado's legal environment is predictable and progressive, intended parents flock to Denver, Boulder, and Colorado Springs. The comprehensive process involves four primary cost categories: surrogate base compensation and benefits ($55,000 to $75,000), surrogacy agency matching and screening fees ($25,000 to $40,000), IVF clinical embryo transfer procedures ($15,000 to $35,000), and specialized legal/escrow management ($10,000 to $18,000).

Surrogate Compensation and Monthly Stipends in Colorado

A first-time gestational carrier residing in Colorado typically receives a base compensation of $45,000 to $60,000. Experienced surrogates who have successfully carried healthy surrogacy pregnancies previously command $60,000 to $75,000+. Base compensation is paid out in 10 equal monthly disbursements starting upon confirmation of a fetal heartbeat via ultrasound (typically around 6 to 8 weeks gestation).

Beyond base pay, the gestational carrier contract includes a structured schedule of allowances and contingent payments: a $250 to $350 monthly out-of-pocket miscellaneous allowance, a $1,000 to $1,500 maternity clothing allowance, a $500 embryo transfer fee, invasive procedure fees (such as amniocentesis, $500), and a cesarean section delivery fee ($2,500 to $4,000). If the surrogate is put on physician-ordered bed rest, the intended parents cover documented lost wages and childcare services.

Medical Insurance, Escrow, and Pre-Birth Parentage Orders

Securing health insurance for the surrogate's pregnancy is one of the most critical risk management tasks. Many standard employer health insurance policies contain explicit 'surrogacy exclusions' that deny coverage for maternity care when acting as a gestational carrier. If excluded, intended parents must purchase a surrogate-specific policy or an Affordable Care Act (ACA) compliant policy during open enrollment, budgeting $12,000 to $25,000 for premiums, high deductibles, and co-pays.

All funds must be deposited into an independent, bonded, insured escrow management account (like SeedTrust or Trellis) prior to medical screening. Legal contracts are formalized by specialized Colorado fertility lawyers. Under the Colorado Surrogacy Agreement Act, attorneys file a Petition for Pre-Birth Order in District Court around the 20th week of pregnancy, directing the hospital and Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE) to name the intended parents directly on the birth certificate.

How to Plan and Budget a Surrogacy Journey in Colorado

Follow these five strategic steps to navigate a legal, financially secure surrogacy journey in Colorado.

  1. Consult with a Colorado Surrogacy Agency or Fertility Attorney

    Select an established surrogacy agency or reproductive attorney familiar with the Colorado Surrogacy Agreement Act.

  2. Complete an In-Depth Health Insurance Policy Review

    Hire an independent insurance broker (like Art Risk) to audit your prospective surrogate's health plan for surrogacy exclusions.

  3. Fund a Bonded, Third-Party Escrow Account

    Deposit the required initial escrow reserve ($80k to $120k) before initiating medical medications or clinic visits.

  4. Execute the Gestational Surrogacy Agreement (GSA)

    Draft comprehensive legal contracts with separate independent legal counsel representing both the intended parents and the carrier.

  5. Obtain a Certified Pre-Birth Parentage Order

    Have your attorney petition the Colorado District Court around month five to secure a pre-birth order establishing full legal parentage.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a surrogate cost in Colorado on average?

The total cost of a surrogacy journey in Colorado typically ranges between $115,000 and $190,000 all-inclusive.

Q2: How much do surrogates get paid in Colorado?

First-time gestational surrogates in Colorado earn a base compensation of $45,000 to $60,000, while experienced surrogates earn $60,000 to $75,000+.

Q3: Is Colorado a surrogacy-friendly state?

Yes, Colorado is exceptionally surrogacy-friendly under the Colorado Surrogacy Agreement Act, which legalizes contracts and guarantees pre-birth parentage orders.

Q4: Can LGBTQ+ couples use a surrogate in Colorado?

Yes, Colorado law provides equal, non-discriminatory surrogacy rights to LGBTQ+ couples, single intended parents, and heterosexual couples alike.

Q5: What is a pre-birth order in Colorado?

A court order issued by a Colorado District Court judge prior to delivery that names the intended parents on the birth certificate immediately at birth.

Q6: Does health insurance cover surrogacy in Colorado?

Some private plans cover maternity care, but many have surrogacy exclusions requiring the purchase of a specialized ACA policy ($12k to $25k).

Q7: How long does the surrogacy process take in Colorado?

From agency matching through IVF embryo transfer to delivery, a typical surrogacy journey requires 14 to 22 months.

Q8: What is the difference between gestational and traditional surrogacy?

In gestational surrogacy (standard), the surrogate has no biological link to the baby; in traditional surrogacy, her own egg is used (rare and legally complex).
