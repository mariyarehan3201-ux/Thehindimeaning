How Much Is It to Rhino Line a Truck Bed?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Protecting your pickup truck cargo bed from abrasive gravel, shifting heavy cargo, impact dents, and moisture corrosion is essential for maintaining both utility and vehicle resale value. On average, getting a professional spray-on Rhino Linings truck bed application costs between $450 and $850 for a standard 5.5-foot or 6.5-foot truck bed, and between $650 and $1,100 for an 8-foot long bed or heavy-duty dually pickup. Comparing Rhino formulation chemistries, over-the-rail coverage options, surface prep standards, and warranty coverage ensures your truck bed receives bulletproof protection.

Rhino Linings Formulations, Elastomeric Thickness, and Texture

Rhino Linings is a pioneered brand name in spray-on elastomeric polyurethane and polyurea protective coatings. Unlike brittle, hard plastic drop-in liners that vibrate and scuff underlying paint, a professional Rhino Linings coating is applied as a heated, two-component liquid chemical elastomer that chemically bonds directly to the prepared steel substrate. Once sprayed at high pressure, it cures into an impenetrable, seamless, watertight membrane measuring roughly 125 to 250 mils (1/8 to 1/4 inch) in thickness.

Rhino offers several distinct chemical formulations tailored to specific user priorities. Their classic heritage product, Rhino HardLine, is a 100% polyurethane formulation with a soft, rubbery, textured finish that provides unmatched impact absorption and prevents cargo tools and equipment from sliding around in the bed. For heavy-duty commercial haulers, Rhino Hybrid and Rhino Extreme incorporate tough aromatic polyurea resins that deliver superior tensile strength, chemical resistance against fuels and solvents, and increased tear resistance for $75 to $150 more.

Application Styles: Under-the-Rail vs. Over-the-Rail and Surface Prep

When booking a Rhino Linings installation, you must specify whether you desire under-the-rail or over-the-rail application coverage. An under-the-rail spray stops just beneath the top metal lip of the truck bed sides, which is the preferred choice for trucks equipped with plastic factory rail caps, tonneau bed covers, or commercial camper shells. An over-the-rail spray carries the thick polyurea elastomer over the top bed rails and tailgate edge, providing maximum protection against loading scuffs for an additional $75 to $150 in masking labor and material.

The true longevity of any spray-in bedliner is fundamentally decided during surface preparation rather than spraying. A certified Rhino Linings technician spends 75 percent of the job masking bodywork with specialized wire-trim tape, de-greasing panels, removing tie-down hooks, and rigorously abrading the factory clearcoat and paint using heavy 36-grit sanding discs or media blasting to create a mechanical anchor profile. If a budget shop cuts corners on scuffing, the liner will inevitably blister, bubble, and peel away in large sheets.

Rhino Linings vs. Line-X and DIY Roll-On Alternatives

Truck owners frequently debate between Rhino Linings and Line-X. While both offer exceptional lifetime warranties at certified dealer networks, their physical characteristics differ noticeably. Traditional Rhino Linings is thicker, more pliable, and rubbery, providing superior cushioning for dropped heavy tools and exceptional slip resistance that keeps cargo stationary. Line-X is a harder, higher-pressure polyurea with a rougher sandpaper-like texture that resists gouging but can be abrasive on kneecaps and cargo boxes.

Do-it-yourself roll-on or spray-can bedliner kits (such as Herculiner, Rust-Oleum, or Raptor Liner) cost between $80 and $180 for retail kits, tempting frugal DIY mechanics. However, retail DIY liners apply at merely 20 to 40 mils of dry film thickness—less than one-quarter the thickness of a professional Rhino spray. DIY coatings lack the heated high-pressure curing chemistry of commercial rigs, often resulting in fading, chipping under shovel blades, and premature wear within two to three years of hard work.

How to Get Your Truck Bed Rhino Lined in 5 Steps

Follow these five strategic steps to prepare your truck, select an authorized installer, and ensure a permanent bond.

  1. Clean and Empty the Truck Bed

    Wash out all dirt, grease, and gravel from the truck bed, and remove all loose toolboxes, bed mats, and aftermarket accessories.

  2. Locate an Authorized Rhino Linings Applicator

    Use the official Rhino Linings dealer locator to find an authorized regional shop with certified high-pressure spray equipment.

  3. Choose Rail Coverage and UV Protection Options

    Specify under-the-rail or over-the-rail coverage, and decide whether your truck requires UV-stable topcoats to resist sun fading.

  4. Allow 3 to 5 Hours for Preparation and Spraying

    Leave your truck at the facility while technicians mask panels, sand the paint, apply chemical etching primers, and spray the hot coating.

  5. Adhere to the Post-Spray Curing Timeline

    While the coating dries to the touch in minutes, wait at least 24 hours for light duty and 72 hours before hauling heavy cargo or gravel.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does Rhino Linings cost on average?

A professional spray-on Rhino Linings application typically costs between $450 and $850 for a standard truck bed.

Q2: What is the difference between Rhino Linings and Line-X?

Rhino Linings is thicker and more rubbery, offering superior impact cushioning and grip, whereas Line-X is harder and sandpaper-like.

Q3: How long does a Rhino Linings spray-on bedliner last?

When properly applied by a certified technician, Rhino Linings lasts the lifetime of your truck and is backed by a nationwide limited lifetime warranty.

Q4: Can Rhino Linings fade in the sun?

Standard aromatic polyurethane coatings can oxidize and lose gloss over years of direct sunlight; opting for UV-stable SolarMax prevents fading.

Q5: Can you spray Rhino Linings over existing rust?

No, all surface rust must be ground away and treated with rust-inhibiting primer before application to ensure permanent chemical adhesion.

Q6: How thick is a professional Rhino Liner?

A professional Rhino Linings coating is applied to a heavy thickness of 125 to 250 mils (one-eighth to one-quarter inch).

Q7: How long after spraying can you use your truck bed?

The liner cures to the touch within an hour, but you should wait 24 hours for light cargo and 72 hours for heavy, sharp, or abrasive materials.

Q8: Is a spray-on bedliner better than a plastic drop-in liner?

Yes, spray-on liners bond directly to the metal, eliminating the vibration, trapped moisture, and hidden rust caused by plastic drop-in liners.
