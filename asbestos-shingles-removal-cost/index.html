Asbestos Shingles Removal Cost

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

The asbestos shingles removal cost typically ranges between $7 and $15 per square foot, with total project expenses averaging between $7,000 and $16,000 for a standard residential home. Pricing depends on whether the project involves exterior wall siding or steep-slope roofing, total square footage, scaffolding and fall protection requirements, subfloor or sheathing repairs, and regional hazardous waste disposal fees.

Cost Breakdown by Application: Exterior Siding vs. Roof Shingles

When estimating the expense of abating vintage asbestos shingles, homeowners must differentiate between exterior wall siding and roof assemblies. While both products consist of pressed Portland cement reinforced with fifteen to twenty-five percent chrysotile fibers (transite), the physical logistics and safety equipment required vary dramatically.

Siding removal is generally less costly per square foot ($7 to $12) because crews can work efficiently from ground level or basic rolling scaffolding. Conversely, roof shingle removal commands higher rates ($9 to $16 per square foot) due to the necessity of full-body fall arrest systems, crawling boards to prevent deck collapse, and crane hoists to lower brittle panels safely without breakage.

Itemized Invoice Components and Cost Variables

A professional asbestos abatement contract reflects multiple distinct operational phases beyond physical demolition. Understanding these invoice components helps homeowners evaluate competing contractor bids and avoid unexpected change orders.

Key cost factors include the complexity of ground containment (laying double layers of 6-mil polyethylene plastic extending ten feet from building walls to catch falling debris), surfactant wet-misting setups, manual nail extraction labor, and hazardous landfill tipping fees.

Invoice Expense Component Average Share of Total Cost Operational Scope Included Cost Reduction Opportunity
Ground Containment & Scaffolding 20% to 25% of bid 6-mil poly drop sheets, OSHA staging scaffolding Trim bushes and clear perimeter access paths beforehand
Certified Hazmat Labor 45% to 55% of bid Surfactant misting, manual unfastening, P100 PPE Bundle roofing and siding into a single abatement phase
Hazardous Waste Hauling & Landfill 15% to 20% of bid Palletizing, double-wrap plastic, EPA manifests Fixed landfill tipping fees (cannot be reduced)
Air Clearance & Permitting 5% to 10% of bid Municipal NESHAP permits, third-party air test Verify permit requirements with local building board

For budget-conscious homeowners, encapsulation is an approved, safe, and highly cost-effective alternative. Installing 3/8-inch contoured fanfold foam board directly over intact asbestos siding shingles and hanging modern vinyl cladding costs between $5 and $9 per square foot, eliminating abatement costs entirely while adding insulation.

However, if shingles are severely cracked, crumbling, or weather-delaminated, complete removal is necessary to eliminate ongoing health hazards and allow durable exterior repairs.

How to Estimate and Plan Asbestos Shingle Removal

Step-by-step guidance for calculating costs and commissioning asbestos shingle abatement.

  1. Measure Total Exterior Square Footage

    Calculate the exact square footage of exterior walls or roof slopes, subtracting window and door openings to establish accurate material quantities.

  2. Verify Asbestos Content via Lab Analysis

    Collect a small broken corner fragment while wearing a P100 respirator, misting with water, and submit it to an NVLAP lab for PLM analysis.

  3. Compare Encapsulation vs. Full Abatement

    Evaluate whether siding over intact shingles with fanfold foam and vinyl ($6,000–$10,000) is viable compared to total removal ($12,000–$22,000).

  4. Solicit Three Itemized Contractor Bids

    Request detailed written proposals from licensed abatement firms detailing ground plastic staging, manual unfastening, and waste disposal.

  5. Collect Waste Disposal Manifests

    Ensure the contractor provides a signed copy of the hazardous waste landfill manifest upon project completion before paying final balances.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to remove asbestos shingles from a house?

Removing asbestos siding from an average-sized home typically costs between $8,000 and $15,000, depending on home height and square footage.

Q2: Is it cheaper to cover asbestos shingles or remove them?

Covering intact shingles with fanfold foam and vinyl siding is significantly cheaper, saving $6,000 to $12,000 in hazardous removal costs.

Q3: Why is asbestos roof shingle removal more expensive than siding?

Roofing requires fall arrest lifelines, crawling boards to prevent roof deck collapse, and crane hoists to lower brittle panels safely.

Q4: Can I remove asbestos siding shingles myself?

While some states allow homeowners to do DIY work on non-friable siding, power sawing or dropping shingles from height is dangerous and illegal.

Q5: Does homeowners insurance cover asbestos shingle removal?

Standard policies exclude routine abatement, but may cover it if shingles were damaged by a covered peril like a severe hailstorm or fallen tree.

Q6: Where do contractors dispose of asbestos shingles?

Shingles wrapped in 6-mil plastic are transported under hazardous waste manifests to state-licensed landfills permitted to receive asbestos.

Q7: How long does professional asbestos siding removal take?

Most single-family residential siding projects require three to five days for ground containment, manual unfastening, and cleanup.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The asbestos shingles removal cost depends on elevation, total square footage, and whether roofing or siding is being abated. While professional removal requires an investment of $7,000 to $16,000, certified contractors ensure non-destructive manual extraction and compliant landfill disposal. If shingles are structurally sound, safe vinyl siding encapsulation provides an approved alternative that saves thousands of dollars.
