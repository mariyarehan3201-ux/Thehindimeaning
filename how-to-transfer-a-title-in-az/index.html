How to Transfer a Title in AZ?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Transferring a motor vehicle certificate of title in Arizona is a mandatory legal process required whenever a car, truck, motorcycle, or recreational vehicle is bought, sold, gifted, or inherited. Governed by the Arizona Department of Transportation (ADOT) Motor Vehicle Division (MVD), Arizona title transfers require strict adherence to state statutory procedures, including signature notarization, odometer disclosure verifications, and mandatory vehicle emissions inspections in metropolitan counties. Navigating either the digital AZ MVD Now electronic portal or visiting an authorized third-party MVD office ensures a seamless, legally binding ownership transfer.

The Core Arizona Title Document: Signature and Notarization Rules

The paper Arizona Certificate of Title is a secure legal document divided into dedicated sections for the seller and buyer. Under Arizona Revised Statutes (ARS Title 28), the seller must sign the back of the physical title in the designated 'Assignment of Title' section, print their full legal name, record the date of transfer, and declare the exact vehicle odometer mileage without alteration or erasure.

Critically, Arizona is one of a handful of states that mandates that the seller's signature on a physical title MUST be formally notarized or witnessed by an authorized MVD agent. If the seller signs the title without a notary public present, the title is legally invalid and will be rejected by the MVD. However, if both the buyer and seller possess verified AZ MVD Now online accounts, the entire title transfer can be executed electronically without physical paperwork or notary involvement.

Emissions Testing (ADEQ) and County Registration Mandates

Before an Arizona title transfer can culminate in newly issued vehicle license plates and registration tags, the vehicle may need to pass a mandatory state emissions inspection administered by the Arizona Department of Environmental Quality (ADEQ). Emissions testing is required if the buyer resides within designated vehicle emissions control areas: Area A (Phoenix metropolitan area) or Area B (Tucson metropolitan area).

Vehicles manufactured within the last five model years, motorcycles, and fully electric vehicles (EVs) are exempt from testing. Gasoline vehicles manufactured in 1996 and newer undergo an On-Board Diagnostic (OBD-II) computer test, while older vehicles undergo tailpipe dynamometer testing. The ADEQ testing station transmits inspection passes electronically to the MVD database in real time, removing registration holds instantly.

The 15-Day Rule and Seller Protections: Sold Notice Filing

Under Arizona law (ARS 28-2051), a buyer must apply for an Arizona title transfer within 15 days of purchasing a vehicle. Failing to transfer the title within 15 days results in an automatic late penalty fee of $8, with an additional $4 penalty added for each subsequent 30-day period of delinquency.

For sellers, the most critical step following the sale of a vehicle is filing an immediate 'Sold Notice' on the AZ MVD Now website. Submitting a Sold Notice is completely free and immediately disassociates your name from the vehicle in law enforcement databases. If the buyer subsequently runs red-light cameras, incurs highway toll violations, or abandons the car, you are completely shielded from civil and criminal liability.

How to Execute an Arizona Vehicle Title Transfer

Follow this step-by-step roadmap to complete an Arizona title transfer, notarize signatures, pass emissions, and register your vehicle.

  1. Verify Paper Title or Access AZ MVD Now Portal

    Confirm the seller possesses a clean Arizona title with zero active liens, or initiate a paperless digital title transfer on AZ MVD Now.

  2. Complete Seller Assignment and Notarize Signatures

    Have the seller fill out the odometer disclosure and sign the title in the presence of a certified notary public or authorized MVD officer.

  3. Draft a Standard Arizona Bill of Sale (Form 96-0236)

    Complete a written Bill of Sale recording the purchase price, VIN, date of sale, and legal names and signatures of both buyer and seller.

  4. Pass ADEQ Vehicle Emissions Inspection if Required

    Take the vehicle to an ADEQ emissions station in Phoenix or Tucson metro areas to ensure inspection data transmits to the MVD database.

  5. Submit Paperwork to MVD and Pay Applicable Taxes

    Visit an MVD branch or authorized third-party provider within 15 days to submit paperwork, pay the $4 title fee, and calculate Vehicle License Tax.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does an Arizona title need to be notarized to be valid?

Yes. In Arizona, the seller's signature on a physical paper title must be notarized by a notary public or witnessed by an authorized MVD agent. If not notarized, the title transfer will be rejected.

Q2: Can you transfer an Arizona title online without going to the MVD?

Yes. If both the buyer and seller have verified accounts on the official AZ MVD Now portal, and the vehicle has an electronic Arizona title, the entire transfer can be completed online without paperwork or a notary.

Q3: How much does it cost to transfer a title in Arizona?

The statutory state title transfer fee is only $4.00. However, total registration costs will include standard license plate fees and Arizona Vehicle License Tax (VLT), which is assessed based on the vehicle's factory value and age.

Q4: How long do you have to transfer a title in Arizona after buying?

Under Arizona law, the buyer must submit the title transfer application within 15 days of the sale date to avoid late penalty fees ($8 initially, plus $4 for each additional 30-day delay).

Q5: What is an Arizona Sold Notice and why is it important?

A Sold Notice is an official form filed by the seller on AZ MVD Now immediately after selling a car. It relieves the seller of civil and criminal liability if the vehicle is later abandoned or involved in accidents.

Q6: Do you have to pay sales tax on a private party car sale in Arizona?

No. Arizona does not charge state sales tax (Transaction Privilege Tax) on casual, private-party vehicle sales between individuals. Sales tax applies only when purchasing from licensed automotive dealerships.

Q7: What is Arizona Vehicle License Tax (VLT)?

VLT is an annual ad valorem personal property tax assessed in lieu of standard property taxes, calculated from 60% of the vehicle's original manufacturer's suggested retail price (MSRP) depreciating annually.

Q8: What is a Level 1 MVD inspection in Arizona?

A Level 1 inspection is a visual verification conducted by MVD personnel or authorized third-party inspectors to match the vehicle's public VIN plate with registration documents, mandatory for out-of-state titles.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Transferring a vehicle title in Arizona is a straightforward legal process when you follow statutory rules. Always ensure the seller's signature on a physical title is authenticated by a notary public or complete the transaction digitally via AZ MVD Now. If registering in Maricopa or Pima county, verify ADEQ emissions compliance, pay the nominal $4 state title fee along with the Vehicle License Tax, and file an immediate Sold Notice if you are the seller to protect against future liability.
