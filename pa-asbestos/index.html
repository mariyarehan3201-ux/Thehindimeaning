PA Asbestos

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

PA asbestos regulations govern the inspection, notification, abatement, and disposal of asbestos-containing materials across the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. Administered primarily by the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) alongside the Pennsylvania Department of Labor and Industry (DLI), these administrative statutes enforce strict worker accreditation, project design standards, and advance notification thresholds to eliminate occupational and public exposure risks during building renovations and demolitions.

Pennsylvania Regulatory Framework: DEP and DLI Oversight

The regulatory structure for asbestos across the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania operates under a dual-agency administrative model. The Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection (DEP), under Title 25 of the Pennsylvania Code (Environmental Protection, Article III Air Resources), enforces emissions standards, project notifications, and waste disposal rules that incorporate the federal EPA NESHAP mandates. Simultaneously, the Pennsylvania Department of Labor and Industry (DLI) enforces the Asbestos Occupations Accreditation and Certification Act (Act 1990-194), which directly oversees the training, testing, and licensing of individual abatement workers, supervisors, inspectors, management planners, and project designers.

Pennsylvania features a unique regulatory landscape because two major metropolitan jurisdictions maintain independent local air pollution control programs with statutory authority granted by the Commonwealth. In Allegheny County, the Allegheny County Health Department (ACHD) Air Pollution Control Regulations, codified under Article XXI, enforce localized asbestos notification forms, distinct inspection criteria, and separate permit fees. In Philadelphia, the Philadelphia Department of Public Health Air Management Services (AMS) enforces the Philadelphia Asbestos Control Regulations under Title 6 of the Philadelphia Code, which mandates local certified project inspector oversight and municipal permits.

Notification Requirements, Permitting, and Work Practice Controls

Prior to conducting any structural demolition or interior renovation that may disturb suspect building products in Pennsylvania, building owners must commission a comprehensive survey performed by a certified PA asbestos building inspector. If the inspection confirms the presence of friable asbestos exceeding federal NESHAP action thresholds—specifically 260 linear feet on piping, 160 square feet on structural surfaces, or 35 cubic feet of total volume—an advance notification must be submitted to the PA DEP Air Quality Program at least ten working days prior to beginning work.

The standard notification document in the Commonwealth is DEP Form 2700-FM-AQ0021, titled the Asbestos Abatement and Demolition/Renovation Notification Form. Submitting this form informs both the state environmental authorities and the federal EPA of the project start date, abatement methodology, licensed contractor details, and selected landfill. In Allegheny County and Philadelphia, contractors must submit through independent local portals (such as ACHD e-Permitting and Philadelphia AMS Online) and pay specific municipal review fees that vary based on project square footage and building occupancy.

Waste Handling, Residual Waste Manifests, and Landfill Disposal in PA

Asbestos waste generated in Pennsylvania must be managed according to the state residual waste regulations under 25 Pa. Code Chapters 287 through 299. Pennsylvania law classifies friable asbestos waste as special handling municipal or residual waste. The material must be sealed in double six-mil polyethylene bags labeled with state-mandated warnings, generator name, and remediation site address. Transport must be conducted by registered waste haulers utilizing enclosed, leak-proof cargo vehicles equipped with appropriate hazardous materials placards.

Receiving landfills across the Commonwealth must hold an active permit from the PA DEP Bureau of Waste Management explicitly authorizing the acceptance of asbestos waste. Haulers must present completed Waste Shipment Records upon arrival at the landfill scale house. The landfill operator inspects container integrity, verifies bag counts against the manifest, signs the chain-of-custody transfer, and deposits the containers into designated disposal cells covered immediately with non-asbestos daily cover to prevent any airborne fiber dispersion into Pennsylvania communities.

How to Comply with PA Asbestos Abatement and Notification Procedures

Step-by-step procedural roadmap for executing legally compliant asbestos inspections, notifications, and remediation projects in Pennsylvania.

  1. Performing Comprehensive Facility Inspection by a Certified PA Inspector

    Engage an inspector certified through the PA Department of Labor and Industry to conduct sampling and issue an accredited asbestos survey report.

  2. Submitting Written Advance Notifications to PA DEP and Local Agencies

    Complete and submit Form 2700-FM-AQ0021 at least ten working days prior to project initiation, ensuring local ACHD or Philadelphia AMS filings are completed.

  3. Executing Abatement Under Certified PA Supervisors and Workers

    Implement negative-pressure HEPA containment enclosures, wet stripping protocols, and complete work utilizing licensed Pennsylvania remediation crews.

  4. Conducting Independent Air Clearance and Securing Waste Manifests

    Hire an independent third-party monitoring firm to verify clearance air quality and obtain signed waste shipment records from an authorized PA landfill.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Which state agency oversees asbestos contractor licensing in Pennsylvania?

The Pennsylvania Department of Labor and Industry (DLI) oversees individual worker accreditations and contractor licenses under PA Act 1990-194.

Q2: What is the advance notification timeline for asbestos abatement in Pennsylvania?

Contractors must submit an official notification to the PA DEP (or local health department) at least ten working days prior to beginning work.

Q3: Does Allegheny County have separate asbestos regulations from the rest of PA?

Yes, Allegheny County enforces Article XXI through the Allegheny County Health Department, requiring separate county notifications and fees.

Q4: What agency regulates asbestos remediation within the City of Philadelphia?

Philadelphia Air Management Services (AMS), a division of the Philadelphia Department of Public Health, regulates city asbestos projects.

Q5: What is the standard PA DEP notification form for asbestos projects?

The standard document is DEP Form 2700-FM-AQ0021, the Asbestos Abatement and Demolition/Renovation Notification Form.

Q6: Are single-family residential homes in PA subject to PA DEP asbestos notification?

Residential buildings with four or fewer dwelling units are generally exempt from federal and PA DEP NESHAP notification, though local disposal rules apply.

Q7: What training refresher is required to maintain a PA asbestos supervisor license?

Supervisors must complete an accredited eight-hour annual refresher course and submit a recertification application to the PA DLI.

Q8: Where can asbestos waste generated in Pennsylvania be legally deposited?

Waste must be transported to a permitted municipal solid waste or residual landfill specifically authorized by the PA DEP to receive asbestos.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Successfully managing asbestos projects in the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania demands close coordination across state agencies and municipal health departments. Property owners, environmental engineers, and remediation contractors must remain vigilant regarding regional jurisdictional lines, ensuring that PA DEP, DLI, ACHD, and Philadelphia AMS rules are observed. By prioritizing certified inspections, timely ten-day notifications, and documented landfill disposal, projects move forward safely while maintaining total compliance with Pennsylvania environmental laws.
