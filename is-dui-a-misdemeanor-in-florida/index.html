Is DUI a Misdemeanor in Florida?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Yes, in the vast majority of cases, a first or second Driving Under the Influence (DUI) offense in Florida is classified as a First-Degree Misdemeanor under Florida Statutes § 316.193. However, a DUI offense is immediately elevated to a Third-Degree or Second-Degree Felony if the incident involves a third DUI within ten years, fourth lifetime DUI, serious bodily injury, or DUI manslaughter.

The Statutory Framework: Florida Statutes § 316.193

In the State of Florida, the criminal charge of Driving Under the Influence is codified under Florida Statutes § 316.193. A motorist is legally guilty of DUI if they are driving or in Actual Physical Control of a vehicle within the state while under the influence of alcoholic beverages, chemical substances, or controlled substances to the extent that their normal faculties are impaired, or if they have a Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) or Breath Alcohol Level (BAL) of 0.08 or higher.

Under Florida law, crimes are divided into Misdemeanors and Felonies. A standard first-offense DUI without aggravating factors is classified as a First-Degree Misdemeanor, punishable by up to six months in county jail, statutory fines ranging between $500 and $1,000, mandatory completion of DUI substance abuse school, fifty hours of community service, and a minimum six-month driver's license revocation.

However, despite being labeled a misdemeanor, a Florida DUI conviction carries catastrophic, irreversible lifetime legal consequences. Unlike many other first-degree misdemeanors, Florida law strictly prohibits 'Withholding of Adjudication' for DUI. If convicted or pleading guilty, you will have a permanent criminal conviction on your public record that can never be expunged or sealed under state law. When Does a Florida DUI Elevate to a Felony?

While standard initial DUI offenses remain misdemeanors, Florida prosecutors aggressively elevate DUI charges to serious state prison felonies when specific statutory aggravating triggers are present.

The primary felony escalation occurs based on prior convictions. Under Fla. Stat. § 316.193(2)(b), a Third DUI committed within ten years of a prior conviction is automatically elevated to a Third-Degree Felony, carrying up to five years in Florida State Prison, fines up to $5,000, and a mandatory 10-year driver's license revocation. Furthermore, any Fourth Lifetime DUI—regardless of how many decades have elapsed between arrests—is a permanent Third-Degree Felony.

Even more severe are DUI offenses involving physical trauma. A DUI causing Serious Bodily Injury (defined as injury creating a substantial risk of death, serious personal disfigurement, or protracted loss of organ function) is a Third-Degree Felony with mandatory sentencing guidelines. If a DUI collision causes the death of any human being or unborn quick child, it is charged as DUI Manslaughter, a Second-Degree Felony carrying a mandatory minimum sentence of four years in state prison up to fifteen years maximum, and permanent lifetime driver's license revocation. Administrative vs Criminal License Suspensions: The 10-Day Rule

An arrest for a misdemeanor DUI in Florida immediately triggers two parallel, independent legal battles: the Judicial Criminal Court Case and the Administrative DHSMV License Case.

Under Florida's Implied Consent Law (Fla. Stat. § 327.352), driving on state roadways implies consent to submit to an approved breath, blood, or urine test. If you blow 0.08 or higher, the arresting officer immediately confiscates your physical driver's license, issuing your citation as a temporary 10-day driving permit. If you refuse the breathalyzer, your license is administratively suspended for twelve months on a first refusal (and eighteen months with misdemeanor charges on a second refusal).

Crucially, motorists have exactly Ten Calendar Days from the arrest date to file a formal petition with the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (FLHSMV). You must choose between requesting a Formal Administrative Review Hearing to challenge the suspension or waiving the hearing to immediately obtain a Business Purpose Only (BPO) Hardship License under Florida's first-time offender waiver program.

What to Do If Arrested for a DUI in Florida

Step-by-step master legal protocol to navigate the initial 10 days following a Florida DUI arrest.

  1. Exercise Your Constitutional Right to Remain Silent

    Politely decline to answer roadside police questions regarding alcohol consumption, destination, or activities; never admit intoxication.

  2. Bond Out of County Jail and Secure All Citation Paperwork

    Post bond through a licensed bail bondsman; carefully review the charging citation, noting your exact 10-day administrative deadline.

  3. Retain a Qualified Florida DUI Defense Trial Attorney

    Hire an attorney specializing in Florida drunk driving defense to review police bodycam video and breathalyzer Intoxilyzer 8000 maintenance logs.

  4. File the 10-Day FLHSMV Administrative License Petition

    Within 10 calendar days, have your attorney file for a formal review hearing or execute the eligibility waiver for an immediate BPO hardship license.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can a Florida DUI misdemeanor be expunged from my record?

No. Florida law strictly prohibits the sealing or expungement of any DUI conviction; it remains permanently on your public criminal history.

Q2: What is a 'Wet Reckless' plea deal in Florida?

A wet reckless is a reduced charge of reckless driving with alcohol conditions, avoiding mandatory DUI penalties and insurance spikes.

Q3: Is jail time mandatory for a first DUI in Florida?

Jail is not mandatory for a standard first DUI (though up to 6 months is permitted), but probation, community service, and license suspension are mandatory.

Q4: What happens if I refuse a breathalyzer test in Florida?

A first refusal triggers an automatic 1-year administrative license suspension; a second refusal is a separate first-degree misdemeanor crime.

Q5: What is FR-44 insurance in Florida after a DUI?

FR-44 is mandatory high-risk auto insurance requiring 100k/300k/50k liability limits, typically increasing insurance costs by 200% to 400% for 3 years.

Q6: When is an Ignition Interlock Device (IID) required in Florida?

An IID is mandatory for at least 6 months for a first DUI with BAC over 0.15, at least 1 year for a second DUI, and 2 years for a second DUI within 5 years.

Q7: Can sleeping in my parked car result in a DUI in Florida?

Yes. If the keys are accessible in the ignition or cabin, Florida courts rule you are in 'Actual Physical Control' of the motor vehicle.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

While a first or second DUI in Florida is legally classified as a first-degree misdemeanor, the criminal and financial repercussions rival those of serious felonies. With mandatory convictions, license revocations, exorbitant SR-22/FR-44 high-risk insurance premiums, and mandatory interlock devices, a DUI arrest disrupts personal liberty and career prospects. Act within ten days of arrest to protect your driving privileges, and retain an experienced Florida criminal defense attorney to investigate breathalyzer calibration flaws, challenge traffic stop legality, and negotiate possible reduction to reckless driving (wet reckless).
