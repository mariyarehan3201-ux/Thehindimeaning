Can You Sue a Nail Salon for Fungus?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Yes, you can sue a nail salon for fungus if the establishment's negligent sanitation practices, unsterilized tools, or contaminated whirlpool pedicure basins caused a severe fungal infection such as onychomycosis or tinea unguium. Commercial general liability insurance policies cover client injuries resulting from salon health and cosmetology code violations.

The Pathology of Salon-Acquired Onychomycosis

Visiting a nail salon for a manicure or pedicure is an investment in personal grooming and self-care. Clients place total confidence in salon cleanliness, expecting technicians to operate in hygienic environments with sterile implements. However, when salons neglect basic sanitation standards, clients face exposure to virulent fungal pathogens.

The most common fungal infection contracted in commercial nail salons is Onychomycosis (also known as Tinea Unguium). Onychomycosis is caused primarily by dermatophyte fungi (specifically Trichophyton rubrum and Trichophyton mentagrophytes), alongside yeasts (Candida) and non-dermatophyte molds. These fungal spores thrive in warm, damp, organic environments—making poorly cleaned pedicure foot baths and moist towel warmers ideal breeding grounds.

When an aggressive technician cuts too deep into living cuticle tissue or files down the dorsal nail plate, the protective nail seal is breached. Fungal spores invade the nail bed and matrix. What begins as a subtle yellow or white spot beneath the nail edge gradually progresses into severe, unsightly dystrophy: the nail plate thickens, becomes brittle and crumbly, separates painfully from the underlying nail bed (onycholysis), and emits a foul odor. The table below delineates the clinical stages of nail salon fungal infections.

Fungal Infection Stage Clinical Pathology & Symptoms Medical Treatment Required Long-Term Prognosis
Early Distal Subungual Yellow-white discoloration at outer nail edge Topical antifungal lacquers (Ciclopirox / Jublia) Manageable if diagnosed immediately
Moderate Total Dystrophic Severe nail thickening, subungual hyperkeratosis Oral Terbinafine (Lamisil) 12-week course + blood liver tests 6 to 12 months for new nail to grow out
Severe Chronic Onychomycosis Complete nail destruction, throbbing pain, ingrown edges Surgical nail avulsion (permanent removal) + laser therapy Permanent nail matrix disfigurement

The Pedicure Basin Trap: Whirlpool Pipes and Biofilms

In cosmetology negligence litigation, the primary culprit in commercial nail salon fungal outbreaks is the Whirlpool Pedicure Foot Basin.

Traditional pedicure thrones feature circulating whirlpool jets connected by internal plastic plumbing pipes hidden behind the basin walls. When a client soaks their feet, dead skin flakes, oils, calluses, and fungal spores are sucked through the intake filter into the internal pipes. When the salon drains the basin and merely wipes the surface with a disinfectant spray, the internal pipes remain coated with a thick, slimy biological residue known as a Bacterial and Fungal Biofilm.

State cosmetology licensing boards enforce strict statutory sanitation mandates. Between every client, technicians are legally required to drain the tub, scrub the basin with soap and water, flush the internal pipes with hospital-grade disinfectant for at least 10 minutes, and completely disassemble the impeller screens nightly for bleach soaks. When rushed technicians bypass these mandatory cleaning cycles to turn chairs faster, the next customer places their freshly soaked, vulnerable feet directly into water circulating millions of active fungal spores. Multi-Use Metal Implements Autoclave steam sterilization at 250°F (spore tested) Wiping with Barbicide liquid or storing in dirty drawer
Porous Tools (Files / Buffers) Single-use: must be discarded in trash after each client Reusing emery boards and pumice stones across customers
Pipeless / Disposable Basin Liners Fresh medical-grade plastic basin liner for every client Reusing plastic liners or using unlined piped tubs

Building a Legal Lawsuit: Proof, Causation, and Damages

To successfully sue a nail salon for a fungal infection, your personal injury lawyer must prove Negligence and direct Medical Causation under civil tort law.

Proving causation requires prompt medical documentation. You must visit a board-certified dermatologist or podiatrist immediately upon noticing nail discoloration. The physician will take nail clippings for a periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) stain and fungal culture to scientifically identify the exact fungal species. A timing timeline connecting your salon visit to the onset of symptoms (typically 7 to 21 days post-appointment) is essential.

Furthermore, damages in severe onychomycosis cases are substantial. Treating chronic toenail fungus is extraordinarily expensive: oral antifungal medications like Terbinafine require routine liver enzyme blood monitoring, while advanced FDA-cleared PinPointe FootLaser treatments cost upwards of $1,000 to $2,500 out-of-pocket and are rarely covered by health insurance. When an infection spreads to multiple nails, causes permanent cosmetic disfigurement, or forces surgical nail removal, your attorney can recover thousands in medical bills, pain and suffering, and emotional distress from the salon's Commercial General Liability (CGL) insurer.

How to Build a Claim for Nail Salon Fungal Infection

Step-by-step roadmap to document salon negligence and recover medical expenses.

  1. Visit a Dermatologist or Podiatrist for a Fungal Culture

    Have a physician perform a nail clipping biopsy and fungal culture to scientifically document the fungal strain (e.g., Trichophyton rubrum).

  2. Preserve Salon Proof of Service and Payment

    Locate credit card statements, digital booking emails, and text confirmations verifying the exact date, time, and technician who performed your service.

  3. File a Formal Sanitation Complaint with State Licensing Board

    Submit a complaint to your state board of cosmetology to trigger an unannounced state inspection of the salon's pedicure tubs and autoclaves.

  4. Retain a Personal Injury Attorney to Demand Insurance Payout

    Hire an attorney to submit a formal bodily injury demand package to the salon's commercial general liability insurance company.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I sue a nail salon for toenail fungus?

Yes, if the salon's failure to sanitize tools or clean whirlpool foot basins caused severe onychomycosis requiring medical treatment.

Q2: How do I prove I got fungus from a specific nail salon?

Proof requires prompt medical diagnosis, lab culture identification, proof of appointment dates, and establishing no prior history of nail infections.

Q3: What is the settlement value for a nail salon fungus lawsuit?

Settlements range from $15,000 for moderate infections to over $75,000 if the infection required surgical removal, laser therapy, or caused cellulitis.

Q4: Why are whirlpool pedicure chairs dangerous for fungus?

Whirlpool foot basins harbor internal pipes that accumulate skin biofilms and fungal spores if not flushed with disinfectant for 10 minutes between clients.

Q5: Who pays the settlement in a nail salon lawsuit?

Settlements are paid directly by the nail salon's Commercial General Liability (CGL) business insurance policy.

Q6: Are nail salons required to use an autoclave?

In most states, cosmetology regulations mandate medical-grade autoclaves or dry-heat sterilizers to 100% sterilize multi-use metal implements.

Q7: How long do I have to file a claim against a nail salon?

The statute of limitations for personal injury typically allows two to three years from the date the infection was diagnosed, depending on your state.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

You can sue a nail salon for fungus if negligent sanitation, unsterilized cuticle nippers, or filthy whirlpool pedicure foot basins caused severe onychomycosis. Fungal infections are painful, persistent, and can take over a year of intensive medical therapy to cure. Visit a dermatologist or podiatrist for clinical culture testing, save salon appointment receipts, file a formal complaint with your state board of cosmetology, and consult a personal injury attorney to hold the salon accountable for your recovery.
