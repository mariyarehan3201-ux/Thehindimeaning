Can I Sue Uber As a Driver?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Yes, you can sue Uber as a driver, but your legal options are governed by whether you opted out of Uber's mandatory arbitration agreement, the classification of drivers as independent contractors, and the nature of your claim. In car accidents caused by other motorists, drivers recover primarily through third-party personal injury claims and Uber's $1,000,000 commercial uninsured motorist coverage.

The Contractual Hurdle: Mandatory Arbitration Clauses

Driving for Uber provides millions of gig economy workers with flexible earnings across thousands of American cities. However, when an Uber driver experiences a catastrophic highway collision while transporting a passenger, suffers a violent assault by an unruly rider, or faces wrongful account deactivation based on fraudulent customer reports, the driver naturally wonders: can I sue Uber directly in court?

The foremost legal obstacle confronting every Uber driver is the contractual Technology Services Agreement. When you download the Uber Driver app and tap 'I Agree' to activate your driver account, you execute a legally binding commercial contract. Buried within that multi-page agreement is a comprehensive, enforceable Mandatory Binding Arbitration Agreement governed by the Federal Arbitration Act (FAA).

Under this clause, drivers explicitly surrender their constitutional right to file a public civil lawsuit against Uber in a state or federal court before a jury. Instead, all disputes—including personal injury claims, wage disputes, and deactivations—must be resolved through confidential private arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association (AAA) or JAMS. Unless an astute driver formally exercised their legal right to opt out of the arbitration clause in writing within 30 days of signing up, courts will grant Uber's legal motion to compel arbitration and dismiss your lawsuit. Car Accidents While Driving for Uber: Where Does the Money Come From?

When an Uber driver is involved in a motor vehicle collision, the immediate legal reality is that suing Uber directly is rarely necessary to secure full financial recovery. The insurance recovery framework for Uber drivers is dictated by which phase of the app the driver was operating in, and which motorist caused the wreck.

If you were driving with an active passenger or en route to pick up a passenger (Periods 2 and 3) and were rear-ended or broadsided by a negligent third-party driver, you file a traditional third-party personal injury lawsuit against that at-fault driver's automobile liability insurance. However, because many negligent drivers carry only minimal $25,000 state-minimum insurance policies, an injured Uber driver with severe fractures or spinal disc herniations faces a massive coverage deficit.

This is where Uber's commercial insurance protections apply. During Periods 2 and 3, Uber provides a mandatory $1,000,000 Uninsured / Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM) commercial policy. If the at-fault driver was uninsured, fled the scene in a hit-and-run, or carried insufficient liability limits, you can file an Underinsured Motorist claim against Uber's corporate commercial policy to collect up to $1,000,000 for your medical bills, lost wages, and pain. Period 1: Online (Waiting for Trip) Logged in, waiting for ride request ping Contingent liability ($50k/$100k/$25k); No UIM Primary unless rideshare endorsement purchased

Suing Uber for Passenger Assaults and Safety Failures

While motor vehicle collisions are routed through insurance policies and arbitration, an entirely separate category of lawsuits involves violent physical or sexual assaults committed against Uber drivers by intoxicated or violent passengers.

Historically, Uber forced assaulted drivers into confidential arbitration. However, following the passage of the federal Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Act (EFAA), drivers who experience sexual assault or harassment have the absolute federal statutory right to void arbitration clauses and sue Uber directly in open civil court. Drivers can assert direct corporate negligence claims against Uber for failing to verify passenger identities, permitting banned violent riders to open burner accounts with prepaid cards, and failing to provide in-app panic safety features.

How an Injured Uber Driver Can Recover Maximum Compensation

Step-by-step roadmap for an Uber driver to file injury claims after a crash during an active trip.

  1. Screenshot In-App Trip Status and Waybill Instantly

    Capture screenshots of your active passenger trip, the rider's name, GPS navigation route, and the digital electronic waybill in the driver app.

  2. Report the Crash via the Uber Driver App Safety Portal

    Submit a formal accident report through the Uber app to generate an official internal claim tracking number with Uber's third-party claims carrier.

  3. File Bodily Injury Claim Against At-Fault Driver

    Open an insurance claim with the negligent third-party driver who hit your vehicle to exhaust their primary liability policy limits.

  4. Submit a $1,000,000 UIM Claim to Uber's Commercial Carrier

    Have your rideshare accident attorney submit an Underinsured Motorist demand to Uber's commercial insurer (such as Progressive Commercial or Allstate).

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does Uber have workers' comp for drivers?

Because Uber drivers are classified as independent contractors, Uber does not provide standard statutory workers' compensation in most states.

Q2: What is Uber's Optional Injury Protection insurance?

A low-cost elective insurance program drivers can buy through the app that pays up to $1,000,000 in medical expenses and disability earnings replacement.

Q3: Can an Uber driver sue Uber for getting deactivated?

You can file a wrongful deactivation claim, but it must be resolved through confidential binding arbitration under your driver terms of service.

Q4: How do I opt out of Uber's arbitration agreement?

You must send an explicit written opt-out notice via email or certified mail to Uber's legal department within 30 days of creating your account.

Q5: Does Uber's $1,000,000 insurance cover the driver if hit by an uninsured car?

Yes. During Periods 2 and 3, Uber provides $1,000,000 in Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM) coverage that protects the driver.

Q6: Can an Uber driver sue Uber for a passenger assault?

Yes. Under federal EFAA law, sexual assault claims can bypass arbitration and be filed directly in public civil court against Uber.

Q7: Will my personal auto insurance cover an accident while driving for Uber?

Standard personal auto policies exclude commercial ridesharing unless you purchased a specific 'rideshare endorsement' on your policy.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

You can sue Uber as a driver, but your legal strategy depends on whether your dispute involves a traffic accident, an assault, or a contractual deactivation. While mandatory arbitration agreements restrict ordinary breach of contract lawsuits, injured drivers can tap into Uber's powerful $1,000,000 Underinsured Motorist policy to cover devastating accident injuries, or take Uber directly to civil court for safety failures and assaults. Retaining an experienced rideshare attorney ensures your driver rights are aggressively defended.
