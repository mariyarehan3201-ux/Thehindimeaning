What Does TPMS Mean on a Honda?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

The Tire Pressure Monitoring System (TPMS) is an essential vehicular safety system engineered to continuously monitor the pneumatic air pressure inside pneumatic tires. Mandated on all passenger vehicles sold in the United States since 2007 under the TREAD Act, TPMS alerts drivers when one or more tires drop twenty-five percent or more below recommended cold inflation pressure. The system communicates via a standardized dashboard telltale resembling an open tire cross-section enclosing an exclamation point. Understanding how TPMS functions helps motorists maintain safety, maximize fuel economy, and prolong tire longevity.

How to Troubleshoot and Reset a TPMS Warning Light

Follow this procedure when a tire pressure warning light illuminates on your instrument panel.

  1. Step 1

    Observe whether the dashboard light is glowing solid (low air pressure) or flashing for one minute first (sensor system fault).

  2. Step 2

    Park the vehicle on level ground and allow the tires to cool for at least one hour to obtain accurate cold tire pressure readings.

  3. Step 3

    Locate the vehicle tire specification placard affixed to the driver door frame to find recommended cold PSI numbers.

  4. Step 4

    Check all four road tires and the spare wheel using a calibrated digital or dial tire pressure gauge.

  5. Step 5

    Add compressed air to any underinflated tire until it matches the exact door placard pressure, taking care not to exceed maximum tire sidewall PSI.

  6. Step 6

    Drive the vehicle at speeds above twenty-five miles per hour for ten to twenty minutes to allow direct TPMS sensors to transmit updated readings.

  7. Step 7

    Perform a manual TPMS recalibration through the dashboard vehicle menu or under-dash reset button if operating an indirect TPMS system.

Q1: What is the primary significance of what does tpms mean on a honda?

Q2: Why does my TPMS light flash and then stay solid?

A flashing TPMS light indicates a system fault—such as a dead sensor battery, damaged sensor, or communication error—rather than simply low tire pressure.

Q3: Why does my TPMS light come on during cold weather?

Tire pressure drops approximately 1 to 2 PSI for every 10 degrees Fahrenheit decrease in ambient temperature, causing marginally inflated tires to drop below the warning threshold on cold mornings.

Q4: How long do TPMS sensor batteries last?

Internal lithium batteries inside direct TPMS sensors typically last between seven and ten years, or roughly 100,000 miles of driving.

Q5: Is it safe to drive with the TPMS light on?

You should check tire pressures as soon as possible; driving on an underinflated tire increases braking distances, impairs handling, and risks catastrophic blowout.

Q6: What is the difference between direct and indirect TPMS?

Direct TPMS uses physical pressure sensors mounted inside each wheel, while indirect TPMS calculates pressure drops by monitoring wheel rotational speed differences through the ABS sensors.

Q7: Does my spare tire have a TPMS sensor?

Many trucks and SUVs equipped with full-size spare tires include a fifth TPMS sensor, but compact donut spare tires do not have sensors.
