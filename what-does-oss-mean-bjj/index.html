What Does OSS Mean BJJ?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Step inside any Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ) academy or mixed martial arts gym, and you will immediately hear practitioners, coaches, and black belts enthusiastically exclaiming "OSS!" (pronounced "Oss" or "Osu"). In Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, OSS is a universal martial arts greeting, an affirmation of respect, an acknowledgment of instruction, and a philosophical declaration of perseverance under pressure.

The Historical and Linguistic Origins of OSS / Osu

While OSS is synonymous with modern Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, its linguistic roots trace back to Japanese martial arts culture. Linguistic scholars and martial arts historians identify OSS as an abbreviation of the Japanese phrase Oshi Shinobu (押し忍ぶ). The first kanji, Osu (押), translates to "to push" or "to pressure." The second kanji, Shinobu (忍), means "to endure," "to persevere," or "to bear hardship with patience."

Combined, the philosophical foundation of Oshi Shinobu translates to pushing yourself to your absolute physical and mental limits while enduring pressure without complaint. In early 20th-century Japan, the phrase was adopted by the Imperial Japanese Naval Academy and later popularized by legendary Kyokushin Karate founder Masutatsu Oyama as a daily greeting among dedicated disciples.

When Japanese judoka and martial arts instructors?"including Mitsuyo Maeda?"introduced grappling arts to Brazil in the early 1900s, traditional terminology merged with Brazilian culture. Pioneering instructors like Grandmaster Carlson Gracie actively embraced OSS, embedding it into the worldwide lexicon of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu.

The Multi-Faceted Roles of OSS in Everyday Training

In a Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu academy, OSS functions much like the word "Aloha" in Hawaiian culture?"it serves multiple communication purposes depending on vocal inflection, body language, and context.

When an instructor completes demonstrating an intricate guard sweep or submission escape, they will ask if anyone has questions before clapping twice. The entire class responds in unison with a resonant "OSS!" before breaking into pairs to drill. Here, OSS replaces lengthy verbal responses with a sharp, unified confirmation that the instruction was understood.

Furthermore, OSS embodies the gritty ethos of the grappling arts. Jiu-jitsu is a physically demanding discipline where practitioners spend hours caught in heavy side control, trapped in tight triangles, or gasping for oxygen. Saying OSS reminds practitioners to remain mentally calm under suffocating pressure, embrace discomfort, and keep moving forward.

BJJ Mat Etiquette: When and When Not to Use OSS

While OSS is widely embraced across most contemporary BJJ academies, understanding mat etiquette ensures you use the phrase appropriately. In casual western academies, overusing OSS outside the gym?"such as saying it in grocery stores, corporate boardrooms, or family dinners?"can appear eccentric or performative.

Moreover, in traditional Japanese society outside martial arts dojos, the word "Osu" is regarded as rough, hyper-masculine military slang. Using it when speaking to native Japanese speakers in formal business settings or casual restaurants is considered unrefined and impolite. Within the sacred space of the BJJ training mat, however, it remains a beloved badge of fraternity and mutual respect.

How to Practice Proper Mat Etiquette and Use OSS in BJJ

Step-by-step guidance on integrating OSS respectfully into your martial arts academy training.

  1. Bow and Utter OSS When Entering the Mat

    Remove your footwear, face the center of the training mat, execute a slight bow from the waist, and softly say 'OSS'.

  2. Acknowledge Technique Demonstrations

    When the head instructor finishes teaching a technique and claps hands, join the class in calling out a crisp 'OSS!' before drilling.

  3. Salute Your Sparring Partner

    Before starting a round of live rolling, slap hands, bump fists, look your partner in the eyes, and exchange a respectful 'OSS'.

  4. Express Post-Roll Gratitude

    After the sparring timer sounds, shake hands with your training partner and say 'OSS, thank you' for a safe and challenging round.

  5. Bow to the Line of Black Belts

    At the conclusion of class during the formal lineup, bow to the head instructor and visiting professors while repeating 'OSS'.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does OSS stand for in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu?

OSS is derived from the Japanese phrase 'Oshi Shinobu', meaning to push through adversity and persevere with patience and humility.

Q2: How do you pronounce OSS in BJJ?

It is pronounced with a short, punchy 'Oss' sound, rhyming with 'boss' or 'moss'.

Q3: When should you say OSS in a BJJ gym?

Say it when entering or leaving the mat, acknowledging a coach's instruction, and before or after rolling with a training partner.

Q4: Is OSS used in other martial arts besides BJJ?

Yes, OSS originated in Japan and is widely used in Kyokushin Karate, traditional Judo dojos, and Japanese Kendo academies.

Q5: Who brought OSS into Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu?

Grandmaster Carlson Gracie and early Gracie family practitioners adopted the term from Japanese judoka who taught grappling in Brazil.

Q6: Is OSS considered rude in Japan?

In general civilian Japanese society, 'Osu' is considered rough, informal slang; it is strictly appropriate only within martial arts dojos.

Q7: Do you have to say OSS to train BJJ?

No, saying OSS is a cultural tradition of respect, but a polite 'thank you' or handshake is equally respected in modern academies.

Q8: What does OSS mean at the end of a roll?

It signifies mutual respect and gratitude between training partners for testing each other's technique safely and honorably.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The exclamation OSS represents the enduring soul of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu culture. Combining historical Japanese principles of perseverance with Brazilian warmth and martial camaraderie, OSS is a concise reminder that mastery on the mats requires humility, patience, and relentless dedication. Every time you utter OSS, you honor the lineage of coaches, training partners, and personal struggles that forge true martial character.
