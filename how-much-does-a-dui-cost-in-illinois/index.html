How Much Does a DUI Cost in Illinois?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a DUI cost in Illinois emphasizes the strict financial and administrative penalties enforced across the Prairie State. According to official estimates from the Illinois Secretary of State, an average first-offense DUI costs approximately $14,500 to $18,000 when accounting for attorney fees, statutory court fines, mandatory alcohol evaluations, ignition interlock requirements (BAIID), and high-risk insurance surcharges. Aggravated felony DUIs or repeat violations easily exceed $25,000 to $35,000.

Statutory Fines, Court Assessments, and Administrative Costs in Illinois

In Illinois, a first-time DUI is classified as a Class A misdemeanor under 625 ILCS 5/11-501. While statutory criminal fines can reach up to $2,500, judges typically assess fines between $500 and $1,500 for a first offense. In addition to statutory fines, the court imposes mandatory statutory surcharges, including a $750 trauma center assessment, a $200 road fund assessment, local clerk filing fees, and court automation funds totaling $800 to $1,500.

The Illinois Secretary of State administrative process imposes severe supplementary fees. An administrative statutory summary suspension reinstatement fee costs $250 for a first offense ($500 for repeat offenders). If convicted of DUI, full driver's license reinstatement costs $500. Drivers must also pay for vehicle impound towing and administrative hold release fees, which commonly total $350 to $700 in Cook, DuPage, Lake, and Will counties.

BAIID Ignition Interlock and Alcohol Remediation Requirements

Illinois enforces strict ignition interlock requirements through its Breath Alcohol Ignition Interlock Device (BAIID) program. First-time offenders seeking a Monitoring Device Driving Permit (MDDP) during statutory summary suspension must install a certified BAIID unit. Installation costs $100 to $150, monthly equipment lease fees run $80 to $110, and the Secretary of State assesses a mandatory $30 monthly administration fee. Over a typical 6 to 12-month period, BAIID expenses total $1,000 to $1,800.

Before any sentencing or driving permit issuance, Illinois law mandates an official Alcohol and Drug Uniform Evaluation conducted by a licensed provider ($200 to $300). Based on the evaluation, drivers are classified into risk levels (Minimal, Moderate, Significant, or High). Satisfying required remedial education and treatment hours adds $250 to over $1,000 in clinical program fees.

SR-22 Insurance Surcharges and Legal Defense Value

Following a DUI revocation or suspension in Illinois, drivers must submit an SR-22 Financial Responsibility insurance certificate to the Secretary of State for three continuous years. An SR-22 certification labels the motorist as high-risk, typically doubling or tripling auto insurance premiums. Motorists who previously paid $1,500 annually can expect premiums of $3,000 to $4,500, resulting in $4,500 to $9,000 in cumulative insurance surcharges over the three-year statutory period.

Private DUI defense attorneys in Illinois charge between $2,500 and $6,500 for misdemeanor defense. While legal representation requires upfront capital, retaining a skilled attorney can yield substantial savings. Successfully rescinding the statutory summary suspension, winning an acquittal, or negotiating court supervision avoids a formal conviction, saves thousands in reinstatement fees, and protects the driver from skyrocketing insurance rates.

How to Navigate an Illinois DUI Charge Economically

Follow these five strategic steps to manage and mitigate expenses following an Illinois DUI arrest.

  1. File a Petition to Rescind Suspension Within 90 Days

    Have your attorney file a formal hearing petition to challenge the Secretary of State's automatic statutory summary suspension.

  2. Complete Your Uniform Alcohol Evaluation Promptly

    Schedule an evaluation with a state-licensed provider immediately to establish your risk classification before court proceedings.

  3. Apply for a Monitoring Device Driving Permit (MDDP)

    Submit your MDDP paperwork and install an approved BAIID unit to preserve legal driving privileges for work and medical needs.

  4. Pursue Court Supervision Over a Conviction

    Work with defense counsel to seek court supervision, which avoids license revocation and prevents mandatory conviction records.

  5. Shop Multiple Non-Standard SR-22 Insurance Providers

    Compare SR-22 high-risk quotes across specialized brokers to avoid the exorbitant penalties of major standard carriers.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the average total cost of a DUI in Illinois?

According to the Illinois Secretary of State, the total average cost of a first-offense DUI ranges between $14,500 and $18,000.

Q2: What is court supervision for DUI in Illinois?

Court supervision is a deferred sentence; if completed successfully without violation, it avoids a formal conviction and driver's license revocation.

Q3: How much does a BAIID ignition interlock cost in Illinois?

BAIID costs approximately $1,000 to $1,800 for 6 to 12 months, including installation, monthly device rental, and $30 monthly state fees.

Q4: How long is your license suspended for a first DUI in Illinois?

Failing chemical testing results in a 6-month statutory summary suspension; refusing testing results in a mandatory 12-month suspension.

Q5: What is the fine for a first DUI in Illinois?

Statutory criminal fines can reach up to $2,500, with typical assessments ranging from $500 to $1,500 plus $1,000+ in mandatory surcharges.

Q6: How much does a DUI lawyer cost in Chicago?

Experienced DUI defense attorneys in the Chicago metropolitan area typically charge flat fees between $3,000 and $7,000 for pre-trial defense.

Q7: How long do you need SR-22 insurance in Illinois after a DUI?

Illinois law requires drivers to maintain continuous SR-22 financial responsibility insurance for three consecutive years.

Q8: Can you refuse a breathalyzer test in Illinois?

Under implied consent laws, refusing a chemical test carries an automatic 12-month license suspension for first offenders, even if acquitted.
