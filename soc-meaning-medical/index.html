Start of Care (SOC) in Home Health and CMS Episode Management

In home health agency administration, post-acute nursing, and Medicare reimbursement, SOC stands for Start of Care. It represents the official, legally binding date on which a home health clinician (such as a registered nurse, physical therapist, or speech-language pathologist) conducts the initial in-person comprehensive clinical evaluation in the patient residence.

Under federal Medicare Conditions of Participation, the SOC assessment must be completed within 48 hours of hospital discharge or within 48 hours of physician referral. The clinician completes the extensive Outcome and Assessment Information Set (OASIS) document, establishing the patient baseline clinical status, functional mobility, wound care demands, and medication reconciliation, while initiating the 60-day Medicare home health certification episode.

Standard of Care (SOC) as a Legal and Clinical Practice Benchmark

In clinical medicine, medical malpractice law, and bioethics, SOC stands for Standard of Care. It defines the level and type of care that a reasonably competent, skilled healthcare professional with similar training and in the same medical community would provide under similar clinical circumstances.

Standard of care is not defined by perfection, but rather by evidence-based clinical guidelines established by medical societies, peer-reviewed clinical research, and national specialty consensus statements. In clinical research trials, the investigational therapy is rigorously compared against the current standard of care to prove clinical superiority or non-inferiority.

Whether designating the initiation of home health services or establishing legal benchmarks of clinical competency, SOC represents fundamental accountability across modern healthcare delivery.

How Home Health Clinicians Conduct a Start of Care (SOC) Visit

A four-step clinical workflow detailing how registered nurses and physical therapists execute a compliant Medicare Start of Care evaluation.

  1. Initial In-Home Physical Assessment

    The clinician arrives at the patient residence within 48 hours of discharge to assess vital signs, cardiopulmonary status, and surgical wounds.

  2. Comprehensive Medication Reconciliation

    Inspect all prescription bottles, over-the-counter vitamins, and discharge instructions to identify duplicate therapies or adverse drug interactions.

  3. OASIS-E Functional Mobility Scoring

    Evaluate the patient ability to safely ambulate, transfer from bed to chair, bathe, dress, and prepare meals without falling.

  4. Formulate Physician Plan of Care (CMS-485)

    Draft the formal Home Health Certification and Plan of Care outlining visit frequencies, therapy goals, and emergency contacts for physician signature.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does SOC mean in home health?

In home health, SOC stands for Start of Care, the initial comprehensive clinical assessment that begins a patient home healthcare episode.

Q2: What does SOC mean in medical malpractice law?

In medical law, SOC stands for Standard of Care, the level of care a reasonably competent healthcare provider would provide in similar circumstances.

Q3: How quickly must a home health SOC visit occur?

Medicare rules require the SOC visit to be conducted within 48 hours of hospital discharge or within 48 hours of the physician referral date.

Q4: Who can perform a Start of Care visit?

A Start of Care assessment must be conducted by a registered nurse (RN), physical therapist (PT), or speech-language pathologist (SLP).

Q5: What is the CMS-485 form in home health?

The CMS-485 is the official Home Health Plan of Care document created during the SOC visit that must be signed by the ordering physician.

Q6: What does SOC mean in clinical research trials?

In clinical trials, SOC refers to the current standard of care therapy used as the control baseline to test the efficacy of a new experimental drug.

Q7: What assessment tool is completed during an SOC visit?

Clinicians complete the federally mandated OASIS (Outcome and Assessment Information Set) assessment tool during the Start of Care visit.

Q8: Can a medical assistant perform a Start of Care evaluation?

No. Federal and state regulations strictly require licensed clinicians (RN, PT, or SLP) to conduct comprehensive SOC evaluations.
