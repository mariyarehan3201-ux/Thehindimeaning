How Much for a Ton of Gravel?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

A ton of plain crushed stone or driveway gravel typically costs between $25 and $65 when picked up at a quarry or local landscape yard, while decorative varieties such as river rock, pea gravel, or marble chips range from $50 to $120 per ton. When ordering bulk delivery for a residential driveway or patio subbase, minimum dump truck delivery charges add $50 to $150 per load. Understanding how stone size, geological composition, cubic yard conversion rates, and transport logistics influence aggregate pricing ensures you order the exact tonnage without overspending.

Gravel Types and Pricing: Crushed Run vs. Decorative Rock

The wide pricing spectrum for bulk gravel reflects the extraction, crushing, and sorting processes required at aggregate quarries. The most economical option is crusher run (also known as dense grade aggregate or quarry process), which costs $20 to $38 per ton. Crusher run blends angular three-quarter-inch crushed stone with fine stone dust, creating an interlocked, compacted base that forms the rock-solid structural foundation of driveways, pavers, and retaining walls.

Decorative landscaping aggregates command significantly higher market prices because they require washing, mechanical tumbling, or long-distance transport from riverbed quarries. Naturally rounded pea gravel and smooth river stones range from $45 to $85 per ton, while exotic mineral varieties like crystalline white marble chips, volcanic black lava rock, or slate chips frequently exceed $90 to $140 per ton. These decorative stones are non-compacting and are prized for walking paths, French drains, and garden borders.

Tons vs. Cubic Yards: Calculating Coverage and Volume

A frequent source of confusion when ordering bulk gravel is the mathematical conversion between volume (cubic yards) and weight (tons). Quarry weigh scales sell material by the ton (2,000 pounds), whereas landscape blueprints and driveway dimensions are calculated in cubic feet and cubic yards. On average, one cubic yard of standard crushed stone weighs approximately 1.3 to 1.5 tons (2,600 to 3,000 pounds), depending on stone moisture content and mineral density.

To calculate the tonnage needed for your project, multiply the length by the width in feet, multiply by the desired depth in feet (for example, a three-inch layer is 0.25 feet), and divide by twenty-seven to determine cubic yards. Multiplying your total cubic yards by a factor of 1.4 yields the required tonnage. Adding ten percent for compaction settling and ground contour variations ensures your dump truck delivery completes the job without requiring an expensive partial second run.

Delivery Logistics: Tri-Axle Loads and Tailgate Spreading

Bulk gravel transport logistics represent a major component of total project costs. Commercial tri-axle dump trucks carry fourteen to twenty-two tons per trip. Quarries and material suppliers charge a delivery fee ranging from $50 to $150 per haul within a ten to fifteen-mile radius, with mileage surcharges added for remote rural properties. Ordering a full twenty-ton truckload is far more economical per ton than ordering a small five-ton partial load, which often incurs short-load minimum penalty fees.

When the dump truck arrives, you can often take advantage of tailgate spreading. Experienced dump truck drivers can set the tailgate release chains to a three-inch gap, raise the hydraulic dump bed, and slowly roll forward down your driveway, spreading the gravel relatively evenly as it discharges. While tailgate spreading saves hours of manual wheelbarrow labor, homeowners must ensure overhead tree branches, low power lines, and septic tank drain fields do not obstruct the truck's path.

How to Order and Install Bulk Driveway Gravel

Follow these five organized steps to measure, purchase, and lay bulk crushed stone accurately.

  1. Calculate Square Footage and Depth

    Measure driveway length and width, choosing a depth of four to six inches for new bases or two inches for top-dressing.

  2. Convert Cubic Yards to Tonnage

    Divide cubic feet by twenty-seven to find cubic yards, then multiply by 1.4 to calculate the required quarry tons.

  3. Verify Delivery Site Access

    Inspect the driveway for low telephone wires, low tree limbs, soft turf, or hidden septic tanks that could trap a heavy truck.

  4. Request Tailgate Spreading

    Coordinate with the dispatch driver to chain the tailgate and spread the stone evenly along the driveway length upon delivery.

  5. Grade and Compact the Surface

    Use a landscape rake or tractor box blade to crown the center for water runoff, and roll with a mechanical plate compactor.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How many square feet does one ton of gravel cover?

One ton of gravel covers approximately one hundred square feet at a depth of two inches, or roughly fifty square feet at a depth of four inches.

Q2: How much does a full dump truck load of gravel cost?

A commercial tri-axle dump truck carrying fifteen to twenty tons of driveway gravel typically costs between $450 and $1,100 including delivery.

Q3: What is the best gravel to use for a residential driveway?

Number 57 crushed limestone or crusher run is best for driveways because the angular crushed rocks interlock tightly and resist vehicle tire displacement.

Q4: Why is pea gravel not recommended for driveways?

Pea gravel consists of rounded, smooth pebbles that act like ball bearings under car tires, rutting deeply and shifting instead of compacting.

Q5: How much does a cubic yard of gravel weigh?

One cubic yard of crushed gravel weighs approximately 2,600 to 2,900 pounds, which is roughly 1.3 to 1.45 tons.

Q6: Can a dump truck deliver gravel over a residential lawn?

Driving a fully loaded twenty-ton dump truck across residential grass will cause deep ruts and can crush buried sprinkler lines or septic drain pipes.

Q7: Is crushed stone cheaper than bagged gravel from hardware stores?

Bulk gravel is dramatically cheaper; buying one ton in fifty-pound bags costs $180 to $260, compared to $30 to $50 per ton in bulk.

Q8: How thick should gravel be on a new driveway?

A new gravel driveway requires a total thickness of eight inches: six inches of compacted crusher run base topped with two inches of clean angular stone.
