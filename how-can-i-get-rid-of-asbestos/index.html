How Can I Get Rid of Asbestos? Safe Steps

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 23, 2026

How can I get rid of asbestos? Safely getting rid of asbestos from a property requires either hiring a licensed asbestos abatement contractor or, where legally permitted for homeowners, implementing strict negative pressure containment, continuous wet-scraping techniques, and certified hazardous waste landfill disposal. Because disturbing dry asbestos releases millions of microscopic, carcinogenic fibers that linger in indoor air for days, understanding compliant disposal protocols is vital.

Assessing Whether You Truly Need to Remove It

When discovering asbestos in your home or facility, the initial instinct is often to rip it out immediately. However, public health organizations—including the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and the World Health Organization (WHO)—strongly advise that intact, undamaged asbestos materials pose virtually zero immediate danger to human health.

As long as materials like vinyl floor tiles, exterior transite siding, or acoustic ceiling plaster remain bonded, sealed, and undamaged, microscopic mineral fibers are trapped safely within the solid product matrix. Removal is necessary only when materials are crumbling, flaking, or water-damaged (friable), or when planned structural remodeling will inevitably crush and pulverize the material.

Professional Abatement vs. Amateur DIY Dangers

While some state laws allow single-family homeowners to remove asbestos from their private owner-occupied residence, attempting DIY abatement without commercial equipment is extraordinarily hazardous. Standard household shop vacuums lack sealed HEPA filtration; their powerful exhaust fans pulverize mineral dust and blow millions of invisible fibers into room air, contaminating furniture, carpets, and central HVAC ductwork.

Professional abatement contractors utilize specialized engineering controls designed to prevent cross-contamination. Technicians construct an airtight containment chamber using double layers of fire-retardant six-mil polyethylene sheeting, install industrial HEPA negative air scrubbers that exhaust outside the building, and pass through multi-stage decontamination airlocks where protective equipment is decontaminated before workers exit.

Legal Packaging, Transport, and Clearance Testing

Getting rid of asbestos waste requires compliance with EPA NESHAP and Department of Transportation (DOT) hazardous material transport rules. All asbestos debris must be saturated with surfactant water, placed into heavy six-mil polyethylene disposal bags, sealed with an airtight gooseneck duct tape fold, and placed into a secondary six-mil hazard bag with printed OSHA danger warnings.

Asbestos waste cannot be discarded in residential trash cans or standard construction dumpsters. It must be transported by licensed hazardous waste haulers accompanied by an official Waste Shipment Record (WSR) to an approved landfill permitted to bury asbestos. The final step involves hiring an independent industrial hygiene firm to conduct aggressive air clearance testing before containment barriers are taken down.

How to Safely Get Rid of Asbestos on Your Property

  1. Confirm Mineral Composition with Lab Test

    Collect a small wet sample wearing a P100 respirator and submit it to an NVLAP-accredited lab for Polarized Light Microscopy.

  2. Determine if Encapsulation is Viable

    If the material is intact and undamaged, cover it with new flooring or drywall to encapsulate the fibers without disturbance.

  3. Retain a Licensed Abatement Contractor

    If removal is necessary, hire a licensed abatement firm to establish negative pressure containment and HEPA filtration.

  4. Package Waste in Double 6-Mil Hazard Bags

    Ensure all wet debris is sealed in two separate six-mil poly bags with airtight gooseneck seals and OSHA warning labels.

  5. Verify Landfill Manifest and Air Clearance

    Receive a signed copy of the landfill Waste Shipment Record and pass an independent third-party air clearance test.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I throw asbestos away in my regular household trash?

No, disposing of asbestos in regular household trash is illegal under federal and state law, carrying severe civil and criminal penalties.

Q2: Is it better to remove or encapsulate asbestos?

If asbestos is intact and undamaged, the EPA recommends encapsulating it in place, which avoids fiber release and saves thousands of dollars.

Q3: How much does it cost to get rid of asbestos in a house?

Residential removal typically costs between $1,500 and $4,500 for single projects, with whole-house abatement reaching $10,000 to $20,000.

Q4: Can I use a regular shop vac to clean up asbestos dust?

No, standard shop vacs leak around seals and blow microscopic fibers into the air; only certified sealed HEPA vacuums are safe.

Q5: What is an airtight gooseneck seal?

A gooseneck seal involves twisting the neck of a plastic waste bag, folding it over on itself, and wrapping it tightly with duct tape.

Q6: How long does professional asbestos removal take?

Most residential projects, such as popcorn ceiling removal or pipe lagging abatement, take between one and three working days.

Q7: What should I do if I find broken asbestos in my basement?

Evacuate the area, turn off your HVAC system, avoid sweeping or vacuuming, and call a certified environmental abatement firm.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

When asking how can I get rid of asbestos, the safest answer is usually hiring a state-licensed abatement specialist or choosing non-disturbing encapsulation. Never attempt dry mechanical scraping, sanding, or household vacuuming near suspect materials. By prioritizing negative pressure containment, wet removal methods, and certified landfill disposal, you ensure your living environment remains completely safe.
