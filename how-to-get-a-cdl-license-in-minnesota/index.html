How to Get a CDL License in Minnesota?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

Obtaining a Commercial Driver's License in Minnesota opens lucrative career pathways across freight hauling, regional delivery, public transit, and heavy civil construction. Governed jointly by the Minnesota Department of Public Safety Driver and Vehicle Services division and the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration, obtaining a commercial license requires structured written testing, clinical medical certification, rigorous Entry-Level Driver Training, and a multi-part practical skills evaluation. Whether you intend to operate tractor-trailers, municipal buses, or hazardous material tankers, adhering to state statutory milestones ensures a seamless path to commercial credentialing.

Minnesota CDL Classes, Endorsements, and Federal Prerequisites

Prospective commercial operators in Minnesota must first understand the classification structure defining commercial motor vehicles across state and federal highways. A Class A commercial license is required to operate combination vehicles with a Gross Combination Weight Rating of 26,001 or more pounds, provided the vehicle being towed exceeds 10,000 pounds. A Class B license permits the operation of single vehicles with a Gross Vehicle Weight Rating of 26,001 or more pounds, or any such vehicle towing a trailer not in excess of 10,000 pounds. A Class C license applies to single or combination vehicles that do not meet Class A or B definitions but are designed to transport 16 or more passengers including the driver or haul placardable quantities of hazardous materials.

Every commercial candidate must be at least 18 years of age for intrastate driving within Minnesota boundaries or at least 21 years of age for interstate commerce and hazardous materials transportation. Drivers must hold a valid standard Minnesota Class D driver's license in good standing. Under federal law, candidates must undergo a medical examination conducted by a certified medical examiner registered on the National Registry of Certified Medical Examiners. Passing this physical yields a Medical Examiner's Certificate, commonly known as a DOT medical card, which must be self-certified with Minnesota Driver and Vehicle Services as non-excepted interstate, non-excepted intrastate, excepted interstate, or excepted intrastate status.

Entry-Level Driver Training and Minnesota DVS Testing Stages

Under the FMCSA Entry-Level Driver Training mandate codified in 49 CFR Part 380, all first-time CDL applicants, drivers upgrading their classification, and those seeking passenger, school bus, or hazardous materials endorsements must complete a certified training program. Training providers must be listed on the federal Training Provider Registry. The curriculum consists of two separate components: theoretical instruction covering federal regulations, vehicle systems, and advanced operating practices, alongside behind-the-wheel instruction encompassing range maneuvers and supervised public road driving.

Before beginning behind-the-wheel instruction, candidates must obtain a Commercial Learner's Permit by passing computerized knowledge examinations at an authorized Minnesota Driver and Vehicle Services exam station. Testing includes the General Knowledge test consisting of 50 questions, followed by specialized knowledge tests such as Air Brakes, Combination Vehicles, or Tankers. Once the permit is secured and recorded on the Minnesota state database, the driver must observe a mandatory 14-day holding period before scheduling their state skills examination. During this interval, behind-the-wheel training is completed under the direct supervision of a licensed commercial driver holding the appropriate class and endorsements.

The state skills road test is conducted by a state examiner or certified third-party testing organization and consists of three distinct phases. The applicant must first demonstrate mastery of the Pre-Trip Vehicle Inspection, explaining internal and external component safety checks across the engine compartment, steering linkages, braking systems, and coupling assemblies. Next, the driver executes Basic Vehicle Control maneuvers on a designated closed course, including straight-line backing, offset backing, and alley docking. Finally, the applicant undertakes the On-Road Driving test through real-world urban and highway traffic environments, evaluated on lane positioning, gear changes, defensive observations, bridge clearance awareness, and railroad crossing safety.

Minnesota DVS Paperwork Processing and CDL Card Issuance

Upon achieving passing marks across all three practical testing phases, the examiner submits electronic test scores into the Minnesota Driver and Vehicle Services system. The applicant must then present their test confirmation slip, valid Minnesota Class D driver's license, active DOT medical certificate, proof of United States citizenship or lawful permanent residency (such as a certified birth certificate or valid U.S. passport), and proof of Minnesota residential address to a full-service deputy registrar office.

Applicants adding a Hazardous Materials endorsement must also complete a Transportation Security Administration security threat assessment, including fingerprinting and federal background clearance, prior to credential issuance. Once fees are remitted, DVS issues a temporary paper driving credential authorizing commercial vehicle operation. The official laminated Minnesota Commercial Driver's License card arrives by postal mail within several weeks, valid for four years before requiring standard renewal and medical card recertification.

How to Obtain a Commercial Driver's License in Minnesota

A step-by-step roadmap for prospective drivers to satisfy FMCSA mandates, pass Minnesota DVS written and road tests, and secure an official commercial driver's license.

  1. Verify Eligibility and Secure DOT Medical Certification

    Confirm you are at least 18 years old for intrastate driving or 21 for interstate driving, hold a valid Minnesota Class D license, and undergo an examination by a registered medical professional to obtain an active DOT medical card.

  2. Study the Minnesota Commercial Driver's Manual

    Acquire the official Minnesota DVS commercial driver handbook and thoroughly review sections on General Knowledge, Air Brakes, Combination Vehicles, and any specific endorsements you plan to acquire.

  3. Pass Computerized Knowledge Exams for Your CLP

    Visit a Minnesota DVS exam station with required identity and residency documentation, pay examination fees, and successfully pass the computer-based knowledge tests to receive your Commercial Learner's Permit.

  4. Enroll in FMCSA Approved Entry-Level Driver Training

    Register with a certified training provider listed on the federal Training Provider Registry to complete required classroom theory and behind-the-wheel range and public road driving instruction.

  5. Pass the Three-Part Minnesota DVS Road Skills Test

    After holding your CLP for at least 14 days and finishing ELDT coursework, schedule and pass the Pre-Trip Inspection, Basic Control Maneuvers, and On-Road Driving exam at an authorized state testing site.

  6. Finalize Paperwork and Receive Your Commercial License

    Present your passing test results, identity verification documents, medical certificate, and license fees at a Minnesota deputy registrar office to receive your temporary permit and permanent CDL.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How long does it take to get a CDL license in Minnesota?

Most students complete the entire process within four to eight weeks, depending on whether they enroll in full-time or part-time Entry-Level Driver Training programs and the scheduling availability of Minnesota DVS road test appointments.

Q2: What is the minimum age to get a CDL in Minnesota?

The minimum age is 18 years old for an intrastate commercial license, which allows driving commercial vehicles exclusively within Minnesota state lines. Drivers must be at least 21 years old to transport cargo across state lines or haul hazardous materials.

Q3: Do I need to attend trucking school to get a CDL in Minnesota?

Yes, under federal Entry-Level Driver Training regulations, all individuals applying for a Class A or Class B CDL for the first time must complete theory and behind-the-wheel training through an approved provider registered with the FMCSA.

Q4: What tests are required to get a Minnesota commercial learner permit?

To earn a Commercial Learner's Permit, applicants must pass the 50-question General Knowledge test. Those seeking to operate vehicles with air brakes or combination trailers must also pass the Air Brakes and Combination Vehicles written tests.

Q5: How much does a Minnesota CDL license cost?

Initial state application fees range from approximately 37.25 to 57.25 dollars depending on classification, plus 10.50 dollars for the learner permit. Comprehensive commercial driving school tuition typically ranges between 3,000 and 7,000 dollars.

Q6: How long must you hold a commercial learner permit before taking the road test?

Federal and Minnesota state rules require drivers to hold their Commercial Learner's Permit for a minimum of 14 consecutive days before they are legally eligible to sit for the practical state skills road examination.

Q7: What happens if you fail the air brake test during the pre-trip inspection?

Failing any portion of the three-part air brake check during the pre-trip inspection results in an automatic, immediate failure of the skills examination because air brake integrity is considered a paramount vehicle safety system.

Q8: How long is a Minnesota commercial driver license valid?

A standard Minnesota CDL is valid for four years and expires on the licensee's birthday. Commercial drivers must keep their DOT medical card continuously updated and self-certified with the state to prevent commercial driving privilege cancellation.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Earning a Commercial Driver's License in Minnesota demands disciplined preparation, from passing comprehensive general knowledge exams to mastering complex air brake leak-down diagnostics and on-road safety maneuvers. By partnering with an accredited ELDT training facility, upholding rigorous DOT medical standards, and methodically studying official Minnesota DVS commercial manual guidelines, aspiring drivers can launch a rewarding, highly resilient career across regional logistics and interstate transportation.
