Whitelist a Domain Office 365

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

To whitelist a domain in Office 365 (Microsoft 365), you must configure Microsoft Defender for Office 365 using the Tenant Allow/Block List (TABL) or modify the Inbound Anti-Spam Policy in the Microsoft Defender security portal. For advanced routing, IT administrators can create Exchange Online mail flow transport rules while enforcing DMARC, DKIM, and SPF authentication to prevent email spoofing.

The Security Architecture of Exchange Online Protection (EOP)

In modern enterprise cloud administration, Microsoft 365 (formerly Office 365) serves as the primary corporate communications engine for hundreds of millions of enterprise users globally. Behind every corporate Outlook inbox operates Exchange Online Protection (EOP) and Microsoft Defender for Office 365—sophisticated, AI-driven email filtering pipelines that analyze billions of incoming messages daily.

EOP evaluates incoming email through multi-tiered filtering engines: analyzing IP reputation, sender domain authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), email content heuristics, zero-day attachment sandboxing (Safe Attachments), and URL rewriting (Safe Links). While this security apparatus blocks millions of malicious phishing and ransomware attacks, it can occasionally misclassify legitimate business communications from trusted vendors, partners, or client portals as spam or quarantine them.

When critical invoices, automated ERP notifications, or client emails are trapped in quarantine, system administrators must 'whitelist' the sender domain. However, blindly whitelisting domains without understanding modern Microsoft 365 security architecture introduces severe security vulnerabilities. Attackers frequently spoof trusted business domains to execute Business Email Compromise (BEC) attacks. The table below delineates the primary methods of whitelisting domains in Microsoft 365 and their security risk profiles.

Method 1: The Tenant Allow/Block List (Microsoft's Recommended Approach)

Historically, system administrators whitelisted domains by adding them to the Allowed Senders list in anti-spam policies. However, Microsoft has fundamentally re-engineered its email filtering stack, designating the Tenant Allow/Block List (TABL) as the primary, most secure administrative tool.

To configure a domain allow entry via the Tenant Allow/Block List, navigate to the Microsoft Defender Security Portal (https://security.microsoft.com) as a Global Administrator or Security Administrator. From the navigation pane, expand 'Email & Collaboration' and select 'Policies & Rules.' Click on 'Threat Policies' and select 'Tenant Allow/Block Lists' under the Rules section.

Click on the 'Domains & Addresses' tab, select 'Allow' (+), and enter the trusted domain (e.g., vendorcompany.com). You must specify an expiration date (Microsoft limits manual allows to 30 days unless extended) or link the entry to a formal False Positive submission. The decisive security advantage of the Tenant Allow/Block List is that it bypasses standard spam filtering while continuing to inspect incoming messages for dangerous attachments, malicious URLs, and high-confidence phishing exploits. Method 2: Configuring Inbound Anti-Spam Allowed Senders

If your enterprise requires a standing, non-expiring domain exception for trusted partner ecosystems, configuring the Inbound Anti-Spam Policy is the standard administrative protocol.

Within the Microsoft Defender portal (Threat Policies > Anti-Spam Policies), select the active 'Anti-Spam Inbound Policy (Default)' and click 'Edit allowed and blocked senders and domains.' Scroll down to the 'Domains' section and click 'Manage domains.' Here, you click 'Add domains' and input the domain name (such as partnerdomain.com). Save your settings.

Crucially, to prevent cyber criminals from spoofing this whitelisted domain, you must verify that the sender domain maintains valid email authentication records: Sender Policy Framework (SPF), DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM), and Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance (DMARC). If a cyber attacker attempts to send an email pretending to come from partnerdomain.com, but the message fails SPF and DMARC verification, Microsoft Defender's anti-spoofing intelligence will override your allow list and drop the fraudulent message into quarantine.

How to Whitelist a Domain in Microsoft Defender 365

Step-by-step IT administrator workflow to whitelist a sender domain in Microsoft 365.

  1. Log into the Microsoft Defender Security Admin Portal

    Open your browser and navigate to security.microsoft.com; log in using your Microsoft 365 Global Admin or Security Admin credentials.

  2. Navigate to Threat Policies and Anti-Spam Settings

    From the left menu, select 'Email & Collaboration' > 'Policies & Rules' > 'Threat Policies' > 'Anti-Spam Policies'.

  3. Edit the Inbound Anti-Spam Policy Allowed Domains

    Click on 'Anti-Spam Inbound Policy (Default)' and scroll down to 'Edit allowed and blocked senders and domains'.

  4. Add the Trusted Domain and Save Policy Configuration

    Click 'Manage domains' under Allowed Domains, click 'Add domains', input the domain (e.g., trustedpartner.com), and click 'Save'.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the difference between Tenant Allow List and Anti-Spam whitelist?

Tenant Allow/Block Lists provide temporary, granular security overrides; Anti-Spam policies provide permanent domain-level spam bypass rules.

Q2: Can an attacker spoof a whitelisted domain in Office 365?

Yes, if SPF/DMARC is not enforced; however, Microsoft Defender's anti-spoofing intelligence blocks spoofed messages even if the domain is whitelisted.

Q3: How long does it take for a whitelisted domain to take effect?

Changes made in the Microsoft Defender portal typically replicate across Microsoft's global Exchange servers within 15 to 30 minutes.

Q4: Can I whitelist a domain using Exchange Mail Flow Rules?

Yes, you can create a transport rule to set SCL (Spam Confidence Level) to -1, but this can bypass phishing checks and is not recommended by Microsoft.

Q5: Why are emails from my whitelisted domain still going to spam?

Emails will still be blocked if they contain known malware, high-confidence phishing links, or if the sender's IP is on an international blocklist.

Q6: Can individual users whitelist a domain in Outlook?

Yes. Users can add a domain to their individual 'Safe Senders' list in Outlook, which routes messages directly to their personal inbox.

Q7: How do I whitelist an entire top-level domain (like dot-com)?

Microsoft strictly blocks wildcard whitelisting (such as dot-com or dot-org) because it would dismantle enterprise email cybersecurity.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Whitelisting a domain in Office 365 ensures seamless, uninterrupted delivery of critical business emails while preserving enterprise cybersecurity. By utilizing modern Microsoft Defender Tenant Allow/Block Lists rather than blunt, unconditioned mail flow bypass rules, IT administrators prevent false positives without opening backdoors to phishing and malware attacks. Verify SPF, DKIM, and DMARC authentication on trusted sender domains, audit allow lists quarterly, and keep your enterprise communication pipeline fast, reliable, and secure.
