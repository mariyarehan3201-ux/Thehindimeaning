How to Handle a Stalled 'In Transit' Package and Recover Your Delivery

Step-by-step consumer checklist for resolving packages delayed in transit.

  1. Review the Last Physical Location Scan

    Scroll to the bottom of the tracking history to identify the last physical city and state where the package was scanned before moving.

  2. Allow a 2 to 3 Business Day Buffer for Weather

    If severe weather, blizzards, or hurricanes occurred between the origin and destination hubs, allow 72 hours for trucking lines to clear.

  3. Sign Up for Email or SMS Tracking Alerts

    Click 'Get Updates' on the USPS or carrier tracking page to receive instant notifications the second a new physical scan is logged.

  4. Submit a USPS Help Request Form Online

    If stalled for five days, visit usps.com/help to submit an online service inquiry; local postmasters will search sorting bins directly.

  5. Initiate an Official Missing Mail Search

    After seven days without tracking movement, submit a Missing Mail Search with a description of the box and invoice to recover the package.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the primary significance of what does in transit mean for usps?

When evaluating what does in transit mean for usps in practice, identifying real-world use cases, procedural steps, and common missteps is essential. In transit means USPS has received the package and it is actively traveling through the postal distribution network between sorting hubs to your city.

Q2: Does in transit mean out for delivery?

No. In transit means moving between cities or sorting hubs; 'Out for Delivery' means it is loaded onto the local mail truck for delivery today.

Q3: Why has my package been in transit for days without an update?

When parcels travel long distances on cross-country trucks, they cannot be scanned while sealed inside the trailer, leaving tracking paused for 2-3 days.

Q4: What does 'In Transit, Arriving Late' mean?

It is an automated message triggered when a package misses its scheduled delivery window due to transit delays, bad weather, or sorting bottlenecks.

Q5: How long can a package stay in transit before it is lost?

A package is generally considered missing if it receives zero scans for 7 business days for domestic mail, or 30 days for international shipments.

Q6: Can I pick up a package that is in transit?

No. You cannot intercept a package while in transit between sorting hubs; you must wait until it arrives at your local post office and request a hold.

Q7: What should I do if my package has been in transit for over a week?

Submit a Help Request Form on usps.com so customer service can contact the last scanned facility, or file an official Missing Mail Search.
