Defining Corp-to-Corp (C2C): Business-to-Business Contracting

In corporate law, staffing procurement, and commercial taxation, Corp-to-Corp (commonly abbreviated as C2C) refers to a commercial business-to-business (B2B) contractual agreement where one registered corporate business entity contracts services directly to another registered corporation.

In a C2C engagement, the individual consultant is not an employee of the hiring client. Instead, the consultant owns a legally incorporated entity—typically a Limited Liability Company (LLC) or an S-Corporation. The client company signs a Master Services Agreement (MSA) and Statement of Work (SOW) with the consultant corporate entity. Payments are remitted directly to the corporate bank account under a Federal Employer Identification Number (FEIN), completely bypassing standard individual payroll.

Tax Advantages, Business Deductions, and Corporate Liability Shielding

High-earning technical specialists, IT project managers, and executive consultants often prefer C2C arrangements because of substantial tax optimization opportunities. Operating through an S-Corporation allows consultants to pay themselves a reasonable W-2 salary while taking remaining corporate profits as business distributions, shielding distributions from the 15.3 percent self-employment (FICA) tax.

Furthermore, a C2C entity can deduct legitimate ordinary and necessary business expenses—including business travel, home office space, technology hardware, continuing education, and corporate health insurance premiums—before calculating taxable net income. Operating through an incorporated entity also creates a corporate liability shield, ensuring that business disputes or contractual liabilities do not jeopardize personal assets like family homes or personal bank accounts.

How to Establish an Authentic Corp-to-Corp (C2C) Consulting Entity

Follow this legal and financial roadmap to launch a compliant C2C contracting business.

  1. Register an LLC or Corporation with Your State

    File Articles of Organization or Incorporation with your Secretary of State to create a distinct legal business entity.

  2. Obtain a Federal Employer Identification Number (FEIN)

    Apply for a free FEIN directly from the IRS website; never use your personal Social Security Number for C2C billing.

  3. Open a Dedicated Business Checking Account

    Establish a separate business bank account; never commingle personal funds with corporate client revenues to preserve liability protections.

  4. Secure Commercial General and Professional Liability Insurance

    Purchase Errors and Omissions (E&O) and General Liability insurance policies, typically requiring $1 million to $2 million coverage minimums.

  5. Execute Master Services Agreements and Statements of Work

    Ensure client contracts are signed between both corporate entities with clear payment terms (Net 15 or Net 30) and deliverables.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does Corp-to-Corp mean?

Corp-to-Corp (C2C) is a business contract where a client company hires another registered business entity rather than an individual employee.

Q2: What is the difference between C2C and 1099?

1099 is paid to an individual sole proprietor; C2C is paid to an incorporated legal entity (LLC or Corporation) using a federal tax ID.

Q3: Why do companies prefer C2C contracts?

Companies avoid payroll tax withholding, health insurance liabilities, workers comp claims, and wrongful termination unemployment claims.

Q4: Can I do C2C with an LLC?

Yes. A registered Limited Liability Company (LLC) is one of the most common entity types used for C2C contracting.

Q5: Do C2C contractors get benefits?

No. C2C contractors do not receive health insurance, paid time off, or retirement matching from the client; they fund their own benefits.

Q6: What insurance do C2C contractors need?

Clients typically require $1 million in General Liability, $1 million in Errors and Omissions (E&O), and Workers Compensation insurance.

Q7: How do C2C contractors pay taxes?

The client pays the corporate entity gross without withholding; the contractor pays corporate and individual income taxes quarterly via the IRS.

Q8: Can foreign nationals on H-1B do C2C?

Generally, H-1B visa holders can only work for their petitioning employer; however, staffing agencies can subcontract them via C2C to end clients.
