DUI in Florida a Felony

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

In Florida, driving under the influence (DUI) is classified as a felony under four specific statutory conditions governed by Florida Statute § 316.193: a third DUI conviction within 10 years, a fourth or subsequent DUI regardless of timing, a DUI resulting in serious bodily injury, or a DUI resulting in death (DUI Manslaughter).

The Statutory Thresholds: When a Florida DUI Becomes a Felony

In the State of Florida, driving under the influence of alcohol or chemical substances is governed by Florida Statute Section 316.193 (Fla. Stat. § 316.193). For the vast majority of motorists arrested for an initial drunk driving offense, a Florida DUI is legally classified as a misdemeanor. First and second-offense DUIs carry painful administrative penalties, driver's license suspensions, probation, and potential county jail time up to six or nine months, but they do not permanently strip an individual of their basic civil rights.

However, the Florida Legislature and state prosecutors enforce an exceptionally aggressive zero-tolerance approach toward repeat offenders and impaired drivers who cause physical harm to the public. Under specific statutory circumstances codified in Fla. Stat. § 316.193, the criminal charge undergoes an immediate, severe statutory escalation from a county misdemeanor to a high-level Florida State Felony.

A felony DUI conviction permanently stains your public record, subjects you to multi-year incarceration in Florida state prison (not county jail), imposes mandatory minimum prison terms, results in permanent lifetime driver's license revocation, and strips you of fundamental constitutional civil liberties—including your right to vote and your Second Amendment right to own firearms. The table below delineates the four specific statutory conditions under Florida law that transform a DUI into a felony.

DUI with Serious Bodily Injury: Florida Statute § 316.193(3)(c)2

One of the most frequently prosecuted felony DUI charges in Florida courts is DUI Causing Serious Bodily Injury. Unlike repeat DUI charges—which require prior convictions—a motorist can be charged with felony DUI with serious bodily injury on their very first arrest, even with a previously spotless criminal record.

Under Florida law, 'serious bodily injury' is defined as a physical condition that creates a substantial risk of death, serious personal disfigurement, or protracted loss or impairment of the function of any bodily member or organ. If an impaired driver collides with another vehicle and an occupant suffers compound bone fractures requiring surgical titanium rods, a traumatic brain contusion, or a ruptured spleen, state prosecutors will immediately charge the driver with a Third-Degree Felony.

Under the Florida Criminal Punishment Code (CPC) sentencing scoresheet, DUI with serious bodily injury is classified as a Level 7 offense (scoring 56 base points, plus additional victim injury points). If a victim suffers severe injury, the mandatory minimum sentencing calculation under state sentencing guidelines routinely mandates a minimum state prison sentence of 21 to 36 months, even for a first-time offender with zero prior criminal history. DUI Manslaughter: The Ultimate Florida Felony Penalty

The most catastrophic DUI charge in Florida jurisprudence is DUI Manslaughter under Fla. Stat. § 316.193(3)(c)3. When an impaired driver causes or contributes to the death of any human being (including an unborn quick child), the charge is classified as a Second-Degree Felony punishable by up to 15 years in state prison.

Under Florida law, DUI Manslaughter carries a mandatory minimum prison sentence of four years (48 months) under the Florida Criminal Punishment Code. If the driver knew or should have known that a collision occurred and failed to give information or render aid (fleeing the scene in a hit-and-run), the offense escalates into a First-Degree Felony punishable by up to 30 years in state prison.

Furthermore, an individual convicted of DUI Manslaughter faces mandatory permanent lifetime driver's license revocation from the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (FLHSMV), with zero eligibility for any hardship business license for at least five years, alongside the installation of an ignition interlock device (IID) for life.

How to Build a Defense Against Florida Felony DUI Charges

Step-by-step legal defense protocol for an individual charged with felony DUI under Florida Statute § 316.193.

  1. Retain a Board-Certified Criminal Defense Trial Specialist

    Hire an attorney certified in criminal trial law who specializes specifically in high-stakes Florida felony DUI litigation.

  2. Subpoena Hospital Forced Blood Draw Protocols

    Challenge the legality of forced blood draws under the Fourth Amendment, verifying FDLE Rule 11D-8 chain of custody and medical blood conversions.

  3. Retain Independent Forensic Accident Reconstructionist

    Hire a mechanical engineer to laser-scan the crash site to prove the other driver's speeding or lane change caused the collision, defeating causation.

  4. Scrutinize Prior Out-of-State DUI Convictions

    If charged with a 3rd DUI within 10 years, audit whether prior out-of-state convictions qualify as substantially equivalent under Florida law.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is a first-time DUI in Florida a felony?

A standard first-time DUI is a misdemeanor; however, if the crash caused serious bodily injury or death to another person, it is charged as a felony immediately.

Q2: When does a third DUI become a felony in Florida?

Under Fla. Stat. § 316.193, a third DUI is a third-degree felony if the offense occurred within 10 years of a prior DUI conviction date.

Q3: What is the penalty for felony DUI with injury in Florida?

It is a third-degree felony punishable by up to 5 years in Florida state prison, up to $5,000 in fines, and a minimum 3-year driver's license revocation.

Q4: What is the mandatory minimum sentence for DUI Manslaughter in Florida?

Florida law imposes a strict mandatory minimum sentence of 4 years (48 months) in state prison, with a maximum sentence of up to 15 years.

Q5: Can you get a hardship license after a felony DUI in Florida?

For repeat felony DUIs or DUI manslaughter, a hardship license is heavily restricted and requires waiting at least 5 years with zero alcohol and an ignition interlock.

Q6: Can a Florida felony DUI charge be reduced to a misdemeanor?

Yes. An experienced defense lawyer can challenge blood alcohol testing and crash causation to negotiate a reduction down to misdemeanor reckless driving.

Q7: What is considered 'serious bodily injury' in a Florida DUI?

An injury creating substantial risk of death, serious personal disfigurement, or protracted loss/impairment of any bodily organ (such as fractures or brain bleeds).

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

In Florida, driving under the influence transitions into a devastating felony under strict statutory conditions: repeat convictions within lookback periods, causing serious bodily injury, or causing a fatal crash. A Florida felony DUI conviction results in mandatory state prison time, permanent loss of civil rights, and lifetime driver's license revocations. If you or a loved one faces felony DUI charges in Florida, retaining an elite board-certified criminal defense litigator immediately is essential to challenge blood draws, scrutinize crash reconstruction, and fight for your freedom.
