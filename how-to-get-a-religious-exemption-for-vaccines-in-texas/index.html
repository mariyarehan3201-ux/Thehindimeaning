How to Get a Religious Exemption for Vaccines in Texas?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

In the state of Texas, parents and adult students seeking exemption from mandatory immunization schedules for public, private, or charter school enrollment, child care centers, and institutions of higher learning possess clear statutory protections. Governed by the Texas Health and Safety Code and enforced through the Texas Department of State Health Services, Texas provides exemptions based on medical contraindications as well as 'reasons of conscience, including a religious belief.' However, obtaining an exemption is not accomplished by writing an informal personal note; applicants must request an official pre-printed state affidavit form, execute it under notarial seal, and submit it within strict statutory timelines.

Texas Statutory Framework for Immunization Exemptions

Immunization requirements for entry into Texas educational facilities and child care facilities are codified within the Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 161 and the Texas Education Code Section 38.001. Under Texas law, all students enrolled in Texas elementary or secondary schools, child care operations, and public universities must present verified proof of vaccination against preventable infectious diseases, including measles, mumps, rubella, polio, diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, hepatitis A, hepatitis B, and varicella.

However, the Texas Legislature enacted robust statutory provisions protecting individual and parental autonomy. Under Texas Health and Safety Code Section 161.0041, the state explicitly recognizes exemptions from mandatory immunizations for two distinct reasons: medical contraindications certified by an authorized physician, and 'reasons of conscience, including a religious belief.' While commonly referred to colloquially as a 'religious exemption,' the Texas statute deliberately incorporates religious objections under the broader, all-encompassing legal standard of 'reasons of conscience.' This statutory definition ensures that parents do not need to prove membership in a recognized ecclesiastical denomination to exercise their legal rights.

The Official Texas DSHS Affidavit Form and Submission Procedures

A critical procedural mandate under Texas law is that schools and daycares cannot accept photocopies, downloaded generic forms, or handwritten statements from parents claiming a religious exemption. The parent, legal guardian, or adult student must obtain the official, pre-printed 'Affidavit Request for Exemption from Immunizations for Reasons of Conscience' directly from the Texas Department of State Health Services (DSHS) in Austin. Each official affidavit form is printed on specialized, watermark-protected paper bearing an individual state serial tracking number.

Parents can request official DSHS affidavit forms through two methods: by submitting an online request via the secure Texas DSHS Immunization Section website, or by mailing a written request form to the DSHS Immunization Unit in Austin. The department allows applicants to request up to five official forms per child at no financial cost. Processing and delivery typically take between one and two weeks via United States postal mail. Once the physical pre-printed forms arrive, the parent or guardian must sign the document in the physical presence of a commissioned notary public, who administers the oath and affixes their official notarial seal.

School Compliance, 90-Day Submission Windows, and Outbreak Exclusions

Timing is crucial when executing an immunization exemption in Texas. Under administrative guidelines, the notarized DSHS affidavit must be submitted to the school nurse, registrar, or daycare administrator within ninety calendar days of the date it was notarized. If a parent has the document notarized but holds onto it for more than ninety days before submitting it to the school, the form becomes legally void, requiring the parent to start the entire process over with a fresh DSHS affidavit form.

Once properly submitted within the 90-day window, the exemption remains legally valid for exactly two years from the date of notarization. School administrators and registered nurses are statutorily required under Texas law to accept the valid affidavit; school districts cannot create their own local rules denying state-authorized conscience exemptions. However, parents must be aware of an important public health caveat: under Texas Health and Safety Code Section 161.0041(d), in the event of an active epidemic or emergency disease outbreak declared by the commissioner of health or local health authority, school districts have the legal authority to temporarily exclude all exempt, non-vaccinated students from school facilities until the outbreak subsides.

How to Obtain a Texas Conscience or Religious Vaccine Exemption

A step-by-step roadmap for requesting official DSHS affidavit forms, completing notarization, and filing with Texas schools.

  1. Request Official Forms from Texas DSHS

    Visit the Texas Department of State Health Services website to order official pre-printed Exemption for Reasons of Conscience affidavit forms.

  2. Await Postal Delivery of Watermarked Forms

    Allow 7 to 14 business days for DSHS to mail the serialized, watermarked affidavit forms to your Texas mailing address.

  3. Sign the Affidavit in the Presence of a Notary

    Take the physical form to a commissioned notary public, present a valid photo ID, and sign the document while the notary applies their official stamp.

  4. Submit Original Form to School Within 90 Days

    Deliver the original notarized affidavit to your child's school nurse, registrar, or daycare director within 90 days of the notarization date.

  5. Retain a Photocopy for Your Personal Records

    Make high-resolution photocopies of the notarized form to keep in your home files before surrendering the original to the school.

  6. Track the Two-Year Expiration Date for Renewal

    Mark your calendar for renewal, as Texas conscience exemptions expire exactly two years from the notarization date and require a new form.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does Texas have a specific religious exemption for vaccines?

Texas law covers religious objections under a broader statutory category called 'Reasons of Conscience, including a religious belief,' codified in Texas Health and Safety Code Section 161.0041.

Q2: Can I print a Texas vaccine exemption form from home?

No, Texas schools will reject printed or downloaded copies. You must obtain official, serialized, watermarked paper forms directly from the Texas Department of State Health Services.

Q3: How long does a Texas vaccine exemption affidavit last?

A Texas reasons of conscience immunization exemption affidavit is valid for exactly two years from the date it is signed and stamped by the notary public.

Q4: How long do you have to submit the affidavit after notarizing it?

You must submit the original notarized form to the school or child care facility within 90 calendar days of the date it was notarized, or it becomes legally void.

Q5: Can a Texas public school refuse to accept a valid DSHS affidavit?

No, Texas public schools and charter schools are legally mandated under state law to accept valid, properly notarized DSHS exemption affidavits.

Q6: How much does the Texas DSHS vaccine exemption affidavit cost?

The official affidavit forms are provided completely free of charge by the Texas Department of State Health Services, though standard notary fees (typically 6 to 10 dollars) apply.

Q7: Can exempt children be kept out of school during a disease outbreak?

Yes, under Texas law, if the state health commissioner or local health authority declares a disease emergency or outbreak, schools may temporarily exclude non-vaccinated students.

Q8: Do college students in Texas need a vaccine exemption?

Yes, students under 22 enrolling in Texas colleges must provide proof of the bacterial meningitis vaccine or submit an official DSHS higher education exemption form.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Securing an immunization exemption for reasons of conscience or religious belief in Texas is a well-established legal right that requires careful compliance with DSHS administrative rules. By ordering the official serialized forms in advance, signing before a licensed notary, and delivering the original document to school administrators within ninety days, parents and adult students can successfully fulfill Texas enrollment requirements while honoring their personal, philosophical, and religious convictions.
