Asbestos Training Michigan

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

Asbestos training Michigan is strictly regulated by the Michigan Occupational Safety and Health Administration (MIOSHA) Asbestos Program under Michigan Public Act 440 of 1988 (Asbestos Workers Accreditation Act) and Act 135 of 1986. Anyone seeking to inspect, manage, or physically remove asbestos in Michigan must complete accredited training courses approved under the federal EPA Model Accreditation Plan and obtain state certification.

The MIOSHA Asbestos Program and Public Act 440

In Michigan, environmental remediation and occupational safety oversight are administered by the MIOSHA Asbestos Program within the Michigan Department of Labor and Economic Opportunity (LEO). Under Michigan Public Act 440 of 1988, any individual who performs asbestos abatement, air monitoring, project design, or building inspection must complete state-accredited coursework and hold an active MIOSHA accreditation card. Contracting firms must also obtain a dedicated Michigan Asbestos Contractor License under Act 135.

Practicing without active MIOSHA accreditation is a serious regulatory violation, exposing contractors to stop-work orders, administrative civil penalties, and permanent revocation of business licensing. The MIOSHA Asbestos Program maintains active field enforcement across Detroit, Grand Rapids, Warren, Ann Arbor, and Lansing, auditing job sites to ensure containment barriers, negative pressure ventilation, and worker training credentials meet state standards.

Curriculum Requirements and Hands-On Workshop Protocols

Initial MIOSHA-approved training courses combine comprehensive classroom instruction with rigorous practical workshop simulations. In the 32-hour worker and 40-hour supervisor courses, students study mineralogical characteristics, health hazards and cellular latency, respiratory protection fit-testing, and waste shipment manifesting. A critical component is hands-on workshop training: students must construct functional three-stage decontamination airlocks, attach and manipulate glovebags on pressurized steam piping, and practice emergency spill containment.

Upon completing the training course, candidates must pass a state-approved multiple-choice examination demonstrating thorough knowledge of MIOSHA Part 602 (Asbestos Standards for Construction) and federal NESHAP regulations. Applicants must also submit documentation demonstrating physical fitness for respirator use through a certified physician’s medical clearance exam. Once issued, MIOSHA accreditation is valid for one year, requiring completion of an accredited annual refresher course to maintain active status.

Training agencies in Michigan operate accredited facilities across major metropolitan areas, including Detroit, Grand Rapids, Flint, and Lansing. Initial worker courses generally cost between $450 and $650, while supervisor courses range from $600 to $850, excluding state accreditation application fees.

Contractors should ensure their employees renew their training certificates prior to their annual expiration dates. Allowing training to lapse requires completing the entire multi-day initial course again.

How to Obtain Asbestos Certification in Michigan

Step-by-step roadmap for becoming a licensed asbestos professional in Michigan.

  1. Select a MIOSHA-Approved Training Provider

    Confirm that the vocational school or safety training academy holds active MIOSHA Asbestos Program accreditation.

  2. Complete the 32-Hour or 40-Hour Course

    Attend all mandatory classroom and hands-on workshops covering containment staging, wet-removal, and respirator safety.

  3. Obtain Physician Medical Clearance

    Undergo an OSHA-compliant occupational medical exam confirming physical fitness to wear tight-fitting negative-pressure respirators.

  4. Submit Application and Receive MIOSHA Card

    Pass the proctored exam and submit your training certificate, physician clearance, photo, and fee to MIOSHA to receive your card.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Who regulates asbestos training in Michigan?

The MIOSHA Asbestos Program within the Michigan Department of Labor and Economic Opportunity (LEO) regulates training and worker licensing.

Q2: How long is the Michigan asbestos worker training course?

The initial worker course is thirty-two hours (four days), while the asbestos supervisor course is forty hours (five days).

Q3: Can I take Michigan asbestos training online?

No. Initial licensing courses require mandatory hands-on workshop training and proctored testing that must be completed in person.

Q4: How much does asbestos training cost in Michigan?

Initial worker courses cost $450 to $650, supervisor courses cost $600 to $850, and 8-hour refresher courses cost $150 to $250.

Q5: How often must Michigan asbestos credentials be renewed?

Workers and supervisors must complete an approved eight-hour refresher course annually to maintain active state accreditation.

Q6: What is required to get a MIOSHA asbestos card?

You must complete an accredited course, pass the examination, submit a physician's medical clearance, and pay the state licensing fee.

Q7: Can an out-of-state asbestos license transfer to Michigan?

Michigan grants reciprocity for EPA MAP courses from other states, provided applicants submit valid certificates and apply for a MIOSHA card.

Q8: What happens if you work without asbestos certification in Michigan?

Unlicensed work results in immediate job shutdown, administrative civil penalties exceeding $1,000 per day, and corporate licensing revocation.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Completing accredited asbestos training Michigan is the only lawful path to working in Michigan's environmental remediation sector. By enrolling in MIOSHA-approved courses and securing state accreditation, environmental technicians ensure safe worksites and advance their professional trade careers.
