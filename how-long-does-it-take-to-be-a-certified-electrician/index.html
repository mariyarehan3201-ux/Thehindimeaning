How Long Does It Take to Be a Certified Electrician?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how long does it take to be a certified electrician is an essential career planning inquiry for ambitious individuals seeking a highly paid, recession-proof, and respected skilled trade. Electrical work involves complex physics, high-voltage safety protocols, and strict adherence to the National Electrical Code (NEC). To become a fully licensed, certified Journeyman Electrician typically takes between 4 and 5 years, requiring an accumulation of 8,000 hours of documented on-the-job training (OJT) alongside hundreds of hours of mandatory classroom instruction.

The 8,000-Hour Rule: The Journeyman Apprenticeship Framework

In virtually every US state, electrical licensing boards mandate the rigorous '8,000-Hour Rule.' To earn a Journeyman Electrician license, candidates must complete an accredited apprenticeship program combining 8,000 hours of paid on-the-job training under the direct supervision of licensed journeymen and master electricians.

Working a standard full-time schedule of 40 hours per week (roughly 2,000 working hours per year), accumulating 8,000 hours takes exactly four full calendar years. Furthermore, state licensing boards mandate that apprentices must simultaneously complete between 576 and 900 hours of related technical classroom instruction (typically 144 to 180 hours per year) covering electrical theory, blue-print reading, conduit bending math, motor controls, and National Electrical Code (NEC) regulations.

Union vs. Non-Union Pathways: IBEW vs. IEC / ABC

Aspiring electricians typically pursue certification through one of two primary educational structures: union or non-union apprenticeships. The union route is administered by the International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers (IBEW) and the National Electrical Contractors Association (NECA) through the Electrical Training Alliance (JATC).

Union apprenticeships are structured as comprehensive 5-year programs. They are highly competitive, requiring aptitude testing in algebra and reading comprehension, but offer unmatched benefits, guaranteed scheduled wage increases every 1,000 hours, pension contributions, and full employer-paid healthcare. Conversely, non-union apprenticeships—coordinated by the Independent Electrical Contractors (IEC) or Associated Builders and Contractors (ABC)—typically follow a 4-year curriculum, allowing apprentices to gain diverse residential and commercial experience with independent electrical contractors.

The Journeyman Licensing Examination: Mastering the NEC

After completing 8,000 hours of field experience and all classroom modules, apprentices reach the final milestone: the State Journeyman Electrician Licensing Exam. This rigorous 4-to-5-hour open-book examination is administered by state licensing boards or third-party testing services (like PSI or Pearson VUE).

The exam tests speed and mastery of the National Electrical Code (NFPA 70). Candidates must quickly navigate code articles to calculate branch circuit conductor sizing, box fill calculations, motor overcurrent protection ratings, conduit bending fill tables, and transformer grounding specifications. Passing the exam (typically requiring a score of 70% or 75%) officially grants your Journeyman license, giving you the legal right to work independently without supervision anywhere in the state.

How to Become a Certified Journeyman Electrician

Follow these five strategic steps to enter an apprenticeship, accumulate hours, and pass the licensing exam.

  1. Meet Basic Entry Educational Requirements

    Ensure you have a high school diploma or GED with a passing grade of 'C' or higher in high school algebra.

  2. Apply to a Local Apprenticeship Program (IBEW or IEC)

    Submit applications to local JATC union halls or IEC non-union contractor chapters, passing the aptitude test.

  3. Complete 8,000 Hours of Paid On-the-Job Training

    Work full-time for 4 to 5 years under licensed electricians, logging hours across residential, commercial, and industrial sites.

  4. Attend Mandatory Technical Classroom Lectures

    Complete at least 576 to 900 hours of evening or weekend classroom instruction in electrical theory and the NEC.

  5. Pass the State Journeyman Licensing Examination

    Study NEC code calculation handbooks and pass the state open-book proctored exam to receive your certification.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can you become an electrician in 2 years?

You can earn an associate degree in 2 years, but state licensing boards still require 8,000 hours (4 years) of on-the-job training to earn a journeyman license.

Q2: Do you get paid while doing an electrical apprenticeship?

Yes, electrical apprentices earn competitive hourly wages from Day 1, starting around $18-$23/hr and receiving raises every 1,000 hours.

Q3: What math do you need to be an electrician?

Basic high school algebra, geometry, and trigonometry are essential for calculating electrical loads, voltage drops, and conduit bending angles.

Q4: What is the difference between a journeyman and a master electrician?

A journeyman works independently under an employer; a master electrician has 2-4 years more experience and can pull permits and own an electrical business.

Q5: Is the electrical journeyman exam open book?

Yes, the journeyman exam is open-book, but tests your ability to rapidly index and calculate complex formulas from the National Electrical Code (NEC).

Q6: Is becoming an electrician hard on your body?

It is physically demanding: electricians crawl through attics, pull heavy wire, bend conduit, and work on ladders in all weather conditions.

Q7: How much does a licensed journeyman electrician make per year?

Journeyman electricians earn between $70,000 and $110,000+ annually, with significant overtime and commercial prevailing wage opportunities.

Q8: Does an electrical license transfer between states?

Many states have reciprocity agreements allowing journeyman licenses to transfer without re-testing, provided requirements match.
