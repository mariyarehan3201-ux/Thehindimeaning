How to Get Rid of Asbestos?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Knowing how to get rid of asbestos safely is one of the most critical responsibilities a property owner can face during residential renovations or commercial property maintenance. Because asbestos minerals were woven into over three thousand diverse building materials—including pipe lagging, textured ceilings, floor tiles, siding shingles, and roofing felts—disturbing these items without proper containment can contaminate your entire property with invisible, carcinogenic fibers. Safely eliminating asbestos requires a disciplined, step-by-step approach involving professional testing, risk assessment, workspace containment under negative air pressure, specialized wet removal techniques, and legally compliant hazardous disposal.

Assessing Friability and Deciding Between Removal and Encapsulation

The initial phase of getting rid of asbestos is determining whether complete physical extraction is truly necessary. Environmental agencies, including the EPA, emphasize that undisturbed, undamaged asbestos-containing materials are often safer left alone or encapsulated than disturbed. Non-friable products like vinyl composition floor tiles or exterior cement transite siding do not emit fibers while solid. In such scenarios, encapsulation—such as applying an elastomeric sealant or covering the floor with new floating laminate—permanently locks fibers in place with zero airborne danger.

Physical removal becomes non-negotiable when materials are friable (such as deteriorated popcorn ceilings, loose vermiculite attic insulation, or frayed pipe elbow lagging) or when planned structural renovations will inevitably cut, saw, or shatter the hazardous surfaces. Once removal is designated, property owners must understand that standard household vacuum cleaners, domestic fans, and casual sweeping must never be employed, as these actions merely aerosolize deadly microscopic dust.

Engineering Controls: Containment Chambers and HEPA Filtration

When executing complete asbestos abatement, establishing rigorous engineering controls is paramount. Abatement technicians isolate the contaminated area from the rest of the building by hanging two layers of six-mil flame-retardant polyethylene sheeting across all doorways, windows, wall vents, and HVAC registers. An airtight three-stage decontamination chamber—comprising a dirty room, shower room, and clean room—is erected at the entrance to ensure workers can decontaminate and change before leaving the work zone.

Crucially, the workspace is placed under continuous negative air pressure utilizing industrial HEPA air scrubbers. These machines extract air from the containment chamber, pass it through certified 99.97% HEPA filters, and exhaust the clean air to the building exterior. This pressure differential ensures that if a seal breaches, ambient outdoor air rushes inward into the containment zone, preventing any escaped asbestos fibrils from migrating into occupied living areas of the property.

Safe Extraction, Waste Bagging, and Clearance Verification

The actual extraction of asbestos materials demands gentle, wet handling techniques. Workers spray all target surfaces with surfactant-amended water to saturate the fibers completely before carefully prying, unbolting, or cutting components. Saturated debris is transferred immediately into heavy, OSHA-labeled six-mil plastic bags. Heavy or sharp items like transite siding or pipe sections are double-wrapped in six-mil poly sheeting and taped securely with duct tape to prevent tearing.

Once all hazardous materials have been stripped, the entire containment zone undergoes meticulous decontamination. Technicians vacuum all framing, subflooring, and plastic barriers using certified HEPA vacuums, followed by thorough wet-wiping with clean disposable rags. After a settling period, an independent environmental consultant performs aggressive air clearance sampling using Phase Contrast Microscopy (PCM) or Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM). Only after air samples demonstrate fiber concentrations well below 0.01 fibers per cubic centimeter is containment dismantled.

How to Safely Get Rid of Asbestos in 5 Steps

Follow these five certified procedures to test, contain, remove, and verify the complete elimination of asbestos hazards.

  1. Commission an Accredited Asbestos Inspection

    Hire an independent licensed inspector to take core samples and identify the exact location and friability of asbestos materials.

  2. Establish Containment and Negative Air Pressure

    Seal the work zone with double 6-mil poly barriers and operate HEPA negative air scrubbers exhausted to the outdoors.

  3. Saturate and Manually Strip Materials

    Spray all target products thoroughly with amended soapy water and pry them loose without using power tools or dry scraping.

  4. Double-Bag and Seal Hazardous Debris

    Pack wet debris into double 6-mil OSHA-labeled hazardous waste bags, goose-neck tape the closures, and wipe down bag exteriors.

  5. Pass Independent Aggressive Air Clearance

    Engage a third-party environmental consultant to verify that airborne fiber levels are below 0.01 fibers/cc before tearing down containment.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can I get rid of asbestos myself without hiring a contractor?

While single-family homeowners are legally allowed to remove asbestos in some states, doing so without specialized HEPA equipment poses extreme health risks.

Q2: Is it better to remove asbestos or encapsulate it?

If asbestos is intact and undamaged, encapsulation is generally safer and less expensive because it avoids disturbing dangerous airborne fibers.

Q3: What is the safest way to dispose of asbestos debris?

Asbestos debris must be saturated with water, sealed in double 6-mil poly bags, labeled with OSHA warnings, and taken to a permitted hazardous landfill.

Q4: How much does it cost to get rid of asbestos in a house?

Whole-house asbestos removal typically costs between 2,500 and 12,000 dollars depending on square footage, materials involved, and accessibility.

Q5: Can standard vacuum cleaners pick up asbestos dust?

No, standard vacuum cleaners cannot trap microscopic asbestos fibers and will exhaust millions of carcinogenic particles directly into the air.

Q6: What is amended water in asbestos abatement?

Amended water is water mixed with a chemical surfactant (soap) that lowers surface tension, allowing it to penetrate and bind asbestos fibers.

Q7: How do you know if all asbestos is gone after removal?

A licensed third-party environmental consultant conducts a visual inspection and aggressive air clearance testing to ensure airborne fiber counts are safe.

Q8: What permits are required to remove asbestos?

In most jurisdictions, contractors must file an EPA NESHAP notification at least ten business days before commencing regulated asbestos removal.
