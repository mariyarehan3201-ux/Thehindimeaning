How Much Is a Oil Change for a Ferrari?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Understanding how much is a oil change for a ferrari requires examining exotic dry-sump lubrication architectures, labor-intensive underbody aerodynamic disassembly, specialized OEM lubricants, and factory scheduled maintenance programs. At an authorized Ferrari dealership, an annual oil change service typically costs between $700 and $1,800, with specialized V12 hypercars or classic gated-shifter models occasionally commanding upwards of $2,500. Independent exotic automotive specialists standardly perform identical maintenance for $500 to $1,000. However, many modern Ferrari owners pay zero out-of-pocket for routine oil services thanks to the brand's complimentary seven-year factory maintenance warranty.

Exotic Engineering: Dry-Sump Systems, Multiple Plugs, and Aerodynamic Panels

Changing the engine oil on a Ferrari is fundamentally more complicated than servicing a standard passenger car. Modern Ferraris—such as the 458 Italia, 488 GTB, F8 Tributo, and SF90 Stradale—utilize high-performance dry-sump lubrication systems engineered to prevent oil starvation during violent high-G cornering on race tracks. Unlike conventional wet-sump engines with a single drain plug at the bottom of the engine, a Ferrari dry-sump engine stores oil in an external reservoir tank and incorporates multiple magnetic drain plugs across the crankcase and oil tank, each requiring meticulous draining and new copper crush washers.

Furthermore, accessing these drain points requires substantial labor to dismantle intricate aerodynamic underbody diffusers. Mechanics must hoist the vehicle on specialized low-profile lifts and remove dozens of precision fasteners, Torx bolts, and carbon-fiber undertray undertrays to access the engine block and oil filter housings. The procedure also requires filling with 10 to 14 quarts of specialized synthetic oil (such as Shell Helix Ultra 5W-40 or Pennzoil Platinum Euro) and warming the engine to precise operating temperatures before checking oil levels via digital dipsticks or onboard telemetry while the engine is idling.

The Ferrari 7-Year Genuine Maintenance Program Advantage

A remarkable fact that surprises many prospective supercar buyers is that routine oil changes for newer Ferraris are often completely free. Beginning with the 2012 model year, Ferrari introduced its groundbreaking 'Ferrari Genuine Maintenance' program. Every new Ferrari sold through an authorized global dealership network includes seven years of complimentary factory scheduled annual maintenance, covering all standard annual oil changes, genuine oil filters, brake fluid renewals, auxiliary drive belts, and thorough computerized diagnostic health scans.

Crucially, this seven-year maintenance coverage is legally attached to the vehicle's unique Chassis Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) rather than the original purchaser. If you purchase a four-year-old pre-owned Ferrari 488 or Roma that was originally delivered with the Genuine Maintenance package, the remaining three years of free annual services transfer automatically to you as the subsequent owner. This transferable benefit saves owners thousands of dollars and ensures the supercar maintains an unbroken provenance of authorized dealer maintenance records.

Labor Rates, Specialty Tools, and Provenance Value

For Ferraris that have aged past the seven-year factory program, maintenance costs are driven heavily by authorized dealership technician hourly labor rates. Elite franchised Ferrari dealers in major markets like Los Angeles, Miami, Greenwich, or Dallas bill shop labor between $250 and $450 per hour. Changing the oil, properly draining the multiple dry-sump plugs, removing undertrays, torquing fasteners to exact factory foot-pound specifications, and executing a digital system scan requires 2.5 to 4 billed labor hours.

While an enthusiast mechanic might be tempted to perform a DIY oil change in their garage, doing so on a Ferrari carries substantial financial downside. Exotic car buyers demand immaculate, documented service records; a Ferrari lacking professional stamped service books or digital dealer records suffers immediate depreciation of $10,000 to $30,000 in secondary resale value. Furthermore, connecting to the car's proprietary electronic control units to clear maintenance alerts requires factory diagnostic consoles (Ferrari DEIS or Leonardo systems) that cost tens of thousands of dollars, making independent specialist or dealer service well worth the expenditure.

How to Properly Plan and Service Engine Oil on a Ferrari

Follow these five vital steps to verify maintenance warranty coverage and preserve supercar provenance.

  1. Verify 7-Year Genuine Maintenance Eligibility

    Contact an authorized Ferrari dealer with your VIN to verify if complimentary factory maintenance remains active.

  2. Locate a Factory-Trained Technician

    Choose an authorized Ferrari dealership or an independent specialist employing Ferrari Master certified mechanics.

  3. Insist on OEM Filters and Approved Fluids

    Verify that only Shell Helix Ultra 5W-40 or Pennzoil Platinum Euro and genuine Ferrari Purflux filters are used.

  4. Require Proper Multi-Plug Dry-Sump Draining

    Ensure the technician removes all crankcase and oil tank plugs and installs fresh copper crush seals.

  5. Stamp and Archive the Factory Service Booklet

    Ensure the dealership officially stamps your physical maintenance book and logs the service in the global Ferrari Modis database.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does an oil change cost on a Ferrari?

At an authorized dealership, it costs between $700 and $1,800; independent exotic specialists typically charge $500 to $1,000.

Q2: Are Ferrari oil changes free for seven years?

Yes, all Ferraris sold from the 2012 model year onward include 7 years of free annual scheduled maintenance attached to the VIN.

Q3: How often does a Ferrari need an oil change?

Ferrari mandates an engine oil and filter change once every 12 months or every 12,500 miles, whichever comes first.

Q4: Why is a Ferrari oil change so expensive?

Dry-sump systems have multiple drain plugs, require removing extensive aerodynamic undertrays, take 3+ labor hours, and use specialized synthetic oil.

Q5: What kind of oil goes into a modern Ferrari?

Modern Ferraris utilize factory-approved Shell Helix Ultra 5W-40 or Pennzoil Platinum Euro full-synthetic motor oil.

Q6: Does changing your own oil hurt Ferrari resale value?

Yes, supercars without documented dealer or recognized specialist service stamps suffer severe value penalties on the secondary market.

Q7: Can independent mechanics service modern Ferraris?

Yes, specialized independent shops with expensive diagnostic computers (like Leonardo scanners) can perform factory-spec services.

Q8: How much oil does a Ferrari engine hold?

Ferrari dry-sump V8 and V12 engines typically hold between 10 and 15 quarts (9.5 to 14 liters) of synthetic oil.
