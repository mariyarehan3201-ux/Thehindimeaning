WD Category Full Form: Widow Destitute Quota

Full Form
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 18, 2026

The full form of WD category in government recruitment, public service examinations, and social welfare schemes stands for Widow and Destitute category (or Widow / Divorced / Destitute category). It represents an affirmative action reservation quota and welfare classification established by government bodies to provide age relaxations, fee concessions, and reserved public employment opportunities for socially and economically vulnerable women.

Understanding WD Category: Widow Destitute Quota

Public administration and constitutional welfare governance in India place immense emphasis on affirmative action to empower historically marginalized and socially disadvantaged communities. Within public service recruitment examinations, higher education entrance admissions, and social security pension allotments, the WD category (Widow and Destitute category) provides a legally mandated quota system that protects women who have faced the loss of their spouse or severe economic abandonment.

The core objective of creating the WD reservation quota is to foster financial self-reliance and dignified livelihoods. Women facing the sudden demise of a husband or marital dissolution frequently encounter profound economic vulnerability, loss of shelter, and social ostracism. By designating a horizontal or vertical reservation quota in competitive recruitment examinations—such as state Public Service Commissions (PSC), Subordinate Staff Selection Boards (SSSC), railway boards, and municipal corporations—the state guarantees structured access to stable public sector employment.

The structural benefits provided under the WD reservation quota category span multiple employment and administrative provisions:

Technical Specifications and Operational Comparison

Qualifying under the WD category requires strict evidentiary compliance to prevent fraudulent reservation claims. Applicants must furnish statutory legal certificates issued by designated revenue authorities, executive magistrates, or judicial courts:

Sub-Category Status Required Statutory Document Competent Issuing Authority
Widow (WD) Official Death Certificate of Husband + Marriage Proof Municipal Registrar of Births and Deaths / Gram Panchayat
Divorced Woman (DV) Final Decree of Dissolution of Marriage Competent Family Court / Civil District Court
Destitute / Abandoned Woman Destitute Certificate proving abandonment & no income Revenue Divisional Officer (RDO) / Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM)
Non-Remarriage Declaration Self-sworn affidavit certifying non-remarriage status Notary Public / Judicial Magistrate First Class

In addition to competitive job recruitment, the WD classification is universally applied in state-level social safety nets, including the Indira Gandhi National Widow Pension Scheme (IGNWPS), food security ration card priority allocations, and state-subsidized vocational skill training initiatives.

How to Apply for Benefits Under the WD Reservation Category

Follow these official steps to obtain the necessary certifications and apply for government recruitment under the Widow and Destitute category.

  1. Obtain Official Death Certificate or Divorce Decree

    Secure certified original copies of the husband's death certificate from the registrar, or the final divorce judgment from the family court.

  2. Draft and Notarize Non-Remarriage Affidavit

    Prepare a sworn legal affidavit on non-judicial stamp paper stating that you have not remarried after the death or divorce.

  3. Apply for Destitute / Widow Certificate at Tehsildar Office

    Submit identity documents, ration card, and death/divorce proofs to the local Tehsildar or SDM office to receive the official certificate.

  4. Select WD Category in Recruitment Portal

    During online recruitment registration, select the 'WD' or 'Widow / Divorced' category checkbox and upload the certificate numbers.

  5. Present Original Documents During Document Verification

    Produce all original certificates, marriage verification, and the latest non-remarriage affidavit before the selection board during final verification.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the full form of WD category?

The full form of WD category in public recruitment and welfare schemes is Widow and Destitute category (or Widow / Divorced).

Q2: Who is eligible to claim reservation under the WD category?

Legally widowed women, court-decreed divorced women, and certified destitute or abandoned women with no independent financial support.

Q3: What age relaxation is typically granted under the WD quota?

Most state recruitment boards grant age relaxations of 5 to 10 years, frequently raising the upper age eligibility limit up to 45 years.

Q4: Is an affidavit required to prove non-remarriage?

Yes, candidates must submit a notarized affidavit affirming that they have not remarried as of the date of application and appointment.

Q5: Does the WD quota apply horizontally or vertically?

It generally applies as a horizontal reservation across vertical social categories (General, OBC, SC, ST, EWS).

Q6: What happens if a WD category candidate remarries after selection?

Once legally selected and confirmed in service, post-appointment marital status changes do not disqualify the employee from their position.

Q7: Can divorced women apply under the WD category?

Yes, many state boards combine Widow and Divorced (WD/DV) applicants under the same horizontal affirmative action umbrella.

Q8: Who issues the official Destitute Certificate in India?

The certificate is issued by designated revenue authorities, typically the Revenue Divisional Officer (RDO), Tehsildar, or Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM).

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The WD category (Widow and Destitute category) is an essential affirmative action framework that upholds social justice and gender equity. By providing relaxation in qualifying marks, age extensions, and designated public job quotas, the WD category enables vulnerable women to rebuild their lives with dignity, security, and economic independence.
