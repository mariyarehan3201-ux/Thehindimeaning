How Much Is a DNA Test in NY?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Navigating how much is a DNA test in NY involves understanding New York distinct and stringent public health statutes governing genetic testing. Unlike almost all other states, New York prohibits direct-to-consumer at-home DNA paternity kits without authorized medical or legal orders. A legal, court-admissible paternity test in New York ranges from $300 to $600 at clinical collection facilities certified by the New York State Department of Health (NYS DOH). Non-invasive prenatal paternity testing (NIPP) during pregnancy typically costs between $1,300 and $2,000, requiring a physician prescription or judicial authorization.

New York Stringent Genetic Testing Laws and Doctor Prescriptions

New York State enforces some of the most rigorous diagnostic regulations in the nation under Section 576-b of the New York State Public Health Law. Under these provisions, diagnostic genetic testing laboratories cannot analyze biological specimens collected within New York State unless the test is ordered by a licensed physician, physician assistant, nurse practitioner, or ordered directly by a Family Court or Supreme Court judge. Furthermore, the laboratory performing the assay must hold a specific clinical laboratory permit issued by the Wadsworth Center of the NYS DOH.

Because of these statutory constraints, retail drugstores and online providers are legally prohibited from shipping over-the-counter peace-of-mind paternity kits to New York addresses. Direct-to-consumer kits sent from out-of-state laboratories without New York DOH licensure are subject to severe civil fines. Consequently, anyone seeking a DNA paternity test in New York must undergo a formal clinical collection process, establishing legal chain of custody from inception and resulting in baseline costs between $300 and $600.

New York Family Court Proceedings and Child Support Services

In New York Family Court, paternity proceedings are filed under Article 5 of the Family Court Act. When parentage is contested in child support or custody disputes, the Family Court judge or Support Magistrate issues an official order directing the mother, putative father, and child to submit to genetic marker testing under Family Court Act Section 532. The court directs parties to an authorized specimen collection site, often situated directly within or adjacent to the county family courthouse.

If the child receives public assistance or the custodial parent uses New York Child Support Services (OCSS), the local social services district arranges testing through its contracted laboratory provider. The initial testing fee is subsidized by the state. If the genetic testing report establishes paternity with a probability of 95 percent or higher, statutory presumption of paternity is triggered. The court then issues an Order of Filiation and may require the adjudicated biological father to reimburse the county testing cost, usually between $35 and $85.

Prenatal Options and Amending New York Birth Records

Expectant parents in New York seeking prenatal parentage testing can access non-invasive prenatal paternity testing (NIPP) for $1,300 to $2,000. Because New York law requires a physician prescription, an obstetrician or gynecologist must authorize the laboratory requisition slip. The test isolates fetal cell-free DNA from maternal venous blood and correlates it with paternal cheek swabs. Once approved, specialized phlebotomy facilities draw the blood and transfer specimens directly to NYS DOH-licensed molecular genetics laboratories.

Upon obtaining a certified genetic testing report and a Family Court Order of Filiation, parents can amend the child official New York birth certificate. In New York City, birth records are managed independently by the NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOHMH), whereas all other counties are handled by the New York State Department of Health in Albany. Processing fees range from $30 to $40, and the amended certificate establishes full inheritance rights, health coverage eligibility, and parental rights under New York law.

How to Obtain a Legal DNA Paternity Test in New York State

Follow these five necessary steps to navigate New York legal requirements and secure an authorized DNA test.

  1. Obtain a Physician Prescription or Court Directive

    Consult a licensed healthcare provider for a written testing order or obtain an official order from a Family Court Support Magistrate.

  2. Verify New York State DOH Laboratory Certification

    Ensure the diagnostic genetics laboratory holds an active clinical testing permit from the Wadsworth Center.

  3. Schedule Supervised Specimen Collection

    Book an appointment at an authorized New York clinical collection site that complies with chain-of-custody identification rules.

  4. Present Photo IDs and Complete Swabbing

    Provide government identification for adults, allow photo documentation, and have an independent technician collect inner-cheek swabs.

  5. Receive Official Notarized Parentage Report

    Obtain the certified laboratory findings to submit to New York Family Court or the Bureau of Vital Records.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Why can you not buy an at-home DNA test in New York?

New York State Public Health Law Section 576-b prohibits direct-to-consumer testing without a prescription from a licensed doctor or a court order.

Q2: How much does a legal DNA test cost in New York?

A legal, court-admissible paternity test in New York ranges from $300 to $600 due to mandatory clinical oversight and NYS DOH licensing.

Q3: Do you need a doctor prescription for a DNA test in NY?

Yes, all genetic tests performed in New York require a written prescription from a licensed healthcare provider unless ordered directly by a judge.

Q4: How does New York Family Court handle DNA testing costs?

The court or child support agency arranges testing upfront, but the adjudicated father may be ordered to repay $35 to $85 if confirmed.

Q5: What is an Order of Filiation in New York?

An Order of Filiation is an official Family Court judicial ruling declaring a man to be the legal and biological father of a child.

Q6: How much is a prenatal DNA test in New York State?

Non-invasive prenatal paternity testing (NIPP) in New York costs between $1,300 and $2,000 and requires an obstetrician or physician requisition.

Q7: How long does it take to receive DNA results in New York?

Certified legal paternity testing results typically take three to five business days after all specimens reach the laboratory.

Q8: What laboratory certification is required in New York?

Laboratories must hold a clinical testing permit from the New York State Department of Health Wadsworth Center and AABB accreditation.
