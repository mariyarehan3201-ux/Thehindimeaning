Defining LGSW: The Licensed Graduate Social Worker Credential

In professional social work, clinical mental health counseling, and state healthcare licensing regulation, the acronym LGSW stands for Licensed Graduate Social Worker. It represents a recognized master's-level professional regulatory license granted by state social work licensing boards (such as in Maryland, Minnesota, West Virginia, and Washington, D.C.) to social workers who have graduated with a master's degree and passed national board examinations.

The LGSW credential serves as the essential bridge between completing an accredited Master of Social Work (MSW) university program and attaining fully independent, autonomous clinical licensure (such as LCSW, LICSW, or LCSW-C). While an LGSW possesses advanced clinical training and legal authority to provide mental health therapy and psychosocial case management, state statutes mandate that they must practice under the supervision of a fully licensed clinical supervisor.

Supervised Clinical Hours: The Bridge to Autonomous Practice (LCSW)

For an LGSW, the primary career milestone is accumulating the mandatory post-graduate supervised clinical hours required to sit for the Association of Social Work Boards (ASWB) Clinical examination. Depending on state statutory boards, an LGSW must log between 3,000 and 4,000 hours of professional social work practice—including 100 to 150 hours of direct, one-on-one clinical supervision with a board-approved supervisor—over a two-to-four-year timeframe.

During this supervised residency phase, LGSWs gain immersive clinical experience diagnosing complex psychiatric disorders using the DSM-5, formulating individualized psychosocial treatment plans, executing evidence-based modalities (such as Cognitive Behavioral Therapy and trauma-informed care), and navigating mandatory child/elder abuse reporting laws. Once supervised hours are validated, the practitioner advances to the unrestricted LCSW license.

How to Obtain and Advance Your LGSW License

Navigate the professional social work licensure pipeline with this step-by-step career guide.

  1. Graduate from a CSWE-Accredited MSW Program

    Complete your Master of Social Work degree from a university accredited by the Council on Social Work Education (CSWE).

  2. Apply for Licensure with Your State Social Work Board

    Submit official university transcripts, criminal background checks, and license applications to your state regulatory board.

  3. Pass the National ASWB Masters Level Examination

    Prepare thoroughly and pass the standardized four-hour, 170-question ASWB Masters exam covering clinical and ethical standards.

  4. Secure Employment and a Board-Approved Clinical Supervisor

    Obtain employment in a healthcare or community agency and execute a formal supervision contract with an approved LCSW supervisor.

  5. Log 3,000+ Supervised Hours to Advance to LCSW

    Document your direct client contact hours and weekly supervision sessions meticulously to qualify for your independent clinical license.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does LGSW stand for in social work?

LGSW stands for Licensed Graduate Social Worker, a master's-level professional license for social workers practicing under supervision.

Q2: What is the difference between an LGSW and an LCSW?

An LGSW holds a master's degree and must practice under clinical supervision, while an LCSW has completed 3,000+ hours and practices independently.

Q3: Can an LGSW provide private therapy?

In most states, an LGSW cannot operate an independent private practice; they can provide psychotherapy only within an agency under approved supervision.

Q4: What exam do you take to become an LGSW?

Candidates must pass the national ASWB (Association of Social Work Boards) Masters-level licensing examination.

Q5: Which states issue the LGSW license?

States utilizing the LGSW title include Maryland, Minnesota, West Virginia, and the District of Columbia (many other states use LMSW for the same tier).

Q6: How long can you hold an LGSW license?

Most states allow social workers to hold and renew an LGSW indefinitely with continuing education, though most advance to LCSW within two to four years.

Q7: What degree is required for an LGSW?

You must hold a Master of Social Work (MSW) degree from a university program accredited by the CSWE (Council on Social Work Education).
