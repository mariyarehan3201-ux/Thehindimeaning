LARA Asbestos Program

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

The LARA Asbestos Program, administered by the Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs under the Michigan Occupational Safety and Health Administration (MIOSHA), oversees regulatory compliance, contractor licensing, professional accreditation, and mandatory project notifications to safeguard workers and the public from hazardous airborne mineral fibers.

Statutory Framework and Public Act Mandates in Michigan

The State of Michigan maintains one of the nation's most comprehensive regulatory frameworks governing asbestos management, coordinated directly through the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA). Operating within MIOSHA's Construction Safety and Health Division, the LARA Asbestos Program derives its primary statutory authority from two critical pieces of state legislation: Public Act 135 of 1986, known as the Asbestos Abatement Contractors Licensing Act, and Public Act 440 of 1988, recognized as the Asbestos Workers Accreditation Act. Together, these legislative mandates ensure that all asbestos-related activities across Michigan adhere to rigorous environmental safety protocols.

Public Act 135 mandates that any corporate entity or sole proprietorship that engages in the business of asbestos abatement on another person's property must possess a valid, current asbestos abatement contractor license issued by LARA. Concurrently, Public Act 440 establishes strict professional qualifications and mandatory training curricula for individual workers, supervisors, project designers, inspectors, and management planners. By enforcing these state standards in tandem with federal EPA Title 40 CFR Part 763 (AHERA) and OSHA Title 29 CFR Part 1926.1101 regulations, the program mitigates hazardous emissions across residential, educational, and commercial facilities.

Mandatory Project Notifications and Regulatory Enforcement

A core pillar of the LARA Asbestos Program is the administration of Michigan's mandatory ten-day asbestos project notification process. Under state and federal National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants (NESHAP) rules, licensed contractors and facility owners must submit formal notification to LARA at least ten working days prior to initiating any demolition or renovation project that involves friable asbestos materials exceeding specific threshold quantities. Specifically, notification is triggered whenever a project disturbs ten linear feet or fifteen square feet or more of friable asbestos materials.

LARA maintains an integrated online notification portal that streamlines the submittal of project specifics, including building location, facility owner details, scheduled work dates, specific abatement techniques, estimated material volumes, and the designated waste disposal landfill. Notifications require accompanying statutory filing fees, which fund unannounced on-site field audits conducted by MIOSHA industrial hygiene compliance officers. Officers inspect containment barriers, review negative-pressure manometer logs, examine HEPA filtration exhausts, and verify that all on-site personnel carry active state accreditation cards.

Worker Accreditation, Training Verification, and Safe Disposal Oversight

To ensure field competency across Michigan job sites, the LARA Asbestos Program directly audits and approves accredited third-party asbestos training providers. These institutions must adhere to strict state-approved curricula that combine classroom legal instruction with hands-on glove-bagging exercises, negative-air setup simulations, and respirator qualitative fit-testing. Upon successful completion of an examination, graduates receive an official Michigan accreditation card featuring a verified photograph, unique state identification number, and expiration date.

In addition to workplace labor protections, LARA collaborates closely with the Michigan Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy (EGLE) regarding the handling, transportation, and final disposition of hazardous asbestos waste. All removed asbestos-containing waste must be saturated with amended wetting agents, sealed inside two layers of 6-mil leak-tight polyethylene bags, and labeled with OSHA warning statements. Transport must be accompanied by an official Waste Manifest form, ensuring custody tracking from the point of initial building removal to final burial in an EGLE-permitted type II sanitary landfill.

How to Comply with Michigan LARA Asbestos Regulations

A comprehensive procedural guide for facility managers and environmental contractors to achieve full regulatory compliance under Michigan LARA mandates.

  1. Perform a Comprehensive AHERA-Accredited Pre-Renovation Survey

    Engage a building inspector holding active Michigan Public Act 440 accreditation to conduct a thorough bulk-sampling inspection of all structural materials scheduled for disturbance.

  2. Select a Licensed Michigan Asbestos Abatement Contractor

    Verify that the prospective remediation firm holds an active Public Act 135 contractor license in good standing through the official online LARA licensing database.

  3. Submit the Official 10-Day Project Notification and Fee

    Complete and file the joint LARA and EGLE online notification form at least ten working days prior to commencement for any project exceeding ten linear or fifteen square feet.

  4. Establish Engineering Containment and Verify On-Site Credentials

    Ensure the contractor constructs 6-mil polyethylene enclosures under continuous negative air pressure, and confirm all workers carry valid Michigan photo accreditation cards.

  5. Obtain Independent Air Clearance and Retain Waste Manifest Records

    Commission an independent industrial hygiene firm for final phase contrast microscopy air clearance, and secure fully signed waste shipment manifests for permanent recordkeeping.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does LARA stand for in Michigan governance?

LARA stands for the Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs, the state administrative agency responsible for business licenses, occupational safety, and environmental health programs.

Q2: What is the primary role of the LARA Asbestos Program?

The program licenses asbestos abatement contractors, accredits industry workers and inspectors, processes mandatory ten-day project notifications, and enforces MIOSHA construction health standards.

Q3: When is a 10-day notification required by LARA in Michigan?

Notification is required at least ten working days before starting any project that disturbs ten linear feet or fifteen square feet or more of friable asbestos, or before any commercial demolition.

Q4: Can a homeowner in Michigan remove asbestos from their own home?

Michigan law permits owner-occupants to perform asbestos removal in their own single-family residence, but state authorities strongly discourage DIY abatement due to severe contamination hazards.

Q5: How can I verify if an asbestos contractor is licensed in Michigan?

You can verify contractor licenses and supervisor accreditations online through the official LARA Asbestos Program license lookup database maintained on the Michigan state portal.

Q6: What is the penalty for performing unlicensed asbestos abatement in Michigan?

Violating Michigan Public Act 135 through unlicensed abatement can result in severe civil administrative fines, cease-and-desist orders, and potential criminal misdemeanor charges.

Q7: How often must Michigan asbestos abatement workers renew their credentials?

Accredited workers and supervisors must complete an approved state refresher training course and submit an annual accreditation renewal application to LARA every twelve months.

Q8: What are the disposal rules for asbestos waste under Michigan regulations?

Waste must be wet-saturated, double-bagged in 6-mil polyethylene with warning labels, accompanied by an official waste manifest, and buried in a state-licensed EGLE-permitted landfill.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The Michigan LARA Asbestos Program plays an indispensable role in safeguarding industrial tradespeople, public school occupants, and residential homeowners from the catastrophic risks of airborne mineral fibers. By maintaining rigorous contractor licensing standards, transparent project notification systems, and unannounced workplace enforcement, LARA ensures that asbestos remediation across Michigan meets the highest benchmarks of environmental engineering and occupational hygiene.
