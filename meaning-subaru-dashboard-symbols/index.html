Meaning Subaru Dashboard Symbols

Meaning
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Renowned for symmetrical all-wheel drive, boxer engines, and EyeSight driver-assist technology, Subaru vehicles feature an extensive set of cluster warning indicators. Exploring the meaning of Subaru dashboard symbols equips drivers with the knowledge needed to handle Symmetrical AWD notifications, X-Mode states, EyeSight camera alerts, and engine warnings.

Color-Coded Alert Hierarchy on Subaru Multi-Function Displays

Subaru organizes its instrument cluster into distinct color categories to communicate urgency. Red indicators signal critical safety hazards or mechanical failures that require an immediate stop, such as low engine oil pressure, failing brakes, or overheating engine coolant.

Yellow and amber indicators signal cautionary maintenance needs or disabled automated systems, such as check engine notifications, uncalibrated tire pressure monitors, or deactivated Vehicle Dynamics Control (VDC). Green and blue lights confirm active driver aids, including the distinctive blue low-engine-coolant light that illuminates upon cold morning startups.

Decoding Subaru EyeSight and All-Wheel Drive Indicators

Modern Subaru models feature EyeSight, a dual-camera stereo vision system mounted behind the upper windshield glass. EyeSight monitors road geometry, traffic flow, and lane markings to manage adaptive cruise control and pre-collision braking.

When EyeSight functionality is temporarily suspended—commonly due to heavy downpours, thick windshield frost, condensation, or blinding direct sunrise glare—the cluster illuminates an amber EyeSight indicator with the text 'OFF'. Once the windshield defrosts or weather clears, the stereo cameras recalibrate automatically and the alert extinguishes without requiring dealership diagnostics.

Understanding these notifications prevents panic and ensures your Subaru delivers legendary winter traction and all-weather safety.

How to Handle a Subaru Flashing Cruise Control and Check Engine Light

Learn why Subaru disables cruise control when a check engine code triggers, and how to address it.

  1. Understand the Subaru Failsafe Strategy

    Subaru programming intentionally flashes the Cruise Control light and disables EyeSight whenever any Check Engine code occurs to protect the powertrain.

  2. Inspect the Fuel Filler Cap

    A loose gas cap is the leading cause of minor evaporative emission codes; tighten the cap until it firmly clicks three times.

  3. Check Cold Engine Oil Level

    Boxer engines can consume small amounts of oil; verify on the dipstick that the oil level sits squarely between the lower and upper holes.

  4. Scan with an OBD-II Code Scanner

    Plug a diagnostic scan tool into the port above your pedals to read stored P-codes (such as P0420 for catalytic converter or P0171 for lean fuel mixture).

  5. Clear Codes After Making Repairs

    Once the physical defect is repaired, erase diagnostic codes; the check engine, cruise control, and EyeSight lights will clear together.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Why is my Subaru cruise control light flashing with the check engine light?

Subaru safety software automatically disables cruise control, EyeSight, and traction control whenever any check engine code triggers.

Q2: What does the blue thermometer light mean on a Subaru?

The blue coolant light indicates the engine is cold; drive gently until the engine warms up and the light turns off naturally.

Q3: What causes the Subaru AT OIL TEMP light to turn on?

It indicates the Continuously Variable Transmission (CVT) fluid is running too hot or that a valve body solenoid has failed.

Q4: What does the EyeSight OFF light mean on a Subaru?

It means the stereo cameras are temporarily blinded by windshield frost, heavy fog, rain, or blinding direct sunlight.

Q5: What does X-Mode do on a Subaru?

X-Mode recalibrates throttle sensitivity, all-wheel drive clutch pressure, and traction control to conquer deep snow, mud, and steep slippery terrain.

Q6: Can I drive my Subaru with a flashing red coolant light?

No. A flashing or solid red coolant light signals extreme engine overheating; continuing to drive risks blowing head gaskets or cracking engine blocks.

Q7: Why does my Subaru tire pressure light flash when I start the car?

A TPMS light that flashes for 60 seconds before remaining solid indicates a dead sensor battery or broken sensor in one of your wheels.

Q8: What does the car with wavy lines mean on a Subaru?

This is the Vehicle Dynamics Control (VDC) indicator; it flashes when traction control is actively preventing wheels from spinning on slick roads.
