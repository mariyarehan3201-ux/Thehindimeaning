How Much Does It Cost to Be a CNA?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does it cost to be a cna requires analyzing tuition at community colleges vs. private vocational schools, state competency exam fees, background checks, and required clinical supplies. On average, becoming a Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA) costs between $800 and $2,500 in total out-of-pocket expenses. However, many nursing homes and long-term care facilities offer state-approved 100% free employer-sponsored training programs with guaranteed employment upon graduation.

CNA Training Program Tuition: Community Colleges vs. Vocational Schools

To become a Certified Nursing Assistant, state boards of nursing mandate completing an approved training program consisting of 75 to 150 hours of combined classroom lectures, laboratory skills training, and hands-on supervised clinical rotations in nursing homes. Community colleges and technical colleges offer the most affordable formal tuition, typically charging $600 to $1,400 for a 4 to 8-week program.

Private vocational allied health schools and American Red Cross chapters offer fast-track 3 to 4-week accelerated day or evening cohorts, with tuition ranging from $1,200 to $2,200. While private schools charge higher tuition, they often bundle lab supplies, textbooks, and practice materials into their all-inclusive registration fees.

State Competency Exams, Background Checks, and Medical Clearances

Tuition covers only instructional coursework; students must budget for mandatory state licensing and credentialing fees. Upon completing the course, candidates must pass the National Nurse Aide Assessment Program (NNAAP) or Prometric State Competency Exam. The examination consists of a written/oral multiple-choice test ($35 to $55) and an in-person clinical skills evaluation ($70 to $110), totaling $105 to $165 for initial exam registration.

Healthcare facilities strictly mandate criminal and medical clearances prior to patient clinical rotations. State and FBI fingerprint criminal background checks cost $45 to $85. Required medical immunizations—including a 2-step Mantoux tuberculin (TB) skin test or QuantiFERON blood test ($40 to $80), Hepatitis B vaccine series ($120 to $240), physical examination ($50 to $120), and 10-panel drug screening ($35 to $65)—add $250 to $500 in medical onboarding costs.

How to Get CNA Training for Free Through Employer Sponsorship

Due to nationwide healthcare staffing shortages across long-term care and assisted living facilities, thousands of employers offer free CNA training programs. Skilled nursing facilities partner with state workforce development agencies to host certified on-site training academies. The facility covers 100% of student tuition, textbooks, uniforms, and state exam fees.

In exchange for free tuition, students typically sign a 6 to 12-month employment retention agreement, earning immediate hourly wages ($16 to $22 per hour) as nurse aides upon graduation. Furthermore, under federal Medicare/Medicaid regulations (42 CFR § 483.158), if an individual pays for their own CNA training and becomes employed by a Medicare-certified nursing facility within 12 months of certification, the employer is legally mandated to reimburse the employee for their training and testing costs.

How to Become a Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA)

Follow these five straightforward steps to complete training and earn your CNA license.

  1. Enroll in a State Board-Approved CNA Program

    Select an approved training program at a local community college, technical school, or employer-sponsored academy.

  2. Complete Medical Clearance and TB Screenings

    Obtain your physical exam, 2-step TB skin test, and complete background fingerprinting before clinicals start.

  3. Attend Classroom Lectures and 40+ Clinical Hours

    Complete all required lecture modules and hands-on nursing home patient care clinical hours.

  4. Pass the Written and Clinical Skills State Exam

    Register for your state exam to demonstrate five randomly selected nursing skills before an evaluator.

  5. Get Listed on the State Nurse Aide Registry

    Verify that your passing scores are registered on your state's online nurse aide registry to begin working.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to get your CNA certification?

Total out-of-pocket costs range from $800 to $2,500 for tuition, testing, background checks, medical clearances, and uniforms.

Q2: Can you get CNA certification for free?

Yes, many nursing homes and hospitals sponsor 100% free CNA training in exchange for a 6 to 12-month work commitment.

Q3: How long does it take to become a CNA?

Most CNA training programs take 4 to 8 weeks to complete; accelerated full-time programs can be finished in 3 to 4 weeks.

Q4: How much does the CNA state exam cost?

The state competency exam typically costs between $105 and $165 for both the written knowledge test and clinical skills test.

Q5: Do nursing homes reimburse CNA tuition?

Yes, under federal law, Medicare-certified nursing facilities must reimburse CNA training costs if hired within 12 months of passing.

Q6: What is the average hourly pay for a CNA?

CNAs earn between $17 and $24 per hour on average ($35,000 to $48,000 annually), with higher rates in hospital and travel settings.

Q7: Is becoming a CNA worth it?

Yes, it is the fastest, lowest-cost entry point into healthcare, providing invaluable direct patient clinical hours for future LPN, RN, or PA school.

Q8: What supplies do you need for CNA class?

Students need 2 sets of medical scrubs, non-slip nursing shoes, a stethoscope, blood pressure cuff, analog watch with second hand, and textbooks.
