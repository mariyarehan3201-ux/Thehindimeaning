How Much Does Asbestos Siding Removal Cost?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

Determining how much asbestos siding removal costs involves evaluating total exterior wall surface area, regional environmental disposal fees, hazardous permitting requirements, and contractor labor rates. For an average residential home, complete professional removal of cementitious transite asbestos siding generally ranges from $7 to $15 per square foot, translating to total project costs between $8,000 and $20,000.

Cost Breakdown and Square-Foot Pricing Factors

Homeowners exploring how much asbestos siding removal costs must consider several interconnected project variables. Asbestos siding, commonly engineered as rigid cement shingles under trade names like Transite, features a composite matrix of Portland cement and twelve to twenty-five percent chrysotile asbestos fibers. While transite is classified as non-friable when intact, state and local environmental regulations govern its removal to prevent breakage and fiber aerosolization.

On average, certified environmental contractors charge between $7 and $15 per square foot for the safe manual removal, packaging, and disposal of asbestos siding shingles. For a typical single-family home with 1,500 square feet of exterior wall area, removal expenses range from $10,500 to $22,500. This estimate covers regulatory notifications, perimeter drop cloths, manual nail pulling, double-wrapping in six-mil poly plastic, and hazardous waste transportation fees.

Encapsulation and Siding-Over Versus Full Abatement

Because complete removal represents a major financial investment, many homeowners evaluate siding encapsulation as an alternative strategy. Siding-over involves leaving the intact asbestos shingles on the building and fastening high-density rigid foam insulation board directly over them, followed by modern vinyl, wood, or fiber-cement siding. This approach encapsulates the asbestos shingles and eliminates hazardous disposal expenses.

While encapsulation reduces immediate upfront costs by forty to sixty percent, it carries notable trade-offs. Driving thousands of siding nails through transite shingles can crack the brittle cement panels, releasing minor fiber plumes. Additionally, encapsulated asbestos must be disclosed upon future real estate sales, and any future structural renovations or window replacements will still require handling the underlying hazardous material.

Key Cost Variables and Hidden Remediation Expenses

Several underlying site conditions can elevate the total cost of asbestos siding removal. Multi-story homes require complex scaffolding and safety harness rigging, which adds ten to twenty percent to labor expenses. Furthermore, environmental regulations require soil protection beneath the exterior walls using heavy poly tarps to capture any chipped fragments or dislodged paint chips.

Underlying structural discoveries often incur secondary expenses. When transite shingles are stripped away, contractors frequently uncover water damage, rotted plywood sheathing, or underlying asphalt felt papers that also contain asbestos. Property owners should set aside a contingency reserve of fifteen to twenty percent of the project budget to address structural repairs, new vapor barriers, and exterior trim restoration.

How to Budget and Hire for Asbestos Siding Removal

A methodical guide for homeowners to estimate expenses, evaluate quotes, and retain certified asbestos siding contractors.

  1. Calculate Exterior Wall Surface Area Accurately

    Measure the perimeter and height of your home exterior walls, subtracting window and door openings to establish total square footage.

  2. Confirm Asbestos Content Through Certified Laboratory Testing

    Have an accredited inspector sample a broken shingle to verify whether the material is chrysotile cement or non-hazardous composite board.

  3. Solicit Multiple Itemized Estimates from Licensed Abatement Firms

    Request written bids that clearly separate regulatory notification fees, containment setup, manual removal, disposal tipping fees, and cleanup.

  4. Verify Contractor Insurance, Licensing, and Waste Landfill Proof

    Ensure the chosen contractor holds specific pollution liability insurance, state asbestos certification, and provides landfill disposal manifests.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the average cost per square foot for asbestos siding removal?

Professional removal of asbestos siding typically costs between $7 and $15 per square foot, covering manual removal, containment, hauling, and disposal fees.

Q2: Can I simply install new vinyl siding over old asbestos siding?

Yes, many building codes allow installing rigid insulation board and new vinyl siding directly over intact asbestos shingles as an accepted encapsulation method.

Q3: Why is asbestos siding removal so expensive?

Costs are driven by state regulatory notifications, specialized labor requirements, personal protective gear, poly drop-cloth containment, and high hazardous landfill tipping fees.

Q4: Can a homeowner legally remove their own asbestos siding?

In some jurisdictions single-family homeowners are legally allowed to remove siding themselves, but environmental agencies strongly advise against it due to severe health and liability risks.

Q5: How long does it take to remove asbestos siding from an entire house?

A complete professional exterior siding abatement for an average residential home typically takes between two and four working days.

Q6: Does removing asbestos siding increase home property value?

Yes, complete removal eliminates a known environmental defect, simplifies future remodeling, removes real estate disclosure stigmas, and appeals to potential buyers.

Q7: How do contractors prevent asbestos fibers from escaping during siding removal?

Workers spray shingles with amended water, manually pull nails without breaking the tiles, lay heavy plastic ground tarps, and wrap tiles in airtight plastic bundles.

Q8: What should I replace my asbestos siding with after removal?

Common replacement siding options include modern fiber-cement shingles, engineered wood lap siding, insulated vinyl cladding, and traditional cedar shake siding.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Evaluating how much asbestos siding removal costs requires weighing upfront contractor fees against long-term property value, safety, and regulatory compliance. While siding encapsulation provides an economical short-term solution for intact shingles, complete professional removal permanently eliminates environmental liability and structural complications. Securing multiple itemized bids from licensed environmental contractors ensures competitive pricing and adherence to environmental safety protocols.
