How Much Does a Toenail Removal Cost?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a toenail removal cost requires evaluating whether the procedure involves a partial nail avulsion or total nail matrixectomy, local anesthesia requirements, and podiatrist clinical fees. On average, an in-office toenail removal procedure performed by a podiatrist costs between $250 and $800 without health insurance. A standard partial nail avulsion (removing an ingrown border) costs $200 to $500, while permanent matrix ablation with phenol chemical cauterization averages $350 to $750.

Partial Nail Avulsion vs. Total Nail Matrixectomy

Toenail removal is a routine, highly effective minor surgical procedure performed in a podiatrist's office to relieve excruciating chronic ingrown toenails (onychocryptosis), severe fungal nail infections (onychomycosis), or traumatic nail bed crush injuries. The clinical approach is bifurcated into partial removal and total removal based on the extent of nail pathology.

A partial nail avulsion (wedge resection) is the standard treatment for ingrown toenails, accounting for over 85% of clinical procedures. The podiatrist numbs the toe with a digital local block, trims away the offending vertical nail border (roughly 2 to 3 millimeters) down to the germinal matrix, and leaves the healthy central nail plate intact ($200 to $450). A total nail avulsion involves removing the entire nail plate, typically recommended for severe fungal infection or thickened deformed nails (onychogryphosis), costing $300 to $650.

Permanent Matrix Cauterization: Phenolization vs. Laser Ablation

If an ingrown toenail is simply clipped out without addressing the underlying nail root, it carries an 70% to 80% recurrence rate as the nail plate naturally grows forward. To eliminate recurrence permanently, podiatrists perform a chemical matrixectomy following nail border extraction. The podiatrist applies an 89% liquified phenol solution (or sodium hydroxide) directly into the germinal nail matrix pocket using miniature cotton-tipped applicators for 60 to 90 seconds.

The chemical cauterization selectively destroys the specialized germinal matrix cells that generate keratin for that specific lateral nail border, preventing the offending spike from ever regrowing (yielding a 95%+ permanent success rate). While chemical phenolization adds $75 to $200 to the procedure, it eliminates the need for repeated future surgical visits. Some advanced surgical practices offer carbon dioxide (CO2) laser or radiofrequency matrix ablation at $450 to $800.

Health Insurance Coverage, In-Office Procedures, and Recovery Protocols

Most major commercial health insurance policies (including Blue Cross, Aetna, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare), as well as Medicare and Medicaid, cover in-office toenail removals when performed for medically necessary conditions. Chronic pain, recurrent active bacterial paronychia infections, and functional walking impairments satisfy coverage criteria. Insured patients typically pay only their specialist office co-pay ($30 to $75) or their deductible balance.

Post-operative recovery from a partial matrixectomy is fast and uncomplicated. The local digital anesthetic numbs the toe for 2 to 4 hours. Patients resume normal walking immediately in open-toed sandals or loose running sneakers. Starting 24 hours post-op, patients soak the toe in warm Epsom salt water twice daily, apply topical antibiotic ointment, and dress the toe with a sterile Band-Aid. Complete tissue healing occurs within 2 to 4 weeks.

How to Care for Your Toe After Surgical Nail Removal

Follow these five clinical recovery steps to ensure fast, infection-free healing after toenail removal.

  1. Keep the Initial Pressure Dressing Dry for 24 Hours

    Leave the podiatrist's bulky pressure wrap in place overnight to control minor post-operative bleeding.

  2. Soak in Warm Epsom Salt Water Twice Daily

    Starting on day two, soak the toe in warm water with 2 tablespoons of Epsom salts for 15 minutes to cleanse the surgical wound.

  3. Apply Prescription Antibiotic Ointment

    Gently apply bacitracin or mupirocin ointment directly into the nail border groove after each soak.

  4. Cover with a Breathable Sterile Band-Aid

    Protect the healing tissue with a fresh adhesive bandage daily; avoid tight socks that compress the toe.

  5. Wear Wide-Toe-Box Footwear for 10 to 14 Days

    Wear open-toed slides, sandals, or wide athletic sneakers to eliminate lateral pressure on the healing nail groove.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does it cost to have a toenail removed?

Without insurance, having a toenail removed by a podiatrist costs between $250 and $650, or $30 to $75 with insurance co-pays.

Q2: Does getting a toenail removed hurt?

The initial local anesthetic injection pinches for 10 seconds; once numbed, the actual surgical removal is completely painless.

Q3: Will my toenail grow back after removal?

If only the nail is pulled, it regrows in 6 to 12 months; if the podiatrist applies phenol chemical matrixectomy, the border never grows back.

Q4: How long is recovery after toenail removal surgery?

Most patients return to work or school the next day; complete healing of the underlying tissue requires 2 to 4 weeks.

Q5: Can urgent care remove an ingrown toenail?

Urgent care clinics can perform temporary nail avulsions ($150 to $350), but they rarely perform permanent chemical root matrixectomies.

Q6: Does health insurance cover ingrown toenail removal?

Yes, major health insurance plans and Medicare cover toenail removals when performed for pain, recurrent infection, or functional impairment.

Q7: What is a permanent toenail removal called?

It is called a nail matrixectomy, which uses chemical phenol, sodium hydroxide, or radiofrequency to permanently destroy nail root cells.

Q8: Can I walk immediately after toenail removal?

Yes, patients can walk immediately after the procedure, although wearing a loose-fitting sneaker or open-toed shoe is recommended.
