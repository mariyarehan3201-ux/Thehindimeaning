Asbestos Washington

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

In the state of Washington, asbestos management is governed by one of the nation most stringent dual-agency regulatory frameworks, administered jointly by the Washington State Department of Labor and Industries Division of Occupational Safety and Health and local clean air agencies across each jurisdiction. Mandated by Chapter 296-62 and Chapter 296-65 of the Washington Administrative Code, property owners and environmental contractors must adhere to rigorous good faith surveys, certified worker training, advance agency notifications, and controlled waste disposal practices to safeguard worker safety and pristine Pacific Northwest air quality.

Washington State Dual Regulatory Structure: L&I DOSH and Regional Clean Air Agencies

Washington State maintains a comprehensive, decentralized approach to asbestos oversight that pairs state worker safety protections with localized air pollution control authorities. The Washington State Department of Labor & Industries (L&I), through its Division of Occupational Safety and Health (DOSH), enforces Chapter 296-62 WAC (General Occupational Health Standards) and Chapter 296-65 WAC (Asbestos Removal and Encapsulation). DOSH licenses certified asbestos contractors, supervises worker and supervisor accreditation, sets workplace permissible exposure limits, and conducts unannounced safety audits on abatement job sites throughout the state.

Concurrently, environmental emissions and building demolition notifications are governed by regional clean air agencies based on county boundaries. For instance, the Puget Sound Clean Air Agency (PSCAA) regulates King, Pierce, Snohomish, and Kitsap counties; the Southwest Clean Air Agency (SWCAA) covers Clark, Cowlitz, Lewis, Skamania, and Wahkiakum counties; the Olympic Region Clean Air Agency (ORCAA) oversees Thurston, Mason, Clallam, Jefferson, Grays Harbor, and Pacific counties; and the Spokane Regional Clean Air Agency (SRCAA) covers Spokane County. In counties without a local clean air authority, the Washington State Department of Ecology administers air quality compliance directly.

Mandatory Good Faith Asbestos Surveys and Contractor Certification

A distinctive cornerstone of Washington law is the statutory requirement for a Good Faith Asbestos Inspection, codified in WAC 296-62-07721. Before any contractor, subcontractor, or homeowner begins remodeling, repairing, or demolishing any structure—regardless of the year of construction—the building owner must commission a certified, written asbestos survey conducted by an AHERA-accredited building inspector. Even structures erected in recent decades must be verified, debunking the common misconception that post-1980 buildings are automatically exempt in Washington State.

The completed Good Faith Survey report must be posted conspicuously at the job site and provided directly to all bidding contractors before work agreements are finalized. If asbestos-containing material (defined in Washington as containing greater than one percent asbestos) will be disturbed, the work must be executed exclusively by a certified Asbestos Contractor licensed under WAC 296-65. All supervisors and technicians must carry physical L&I certification cards, earned through forty hours of accredited coursework, practical glovebag and negative-air enclosure training, and annual eight-hour refresher curricula.

Waste Packaging, Transport, and Pacific Northwest Landfill Monofills

Asbestos waste generated in Washington State must be handled with meticulous care to protect municipal waste workers and transit corridors. Debris must be continuously wetted with surfactant solution, loaded into double 6-mil polyethylene bags or lined leak-tight rigid drums, and marked with specific OSHA and Washington L&I hazard warning labels detailing the site address and generator identity. Dry removal of asbestos is strictly prohibited under state law unless specifically granted a formal variance by DOSH.

Waste haulers must carry an official Waste Shipment Record and deliver containers directly to designated regional landfills permitted to operate asbestos monofill cells, such as the Columbia Ridge Landfill in Arlington, Oregon, or regional municipal waste monofills in eastern Washington. The facility operator must provide a signed disposal manifest within thirty-five days, formally completing the regulatory audit trail.

How to Execute a Compliant Asbestos Abatement Project in Washington State

A regulatory procedure for property owners, contractors, and developers managing asbestos surveys, agency notifications, and removal in Washington State.

  1. Commission an AHERA-Accredited Good Faith Asbestos Survey

    Retain a certified AHERA building inspector to conduct a comprehensive Good Faith Inspection of all project areas, regardless of the building year of construction.

  2. Provide Written Survey Findings to All Bidding Contractors

    Post the completed survey report conspicuously on the job site and supply digital copies to all general and trade contractors submitting remodeling bids.

  3. Submit Advance Notification to the Local Clean Air Agency

    File an official asbestos notification with PSCAA, SWCAA, ORCAA, SRCAA, or Ecology at least ten calendar or business days prior to scheduled disturbance.

  4. Contract a Washington L&I Certified Asbestos Abatement Firm

    Ensure the selected abatement contractor holds an active license under WAC 296-65 and provides certified supervisors for full containment operations.

  5. Complete Final Visual Inspection and Secure Landfill Disposal Manifest

    Pass independent visual clearance air testing, transport wetted waste with a Waste Shipment Record to an authorized monofill, and file disposal documentation.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is a Good Faith Asbestos Survey in Washington State?

Under WAC 296-62-07721, a Good Faith Survey is a mandatory written inspection report prepared by an accredited AHERA inspector before any remodel or demolition starts.

Q2: Are homes built after 1980 exempt from asbestos surveys in Washington?

No, Washington State regulations do not have a cutoff date; all structures regardless of age must undergo an AHERA good faith survey prior to renovation or demolition.

Q3: Which agency regulates asbestos worker safety in Washington?

The Washington State Department of Labor & Industries (L&I) Division of Occupational Safety and Health (DOSH) enforces asbestos worker safety and licenses contractors.

Q4: What is the Puget Sound Clean Air Agency rule for asbestos?

PSCAA requires a mandatory 10-day advance notification and fee submission prior to any commercial demolition or any project removing regulated quantities of asbestos.

Q5: Can homeowners in Washington remove their own asbestos?

Owner-occupants of single-family residences may perform their own removal in certain jurisdictions, but clean air agency notifications, proper bagging, and authorized landfill disposal remain strictly mandatory.

Q6: What training does a certified asbestos supervisor need in Washington?

A supervisor must complete a 40-hour state-approved training course, pass the L&I state certification examination, and complete an 8-hour refresher course annually.

Q7: Where is asbestos waste disposed of from Washington State?

Waste is transported under manifest to permitted municipal solid waste landfills with dedicated asbestos monofill cells, such as Columbia Ridge Landfill in Oregon or regional eastern Washington sites.

Q8: What happens if a contractor works without an asbestos survey in Washington?

L&I DOSH issues immediate stop-work orders and substantial civil citations reaching thousands of dollars per violation against both the contractor and property owner.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Complying with Washington State asbestos laws requires early planning, strict observance of the Good Faith Survey mandate, and seamless coordination between L&amp;I DOSH worker standards and regional clean air agency rules. By engaging certified AHERA inspectors, utilizing licensed abatement contractors, and securing verified disposal receipts, property owners protect community health, maintain pristine natural environments, and ensure ironclad legal compliance.
