How Much Does a Sinus Lift Cost?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a sinus lift cost requires evaluating the surgical technique (internal crestal vs. lateral window), the volume and type of bone grafting material used, and whether the procedure is performed concurrently with dental implant placement. On average, a sinus lift costs between $1,500 and $3,500 per side without dental insurance. A simple internal crestal osteotome sinus lift typically ranges from $1,200 to $2,200 per side, while a complex lateral window sinus augmentation costs $2,500 to $5,000 per side.

Crestal Approach vs. Lateral Window Technique

A sinus lift—clinically known as sinus floor augmentation or subantral bone grafting—is a specialized oral surgical procedure designed to add vertical bone height to the posterior maxilla (upper jaw) in the premolar and molar regions. When upper back teeth are extracted or lost to severe periodontal disease, the alveolar bone naturally resorbs, and the maxillary sinus air cavity expands downward (sinus pneumatization), leaving insufficient bone thickness to anchor a titanium dental implant securely.

The surgical approach depends on remaining residual native bone height. If the patient has 4 to 6 millimeters of native bone remaining, an internal crestal (osteotome) sinus lift is utilized. Through the dental implant osteotomy preparation hole, the surgeon gently elevates the Schneiderian membrane and injects bone graft granules ($1,200 to $2,200 per side). If native bone is severely depleted (less than 4 millimeters), an open lateral window sinus lift is mandatory, requiring cutting a bone window into the outer sinus wall, gently lifting the membrane, and packing substantial bone matrix ($2,500 to $4,800 per side).

Bone Grafting Materials: Autograft, Allograft, Xenograft, and PRF

The type of bone grafting material placed beneath the Schneiderian sinus membrane significantly impacts both surgical pricing and biological ossification rates. Demineralized Freeze-Dried Bone Allograft (DFDBA) derived from human donor tissue is the most popular clinical material, costing $300 to $600 per cubic centimeter. Bovine-derived mineral matrix (Xenograft, such as Bio-Oss) is also widely utilized for its slow resorption rate, providing a stable volumetric scaffold ($350 to $700 per cc).

Oral surgeons frequently enrich the bone graft with Platelet-Rich Fibrin (PRF) or Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP). The surgeon draws a vial of the patient's own blood and centrifuges it chairside to isolate concentrated autologous growth factors and fibrin matrix. Blending PRF with the particulate bone creates 'sticky bone,' which accelerates vascularization and soft tissue healing while reducing infection risks, adding $300 to $650 per procedure.

Diagnostic Imaging, Anesthesia, and Dental Insurance Coverage

Prior to performing a sinus lift, the oral surgeon or periodontist must obtain a high-resolution 3D Cone Beam Computed Tomography (CBCT) scan ($250 to $550). The 3D CBCT scan provides cross-sectional volumetric views of maxillary bone thickness, detects underlying sinus pathology (such as chronic sinusitis, mucous retention cysts, or septa), and enables computer-guided surgical planning.

Most sinus augmentations are performed in the surgical chair using local anesthesia combined with oral sedatives or nitrous oxide ($100 to $250). For anxious patients undergoing bilateral lateral window surgery, IV conscious sedation administered by the surgeon or a dental anesthesiologist adds $500 to $1,000. Dental insurance rarely covers sinus lifts completely, categorizing them as major adjunctive surgery, but plans may reimburse 20% to 50% ($500 to $1,500) if tooth loss was due to traumatic injury or disease.

How to Prepare for a Sinus Lift Procedure

Follow these five surgical steps to prepare for a successful sinus bone graft and rapid healing.

  1. Obtain a 3D CBCT Sinus Scan

    Undergo a high-resolution cone beam scan to assess bone height, membrane health, and rule out acute sinus infections.

  2. Treat Active Sinus Allergies or Congestion

    Take prescribed oral antihistamines or nasal steroid sprays prior to surgery to ensure your sinus passages are completely clear.

  3. Submit Pre-Treatment Authorizations to Insurance

    Have the surgical coordinator submit diagnostic codes (CDT D7951 or D7952) to verify available restorative benefits.

  4. Strictly Avoid Blowing Your Nose Post-Surgery

    Do not blow your nose, sneeze with your mouth closed, or drink through straws for 3 weeks to prevent sinus membrane tearing.

  5. Take Prescribed Antibiotics and Decongestants

    Complete your full course of oral amoxicillin and use saline nasal mists as directed to maintain surgical site sterility.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a sinus lift cost per side?

A sinus lift costs between $1,500 and $3,500 per side on average; an internal lift costs $1,200 to $2,200, while a lateral window lift costs $2,500 to $4,800.

Q2: Does dental insurance pay for a sinus lift?

Some major dental insurance plans cover 20% to 50% of the procedure, subject to the patient's annual policy maximum benefit.

Q3: Why do I need a sinus lift for a dental implant?

When upper back teeth are missing, the jawbone shrinks and the maxillary sinus expands; a sinus lift adds bone height to anchor implants securely.

Q4: Is a sinus lift painful?

No, local anesthesia numbs the area completely during surgery; post-op recovery involves mild swelling and sinus congestion for 3 to 7 days.

Q5: How long does a sinus lift take to heal before getting implants?

Bone graft integration typically requires 4 to 9 months before titanium implants can be placed or loaded with crowns.

Q6: What happens if the sinus membrane tears during surgery?

The surgeon repairs the small tear using a dissolvable collagen membrane patch; severe tears may require halting the surgery to allow 2-3 months of healing.

Q7: Can a sinus lift and dental implant be done at the same time?

Yes, if the patient has at least 4 to 5 mm of residual native bone to stabilize the implant fixture during initial healing.

Q8: Who performs a sinus lift?

Sinus lifts are performed by board-certified oral and maxillofacial surgeons, periodontists, and highly trained implantologists.
