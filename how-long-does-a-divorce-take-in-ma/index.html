How Long Does a Divorce Take in MA?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how long does a divorce take in ma is an essential legal consideration for spouses filing in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts. Governed by Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 208 and administered by the Massachusetts Probate and Family Court, the timeline to finalize a divorce depends heavily on whether spouses file an uncontested 'Section 1A' no-fault divorce or a contested 'Section 1B' divorce. Furthermore, Massachusetts enforces a mandatory, unique statutory 'Nisi Period' that delays the absolute finality of every divorce.

Section 1A (Uncontested) vs. Section 1B (Contested) Tracks

Under Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 208, no-fault divorces are divided into two distinct legal categories: Section 1A and Section 1B. A Section 1A divorce is a joint, uncontested petition where both spouses agree that an irretrievable breakdown of the marriage has occurred and submit a notarized Separation Agreement resolving property, alimony, and child custody.

In a Section 1A filing, the court schedules an uncontested hearing within 30 to 60 days. If the judge approves the agreement, the court issues a Judgment of Divorce Nisi after 30 days. Conversely, a Section 1B divorce is filed unilaterally by one spouse when the parties cannot agree on terms. Section 1B divorces take significantly longer—typically 9 to 18 months—due to mandatory 6-month statutory waiting periods, discovery, and pretrial court conferences.

The Massachusetts 'Nisi Period': Why You Cannot Remarry Immediately

A unique and frequently misunderstood aspect of Massachusetts divorce law is the 'Nisi Period' (M.G.L. c. 208 § 21). The Latin term 'Nisi' means 'unless.' A Judgment of Divorce Nisi is a conditional judgment: it establishes that the divorce is granted and will become permanent unless someone challenges it or the spouses reconcile.

Under Massachusetts law, the Nisi period lasts exactly 90 days. During these 90 days, you are in legal limbo: you cannot legally remarry another person, and you cannot file single tax returns if the year closes during the Nisi window. Only when the 90 days expire does the divorce automatically become a 'Judgment of Divorce Absolute,' finalizing your single marital status without any further court filings.

Residency Requirements, Separation Agreements, and Child Education Courses

To file for divorce in Massachusetts, spouses must satisfy jurisdictional residency requirements. Under M.G.L. c. 208 § 5, if the grounds for divorce occurred outside Massachusetts, the filing spouse must have resided in the Commonwealth for at least one full continuous year prior to filing. If the grounds occurred while both spouses were living in Massachusetts, only current residency is required.

Furthermore, if the divorcing couple shares minor children, Massachusetts Probate and Family Court Standing Order 2-16 mandates that both parents must complete an approved 4-hour Parent Education Program (two two-hour sessions). Failing to complete this program and submit certificates of completion will halt your case, preventing the judge from scheduling your uncontested hearing.

How to File a Section 1A Uncontested Divorce in Massachusetts

Follow these five strategic steps to prepare, file, and finalize a Section 1A divorce in MA.

  1. Verify Massachusetts Statutory Residency

    Ensure at least one spouse has resided in Massachusetts for 1 year (or that cause occurred in-state).

  2. Draft a Comprehensive Separation Agreement

    Negotiate and notarize a complete agreement resolving property, debt division, alimony, and custody.

  3. Complete Mandatory Parent Education (If Children Involved)

    Attend an approved 4-hour co-parenting class and obtain your official completion certificates.

  4. File Joint Petition Form CJ-D 101A with Probate Court

    Submit your petition, certified marriage certificate, financial statements, and fee to the county court.

  5. Attend Brief Hearing and Await 90-Day Nisi Expiration

    Answer routine questions before the judge, receive your Judgment Nisi, and wait 90 days for absolute finality.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How long does an uncontested divorce take in Massachusetts?

An uncontested Section 1A divorce takes roughly 5 to 7 months total, including the mandatory 30-day approval window and 90-day Nisi period.

Q2: What is the Nisi period in a Massachusetts divorce?

The Nisi period is a mandatory 90-day cooling-off window after the judge's initial ruling before the divorce becomes legally absolute.

Q3: Can you remarry during the Nisi period in MA?

No, you are still legally married during the 90-day Nisi period; remarrying before the Judgment Absolute is entered constitutes bigamy.

Q4: What is the difference between a 1A and 1B divorce in MA?

Section 1A is a joint uncontested filing with a signed agreement; Section 1B is a contested complaint filed by one spouse alone.

Q5: How much is the filing fee for a divorce in Massachusetts?

The Probate and Family Court filing fee is $200 for the petition plus a $15 surcharge, totaling $215.

Q6: Can you get divorced without going to court in Massachusetts?

Generally no; at least one brief formal hearing before a Probate and Family Court judge is required, though some courts conduct them via Zoom.

Q7: Is there a waiting period to file a 1B divorce in MA?

Under M.G.L. c. 208 § 1B, a judge cannot schedule a trial earlier than 6 months from the initial filing date.

Q8: How are assets divided in a Massachusetts divorce?

Massachusetts is an equitable distribution state under M.G.L. c. 208 § 34; judges divide marital property fairly, which is not always a 50/50 split.
