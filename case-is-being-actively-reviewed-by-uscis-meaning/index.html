Case is Being Actively Reviewed by Uscis Meaning

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 17, 2026

When checking your case status online through the US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) portal, understanding case is being actively reviewed by USCIS meaning brings relief and clarity to the waiting process. This status indicates that your petition has passed initial intake and an adjudications officer has formally opened your file.

For thousands of immigrants, visa applicants, and petitioning family members, tracking online statuses can be an emotional rollercoaster. Because the phrase "actively reviewed" sounds as though an adjudicator is scrutinizing the file at that very second, many applicants expect an immediate decision within days. Understanding the mechanical realities behind the digital status helps manage expectations during long bureaucratic timelines.

The Two Main Triggers for "Actively Reviewed" Status

In the USCIS electronic Case Information Network, the transition to "Case Is Being Actively Reviewed" typically occurs under two distinct circumstances during the lifecycle of an immigration petition.

The table below illustrates the primary administrative triggers that initiate the active review status and their operational significance:

Trigger Event Typical Timeline Behind-the-Scenes Administrative Action Applicant Expectation
Biometrics Milestone Day of or day after ASC appointment FBI background fingerprint check completed and linked to master A-File Standard automated milestone; file waits in queue for adjudicator availability
Officer Assignment / Transfer Months after biometrics (varies by center) Adjudications officer opens file to review eligibility, affidavits, and bonafides Substantive review phase; decision or evidence request expected soon
Field Office Jurisdiction Shift Prior to in-person interview scheduling Service center transfers physical or digital file to local field office Often manifests as a "second" active review timestamp on portal

For many applicants, the status updates just hours after walking out of their Application Support Center (ASC) biometrics appointment. This occurs because the automated system confirms that fingerprint and photograph data successfully cleared FBI criminal background databases, officially placing the petition in the adjudicative pool.

Navigating the Next Status Milestones

Once an application enters active review, it will remain in that state until an officer takes formal administrative action. Understanding the possible outcomes enables petitioners to prepare supporting evidence proactively.

Review the matrix below detailing the next potential status updates following the active review phase:

Subsequent Case Status Administrative Cause Required Applicant Action
Case Was Approved Officer verified all statutory requirements; petition granted Await physical Form I-797 Notice of Action and document delivery
Request for Evidence (RFE) Sent Additional documentation required (e.g. tax transcripts, birth records) Carefully compile and submit all requested evidence before stated deadline
Interview Was Scheduled File transferred to local field office for in-person interview Download interview notice and gather original supporting documents
Notice of Intent to Deny (NOID) Officer found serious statutory eligibility defect or fraud concern Consult qualified immigration attorney immediately to draft legal response

While waiting, petitioners should periodically check their official online accounts rather than relying solely on paper mail. Any critical Request for Evidence (RFE) PDF will appear in the "Documents" tab days before physical letters arrive in home mailboxes.

What to Do When Your Case is Actively Reviewed by USCIS

  1. Log Into Your Official MyUSCIS Online Account

    Check your digital USCIS account to review the exact date and timestamp of the latest case status update.

  2. Inspect the Documents and Notices Tab

    Review the "Documents" tab for newly uploaded PDF notices, such as a Request for Evidence (RFE) or Biometrics Reuse notice.

  3. Check Processing Times for Your Service Center

    Compare your filing date against historical median processing times published for your specific service center (Potomac, Texas, Nebraska, or California).

  4. Keep Contact and Residential Address Current

    Ensure your physical address is updated via Form AR-11 within 10 days of any move to avoid missing mailed notices or interview appointments.

  5. Prepare Documentation for Next Steps

    Organize original civil documents, tax returns, and relationship evidence in anticipation of an approval notice or in-person interview request.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does "Case Is Being Actively Reviewed By USCIS" mean?

It means USCIS has received your application, verified receipt fees, completed initial document intake, and either linked your biometrics or assigned your file for review.

Q2: Why did my status change to "actively reviewed" right after my biometrics appointment?

This is standard automated procedure. Once your fingerprints and photo are captured at an Application Support Center (ASC), the system automatically updates the case status to actively reviewed.

Q3: How long does a case stay in actively reviewed status?

It can range from a few weeks to many months (or even over a year for family-based I-130 petitions) depending on service center backlogs and visa bulletin priority dates.

Q4: Does "actively reviewed" mean an officer is looking at it this exact moment?

Not necessarily continuously. It signifies that your file is officially in the adjudication queue and has moved through preliminary security checks.

Q5: What status comes after "case is being actively reviewed"?

The next status is typically "Request for Additional Evidence Sent," "Case Was Approved," or "Interview Was Scheduled."

Q6: Why did my case status update twice to the exact same message?

A "second active review" often occurs when your file is physically transferred from a regional service center to a local field office or when an officer conducts final pre-approval review.

Q7: Can I expedite a case that is actively being reviewed?

You can submit an expedite request through the USCIS Contact Center if you meet strict criteria such as severe financial loss to a company or urgent humanitarian reasons.
