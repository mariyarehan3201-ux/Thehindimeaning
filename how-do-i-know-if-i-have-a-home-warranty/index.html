How Do I Know if I Have a Home Warranty?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how do i know if i have a home warranty is a critical financial check for new homeowners facing sudden breakdowns of central air conditioners, hot water heaters, or kitchen appliances. Unlike standard homeowners insurance policies that strictly cover catastrophic perils like fire, storm damage, and theft, a residential home warranty is a service contract covering mechanical wear and tear of major home systems. Because home sellers and real estate agents routinely purchase one-year home warranties as closing incentives, many new homeowners possess active, fully prepaid coverage without even realizing it.

Closing Disclosure Dossiers and HUD-1 Settlement Statements

The single most reliable place to confirm whether an active home warranty exists on your property is your real estate closing dossier. When you purchased your home, your title company, closing attorney, or escrow officer compiled a comprehensive packet of closing documents, anchored by the Closing Disclosure (CD) or HUD-1 Settlement Statement.

Examine Section H ('Other') or the settlement charges breakdown on your Closing Disclosure. Look for line-item disbursements made to prominent home warranty companies such as American Home Shield, First American Home Buyers Protection, Choice Home Warranty, or 2-10 Home Buyers Warranty. If the line shows a premium paid by either the seller ('Paid by Others') or by you at closing, you have a one-year prepaid service contract that typically took effect on your official closing date.

Real Estate Purchase Contracts and Seller Concessions

If your Closing Disclosure is ambiguous or archived in storage, your original Purchase and Sale Agreement provides vital contractual clues. During home negotiations, buyers frequently request that the seller pay for a one-year home warranty (typically budgeted between $500 and $800) to alleviate fears about aging mechanical equipment. This requirement is formally drafted into the contract under a dedicated 'Home Warranty' or 'Seller Concessions' addendum.

Review your signed agreement to see if the seller agreed to provide a warranty. If the contract stipulates that the seller was obligated to provide a policy from a specific carrier, reach out directly to your real estate agent or the title company that closed the transaction. They can retrieve the escrow disbursement check number, policy receipt, and contact phone number from their permanent digital closing archives.

Contacting Warranty Providers and Manufacturer Registers

If you suspect you have coverage but lack the company's name, you can contact the customer support departments of the top national home warranty providers. Provide your property address and closing date; customer service representatives can search their national policyholder databases by street address to determine if an active contract is registered to your home.

Additionally, do not overlook manufacturer warranties. Even if no third-party home warranty was purchased at closing, newly constructed homes or recently replaced appliances (such as a two-year-old furnace, heat pump, or water heater) may still be covered under their original manufacturer equipment warranty. Look up the serial number and brand stamped on the appliance data plate and verify warranty status on the manufacturer's official support website.

How to Verify if You Have an Active Home Warranty

Follow these five tactical steps to discover and confirm your home warranty coverage details.

  1. Inspect Your Closing Disclosure (CD) Form

    Locate Section H (Other) on your settlement statement to check for paid disbursements to home warranty providers.

  2. Review the Purchase and Sale Contract Addenda

    Verify whether a seller-paid home warranty was negotiated as a binding contractual concession in your offer.

  3. Contact Your Buying Realtor or Title Officer

    Ask your real estate agent or title company to look up the closing disbursement record and provide the warranty binder.

  4. Query Major National Home Warranty Providers

    Call customer support at major providers (e.g., American Home Shield, First American) and query your property address.

  5. Check Appliance Serial Numbers for Factory Warranty

    Look up model and serial tags on your furnace, HVAC, and water heater on manufacturer websites for active coverage.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is a home warranty the same as homeowners insurance?

No, homeowners insurance covers sudden accidental damage (fires, storms), while a home warranty covers mechanical breakdowns from normal wear.

Q2: How long does a seller-provided home warranty last?

Seller-paid home warranties standardly cover one full calendar year beginning on your official closing date.

Q3: What is a trade service call fee in a home warranty?

A trade call fee (typically $75 to $125) is the fixed copay paid to the technician dispatched to diagnose and repair an appliance.

Q4: How do I know which warranty company I have if no papers exist?

Contact your real estate agent or the title escrow company that closed your purchase; their accounting records hold the exact payment recipient.

Q5: Does a home warranty automatically transfer to a new buyer?

Yes, seller-purchased warranties are tied to the property and standardly transfer to the buyer upon closing.

Q6: Can I renew a home warranty after the first year expires?

Yes, warranty companies will contact you prior to expiration offering annual renewal contracts.

Q7: Do brand-new appliances have their own warranties?

Yes, appliances carry 1-to-10-year manufacturer warranties that remain valid independent of any third-party home warranty.

Q8: What happens if a warranty company denies a repair claim?

You can request a second opinion, appeal with maintenance records, or contact your state insurance commissioner or attorney general.
