Asbestos Removal New Hampshire

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

Asbestos removal New Hampshire is strictly regulated by the New Hampshire Department of Environmental Services (NHDES) under New Hampshire Administrative Rule Env-A 1800. Because historic textile mills, colonial farmhouses, and industrial facilities across Manchester, Nashua, Concord, and Portsmouth contain aging thermal pipe lagging and acoustic plaster, certified abatement ensures hazardous fibers are contained and safely transported to licensed disposal facilities.

New Hampshire NHDES Regulatory Framework and Rule Env-A 1800

The state of New Hampshire maintains comprehensive oversight over all asbestos handling, abatement, and disposal through the NHDES Air Resources Division. Under New Hampshire Administrative Rule Env-A 1800, any project that involves disturbing or abating regulated asbestos-containing material (RACM) must be executed by an NHDES-licensed asbestos abatement contractor. Contractors must employ supervisors and workers who have completed accredited training courses approved under the federal EPA Model Accreditation Plan.

Furthermore, NHDES mandates that an official written notification be submitted at least ten working days prior to beginning any asbestos abatement project that involves more than ten linear feet or twenty-five square feet of RACM. This notification allows state environmental inspectors to conduct unannounced on-site audits to verify that negative pressure air filtration systems, three-stage decontamination chambers, and continuous surfactant wetting controls meet rigorous state environmental protection standards.

Common Asbestos Materials in Granite State Properties and Costs

New Hampshire’s historic building inventory presents distinctive abatement challenges. The state’s prominent nineteenth-century brick textile mills, such as the Amoskeag Manufacturing complex in Manchester, feature miles of high-temperature steam lines insulated with chrysotile and amosite pipe wrap. Similarly, mid-century residential homes in Hillsborough, Rockingham, and Merrimack counties frequently contain asbestos-cement transite siding, gravity boiler plaster, and nine-inch vinyl floor tiles.

Abatement pricing across New Hampshire reflects regional labor rates and specialized hazardous waste transport logistics. Minor basement pipe glovebag removals typically cost between $1,500 and $3,000, while whole-house remediation projects encompassing boiler dismantling and floor tile extraction range from $6,000 to $15,000. Because New Hampshire does not host an active commercial asbestos landfill, contractors must haul double-bagged waste under official manifest to regional out-of-state monofills in Maine, New York, or Pennsylvania.

Homeowners in New Hampshire must confirm that their chosen contractor carries specific asbestos pollution liability insurance. Standard commercial contractor insurance policies almost universally exclude toxic substance and environmental remediation claims.

Additionally, hiring an independent project monitor to perform visual inspections and clearance air testing provides verified legal proof that the living space meets state reoccupancy standards prior to barrier teardown.

How to Execute Asbestos Removal in New Hampshire

Step-by-step regulatory procedure for property owners in New Hampshire.

  1. Schedule a Certified NHDES Asbestos Survey

    Hire an independent NHDES-certified asbestos inspector to sample suspect plaster, siding, or pipe wrap for PLM analysis.

  2. Obtain Competitive Bids from Licensed Contractors

    Collect written estimates from at least three NHDES-licensed abatement firms, verifying transparent disposal and filing fees.

  3. File NHDES Ten-Day Advance Notification

    Verify that the contractor submits the official written notification to NHDES Air Resources Division at least ten working days prior to work.

  4. Complete Independent Clearance Air Testing

    Retain a third-party environmental consultant to perform PCM air clearance testing before containment plastic is dismantled.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Does New Hampshire require a licensed contractor for asbestos?

Yes, NHDES Env-A 1800 mandates that all projects disturbing regulated asbestos materials must be performed by state-licensed contractors.

Q2: Can homeowners remove asbestos themselves in New Hampshire?

NHDES allows limited DIY removal in single-family owner-occupied homes, but strongly discourages it due to hazardous airborne fiber risks.

Q3: What is the NHDES 10-day notification requirement?

Contractors must notify NHDES in writing ten working days prior to disturbing more than ten linear feet or twenty-five square feet of asbestos.

Q4: How much does asbestos removal cost in New Hampshire?

Residential projects typically range between $2,000 and $6,500, with minor pipe jobs costing $1,500 and large remediations exceeding $12,000.

Q5: Where is asbestos waste disposed of in New Hampshire?

Asbestos waste is double-bagged in six-mil poly, labeled under DOT rules, and transported under manifest to licensed out-of-state monofills.

Q6: What is the clearance air testing standard in NH?

Air clearance must achieve less than 0.01 fibers per cubic centimeter via Phase Contrast Microscopy before containment is dismantled.

Q7: Do old textile mills in Manchester NH contain asbestos?

Yes, historic nineteenth-century mills contain extensive asbestos pipe lagging, boiler blocks, and acoustic insulation.

Q8: How do I check if an asbestos contractor is licensed in NH?

You can verify licensing status directly through the NHDES Asbestos Management Program online licensee database.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Managing an asbestos removal New Hampshire project requires strict adherence to NHDES Env-A 1800 guidelines. By engaging state-licensed contractors, observing the mandatory ten-day notification period, and securing independent air clearance testing, property owners ensure clean air and protect long-term property value.
