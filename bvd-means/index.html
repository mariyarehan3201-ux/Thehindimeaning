BVD Means

Meaning
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

In the history of American apparel, textile manufacturing, and men's fashion, BVD stands for Bradley, Voorhees & Day—the iconic American clothing company founded in New York City in 1876 by Jeremiah C. Voorhees, Abraham Voorhees, and George C. Bradley. Renowned for revolutionizing men's undergarments by inventing the modern two-piece underwear set and lightweight union suits, BVD became so universally famous throughout the 20th century that the initials entered popular American slang as a generic term for men's underpants ('my B.V.D.s').

The Industrial Innovations That Revolutionized Undergarments

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, men's undergarments were heavy, uncomfortable, and restrictive. Most American men wore thick, scratchy, one-piece woolen flannel 'union suits' that clung to the body, absorbed sweat, and were excruciatingly hot during summer months. In the early 1900s, BVD engineers revolutionized personal comfort by pioneering breathable woven cotton athletic underwear.

BVD introduced lightweight, sleeveless woven cotton undershirts and loose-fitting knee-length boxer shorts, marketing them with the famous trademark slogan: 'Next to Myself I Like B.V.D. Best.' Their lightweight garments were embraced by athletes, manual laborers, and soldiers returning from World War I, cementing BVD as the premier underwear brand in the United States decades before competitors like Fruit of the Loom, Hanes, or Jockey achieved prominence.

Cultural Legacy and Modern Ownership of BVD

Throughout mid-century American culture, BVD held a singular position in literature, film, and radio. Renowned authors like F. Scott Fitzgerald and Ernest Hemingway referenced BVDs in classic novels, while Hollywood comedies routinely featured characters caught in their 'BVDs.' During the 1930s, the company expanded into luxury swimwear, designing Olympic swimsuits worn by legendary swimmer Johnny Weissmuller (who later starred as Hollywood's Tarzan).

In the late 20th century, BVD was acquired by Fruit of the Loom (which subsequently became a subsidiary of Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway conglomerate). Today, BVD continues to manufacture heavy-duty cotton crewneck undershirts, ribbed A-shirts, and classic boxer shorts, retaining a dedicated following among consumers who value durable, vintage-cut American essentials.

How to Identify and Care for Classic BVD Heritage Garments

Practical advice for consumers and vintage apparel collectors.

  1. Look for the Distinctive Red Oval Trademark Label

    Authentic BVD garments feature the classic red-and-white oval 'B.V.D.' logo woven into the inner neckband or exterior waistband.

  2. Inspect Cotton Fabric Weight and Collar Stitching

    Genuine BVD undershirts utilize heavyweight ring-spun combed cotton with reinforced dual-needle cover-stitched ribbed crew collars that resist bacon neck.

  3. Wash in Cold Water to Minimize Cotton Shrinkage

    Because 100% all-cotton fabrics naturally contract under high heat, machine wash BVD basics in cold water with similar cottons.

  4. Tumble Dry on Low Heat or Air Dry Flat

    Dry on delicate tumble cycles or hang dry to preserve the elasticity of the waistband and maintain the garment's original length.

  5. Check Vintage Labels for Made in USA Heritage Pieces

    Collectors seeking vintage 1970s and 1980s BVD pieces should look for union-made tags bearing the ILGWU emblem and 'Made in USA' labels.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does BVD stand for?

BVD stands for Bradley, Voorhees & Day, the last names of the three founders who started the iconic American apparel company in 1876.

Q2: Why did BVD become slang for underwear?

BVD's lightweight two-piece cotton underwear was so immensely popular throughout the early 1900s that Americans began calling all men's undergarments 'BVDs.'

Q3: Who owns BVD today?

BVD is owned by Fruit of the Loom, which is a subsidiary of Warren Buffett's holding company, Berkshire Hathaway.

Q4: What did BVD invent?

BVD pioneered the modern two-piece men's cotton underwear set, replacing hot, scratchy, one-piece woolen union suits with breathable cotton boxers and shirts.

Q5: Is BVD still sold today?

Yes, BVD continues to produce durable, high-quality cotton undershirts, boxer shorts, and briefs available online and at select retailers.

Q6: Did BVD make swimwear?

Yes, in the 1930s BVD was famous for Olympic competitive swimwear and beachwear endorsed by Tarzan actor and Olympic swimmer Johnny Weissmuller.

Q7: Are BVD shirts 100% cotton?

Most classic BVD undershirts and boxers are crafted from 100% heavyweight combed cotton jersey knit for maximum breathability.
