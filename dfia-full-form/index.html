DFIA Full Form: Duty Free Import Authorisation

Full Form
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 17, 2026

The full form of DFIA in international trade, customs regulations, and export-import (EXIM) logistics in India is Duty Free Import Authorisation. Administered by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) under Chapter 4 of the Foreign Trade Policy (FTP), DFIA is an export incentive scheme that permits the post-export, duty-free importation of inputs, raw materials, fuel, and packaging components utilized in the manufacture of export products. Unlike the Advance Authorisation scheme which requires pre-export imports subject to an actual user condition, DFIA authorisations become freely transferable after export obligations are discharged, allowing exporters to sell unused duty credit benefits in the open commercial market.

The Strategic Role of Export Incentive Schemes in Foreign Trade

In global international trade, manufactured goods compete fiercely across global markets based on pricing, quality, and supply reliability. To produce high-quality engineering components, chemical formulations, textiles, or pharmaceuticals, domestic manufacturers frequently need to import specialized raw materials, specialty chemicals, alloy steels, and catalysts that are either scarce or prohibitively expensive in the domestic market. However, imposing heavy basic customs import duties on these raw materials inflates manufacturing costs, rendering Indian exports uncompetitive on the global stage.

Under international World Trade Organization (WTO) principles, governments are entitled to neutralize taxes and tariffs on inputs consumed in the production of export goods ('taxes should not be exported'). The Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) formulates duty exemption and remission schemes under the Foreign Trade Policy. While the Advance Authorisation scheme enables pre-export duty-free imports, the Duty Free Import Authorisation (DFIA) was introduced to provide dynamic liquidity through freely transferable post-export duty benefits.

Comparative Analysis: DFIA vs. Advance Authorisation (AA)

International trade managers and corporate CFOs select between DFIA and Advance Authorisation depending on their manufacturing schedules, cash flows, and supply chains.

Mathematical Calculation of 20% Value Addition under DFIA

A non-negotiable statutory requirement under the Foreign Trade Policy is that exports under DFIA must achieve a minimum value addition (VA) of 20 percent. The value addition percentage is calculated mathematically using the standard DGFT formula:

VA = [ (FOB Value of Export - CIF Value of Inputs) ÷ CIF Value of Inputs ] × 100

For instance, if an exporter imports raw plastic polymer granules valued at a CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight) price of $100,000, the Free on Board (FOB) export value of the finished molded plastic goods must be at least $120,000 to satisfy the 20% threshold. If the realized export value drops below 20%, the regional DGFT authority will reject the transferability endorsement, compelling the exporter to regularize customs duties with penal interest.

Commercial Trading and Market Liquidity of DFIA Scrips

The post-export transferability of DFIA licenses has created a thriving secondary market in India. Many export manufacturers do not need to import raw materials repeatedly; they source inputs domestically or operate on seasonal export contracts.

Once DGFT and Indian Customs endorse transferability onto a DFIA license, the exporter can sell the license to any third-party commercial importer through authorized EXIM scrip brokers. An importer purchasing the DFIA scrip uses it to offset Basic Customs Duty (BCD) on imported raw materials at major maritime ports (such as Mumbai, Mundra, or Chennai). The original exporter realizes immediate cash liquidity at a slight discount (typically 95% to 98% of face value), creating a vital financing lifeline for export-oriented manufacturing enterprises.

How to Apply for and Monetize a DFIA License Under DGFT

  1. Verify SION Standard Input-Output Norms

    Confirm that the manufactured export product has fixed, published Standard Input-Output Norms (SION) listed in the DGFT foreign trade handbook.

  2. File Online Application for DFIA Prior to Exports

    Submit an online DFIA application on the DGFT portal (dgft.gov.in) before commencing exports, providing IEC, GSTIN, and manufacturing details.

  3. Execute Physical Exports with Minimum 20% Value Addition

    Complete physical product exports under the DFIA shipping bill, generating electronic bank realization certificates (e-BRC) showing foreign exchange inward remittances.

  4. Apply for Export Obligation Discharge Certificate (EODC)

    Submit shipping bills, customs export promotion copies, and e-BRC records to the Regional Authority (RA) of DGFT to obtain your official EODC.

  5. Endorse Transferability and Monetize or Import Duty-Free

    Obtain the transferability endorsement from customs, enabling duty-free raw material imports or legal sale of the transferable DFIA license in the open market.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the full form of DFIA?

The full form of DFIA is Duty Free Import Authorisation.

Q2: Which government authority issues DFIA licenses in India?

DFIA authorisations are issued by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT), Ministry of Commerce and Industry.

Q3: What is the minimum value addition required under the DFIA scheme?

Under the Foreign Trade Policy, a minimum value addition of 20% is strictly mandatory for exports under DFIA (except for gems and jewellery).

Q4: Is a DFIA license transferable?

Yes, once export obligations are fully completed and validated by DGFT/Customs, the DFIA license is freely transferable.

Q5: What is the difference between DFIA and Advance Authorisation?

Advance Authorisation has an 'Actual User' condition and is non-transferable; DFIA becomes freely transferable after export completion.

Q6: Can DFIA be claimed for products without SION norms?

No, DFIA authorisations are issued strictly for products that have pre-notified, established Standard Input-Output Norms (SION).

Q7: What customs duties are exempted under a DFIA license?

DFIA exempts basic customs duty (BCD), though integrated GST (IGST) is typically paid and claimed back via input tax credits.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The Duty Free Import Authorisation (DFIA) scheme is one of the most versatile and commercially lucrative export promotion instruments under India’s Foreign Trade Policy. By combining post-export basic customs duty exemptions with unencumbered market transferability, DFIA neutralizes global tariff disadvantages while injecting vital cash liquidity into export manufacturing enterprises. For businesses operating under standardized SION norms and capable of achieving healthy value additions, mastering the strategic application of DFIA licenses provides an unassailable competitive advantage in the arena of global commerce.
