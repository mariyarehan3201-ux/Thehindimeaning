How Much Is a Skirt Steak?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 16, 2026

Whether you are planning a backyard sizzling fajita dinner, firing up authentic Mexican carne asada, or craving a rich marinated steak for the grill, you probably want to know how much is a skirt steak. On average, skirt steak costs between $11 and $18 per pound for standard USDA Choice cuts at major supermarkets, while premium outside skirt, USDA Prime, or American Wagyu varieties range from $20 to over $35 per pound at specialty butcher shops. Understanding the culinary differences between outside and inside skirt cuts and butcher trim yields will help you buy the right cut without overpaying.

Outside Skirt vs. Inside Skirt: The Quality and Price Divide

When shopping for skirt steak, the single most critical factor influencing price and dining satisfaction is whether you are purchasing 'outside skirt' or 'inside skirt.' While both cuts come from the beef diaphragm, they are anatomically distinct muscles with dramatically different tenderness and culinary performance.

The outside skirt (transversus abdominis) is thicker, more uniform, and substantially more tender with intense natural beef flavor. Because commercial restaurant chains and Mexican taquerias purchase almost all available outside skirt at wholesale, retail butcher shops charge a premium of $18 to $26 per pound for this cut. In contrast, the inside skirt is wider, thinner, tougher, and more widely available at grocery store meat counters for $11 to $16 per pound.

Trim Loss and Butcher Yield Economics

When calculating the true cost of skirt steak, you must account for butchering trim loss. Raw, untrimmed skirt steak arrives from wholesale packing plants encased in a tough fibrous membrane (the silverskin) and covered with a thick layer of exterior tallow fat. If you purchase untrimmed 'packer' skirt steak at wholesale club stores for $9 to $11 per pound, removing the tough membrane and excess fat reduces the usable cooking yield by twenty to thirty percent.

Professional butcher shops sell pre-trimmed, 'peeled' skirt steak where the silverskin and hard tallow have already been meticulously removed by hand. While pre-trimmed skirt carries a higher retail sticker price ($16 to $22 per pound), you pay only for one hundred percent edible meat, eliminating waste and saving extensive kitchen preparation time.

Cooking Techniques That Maximize Your Investment

Because skirt steak is an active working muscle characterized by coarse, pronounced muscle grain, improper cooking can transform an expensive $20 steak into shoe leather. To get the maximum culinary return from your skirt steak, it must be cooked either extremely fast over blazing high heat or braised very slowly in liquids.

The gold standard method is grilling or pan-searing on a screaming-hot cast iron skillet for strictly two to three minutes per side until reaching medium-rare (internal temperature of 130 to 135 degrees Fahrenheit). Most importantly, you must let the steak rest for five to ten minutes, and always slice it strictly perpendicular (against the grain) at a 45-degree angle to sever long muscle fibers into tender, melt-in-your-mouth bites.

How to Shop for and Cook the Perfect Skirt Steak

Follow these five culinary steps to select the best skirt cut and prepare it to restaurant-grade tenderness.

  1. Ask the Butcher Specifically for Outside Skirt

    Whenever possible, request outside skirt rather than inside skirt to ensure superior natural tenderness and flavor.

  2. Check Whether the Silverskin Has Been Peeled

    Verify that the shiny white connective tissue membrane has been removed; if not, trim it off with a sharp boning knife.

  3. Marinate With Citrus Acids and Oil for 2 to 4 Hours

    Use lime juice, garlic, cilantro, and olive oil to tenderize surface fibers, avoiding overnight soaks that make meat mushy.

  4. Sear Over Blistering Direct Charcoal or Cast-Iron Heat

    Cook for just two to three minutes per side at 500+ degrees Fahrenheit to form a charred crust while keeping the center pink.

  5. Carve Strictly Against the Muscle Grain

    Identify the parallel muscle lines and slice across them thinly at an angle to ensure every bite is exceptionally tender.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Why is skirt steak so expensive now?

Once an inexpensive butcher scrap cut, skirt steak exploded in popularity due to the rise of fajitas and carne asada, creating massive restaurant demand.

Q2: What is the difference between skirt steak and flank steak?

Skirt steak is thinner, fattier, with a coarser grain and richer beef flavor from the diaphragm, whereas flank steak is wider, leaner, and flatter from the lower belly.

Q3: What is skirt steak called in Mexican butcher shops?

In Mexican carnicerias, skirt steak is called 'arrachera', and is frequently tenderized and marinated in citrus, beer, and spices.

Q4: Is skirt steak cheaper at Costco?

Yes, Costco sells multi-pound vacuum packs of USDA Choice skirt steak for $11 to $14 per pound, which is 20% to 30% cheaper than supermarkets.

Q5: Can skirt steak be cooked well-done?

Cooking skirt steak past medium makes it tough and chewy because it lacks the thick intramuscular marbling of a ribeye; medium-rare to medium is strongly recommended.

Q6: How much skirt steak do I need per person?

Plan for approximately one-half pound (8 ounces) of raw skirt steak per person for tacos or fajitas, or 10 to 12 ounces for standalone steak entrees.

Q7: What can I substitute for skirt steak if it is too expensive?

Flank steak, flat iron steak, or hanger steak make excellent, flavorful substitutes that respond similarly to quick high-heat searing.

Q8: How long does fresh skirt steak last in the refrigerator?

Fresh vacuum-sealed skirt steak lasts 3 to 5 days in the refrigerator, or up to 6 to 12 months when properly wrapped and stored in the freezer.
