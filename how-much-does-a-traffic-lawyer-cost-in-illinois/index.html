How Much Does a Traffic Lawyer Cost in Illinois?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does a traffic lawyer cost in Illinois requires distinguishing between petty traffic offenses and criminal aggravated speeding misdemeanors, as well as navigating local circuit court branches in Cook, DuPage, Lake, and Will counties. On average, hiring a traffic attorney in Illinois costs between $200 and $600 for standard petty speeding citations on a flat-fee basis. Defending against Class A or B misdemeanor aggravated speeding charges typically costs $800 to $2,000.

Illinois Traffic Court Structure and Petty Offense Flat Fees

In Illinois, traffic citations are heard in the Circuit Court of the county where the citation was issued (such as the Richard J. Daley Center or suburban municipal district courts in Cook County). Petty traffic offenses—such as speeding 1 to 25 MPH over the limit, disobeying a traffic control device, or improper lane usage—carry maximum statutory fines of up to $1,000, but do not carry jail time.

Most private traffic attorneys quote flat fees between $250 and $550 to handle petty traffic matters. The attorney files a formal appearance, examines the police officer's radar/laser calibration logs, and negotiates with county or municipal prosecutors. In many traffic courts, an attorney can resolve the ticket without the client having to take a day off work to appear in court.

Class A and B Aggravated Speeding Misdemeanors in Illinois

A unique and punitive aspect of Illinois law is codified under 625 ILCS 5/11-601.5 regarding 'aggravated speeding.' Driving 26 to 34 MPH over the speed limit is classified as a Class B misdemeanor, carrying up to 6 months in county jail and fines up to $1,500. Driving 35 MPH or more over the posted limit is classified as a Class A misdemeanor, punishable by up to 364 days in jail and fines up to $2,500.

Crucially, Illinois state statute strictly prohibits judges from granting court supervision for aggravated speeding in school zones or construction zones, and severely restricts supervision on standard roadways. Because an aggravated speeding conviction results in a permanent, lifelong criminal record that cannot be expunged, hiring an experienced defense attorney ($800 to $2,000) is essential to negotiate a charge amendment down to a petty speeding violation.

The Value of Illinois Court Supervision

In Illinois, the premier objective in resolving any traffic ticket is securing 'Court Supervision' under 730 ILCS 5/5-6-1. Court supervision is an order of deferred judgment. If the driver pays a court assessment and completes a designated supervisory period (typically 90 to 120 days) without committing new traffic infractions, the charges are dismissed and no conviction is entered on the driver's public driving record.

Under Illinois law, accumulating three moving violation convictions within any 12-month period triggers an automatic driver's license suspension by the Secretary of State (two convictions for drivers under age 21). Furthermore, auto insurance companies only surcharge policies when a formal conviction is reported. An attorney ensures court supervision is granted, protecting your license and avoiding thousands in insurance hikes.

How to Fight a Traffic Ticket in Illinois

Follow these five tactical steps to resolve an Illinois traffic ticket without a conviction.

  1. Do Not Pay the Fine by Mail

    Mailing in payment without requesting supervision results in an automatic conviction entered on your Secretary of State driving abstract.

  2. Check If Court Appearance Is Mandatory

    Look at the bottom of the citation; if the 'Court Appearance Required' box is marked, you or your lawyer must appear.

  3. Retain a Local Traffic Attorney in That County

    Hire a lawyer who regularly negotiates with prosecutors in that specific county municipal district.

  4. Request a Reduction to Court Supervision

    Have your attorney petition for an agreed order of court supervision or charge reduction to a non-moving violation.

  5. Pay Court Costs and Complete Traffic School Promptly

    Pay the circuit clerk fees and complete online traffic safety school (if ordered) before the supervision period expires.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a traffic lawyer cost in Illinois?

Hiring a traffic lawyer in Illinois typically costs between $200 and $600 for petty tickets, and $800 to $2,000 for aggravated speeding misdemeanors.

Q2: What is court supervision for a traffic ticket in Illinois?

Court supervision is a deferred sentence; upon completing the supervision period without violations, no conviction appears on your public driving record.

Q3: Can a traffic lawyer get a ticket dismissed in Illinois?

Yes, lawyers can get tickets dismissed due to improper police ticketing procedures, radar errors, or by negotiating zero-point resolutions.

Q4: Is speeding 26 mph over the limit a crime in Illinois?

Yes, speeding 26 to 34 MPH over is a Class B misdemeanor; 35+ MPH over is a Class A misdemeanor, both carrying potential jail time.

Q5: How many traffic convictions suspend a license in Illinois?

Three moving violation convictions within 12 months triggers an automatic suspension (two convictions for drivers under 21).

Q6: Do I have to go to court if I hire a lawyer in Illinois?

For most petty offenses, your lawyer can appear on your behalf; however, Class A and B aggravated speeding misdemeanors may require your presence.

Q7: How long does a traffic ticket stay on your Illinois record?

Moving violation convictions remain on your official Illinois driving record for 4 to 5 years (longer for alcohol-related offenses).

Q8: Does court supervision increase insurance rates in Illinois?

No, court supervision is not reported as a conviction on your public driving record, so insurance companies do not see it or raise rates.
