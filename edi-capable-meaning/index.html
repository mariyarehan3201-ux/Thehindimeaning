Defining EDI Capable: Computer-to-Computer Business Integration

In supply chain logistics, B2B e-commerce, and enterprise resource planning (ERP), EDI capable means that a commercial business possesses the technical software, communication infrastructure, and automated systems required to transmit and receive standardized electronic business documents directly with trading partners without manual human intervention.

EDI stands for Electronic Data Interchange. Before EDI, purchasing involved paper purchase orders mailed between offices, manual faxes, and tedious manual data entry into accounting software. An EDI-capable vendor utilizes standardized electronic protocols (such as ANSI ASC X12 in North America or EDIFACT globally) to exchange business documents directly between computer systems in seconds, eliminating manual data entry errors and accelerating global fulfillment.

Review standard commercial EDI transaction documents used in retail supply chains:

EDI Document Code Standard Document Title Business Operational Function Replaced Paper Document
EDI 850 Purchase Order (PO) Buyer transmits formal electronic purchase order to supplier Paper or emailed corporate purchase order
EDI 855 Purchase Order Acknowledgment (POA) Supplier confirms order receipt, pricing, and ship date Vendor order confirmation email / fax
EDI 856 Advance Shipping Notice (ASN) Detailed packing barcode list transmitted before shipment arrives Physical packing slip / paper bill of lading
EDI 810 Commercial Invoice Supplier automatically bills buyer accounting system for payment Paper or PDF mailed vendor billing invoice
EDI 846 Inventory Inquiry / Advice Supplier feeds real-time warehouse inventory counts to retailer Weekly spreadsheet warehouse stock reports
EDI 997 Functional Acknowledgment Automated technical receipt confirming EDI transmission was received Read receipt confirmation

Why Major Retailers Mandate EDI Compliance for All Vendors

Massive retail enterprises like Amazon, Home Depot, and Costco process millions of product shipments daily across thousands of vendor suppliers. Managing this volume via manual email or PDF invoices is physically impossible. Consequently, major retailers enforce strict EDI compliance mandates as a non-negotiable prerequisite for doing business.

If a manufacturer wants to sell products on retail store shelves, they must prove they are fully EDI capable. When Walmart generates an electronic EDI 850 purchase order, the supplier system must receive the order automatically, transmit an EDI 855 acknowledgment, print standardized UCC-128 GS1 shipping barcodes for pallets, and broadcast an EDI 856 Advance Shipping Notice (ASN) the instant the freight truck pulls away from the loading dock. Failing to transmit timely, error-free EDI documents results in automated financial chargeback penalties assessed against the vendor invoice.

Compare EDI implementation models for growing commercial suppliers:

EDI Implementation Architecture Technical Infrastructure Required Upfront Cost & Maintenance Target Business Volume
Web EDI (Portal Based) Web browser interface; manual data entry into portal Low upfront cost; monthly SaaS subscription Small suppliers processing under 50 orders monthly
Cloud EDI (Managed Services) Third-party provider manages mapping and compliance Moderate setup fee; turnkey outsourced management Growing mid-sized suppliers expanding into major retail chains
Direct / Integrated EDI (ERP) Dedicated software translator integrated directly into SAP/NetSuite Highest upfront cost; requires internal IT specialists High-volume enterprise suppliers shipping thousands of daily orders
AS2 (Applicability Statement 2) Secure direct peer-to-peer encrypted internet protocol Requires dedicated server, static IP, and SSL certificates Direct corporate connections bypassing commercial VAN networks

Becoming EDI capable elevates a small manufacturer into an enterprise-ready supplier capable of scaling with global retail giants.

How a Business Becomes Certified EDI Capable

Follow this implementation roadmap to achieve full vendor EDI compliance.

  1. Request the Retailer EDI Implementation Guideline

    Obtain the trading partner EDI onboarding manual detailing required document types (850, 856, 810), segment qualifiers, and barcode specs.

  2. Select an EDI Solution (Web, Cloud, or ERP Integrated)

    Partner with an established EDI software provider (such as SPS Commerce, TrueCommerce, or SPS) aligned with your accounting software.

  3. Acquire a GS1 Company Prefix and Barcode System

    Register with GS1 US to obtain an official company prefix for printing scannable GS1-128 shipping container pallet labels.

  4. Execute End-to-End Testing Scenarios with Retailer

    Conduct testing cycles transmitting simulated purchase orders, ASNs, and invoices to verify that data mapping satisfies strict partner criteria.

  5. Receive Production Cutover Approval

    Once testing validates with zero syntax errors, receive formal production certification from the retailer EDI compliance department.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does EDI capable mean?

EDI capable means a business has the software and systems to send and receive standardized electronic business documents (like POs and invoices) directly with retailers.

Q2: What does EDI stand for in business?

EDI stands for Electronic Data Interchange, the computer-to-computer exchange of standard business documents between trading partners.

Q3: Why do retailers like Walmart require EDI?

Retailers require EDI to eliminate manual paper entry, speed up supply chain order fulfillment, and prevent expensive shipping errors.

Q4: What is an EDI 850 and EDI 810?

An EDI 850 is an electronic Purchase Order; an EDI 810 is an electronic Invoice billed to the buyer.

Q5: What is an Advance Shipping Notice (ASN)?

An ASN (EDI 856) is an electronic manifest transmitted before a shipment arrives, detailing pallet barcodes, box counts, and contents.

Q6: What happens if an EDI transmission has an error?

The receiving system rejects the transmission, and major retailers assess automated financial chargeback fines against the vendor.

Q7: How much does it cost to become EDI capable?

Basic Web EDI portals cost $50 to $150 per month, while fully integrated cloud ERP systems range from $2,000 to $10,000+ in setup fees.

Q8: What is a Value Added Network (VAN) in EDI?

A VAN is a secure private telecommunications network that acts like a specialized post office routing EDI transmissions between companies.
