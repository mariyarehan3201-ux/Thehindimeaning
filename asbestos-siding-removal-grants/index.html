Asbestos Siding Removal Grants

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 14, 2026

Asbestos siding removal grants provide essential financial assistance for property owners who cannot afford the substantial cost of professional asbestos-cement shingle remediation. Federal, state, and local government programs offer various grant, loan, and rebate mechanisms to support low-income homeowners, housing authorities, and nonprofit organizations in safely removing hazardous asbestos siding from residential structures.

Federal Programs Supporting Asbestos Siding Removal

The primary federal funding mechanism supporting asbestos removal in residential properties is the Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) program administered by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). CDBG funds are distributed to states, counties, and entitlement communities who then establish local programs targeting lead paint and asbestos hazard remediation for low-to-moderate income households. While CDBG program eligibility and amounts vary by locality, grants commonly cover between $5,000 and $25,000 of hazard removal costs per household.

The HUD HOME Investment Partnerships Program provides additional federal housing rehabilitation funding that can be directed toward asbestos remediation as part of comprehensive home rehabilitation projects. Many participating jurisdictions have incorporated asbestos inspection, encapsulation, or removal as eligible rehabilitation activities under HOME-funded programs targeting affordable housing stock preservation.

State and Local Asbestos Removal Assistance Programs

Many states have established dedicated asbestos removal assistance programs funded through environmental trust funds, state general appropriations, or federal pass-through funding. California's Healthy Homes Program, for example, provides grants for low-income households addressing multiple housing hazards including asbestos, lead paint, and mold simultaneously. Similar programs exist in New York, Massachusetts, Illinois, Michigan, Ohio, and other states with significant pre-war housing stock.

Local county and municipal governments frequently administer owner-occupied housing rehabilitation programs that include asbestos hazard remediation among eligible scopes of work. Property owners should contact their county community development, public health, or housing department to inquire about currently funded local programs, as availability and funding levels change annually based on federal and state appropriations cycles.

Alternative Financing Options When Grants Are Not Available

In regions where grant funding is exhausted, oversubscribed, or unavailable, property owners have several alternative financing mechanisms to pursue asbestos siding remediation. HUD Title I Property Improvement Loans offer federally insured financing for home improvement projects including hazardous material remediation, with loan amounts up to $25,000 for single-family homes repayable over up to twenty years.

Some states offer deferred payment loans for qualified low-income homeowners, where no monthly payments are due during occupancy and the loan balance is repaid only upon sale or transfer of the property. Energy efficiency improvement programs administered through utility companies occasionally cover asbestos encapsulation when it is performed alongside insulation or exterior upgrade projects, providing a supplemental funding avenue.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Are there grants available to remove asbestos siding?

Yes. HUD's CDBG program, HOME Investment Partnerships, state hazard remediation programs, and USDA rural development grants provide financial assistance for qualifying low-income homeowners.

Q2: Who qualifies for asbestos siding removal grants?

Eligibility typically requires property ownership, primary residency, and household income at or below 80% of Area Median Income (AMI), though criteria vary by program.

Q3: How do I apply for an asbestos removal grant?

Contact your local county or city housing or community development department, which administers federal CDBG and HOME program funds for housing hazard remediation.

Q4: How much do asbestos siding removal grants pay?

Grant amounts typically range from $5,000 to $25,000, covering full or partial licensed abatement costs depending on program funding availability and property scope.

Q5: Can I choose my own contractor for asbestos grant work?

Usually not. Grant programs require the administering agency to competitively bid the work among licensed contractors meeting program performance and insurance requirements.

Q6: What if I don't qualify for a grant?

Non-qualifying homeowners can pursue HUD Title I Property Improvement Loans, state deferred payment loans, or utility company energy efficiency programs that may include asbestos encapsulation.

Q7: Are grant funds taxable income?

Housing rehabilitation grants specifically for health hazard remediation are generally not treated as taxable income under IRS guidance, but consult a tax professional for specific circumstances.

Q8: How long does the grant application process take?

From initial application to contractor completion, the process typically takes three to six months depending on program waitlists, funding cycles, and contractor availability.
