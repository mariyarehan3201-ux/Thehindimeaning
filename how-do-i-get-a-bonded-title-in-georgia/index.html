How Do I Get a Bonded Title in Georgia?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Discovering how do i get a bonded title in georgia is the indispensable legal procedure for vehicle owners who bought a car, truck, or motorcycle without receiving an official certificate of title or whose ownership paperwork was permanently lost. In Georgia, the Department of Revenue (DOR) Motor Vehicle Division requires a surety bond (Form MV-46) to establish legal vehicle ownership, shielding previous owners, lienholders, and the state from fraudulent claims while granting you lawful registration and transfer privileges.

Georgia DOR Eligibility Criteria and Disqualifying Factors

Before initiating a bonded title application in Georgia, you must determine whether your vehicle qualifies under state law. Under Georgia Motor Vehicle Title Act regulations, bonded titles are available only to bona fide Georgia residents or military personnel stationed within the state. The vehicle must require a title under Georgia law (generally model years 1986 and newer, as vehicles 1985 and older are title-exempt and transfer via bill of sale).

Crucially, certain circumstances immediately disqualify a vehicle from obtaining a bonded title. If the vehicle is branded as junk, salvage, or non-repairable, or if an unresolved stolen vehicle report exists in the National Motor Vehicle Title Information System (NMVTIS), the state will not issue a bond. Furthermore, if an active, unsatisfied lien exists, you must obtain a formal lien release before the Georgia DOR will consider your bonded title petition.

Form T-22B Law Enforcement Inspection and Bond Calculation

The practical process begins by arranging a physical vehicle identification number (VIN) inspection. You must print Georgia Form T-22B (Certification of Inspection) and have it completed by a certified Georgia law enforcement officer (such as a county sheriff, municipal police officer, or state trooper). The officer inspects the public VIN plate on the dashboard, the federal certification label on the door jamb, and verifies against the National Crime Information Center (NCIC) database that the vehicle is not listed as stolen.

Next, you must establish the statutory value of the vehicle to purchase the surety bond. The Georgia DOR utilizes the Motor Vehicle Assessment Manual to establish the vehicle's retail value. State law mandates that your surety bond must equal exactly twice (200%) the determined retail value, with a statutory minimum bond limit of $5,000. You then purchase Georgia Form MV-46 (Motor Vehicle Title Bond) from an insurance or surety brokerage licensed in Georgia, which typically costs between $100 and $250 for vehicles valued under $10,000.

County Tag Office Submission, Bond Duration, and Clean Title Conversion

Once your documentation package—including Form MV-1, Form MV-46, Form MV-46A, Form T-22B, bill of sale, and state title fees ($18 title fee plus standard ad valorem Title Ad Valorem Tax, or TAVT)—is compiled, you submit it in person to your local county tax commissioner tag office. The county clerk reviews the documents and forwards the dossier to the Georgia Department of Revenue in Atlanta for final administrative audit and issuance.

The bonded title issued by Georgia bears a bonded brand. This surety bond remains in effect for exactly four years from the date of issuance. If no prior owner or undisclosed lienholder emerges to dispute your ownership during this four-year statutory period, the bond automatically expires, and you can apply to the Georgia DOR to receive a clean, unbonded Georgia title.

How to Obtain a Bonded Title in Georgia

Follow these five clear steps to successfully apply for and receive a bonded title through the Georgia DOR.

  1. Schedule a Form T-22B Police Inspection

    Have a local Georgia law enforcement officer physically inspect the vehicle VIN and complete Form T-22B.

  2. Determine Vehicle Value with County Tag Office

    Contact your county tax commissioner to obtain the official state retail valuation from the DOR database.

  3. Purchase a 4-Year Georgia Title Surety Bond

    Obtain Form MV-46 from an authorized surety broker for exactly double the state-assessed vehicle value.

  4. Complete and Notarize Form MV-46A Affidavit

    Detail the complete history of how you acquired the vehicle, why a title was unavailable, and have it notarized.

  5. Submit the Complete Dossier to County Tag Office

    Present all forms, proof of insurance, Georgia ID, and pay the $18 title fee and applicable TAVT.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a Georgia title bond cost?

The surety bond typically costs between $100 and $200 for standard vehicles, though rates increase for luxury or commercial vehicles.

Q2: How long is a bonded title active in Georgia?

The bond remains legally active for 4 years; after 4 years with no claims, you can request a standard clean title.

Q3: Can I sell a vehicle with a Georgia bonded title?

Yes, you can legally sell and transfer a vehicle with a bonded title; the bonded brand simply transfers to the new buyer.

Q4: Can out-of-state residents get a Georgia bonded title?

No, bonded titles are strictly reserved for Georgia residents or active-duty military stationed in Georgia.

Q5: Do older vehicles before 1986 need a title in Georgia?

No, vehicles manufactured in 1985 or earlier are exempt from title requirements in Georgia and transfer on a bill of sale.

Q6: What is Georgia Form MV-46A?

Form MV-46A is the mandatory notarized affidavit where the applicant explains under penalty of perjury how the vehicle was obtained.

Q7: Can I get a bonded title if there is an active lien on the car?

No, you must obtain a formal Form T-4 Lien Release from the previous lender before a bonded title can be issued.

Q8: Where do I submit my Georgia bonded title paperwork?

You submit your completed dossier to your local County Tax Commissioner Tag Office where you reside.
