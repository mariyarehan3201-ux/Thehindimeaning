How Do I Become a Mediator in Florida?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Asking how do i become a mediator in florida is the entry point into a prestigious, intellectually rewarding career in alternative dispute resolution (ADR). Florida is recognized as a national pioneer in court-connected mediation, governed by strict certification standards administered by the Florida Supreme Court through the Dispute Resolution Center (DRC). Mediators serve as neutral facilitators who assist litigating parties in resolving conflicts outside of court trials. Navigating the Florida Supreme Court's point system, completing approved training courses, and fulfilling mentorship requirements unlocks official certification.

The Dispute Resolution Center (DRC) and Certification Categories

In the state of Florida, court-ordered mediation is regulated under Part II of the Florida Rules for Certified and Court-Appointed Mediators. The Florida Supreme Court oversees all mediator standards via its Dispute Resolution Center (DRC) in Tallahassee. The DRC certifies mediators across five distinct jurisdictional categories: County Court (small claims disputes up to $50,000), Family (dissolution of marriage, paternity, and child support), Circuit Civil (complex commercial, personal injury, and real estate litigation over $50,000), Dependency (child welfare and foster placement), and Appellate.

Unlike many states that restrict court mediation exclusively to licensed attorneys, Florida maintains an open multidisciplinary qualification pathway. Professionals with backgrounds in psychology, human resources, accounting, social work, education, and business can qualify for Florida Supreme Court certification by satisfying rigorous educational, training, and mentorship point thresholds.

The 100-Point Qualification Matrix: Education and Experience

Under Florida Rule 10.100, candidates for mediator certification must amass at least one hundred objective qualification points before submitting an application. The Florida Supreme Court awards points based on a verified combination of formal education, professional experience, mediation training, and mentorship observations. For example, in Circuit Civil mediation, possessing a Juris Doctor (JD) degree awards fifty points, while a master's degree in a related field awards forty points.

Professional experience contributes significantly to your point total. Candidates earn points for years of professional practice in law, accounting, mental health, or conflict management (typically five points per year of active practice). Completing the mandatory Florida Supreme Court-approved training program contributes thirty points toward the required hundred-point baseline, ensuring all certified candidates possess deep intellectual and practical grounding.

Mentorship Observations, Good Moral Character, and Practice Launch

Completing the forty-hour academic course is only the halfway mark. To qualify for certification, candidates must complete mentorship requirements under the direct supervision of a Florida Supreme Court Certified Mediator. Mentorship involves two distinct phases: observing active live mediations conducted by certified mentors, and co-mediating cases where the candidate conducts the mediation while the certified mentor observes and evaluates their performance.

Upon completing mentorship logs (documented on official DRC Form M-series rosters), candidates submit a formal application packet to the Dispute Resolution Center in Tallahassee, accompanied by a clean criminal background check, character reference affidavits, and licensing fees. Once certified, mediators must complete sixteen hours of Continuing Mediator Education (CME)—including four hours of mediator ethics—every two years to maintain active certified standing on Florida judicial circuit rosters.

How to Become a Certified Mediator in Florida Step by Step

Follow these five statutory steps to achieve official Florida Supreme Court mediator certification.

  1. Calculate Your 100 Qualification Points

    Review Florida Rule 10.100 to ensure your combination of college degrees, professional experience, and training equals at least one hundred points.

  2. Enroll in a DRC-Approved Mediation Course

    Complete an official Florida Supreme Court-approved 28-hour or 40-hour training program in County, Family, or Circuit Civil mediation.

  3. Complete Mandatory Mentorship Observations

    Coordinate with certified Florida mediators to complete the required number of live mediation observations and supervised co-mediations.

  4. Submit Application Packet to the DRC

    Submit your certified course certificate, mentorship forms, college transcripts, character references, and background check fee to the DRC in Tallahassee.

  5. Join Circuit Rosters and Complete CME Hours

    Apply to be listed on local judicial circuit court appointment rosters and complete sixteen hours of Continuing Mediator Education every two years.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Do you have to be a lawyer to be a mediator in Florida?

No, Florida does not require mediators to be lawyers; professionals with bachelor's or master's degrees in psychology, business, or education can certify.

Q2: What is the Dispute Resolution Center (DRC) in Florida?

The DRC is the administrative arm of the Florida Supreme Court in Tallahassee that oversees mediator certification, training, and ethics standards.

Q3: How many points are needed to become a certified mediator in Florida?

Candidates must accumulate at least one hundred qualification points through education, professional experience, training, and mentorship.

Q4: How long is the certified Florida mediation training course?

County Court mediation training requires 28 hours, while Family, Circuit Civil, and Dependency courses each require 40 hours of approved training.

Q5: How much does a certified mediator make in Florida?

Private civil and family mediators typically charge between $200 and $500 per hour, split equally between the disputing parties.

Q6: What is mediator mentorship in Florida?

Mentorship is hands-on apprenticeship where candidates observe and co-mediate live legal cases under the supervision of a certified mentor mediator.

Q7: How often do Florida mediators have to renew their certification?

Mediators must renew certification every two years, completing sixteen hours of Continuing Mediator Education (CME) including four hours of ethics.

Q8: Can a Florida certified mediator practice in other states?

Florida certification is widely respected, but other states have their own specific court rules and certification transfer procedures.
