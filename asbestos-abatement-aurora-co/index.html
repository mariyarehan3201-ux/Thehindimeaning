Asbestos Abatement Aurora CO

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 24, 2026

Asbestos abatement in Aurora, CO is governed by Colorado Air Quality Control Commission Regulation No. 8 and enforced by the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment. Because Aurora features a mix of historic military infrastructure from the former Fitzsimons Army Medical Center, mid-century residential neighborhoods, and expanding commercial developments, certified abatement contractors are vital for public safety.

Colorado State Regulatory Oversight (CDPHE Regulation 8)

In Colorado, asbestos abatement is strictly regulated by the Air Pollution Control Division of the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE) under Regulation No. 8, Part B. Colorado maintains trigger thresholds that are more stringent than federal NESHAP standards for residential properties.

Under Colorado law, any single-family residential project that disturbs more than thirty-two square feet, fifty linear feet, or the volume of a standard fifty-five gallon drum of asbestos-containing material is classified as a regulated abatement project. For public and commercial buildings, the threshold drops to 160 square feet or 260 linear feet. Contractors must hold active CDPHE company licenses, employ state-certified supervisors, and submit official project notifications ten working days prior to work.

Common Asbestos Materials in Aurora Architecture

Aurora housing stock spans post-war suburban developments in Delmar Parkway, historic ranches in Morris Heights, and multi-family communities across Arapahoe and Adams counties. These residences frequently harbor asbestos in drywall joint compound, acoustic popcorn ceilings, vinyl floor tile, and heating duct insulation.

Licensed Colorado abatement contractors construct sealed negative-pressure containments using double layers of six-mil polyethylene sheeting taped over all registers and openings. Continuous computerized differential manometers verify that the containment chamber maintains negative pressure relative to clean living areas, preventing fiber migration during removal.

Following removal, Colorado Regulation 8 mandates aggressive air clearance testing conducted by an independent certified Air Monitoring Specialist before containment barriers can be dismantled. Waste must be transported under state manifests to licensed Colorado landfills such as the Denver Arapahoe Disposal Site (DADS).

How to Coordinate an Asbestos Abatement Project in Aurora, CO

Step-by-step guidance for homeowners and contractors organizing licensed asbestos removal in Aurora.

  1. Commission an Independent Asbestos Survey

    Hire a CDPHE-certified Building Inspector to collect bulk samples and author an official state survey report.

  2. Solicit Bids from CDPHE-Licensed Contractors

    Obtain itemized proposals from contractors holding active General Abatement Contractor licenses with the State of Colorado.

  3. Submit 10-Day CDPHE Project Notification

    Ensure the contractor files the mandatory state notification and pays required review fees to the CDPHE at least ten working days early.

  4. Establish Negative-Pressure Containment

    Confirm that crews erect double six-mil poly barriers and operate continuous HEPA negative air scrubbers venting outside.

  5. Complete Independent Air Clearance Testing

    Hire an independent certified Air Monitoring Specialist to perform visual inspections and aggressive PCM/TEM air clearance.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is Colorado Regulation 8?

Regulation 8 is the Colorado state air quality rule governing asbestos certification, abatement procedures, notifications, and air clearance testing.

Q2: What is the trigger threshold for asbestos in Colorado homes?

For single-family homes, Colorado regulates any project disturbing more than 32 square feet, 50 linear feet, or a 55-gallon drum volume of ACM.

Q3: How much does asbestos removal cost in Aurora, CO?

Residential removal in Aurora typically ranges from $1,500 to $4,000 for pipe wrapping or popcorn ceilings, and $3,500 to $8,000+ for large projects.

Q4: Can an Aurora homeowner remove asbestos themselves?

Single-family owner-occupants may legally remove materials in their own home, but professional removal is strongly advised due to health hazards and strict disposal laws.

Q5: Where is asbestos waste disposed of in the Aurora area?

Waste must be double-bagged, manifested, and hauled to authorized landfills like the Denver Arapahoe Disposal Site (DADS) in Arapahoe County.

Q6: Is independent air clearance required in Colorado?

Yes, Colorado Regulation 8 mandates that final visual and aggressive air clearance testing be conducted by an independent certified Air Monitoring Specialist.

Q7: What permits are required for asbestos removal in Aurora?

Contractors must file a state abatement permit with the CDPHE and secure municipal demolition or building permits from the City of Aurora.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Asbestos abatement in Aurora, CO requires strict compliance with Colorado CDPHE Regulation 8 standards to protect families, workers, and neighborhood air quality. Property owners and developers must partner with state-licensed, insured abatement contractors and independent air monitoring specialists. Securing formal laboratory air clearance guarantees that renovated spaces are thoroughly safe for modern living.
