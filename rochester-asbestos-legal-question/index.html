Historic Industrial Exposure: Kodak Park, Bausch & Lomb, and Utilities

The primary epicenter of asbestos exposure in Rochester was the legendary Eastman Kodak Park complex. Spanning over 1,300 acres with hundreds of buildings, Kodak Park operated as an independent industrial city complete with its own multi-unit electrical power plants, miles of high-pressure steam distribution headers, and chemical refining pipelines. Asbestos was omnipresent throughout Kodak Park: pipefitters, millwrights, and insulators handled asbestos block lagging, corrugated pipe wrap, valve packings, and chemical-resistant gaskets daily.

Beyond Kodak, major manufacturing facilities across Monroe County exposed thousands of workers. Optics giant Bausch & Lomb and photocopying pioneer Xerox operated extensive manufacturing plants with high-temperature furnace and drying operations insulated with asbestos. Heavy automotive parts manufacturing at GM Delco Products, along with Rochester Gas & Electric (RG&E) power generating stations (such as Russell Station and Beebee Station), exposed boilermakers, electricians, and maintenance trades to asbestos turbine blankets and boiler refractory.

New York Asbestos Law: Statutes of Limitations and Liability Framework

New York civil statutes govern personal injury and wrongful death lawsuits arising from asbestos exposure. Under New York Civil Practice Law and Rules (CPLR § 214-c), personal injury claims must be filed within three years from the date the injury was officially diagnosed, or three years from when the disease reasonably should have been discovered through the exercise of reasonable diligence. For surviving family members filing a wrongful death lawsuit, EPTL § 5-4.1 establishes a strict two-year filing deadline from the date of the victim's death.

New York operates under a comparative fault system (Article 14-A of the CPLR). In asbestos product liability litigation, lawsuits target the manufacturers, distributors, and premises owners that negligently marketed asbestos products without warnings. New York courts have established dedicated asbestos trial dockets—such as the 7th Judicial District in Rochester and the New York City Asbestos Litigation (NYCAL) model—that provide expedited trial calendars for terminally ill mesothelioma plaintiffs.

Compensation Channels: Trust Funds, Monroe County Courts, and VA Benefits

Victims of asbestos exposure in Rochester have access to several distinct financial compensation channels simultaneously. First, claims can be submitted to more than sixty active asbestos bankruptcy trust funds holding over $30 billion in dedicated assets. These trusts disburse non-adversarial administrative settlements based on documented employment and pathology records without requiring a courtroom trial.

Second, experienced attorneys file formal civil lawsuits in Monroe County Supreme Court against solvent equipment manufacturers, boiler makers, valve producers, and chemical suppliers. Third, military veterans residing in Rochester who were exposed during naval shipyard service or aboard naval vessels qualify for 100 percent service-connected disability compensation from the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA), alongside specialized medical care at the Canandaigua or Buffalo VA Medical Centers.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the statute of limitations for an asbestos lawsuit in Rochester, New York?

Under New York law, personal injury claims must be filed within 3 years of diagnosis, and wrongful death lawsuits within 2 years of the date of death.

Q2: Were workers at Eastman Kodak Park exposed to asbestos?

Yes, Kodak Park had its own massive power plants, chemical lines, and steam systems heavily insulated with asbestos.

Q3: Can I receive compensation if the Rochester company I worked for closed down?

Yes, lawsuits target the manufacturers of the asbestos products, or claims are submitted to multi-billion-dollar bankruptcy trusts.

Q4: What court handles asbestos lawsuits in Rochester?

Lawsuits are typically filed in Monroe County Supreme Court under the New York 7th Judicial District asbestos docket.

Q5: Do I have to go to court for an asbestos claim in Rochester?

Most asbestos claims are resolved through negotiated out-of-court settlements or administrative trust payouts without a trial.

Q6: Can military veterans in Rochester receive VA benefits for mesothelioma?

Yes, veterans exposed to asbestos aboard naval vessels or bases can receive 100 percent service-connected disability compensation.

Q7: How much does an asbestos lawyer in Rochester cost?

Asbestos litigators work on a contingency-fee basis, meaning you pay zero upfront costs and legal fees are paid solely from recovered funds.

Q8: Can family members receive compensation for secondary take-home asbestos exposure?

Yes, New York courts recognize secondary exposure claims for family members who washed asbestos-contaminated work clothes.
