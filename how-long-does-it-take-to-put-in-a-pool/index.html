How Long Does It Take to Put in a Pool?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how long does it take to put in a pool requires distinguishing between the type of swimming pool you select, municipal permitting delays, and the physical phases of backyard construction. While an above-ground pool can be assembled and filled in 1 to 3 days, installing a permanent in-ground pool takes between 3 to 16 weeks of active construction. In total, when factoring in city building permits, HOA architectural approvals, excavation, plumbing, concrete curing, and deck landscaping, homeowners should plan for a total project horizon of 2 to 6 months.

Pool Types and Timelines: Gunite Concrete vs. Fiberglass vs. Vinyl Liner

The single largest variable governing pool construction time is the pool shell material. Traditional gunite or shotcrete (poured concrete) pools are completely custom-engineered on-site. Building a concrete pool involves excavation, tying intricate rebar steel cages, roughing in plumbing manifolds, pneumatically spraying concrete, waiting 28 days for the concrete shell to hydrate and cure, and applying waterline tile and plaster interior finishes. Consequently, gunite concrete pool construction requires 8 to 16 weeks of construction time.

In sharp contrast, factory-manufactured fiberglass pools offer the fastest in-ground turnaround. The fiberglass pool shell is molded off-site in a climate-controlled factory and delivered on an oversize flatbed trailer. Once the backyard hole is excavated and a level crushed-stone bed is laid, a crane hoists the shell into the excavation in a single afternoon. Fiberglass pools can be set, plumbed, backfilled, and filled with water in just 3 to 6 weeks. Vinyl liner pools fall in between, taking approximately 4 to 8 weeks to assemble galvanized steel or polymer wall panels and fit a custom vinyl membrane.

The Permitting and Engineering Pre-Construction Phase (4 to 8 Weeks)

Homeowners are frequently surprised to learn that before a single excavator scoop of dirt is dug, 4 to 8 weeks are spent navigating bureaucratic approvals. Before issuing a residential swimming pool permit, municipal building departments require comprehensive plot surveys, structural engineering blueprints, gas line calculations (for pool heaters), electrical load calculations (for 240V variable speed pumps), and strict civil drainage and stormwater management plans.

Furthermore, properties located within Homeowners Associations (HOAs) must submit architectural review applications detailing pool deck setbacks, equipment noise shielding, and security perimeter fencing. If construction requires crossing adjacent municipal sidewalks or public easements, obtaining right-of-way permits can add several more weeks before excavation can legally commence.

Unforeseen Delays: Weather, Underground Utilities, and Soil Conditions

Even the best pool builders encounter unforeseen environmental delays. Torrential rainstorms are the bane of pool construction; rainwater can fill an open excavation, collapse dirt trench walls, and turn the backyard into a mud pit that requires days of pumping and drying before heavy machinery can resume work. In addition, plaster and concrete cannot be shot during freezing temperatures or active rain.

Subsurface surprises represent another major source of project delays. If excavation crews strike solid underground granite ledge, hydraulic jackhammers or specialized rock-breaking excavators must be brought in, adding thousands of dollars and 1 to 2 weeks of noisy delays. Similarly, hitting a high water table requires installing continuous dewatering wellpoints to prevent the newly excavated hole from flooding or floating the pool shell.

How to Manage a Backyard Pool Installation Project

Follow these five strategic steps to keep your pool construction on schedule and on budget.

  1. Finalize Pool Design and Secure Permits Early

    Submit architectural plans to your city and HOA in winter or early spring to avoid peak construction permit backlogs.

  2. Mark All Underground Public and Private Utilities

    Call 811 to locate buried electric, water, gas, and cable lines before excavators dig into your property.

  3. Monitor Structural Rebar and Plumbing Inspections

    Ensure city building inspectors sign off on steel bonding and hydrostatic plumbing pressure tests before concrete is shot.

  4. Faithfully Water the Gunite Shell During Curing

    Spray the concrete shell with garden hoses twice daily for 28 days to ensure the cement achieves maximum tensile strength.

  5. Balance Water Chemistry Immediately After Filling

    Run filtration pumps 24/7 and brush new plaster walls daily for 14 days to prevent mineral scaling and staining.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the fastest in-ground pool to install?

Fiberglass pools are the fastest, often installed, backfilled, and plumbed in just 3 to 6 weeks.

Q2: Why does concrete pool construction take so long?

Gunite concrete requires a mandatory 28-day water curing period to reach structural hardness before plaster and tile can be applied.

Q3: How long does the permitting process take for a pool?

City building permits and HOA reviews typically take 4 to 8 weeks depending on local municipal backlogs.

Q4: Can a pool be built during the winter?

Excavation and plumbing can proceed in mild winters, but concrete shooting, plastering, and masonry require temperatures above 40°F.

Q5: How long does it take to dig the hole for a pool?

Excavating a standard residential pool hole takes 1 to 3 days of heavy machine digging unless underground rock is hit.

Q6: How long after filling can you swim in a new pool?

Once filled, water chemistry must be balanced and sanitized, which usually takes 3 to 5 days before it is safe for swimmers.

Q7: Do vinyl liner pools take longer than fiberglass?

Yes, vinyl liner pools take slightly longer (4 to 8 weeks) because wall frames and concrete floors must be constructed on-site.

Q8: What is the best month to start building a pool?

Starting in late fall or early winter ensures permits and construction are completed well before the summer swimming season.
