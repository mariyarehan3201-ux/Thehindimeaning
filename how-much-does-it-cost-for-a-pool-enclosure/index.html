How Much Does It Cost for a Pool Enclosure?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how much does it cost for a pool enclosure requires distinguishing between open-air extruded aluminum screen enclosures (pool cages) and fully conditioned structural glass solariums. On average, a standard aluminum screen pool enclosure costs between $8,000 and $22,000 ($10 to $20 per square foot). Fully enclosed four-season glass pool houses, polycarbonate domes, or motorized telescopic retractable enclosures range from $35,000 to $95,000+ ($50 to $120+ per square foot).

Aluminum Screen Cages vs. Polycarbonate vs. Glass Enclosures

The enclosure framing and glazing materials dictate total project investment and seasonal functionality. Aluminum screen pool cages—ubiquitous throughout Florida, Texas, and the Sunbelt—are designed to keep out mosquitoes, biting midges, leaves, pine needles, and wildlife (frogs, snakes, and alligators) while allowing cooling breezes and sunlight to penetrate. Standard aluminum screen cages cost $10 to $20 per square foot ($9,000 to $18,000 for an average 800-square-foot pool deck).

In northern climates where extending swimming through autumn and winter is desired, homeowners install rigid polycarbonate or double-pane tempered glass enclosures. Retractable motorized telescopic enclosures—featuring arched aluminum arches glazed with multiwall polycarbonate panels that slide open during hot summer days—cost $35,000 to $75,000. Permanent four-season glass solariums with HVAC dehumidification systems cost $60,000 to $120,000+.

Roof Profiles: Flat, Mansard, Gable, and Dome Styles

The engineering geometry of the enclosure roof directly influences structural strength and price. Flat-roof enclosures are the least expensive ($9 to $13 per sq ft), but they collect heavy leaf debris, sag under ponding rainwater, and possess low aesthetic appeal. Mansard roof enclosures—featuring sloped perimeter walls leading to a flat horizontal center—are the industry standard ($12 to $18 per sq ft), maximizing interior headroom and shedding high hurricane winds efficiently.

Gable (A-frame) and dome roof styles cost $15 to $22 per square foot, matching the existing pitch and architectural shingles of the primary residence. In coastal zones subject to hurricane building codes, engineering requirements mandate thicker aluminum purlins, stainless steel tapcon fasteners, and heavy wind cables, adding $1,500 to $3,500 in specialized structural upgrades.

Maintenance, Rescreening, and Insurance Benefits

While an aluminum enclosure requires minimal structural maintenance, the flexible screen mesh naturally degrades from intense ultraviolet radiation, mold accumulation, and storm wind stress. A full pool cage rescreening is typically required every 8 to 12 years, costing $1,800 to $3,500 for a standard 800-square-foot enclosure ($1.50 to $2.50 per square foot of screen surface).

Despite upfront expenses, pool enclosures deliver significant ongoing savings. Enclosures block 90% of airborne leaves, dirt, and organic debris, cutting pool cleaning pump runtimes, reducing chlorine chemical consumption by 40% to 50% ($300 to $600 annual chemical savings), and extending the operating lifespan of pool filters and automatic vacuums.

How to Plan and Build a Pool Enclosure

Follow these five construction steps to design and install a code-compliant pool enclosure.

  1. Determine Desired Seasonal Utility

    Decide whether you want an insect-screening open-air cage ($10-$20/sq ft) or a heated winter glass solarium ($60-$120/sq ft).

  2. Verify Municipal Wind Load and Setback Requirements

    Confirm local building code wind uplift requirements (up to 160 MPH in coastal zones) and property rear setbacks.

  3. Inspect the Existing Concrete Deck Foundation

    Ensure your pool deck concrete is at least 4 inches thick with continuous footings to anchor structural aluminum posts.

  4. Select Specialized Mesh (No-See-Um vs. Florida Glass)

    Choose fine 20x20 micro-mesh if living near coastal marshes, and install solid Florida Glass on bottom panels to block dirt.

  5. Secure Municipal Permits and Engineering Stamps

    Ensure your licensed contractor submits certified wind-engineering calculations to the local building department.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: How much does a typical pool screen enclosure cost?

An aluminum pool screen enclosure typically costs between $8,000 and $22,000 ($10 to $20 per square foot installed).

Q2: How much does a retractable pool enclosure cost?

A motorized or manual retractable polycarbonate pool enclosure costs between $35,000 and $75,000+ depending on size and track height.

Q3: How long do pool screen enclosures last?

The structural aluminum frame lasts 30 to 50+ years; the screen mesh typically requires rescreening every 8 to 12 years.

Q4: Can a pool enclosure withstand hurricane winds?

Engineered enclosures are built to withstand 130 to 160 MPH winds, though screen panels may blow out to relieve structural wind pressure.

Q5: Does a pool enclosure keep the pool warmer or cooler?

Screen enclosures lower water temperature by 2 to 4 degrees by blocking direct solar glare; solid glass/polycarbonate enclosures warm the pool by 10 to 15 degrees.

Q6: How much does it cost to rescreen a pool cage?

Completely rescreening an existing aluminum pool cage costs between $1,800 and $3,500 depending on square footage.

Q7: What is Florida Glass on a pool enclosure?

Florida Glass is a heavy-duty fiberglass mesh laminated with clear vinyl, used on bottom panels to block lawn debris and provide privacy.

Q8: Does a pool enclosure add value to a home?

Yes, especially in southern states like Florida, where homes with quality pool enclosures sell faster and appraise $10,000 to $25,000 higher.
