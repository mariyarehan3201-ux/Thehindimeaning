What Does 102H Mean on a Tire? Load & Speed Guide

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 17, 2026

When shopping for replacement tires or inspecting your vehicle's sidewall markings, you encounter alphanumeric sequences like "P225/65R17 102H." While dimensions like width and rim diameter are familiar to most car owners, the trailing characters—specifically "102H"—are frequently misunderstood. Known in the automotive tire industry as the "Service Description," this critical code dictates the maximum load-carrying capacity and top sustained speed certified for that tire. Understanding what 102H means ensures safe handling, legal compliance, and long tread life.

Tire engineering is an exacting discipline governed by global regulatory bodies including the Tire and Rim Association (TRA) in the United States and the European Tyre and Rim Technical Organisation (ETRTO). When passenger vehicles travel at highway speeds, tires must withstand immense centrifugal forces, mechanical friction, and blistering thermal buildup while supporting thousands of pounds of vehicle weight, cargo, and passengers.

To prevent motorists from overloading tires or driving faster than a tire's physical construction can tolerate, manufacturers stamp a standardized "Service Description" onto the sidewall immediately following the rim diameter. In the marking "102H," the digits "102" represent the Load Index, while the letter "H" designates the Speed Rating.

The Mechanics of the 102 Load Index

The load index is an internationally standardized numerical code that corresponds to the maximum mass or weight a single tire can safely carry at its maximum rated cold inflation pressure.

Load Index Number Pounds (lbs) Per Tire Kilograms (kg) Per Tire Total Capacity Across 4 Tires
100 1,764 lbs 800 kg 7,056 lbs
101 1,819 lbs 825 kg 7,276 lbs
102 1,874 lbs 850 kg 7,496 lbs
103 1,929 lbs 875 kg 7,716 lbs
104 1,984 lbs 900 kg 7,936 lbs

A load index of 102 translates precisely to 1,874 pounds (850 kilograms) per wheel. When four 102-rated tires are installed on a crossover or SUV, the vehicle possesses a maximum gross tire load capacity of 7,496 pounds. This provides ample reserve safety capacity for mid-size vehicles like the Honda CR-V or Subaru Outback, which typically have a curb weight around 3,600 pounds and a Gross Vehicle Weight Rating (GVWR) around 4,800 pounds.

However, tire load capacity is directly dependent on tire pressure. If a tire designed to carry 1,874 pounds at 36 PSI is driven with only 25 PSI, the sidewalls flex excessively. This unmanaged flexing generates extreme internal heat that can cause catastrophic belt separation and sudden highway blowouts.

Decoding the "H" Speed Rating: Thermal Endurance

The trailing letter in the service description indicates the speed rating. In tire testing laboratories, tires are mounted on test wheels and spun against steel drums at escalating speeds in ten-minute increments until failure.

Speed Rating Letter Top Certified Speed (MPH) Top Certified Speed (KM/H) Typical Vehicle Category
S 112 mph 180 km/h Family vans, budget commuter sedans
T 118 mph 190 km/h Standard passenger cars, compact crossovers
U 124 mph 200 km/h Light trucks, mid-range sedans
H 130 mph 210 km/h Sporty crossovers, touring sedans, luxury SUVs
V 149 mph 240 km/h High-performance sport sedans and coupes
W / Y 168 / 186 mph 270 / 300 km/h Exotic supercars and sports performance coupes

The "H" rating occupies a unique historical position in tire naming. Before standardized alphanumeric speed ratings were formalized, tires were categorized simply as S (Standard up to 112 mph) or V (Very High Speed above 130 mph). When engineers developed tires certified for 130 mph, they inserted "H" for "High Speed." Today, H ratings signify superior internal carcass construction, reinforced steel belts, and synthetic cap plies that resist centrifugal deformation and thermal buildup at sustained highway velocities.

How to Read and Verify a Tire Service Description (102H)

  1. Locate the Service Description on the Tire Sidewall

    Find the standardized tire size sequence embossed on the outer sidewall; the two- or three-digit number followed by a single letter appearing immediately after rim size is the service description.

  2. Decode the Load Index Number (102)

    Cross-reference the number "102" on the industry standard Load Index chart, confirming that each individual tire can safely support up to 1,874 pounds (850 kilograms).

  3. Decode the Speed Rating Letter (H)

    Look up the letter "H" on the international Speed Rating table, confirming that the tire is certified for safe continuous highway driving speeds up to 130 mph (210 km/h).

  4. Verify Match Against Driver Door Placard Specifications

    Open the driver door jamb and inspect the Federal Tire Information Placard; never install replacement tires with a load index or speed rating lower than the OEM factory spec.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does 102H mean on a car tire?

102H is the tire's service description: "102" is the load index (1,874 lbs per tire), and "H" is the speed rating (up to 130 mph).

Q2: How much weight can four 102H tires support together?

Four 102-rated tires can support a combined maximum gross vehicle weight of 7,496 pounds (1,874 lbs x 4 tires).

Q3: What is the maximum speed allowed for an H-rated tire?

An "H" speed rating indicates the tire is laboratory tested and certified to sustain speeds up to 130 miles per hour (210 km/h) under proper inflation.

Q4: Can you replace 102H tires with 102V tires?

Yes, you can always install tires with an equal or higher speed rating (V is rated for 149 mph); however, you should never downgrade to a lower rating.

Q5: Can you install tires with a load index lower than 102?

No, installing a lower load index (like 98 or 100) is dangerous, illegal in some states, and can lead to catastrophic tire blowout under vehicle weight.

Q6: What types of vehicles typically use 102H tires?

102H tires are common on mid-size crossovers, compact SUVs, and sedans such as the Subaru Outback, Honda CR-V, Toyota RAV4, and Ford Edge.

Q7: Does cold tire pressure affect the 102 load capacity?

Yes, a tire only achieves its full 1,874 lb capacity when inflated to the maximum cold PSI indicated on the sidewall; underinflation reduces capacity.

Q8: What is the difference between an H rating and a T rating?

A "T" speed rating is certified up to 118 mph (190 km/h), while an "H" rating is certified up to 130 mph (210 km/h), offering better heat dissipation.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

The marking 102H on your tire sidewall is a vital engineering specification that defines your vehicle safe operating limits. With a load rating of 1,874 pounds per tire and an H speed certification for up to 130 mph, 102H tires deliver an ideal balance of robust load-carrying durability, precise steering response, and high-speed thermal stability. When replacing tires, always check your driver door placard to ensure new tires match or exceed the 102H standard for optimal safety.
