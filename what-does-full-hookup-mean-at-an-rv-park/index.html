What Does Full Hookup Mean at an RV Park?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 26, 2026

When booking a campsite at an RV resort, private campground, or state park, the term "Full Hookup" (universally abbreviated as "FHU") indicates that the campsite provides dedicated, individual hookup connections for all three essential utilities: city potable water, electrical power, and municipal or septic sewer drainage directly at your specific parking pad.

The Three Core Utilities of a Full Hookup Campsite

Traveling in a recreational vehicle offers the freedom of the open road combined with the comforts of home, but those amenities rely on utilities. A Full Hookup site eliminates the need to conserve limited onboard battery banks or constantly monitor holding tank capacities. Instead of packing up your motorhome or travel trailer to visit a centralized dump station every few days, an FHU site allows continuous, residential-style living.

The first component is electrical power, provided via an outdoor utility pedestal. These pedestals feature standard 120-volt household outlets alongside dedicated 30-amp and 50-amp RV receptacles. The second component is pressurized city water, connected via a standard threaded brass spigot that supplies continuous water pressure directly to your RV's plumbing fixtures without engaging your noisy onboard 12-volt water pump. The third component is a dedicated in-ground sewer drain pipe, allowing gray water (from sinks and showers) and black water (from the toilet) to be drained cleanly and safely.

Electrical and Plumbing Specifications at Full Hookup Sites

Not every full hookup campsite is identical, and matching your rig's physical requirements to the campsite's infrastructure prevents electrical overloads and plumbing mishaps. Electrical connections come in two primary configurations: 30-amp (120-volt service delivering up to 3,600 watts) and 50-amp (120/240-volt split-phase service delivering up to 12,000 watts). Modern motorhomes and fifth wheels with multiple rooftop air conditioners require 50-amp service to run appliances concurrently.

Plumbing connections also demand careful management. Municipal water spigots frequently produce water pressures ranging between 60 and 100 PSI, which can rupture fragile PEX tubing and plastic fittings inside an RV. Experienced RVers always attach an inline water pressure regulator calibrated to maintain 45 to 55 PSI before connecting their drinking-water hose.

The Critical Rule of Black Tank Valve Management

One of the most dangerous rookie mistakes made at full hookup campsites is leaving the black water (toilet waste) dump valve open while parked. While it seems intuitive to let waste drain out continuously like residential plumbing, an RV holding tank requires liquid volume to flush solids away. If the black valve remains open, liquids drain out immediately while solid waste accumulates directly below the toilet pipe, forming a solid blockage known in the RV community as a "poop pyramid."

To avoid severe plumbing clogs, experienced campers keep both the black and gray tank valves securely closed during their stay at an FHU site. When the black holding tank reaches approximately two-thirds capacity, you open the black valve to let the rushing liquid carry all solids down the sewer hose, followed by opening the gray valve to rinse the hose clean with soapy shower and sink water.

How to Safely Connect Your RV to Full Hookup Utilities

Follow these step-by-step instructions to safely hook up electricity, fresh water, and sewer lines at an RV campsite.

  1. Inspect and Level Your RV on the Pad

    Park your RV on the campsite pad, verify reach to the utility pedestal, and deploy your leveling jacks or wheel chocks securely.

  2. Test and Connect Electrical Power

    Ensure the pedestal breaker is switched OFF, plug in your surge protector to verify clean voltage, connect your RV power cord, and flip the breaker ON.

  3. Connect Potable Water with a Regulator

    Attach a brass pressure regulator and inline sediment filter to the campsite spigot, connect a certified drinking-water hose, and open the valve.

  4. Attach the Sewer Hose and Secure the Seal

    Connect your collapsible sewer hose to the RV waste outlet, secure the clear elbow adapter into the campground sewer port, and keep tank valves CLOSED.

  5. Verify Interior Systems and Appliances

    Turn off your RV's 12-volt water pump, purge air from the faucets, and confirm that your air conditioning and microwave are receiving stable power.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does FHU mean in RV camping?

FHU stands for Full Hookup, indicating a campsite that provides dedicated electricity, potable water, and sewer connections at the site.

Q2: Can I leave my RV gray water valve open at a full hookup site?

Yes, many campers leave the gray tank valve open during showers, but remember to close it a day before departure to collect rinse water for dumping.

Q3: Why should you never leave the black tank valve open at an FHU site?

Leaving the black valve open causes liquids to drain away while solid waste builds up and hardens into a stubborn clog inside the holding tank.

Q4: Is full hookup more expensive than partial hookup?

Yes, full hookup campsites generally cost $10 to $25 more per night than partial hookup sites due to added convenience and sewer infrastructure.

Q5: Do all full hookup sites offer 50-amp power?

Not all; some older campgrounds provide only 30-amp service, so verify pedestal amperages with the campground before booking.

Q6: Do I need a water pressure regulator at a full hookup site?

Yes, city water pressure at campgrounds can exceed 80-100 PSI, which can burst internal RV plumbing lines without a 45-55 PSI regulator.

Q7: Does full hookup include free Wi-Fi and cable TV?

Some upscale RV resorts include cable TV hookups and Wi-Fi within their FHU package, but it varies significantly by park.

Q8: Can small camper vans use full hookup campsites?

Yes, any recreational vehicle equipped with standard RV utility connections can utilize a full hookup site, regardless of vehicle size.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Booking a full hookup campsite transforms RV travel into a seamless, resort-style experience. With continuous electrical power to run air conditioning and kitchen appliances, limitless fresh water for showers and cooking, and on-site sewer disposal, FHU sites provide maximum comfort. By investing in essential protection gear?"such as surge protectors, water regulators, and sturdy sewer hoses?"you can enjoy all the conveniences of home wherever your travels take you.
