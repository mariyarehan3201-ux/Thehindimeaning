How Long Does a Divorce Take in Georgia?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 15, 2026

Understanding how long does a divorce take in georgia is an essential practical and legal consideration for spouses preparing to dissolve a marriage in the Peach State. The timeline to finalize a Georgia divorce ranges dramatically from an absolute statutory minimum of 31 days for a seamless, uncontested divorce, to 12 to 24 months for high-conflict contested divorces involving complex business valuations, alimony disputes, or child custody trials in Georgia Superior Court.

The 31-Day Statutory Minimum: Uncontested Divorces

Under Georgia law (O.C.G.A. Section 19-5-3 and Uniform Superior Court Rule 24.6), the absolute fastest a divorce can legally be finalized is 31 days. In a truly uncontested divorce, both spouses execute a comprehensive, signed Marital Settlement Agreement resolving every single issue—including equitable division of marital property, marital debts, alimony, child custody schedules, and child support calculations using Georgia's mandatory child support worksheet.

The respondent spouse signs an official 'Acknowledgment of Service' and waiver of summons, which is filed with the county Superior Court clerk. Under Georgia statutory rules, a judge cannot sign the final decree of divorce until at least 31 days have elapsed from the exact date the acknowledgment of service was filed. In practice, depending on the county Superior Court judge's caseload and motion docket, uncontested divorces are typically signed within 45 to 60 days.

Contested Divorces: The 6-Month Discovery Phase and Temporary Hearings

When spouses cannot agree on terms, the divorce enters the contested litigation track. The process begins when the petitioning spouse files a Petition for Divorce and has the respondent formally served by the county sheriff or a private process server. The respondent has 30 days to file a formal Answer.

Once the answer is filed, the Georgia Civil Practice Act triggers a mandatory 6-month 'Discovery Period.' During discovery, attorneys exchange written interrogatories, demand bank and tax production, issue third-party subpoenas, and depose both spouses under oath. If financial emergency or child safety issues exist, either party can request a 'Temporary Hearing' within 45 to 60 days of filing, where a judge enters temporary orders for child support, alimony, and exclusive home possession.

Mandatory Court-Ordered Mediation and Georgia Jury Trials

Nearly every judicial circuit in Georgia mandates that contested divorce litigants attend Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) mediation before a judge will grant a final trial date. In court-ordered mediation, spouses and their attorneys meet with a certified neutral mediator for a full-day session. Over 80% of contested divorces in Georgia successfully settle during mediation, eliminating months of courtroom litigation and thousands in trial fees.

Uniquely, Georgia is one of the only states in the entire country that still permits a Jury Trial in divorce cases for property division and alimony (though child custody must always be decided solely by the judge). Demanding a 12-person jury trial dramatically extends the divorce timeline by an additional 6 to 12 months due to jury selection, pre-trial motions, and crowded criminal trial dockets.

How to Navigate the Georgia Divorce Process Efficiently

Follow these five strategic steps to resolve a divorce in Georgia as quickly as possible.

  1. Confirm Georgia Statutory Residency

    Ensure at least one spouse has lived in Georgia for at least 6 consecutive months prior to filing.

  2. Attempt to Reach an Uncontested Settlement

    Negotiate property, custody, and child support terms to utilize Georgia's fast 31-day uncontested track.

  3. Complete Mandatory Financial Affidavits and Child Worksheets

    Fill out the state-mandated Domestic Relations Financial Affidavit (DRFA) and child support schedules.

  4. Engage in Professional Court-Ordered Mediation

    Work with a certified mediator to resolve sticking points and avoid a lengthy 12-month trial docket.

  5. Submit Final Decree for Superior Court Judge Signature

    Present the signed agreement and proposed final judgment for the judge's signature in Superior Court.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: What is the fastest a divorce can be finalized in Georgia?

The absolute fastest timeline is 31 days from the date the acknowledgment of service is filed in an uncontested divorce.

Q2: How long does a contested divorce take in Georgia?

A contested divorce typically takes 6 to 18 months, extending to 2 years or longer if a full custody trial is required.

Q3: Does Georgia have a mandatory separation period before filing?

No, Georgia has no mandatory separation waiting period; spouses only need to be 'separated' (suspending marital relations, even under the same roof).

Q4: What is the residency requirement for divorce in Georgia?

At least one spouse must have been a bona fide legal resident of Georgia for at least 6 months immediately preceding filing.

Q5: Can you have a jury trial for a divorce in Georgia?

Yes, Georgia is unique in allowing jury trials for property division and alimony disputes, though custody is decided strictly by a judge.

Q6: Is mediation mandatory in Georgia divorces?

Yes, nearly all Georgia judicial circuits mandate court-ordered mediation before scheduling a contested final trial.

Q7: How much does a divorce cost in Georgia?

Uncontested divorces cost $500 to $2,500 in total legal and filing fees; contested divorces standardly range from $7,500 to $30,000+ per spouse.

Q8: Can you date while separated during a Georgia divorce?

Dating before the final decree is technically adultery under Georgia law, which can impact alimony claims and child custody determinations.
