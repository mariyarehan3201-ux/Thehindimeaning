Asbestos Siding Removal Companies

Informational
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: October 1, 2026

Engaging professional asbestos siding removal companies is an essential safety requirement when upgrading exterior cladding on residential homes and commercial structures built before 1980. Exterior mineral-fiber shingles, commonly manufactured under trade names like Transite, contain dense chrysotile fibers pressed into portland cement. While durable and weather-resistant, aging shingles become brittle, crack, and release hazardous particulates if unbolted, sawed, or handled improperly during renovation.

Hiring reputable asbestos siding removal companies is crucial for homeowners and property managers planning exterior home improvement or re-siding projects. Cement-asbestos shingles were installed on millions of mid-century suburban homes because of their exceptional fire resistance and longevity. However, when these shingles become chipped or broken during siding upgrades, choosing licensed abatement specialists over standard exterior carpenters ensures environmental safety, echoing regulatory practices seen in asbestos siding removal nj regional projects.

Material Properties of Transite Siding and Disturbance Risks

Exterior cementitious siding typically contains between twelve and thirty percent chrysotile asbestos tightly bound within a hydraulic cement matrix. Environmental regulations categorize intact siding shingles as Category II non-friable materials. This means that as long as the shingles remain unbroken and are removed whole, the risk of airborne fiber release remains relatively low. However, dropping shingles from ladders, using circular power saws, or driving nails through panels instantly pulverizes the cement, generating hazardous friable dust.

Working with specialized asbestos removal companies guarantees that crews utilize non-destructive manual unfastening methods. Technicians carefully extract fastener nails with specialized pry bars rather than smashing panels, protecting both the workers and neighboring properties from hazardous dust dispersion.

Exterior Abatement Procedures and Ground Containment

Professional abatement teams execute exterior removal under strict containment protocols. Technicians spread heavy six-mil polyethylene drop cloths along the building perimeter, extending at least ten feet out from the foundation walls to catch any falling chips or fastener debris. Prior to touching any shingle, workers mist the surface with surfactant-treated amended water to suppress dust generation. Understanding the techniques of asbestos siding removal demonstrates that wetting shingles prevents microscopic particles from floating away on outdoor wind currents.

Packaging, Waste Transport, and Re-Siding Alternatives

Once shingles are detached from the sheathing, technicians wrap stacks in two layers of heavy six-mil polyethylene plastic, seal them with duct tape, and label them with official OSHA warning notices. Many homeowners also evaluate whether total removal is necessary, as building codes often permit installing extruded polystyrene insulation board and new vinyl or fiber cement siding directly over undisturbed, intact asbestos shingles, saving thousands of dollars in disposal expenses.

How to Choose and Oversee an Asbestos Siding Removal Contractor

Follow these five practical steps to select an accredited exterior abatement company and ensure a safe, compliant siding project.

  1. Verify Contractor Licensing and Environmental Insurance

    Ensure the contractor holds a valid state asbestos abatement license and carries dedicated pollution liability coverage for exterior remediation.

  2. Require Perimeter Ground Polyethylene Containment

    Confirm that the work plan specifies laying 6-mil plastic drop cloths at least 10 feet out from the foundation to collect all falling debris.

  3. Ensure Wet Manual Extraction Methods Are Specified

    Verify that workers will continuously mist shingles with amended water and pry nails manually without using high-speed saws or power grinders.

  4. Inspect Plastic Wrapping and Staging on Pallets

    Observe that removed shingles are stacked carefully, double-wrapped in 6-mil poly, sealed with duct tape, and marked with OSHA warning labels.

  5. Obtain Final Waste Manifests and Clean Sheathing Sign-Off

    Inspect the bare exterior wall sheathing with the supervisor and secure copies of signed hazardous waste landfill delivery manifests.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Can standard roofing or siding contractors remove asbestos siding?

No, general siding contractors are not licensed or insured to handle hazardous materials; removing asbestos siding legally requires an accredited abatement contractor.

Q2: Is it safe to side over existing asbestos siding?

Yes, many building codes allow encapsulating intact shingles beneath foam insulation board and new vinyl siding, avoiding expensive removal and disposal costs.

Q3: How can I tell if my exterior siding shingles contain asbestos?

Transite shingles from the 1940s to 1970s are rigid, fireproof, often feature a wavy bottom edge or woodgrain texture, and require lab PLM analysis for confirmation.

Q4: Can asbestos siding be pressure washed before painting?

No, pressure washing asbestos siding is illegal under EPA regulations because high-pressure water streams blast toxic fibers into the air and surrounding soil.

Q5: How much does it cost to remove asbestos siding from an entire house?

Full removal on an average-sized single-family home typically ranges from $5,000 to $12,000, averaging between $4 and $9 per square foot.

Q6: What protective gear do exterior siding abatement workers wear?

Workers must wear NIOSH-approved P100 half-mask respirators, disposable hooded Tyvek protective suits, heavy work gloves, and eye protection.

Q7: How is removed asbestos siding transported to the landfill?

Shingles are stacked, double-wrapped in 6-mil polyethylene sheeting, sealed with duct tape, labeled with generator data, and hauled in enclosed trucks.

Q8: Do I need a city permit to remove asbestos siding?

Yes, almost all municipal building departments require a specialized environmental permit and formal notification before exterior asbestos siding can be removed.

Final Thoughts & Key Takeaways

Selecting experienced asbestos siding removal companies guarantees that exterior renovations proceed safely, efficiently, and in full compliance with environmental laws. By ensuring that contractors utilize ground drop cloths, wet unfastening techniques, and certified hazardous waste transport, homeowners protect their families, neighbors, and community air quality. Investing in licensed environmental specialists eliminates toxic liabilities and paves the way for modern, attractive exterior siding upgrades.
