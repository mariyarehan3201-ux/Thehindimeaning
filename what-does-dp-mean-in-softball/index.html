Defining the DP in Softball: The Designated Player Rule

In competitive fastpitch softball—governed by USA Softball (formerly ASA), the NCAA, and the National Federation of State High School Associations (NFHS)—the abbreviation DP stands for Designated Player. The DP is an official lineup designation for a player who bats in the batting order for another player, but does not necessarily play defense in the field.

The Designated Player rule is the fastpitch softball evolution of baseball's Designated Hitter (DH) rule, but with far greater tactical flexibility. Introduced in 2004 to replace the older 'Designated Hitter' rule, the DP rule is intrinsically paired with a defensive specialist known as the 'Flex' player. Together, the DP/Flex partnership allows softball coaches to optimize team offensive firepower and defensive fielding simultaneously.

Strategic Synergy: Substitution Mechanics and the DP/Flex Dance

The tactical brilliance of the DP/Flex rule lies in its substitution versatility. Unlike baseball's rigid DH rule, a softball DP can play defense at any time. The coach can place the DP in the field for any of the eight defensive players other than the Flex, and that player continues to bat in their original batting spot without leaving the game.

Furthermore, the DP can play defense for the Flex player. When the DP takes the field on defense for the Flex, the Flex player leaves the game, and the lineup temporarily shrinks from ten players to nine. Conversely, the Flex player can bat, but only in the specific batting slot originally occupied by the DP. Because both the DP and Flex possess re-entry rights under high school and collegiate rules, coaches execute sophisticated lineup maneuvers to maximize pinch running and specialty defense.

Mastering the nuances of the Designated Player rule provides softball coaches with a powerful strategic tool to control game tempo and maximize roster talent.

How to Properly Set Up and Manage a DP/Flex Lineup Card

Ensure roster compliance when exchanging lineup cards at the pre-game umpire conference.

  1. List the DP Within the First Nine Batting Spots

    Write the Designated Player's name and jersey number in their designated batting order slot (spots 1 through 9).

  2. List the Flex in the Tenth Spot on the Card

    Place the Flex player's name in spot number 10, specifying their defensive fielding position (e.g., Pitcher or Shortstop).

  3. Confirm Both Players are Listed at the Pre-Game Meeting

    Hand the official lineup card to the home plate umpire, confirming that the DP and Flex are explicitly paired before the first pitch.

  4. Notify the Umpire of All In-Game Role Switches

    Inform the home plate umpire whenever the Flex enters to bat or run, or when the DP takes the field defensively for the Flex.

  5. Track Re-Entry Rights for Both Starters Carefully

    Remember that both starting DP and Flex players have one legal re-entry right under NFHS and NCAA regulations.

Frequently Asked Questions (7 Questions Answered)

Q1: What does DP mean in softball?

DP stands for Designated Player, a player who bats in the lineup for another player (the Flex) but does not have to play defense.

Q2: What is the difference between DP and DH in softball?

The DH (Designated Hitter) can only bat, while the DP (Designated Player) can play defense for any player on the field at any time.

Q3: Can the DP play defense in softball?

Yes. The DP can play defense for any player on the field, including the Flex player.

Q4: Can the Flex player bat in softball?

The Flex can only bat in the exact batting spot occupied by the DP, which temporarily removes the DP from the game.

Q5: How many players are in a softball lineup with a DP?

There are 10 players listed on the lineup card (9 batting spots plus the Flex in the 10th spot), but only 9 players bat.

Q6: What happens when the DP plays defense for the Flex?

The Flex leaves the game defensively, and the lineup shrinks from 10 players to 9 players while the DP continues to bat.

Q7: Can a team add a DP after the game starts?

No. The DP and Flex must be designated on the official lineup card presented to the umpire before the game begins.
