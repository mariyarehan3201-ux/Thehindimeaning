Does a General Dentist Do Root Canals?

Q&A
Published by TheHindiMeaning Admin•Updated: September 12, 2026

Severe, throbbing tooth pain caused by deep bacterial decay or trauma often signals that the living dental pulp inside your tooth is irreversibly inflamed or infected. In this painful situation, a root canal procedure (endodontic therapy) is required to save the natural tooth and eradicate the infection. When scheduling treatment, patients frequently ask whether their regular general dentist can perform a root canal or if seeing a specialized endodontist is mandatory. The answer is yes: licensed general dentists are legally and clinically trained to perform root canals, though complex cases are routinely referred to endodontic specialists.

Dental Education and the Scope of General Dentistry

In the United States, general dentists graduate with either a Doctor of Dental Surgery (DDS) or Doctor of Dental Medicine (DMD) degree from an accredited dental school. The comprehensive four-year doctoral curriculum includes intensive didactic and clinical training in endodontics. Dental students study pulpal histology, learn to read periapical radiographs, and perform endodontic procedures on human patients under the direct supervision of board-certified endodontists.

Upon passing the national dental board examinations and clinical state licensing exams, a general dentist is legally licensed to perform the full spectrum of dental procedures—including cleanings, crowns, extractions, and root canal therapy. Many general dentists thoroughly enjoy performing endodontics and regularly invest in advanced continuing education courses and modern rotary endodontic equipment to treat routine root canals in their everyday practice.

Anterior Teeth Versus Molars: When Complexity Dictates Referral

The anatomical complexity of the diseased tooth is the primary factor that dictates whether a general dentist will treat you or refer you to an endodontist. Front teeth (incisors and canines) and premolars are anatomically straightforward: they typically feature a single, straight, wide root canal with easily accessible pulp chambers. Most general dentists comfortably perform root canals on these anterior teeth with outstanding success rates.

In contrast, posterior molars represent high anatomical complexity. Upper and lower molars feature three to four narrow, severely curved, or calcified root canals, often hiding elusive accessory canals (such as the notorious MB2 canal in maxillary first molars). If a general dentist encounters calcified roots, severely curved roots, a previous failed root canal requiring retreatment, or active root resorption, they will refer the patient to an endodontist.

The Comprehensive Care Advantage: Root Canal and Crown Under One Roof

Having your general dentist perform your root canal offers significant logistical and financial convenience. Following a root canal, the hollowed-out tooth becomes brittle and structurally vulnerable to biting forces, requiring a permanent core buildup and a full-coverage porcelain or zirconia dental crown to prevent catastrophic tooth fracture. When a general dentist performs the root canal, they can often complete the core buildup and crown preparation during the same appointment.

This unified workflow eliminates the need to travel between multiple dental offices, submit paperwork to different insurance networks, or endure weeks of chewing on a fragile temporary filling. However, if your dentist evaluates your diagnostic X-ray and recommends an endodontist, heed their advice: general dentists refer patients to specialists precisely because they prioritize your long-term tooth preservation over convenience.

How to Decide Who Should Do Your Root Canal in 5 Steps

Follow these five practical steps to determine whether your general dentist or an endodontist should perform your root canal.

  1. Undergo Diagnostic Dental X-Rays and Pulp Testing

    Have your general dentist take digital periapical radiographs and perform cold and percussion tests on the painful tooth.

  2. Ask Your Dentist About Tooth Anatomy and Curvature

    Ask your dentist how many root canals the tooth contains and whether the roots exhibit severe curvatures or calcification.

  3. Inquire About the Dentist's Experience with That Specific Tooth

    Ask how many root canals your dentist performs each month and whether they regularly treat second and third molars.

  4. Check In-Network Dental Insurance Coverage

    Review your dental insurance benefits to verify copays and deductibles for endodontic therapy under general vs specialist coverage.

  5. Request an Endodontic Referral If Complexity Is High

    If the tooth is a complex molar or a retreatment, request a referral to a local board-certified endodontist.

Frequently Asked Questions (8 Questions Answered)

Q1: Is a root canal painful?

With modern local anesthesia, a root canal is virtually painless; the procedure actually relieves the severe pain caused by pulpal infection.

Q2: Why do dentists refer root canals to endodontists?

Dentists refer cases involving severely curved roots, calcified canals, retreatments, or high diagnostic complexity that require surgical microscopes.

Q3: What is an endodontist?

An endodontist is a dental specialist who completed two to three additional years of post-doctoral residency training focused exclusively on root canals.

Q4: Do you always need a crown after a root canal?

Posterior molars and premolars almost always require a permanent crown to prevent tooth fracture; front incisors with minimal decay may only need a resin filling.

Q5: Does a specialist cost more for a root canal?

Yes, endodontists typically charge 20 to 35 percent more than general dentists due to advanced surgical microscopes and specialty training.

Q6: How long does a root canal take?

A routine root canal performed by a general dentist or endodontist typically takes 60 to 90 minutes, often completed in a single visit.

Q7: What happens if a root canal fails?

If an infection persists due to a missed canal or fracture, an endodontist can perform an endodontic retreatment or an apicoectomy to save the tooth.

Q8: Can a general dentist do a molar root canal?

Yes, many experienced general dentists routinely perform molar root canals, provided the root anatomy is reasonably straight and accessible.
